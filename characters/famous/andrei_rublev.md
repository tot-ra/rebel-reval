# Andrei Rublev (c. 1360-1370 - c. 1427/1430)

**Confidence label:** `attested` (later historical person; not present in the 1343 game timeline)

**Visual Description:**
Not depicted. Rublev appears in the game only as a name in rumours decades after 1343. Any future portrait must follow the established iconographic tradition (slight, bearded monk of the Moscow school) and is art-tier gated.

**Motivations:**
N/A in 1343. The historical painter's known devotion, humility, and pursuit of a serene, luminous sacred image style are the reference points for the rumour's tone: a gentle man who painted light.

**Ties & Relationships:**
- **Legend wink:** The game's invented Novgorod girl [**Anisia of Novgorod**](../../docs/CHARACTERS/anisia_of_novgorod.md) (Ivan's daughter, "of Rubl") dreams in 1343 of a future son named Andrei. The game never claims she is his mother; the rumour a player may hear years later is that a great painter named Andrei, son of a board-maker, came from the Novgorod road.
- **Historical contemporaries (background only):** Theophanes the Greek and Prokhor of Gorodets are the older masters of his youth in the traditional account; in the game they exist only as Reval rumours and archive sheets.

**History (Biography):**
The historical Andrei Rublev, the greatest of the Moscow school of icon painting, was born around 1360-1370 (tradition once placed it near Moscow; modern scholarship is uncertain) and died around 1427/1430 as a monk. Nothing is recorded about his parents or birthplace, which is exactly the gap the game plays in: it lets the player suspect a Reval-Novgorod origin without ever asserting one. Sources differ on whether "Andrei" was his monastic name; the game's hint therefore goes through the saint (the apostle Andrew), not through a birth-name claim.

**Scene Presence:**
None in 1343. Possible late-campaign framing: a Novgorod rumour fragment (journal entry or overheard line) about "Andrei the board-maker's son" who became the Tsar's painter. Non-canonical for the legend unless a task promotes it.

**Possible Quest Lines:**
- **Rumour of the North (planned, act-gated):** After Anisia's side quest resolves, a Novgorod trader in Reval mentions a painter in Moscow called "Andrei Rublyov" and jokes that Novgorod girls dream too big. The player may connect it to Anisia; the game stays silent.
- **The Ochre Keepsake:** Anisia's pigment pouch (reward of her quest) is described years later as "the ochre of a Novgorod board-maker's daughter" - a faint material echo of the legend.

**Worldview and Philosophy:**
The historical Rublev's art rejected harshness in favour of harmony, gentleness, and light - "the art of the Trinity", as his contemporaries called it. The game mirrors this only in tone: Anisia's small prayer that her future son "paint things people can hold in their hearts".

**Major Creative Works (historical, background only):**
The Trinity (c. 1411) and the frescoes of the Dormition Cathedral in Vladimir and the Zvenigorod cathedral. Referenced only if a rumour line names "the painter of the Trinity"; never shown or recreated in-game.
