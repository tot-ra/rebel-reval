# Social life, daily rhythm, meeting places and travel (Spring 1343 Reval)

Task **R-1967** (SOC-1, epic R-1966). Synthesis dossier: it builds on the project dossiers listed under Cross-references and adds the night, drinking-house, guild-house and travel evidence that the city runtime needs. Confidence labels follow [`docs/CANON.md`](../../../docs/CANON.md): `attested` (a Reval or Livonian source), `plausible composite` (Hanseatic or Baltic analogy), `later comparandum` (evidence after 1343, usable only labelled), `exclude` (anachronistic for April 1343). Project period rule: dated later than 1343 is excluded; an unknown 1343 state takes the simplified later form, labelled.

## Brief for Dev / Map / Character

Stage Reval's social life as **a bell-paced working day with short, dense social moments**: water and bread at dawn, work at the door, dinner at home, a drink or a guild gathering after vespers, and everyone indoors when the night bell rings. Social life is **organised by belonging** (household, parish, guild, ethnicity, ship crew), not by entertainment venues.

**Ship these decisions:**

1. **The day runs on church bells, not clocks.** Before the first public hour-clocks (Holy Spirit 1434, St Nicholas 1460) craft rules counted time from the friary bells: work from the morning mass to the evening *Ave Maria* bell `attested` (stonemasons' *Schragen* 1402, later comparandum for 1343) [1][2]. Use bell events as the schedule's anchors: dawn mass, midday, vespers, Ave Maria, night bell.
2. **Night rest begins at the 9 o'clock bell.** Reval and Riga *Burspraken* set the start of night rest at nine; whoever walks the streets after the bell is taken home (a lord) or locked up (a commoner) `attested` late 14th c. (Reval Bursprake, LUB 4 no. 1516 §80) - for April 1343 `plausible composite` [1]. Beer-shops and wine rooms close at the bell; the watch checks them [1].
3. **Drinking houses exist, but they are houses.** The earliest record of a *krug* (kõrts) in Tallinn is **1280** `attested` (Eesti rahvakultuuri leksikon) [3]. In 1343 the town's beer is sold **from brewers' and alewives' houses** (*kroch*, beer-shop) and wine from the **council wine cellar** under the town hall (*cellarium civitatis*, recorded 1322) `attested` [3][4]. Do not build a free-standing "tavern" with a painted sign board; mark a drinking house by a **bush or wreath on a pole, open diele door, benches and barrels** `plausible composite`.
4. **Guild houses are the evening clubs of the better-off.** St Canute's guild held its own house from **1326**, St Olaf's from **1341** `attested` [5]. Their *drunken* (drinking feasts) and ordinary gatherings happened there; the buildings were the predecessors of Pikk 20 and Pikk 24, **not** the 15th-19th c. halls standing today. The merchants' *Kindergilde* had no hall of its own yet (Great Guild hall 1407-10) `exclude` hall; the merchants gather in the town hall rooms and their own dielen `plausible composite` [5][6].
5. **The suburb krug is where journeymen go.** Journeymen "liked to spend time in the suburb krugs" and had to be back before the gates closed; sleeping outside the walls was fined `attested` 15th c. (later comparandum) [1]. Place one beer-house outside a gate (Viru or Karja suburb) that fills before gate-closing and empties fast.
6. **Saturday is bath day.** German burghers bathe weekly, usually Saturday before Sunday mass, at city-owned baths (Nunne 1310, by St Olaf 1329, Sauna street) `plausible composite` on attested bath sites [7][8]. The bath is a talking place for men and women of the same status; Estonian labourers bathe less often in town.
7. **Sunday is the week's social peak.** Mass at the parish church, news and proclamations at the church door, meat at dinner, visits to kin, a longer drink in the afternoon `plausible composite` [9][10]. No routine trade on Sundays and the high feasts [9].
8. **Ship crews sleep aboard.** "Every shipman shall be on his ship at night" (Reval Bursprake c. 1360, 8 öre fine) `attested` [1]. Foreign sailors drink at the harbour during the day and are not in town after the bell.
9. **Feasts move the night bell.** On feasts, betrothals and weddings night rest was extended to about **11** `later comparandum` (Reval luxury ordinances 1497, 1533; Riga Blackheads 1510) [1]. Use this for wedding and guild-feast evenings only.
10. **Do not show:** the Blackheads (1399/1400) and their May-count or popinjay shooting (15th c.), the Great Guild hall, a council pharmacy (1422), public hour-clocks, a modern pub interior with counter and menu, women serving as entertainment, or masked Shrovetide street dancing (Riga ban 1384, Reval 15th c.) in an April scene `exclude` [1][5][11].

### 3. Drinking houses and their customers

- **Beer:** a daily food-drink for all; brewers' and alewives' houses sell by the can and let customers sit on the diele benches `plausible composite` [10][19]. The census has 39 brewers, 29 alewives and 26 innkeepers: their houses are the town's beer-shops.
- **Wine:** imported, expensive; the council held the wine cellar under the town hall (`cellarium civitatis`, 1322) and later the wine trade monopoly; 15th-c. fines for selling wine after nine show wine rooms ran as evening venues `attested` cellar / `later comparandum` evening trade [1][4].
- **Krug outside the walls:** first Tallinn krug 1280; country krugs on roads only from the 15th c. `attested` [3]. A suburb krug at the Viru or Karja road is `plausible composite` for 1343; a rural roadside krug in Harju is `later comparandum`.
- **Guests:** Hanseatic guest merchants lodged with Reval burghers (*Wirt*, host), not in inns; a burgher host is the "innkeeper" of a long-distance trader `plausible composite` [14]. The census `innkeeper` trade is read as host or beer-seller.
- **Who drinks where:** status and language sort customers. Council and merchants: wine cellar and own houses. Masters: guild house, better beer-shops. Journeymen, sailors, porters, Estonian labourers: beer-shops near the harbour and gates, suburb krug. Respectable women drink at home, at weddings and at the bath; alewives and their maids serve `plausible composite` [1][10].
- **Trouble:** brawls, gambling and after-hours drinking are the commonest night offences in the fine books (1436: a krug kept open until midnight, fine 20 öre) `attested` 15th c. [1]. Church preaching links the tavern with sin (useful tone for *Bitter Brew*) [10].

### 5. Baths

Public baths at Nunne (1310), by St Olaf (1329) and on Sauna street are attested sites; Saturday is the bath day of the German burghers; baths are places to talk, shave and be bled `plausible composite` [7][8]. The game already has `poi.bath.nunne`; the other two bath sites are candidates.

### 6. Night life

Legal night life = feasting indoors before nine (later eleven at feasts) and the watch. Illegal night life = drinking after the bell, gaming, night trade, sleeping outside the walls; masked running at Shrovetide (banned in Riga 1384) `attested`/`later comparandum` [1]. Prostitution: a Reval brothel or "women's house" before 1343 is not documented in the sources reviewed - **do not stage it** `exclude` until evidence. Bakers, brewers and the watch are the only routine night workers `plausible composite` [1].

April 1343 is **post-Easter (Easter 13 April)**: wedding season has ended, Lent is over, the town is in a working spring with Easter feasting just behind it; St George's Day (23 April) is a feast without a known market closure [9].

### 8. Travel between towns

- **Who travels:** long-distance merchants and their servants (Lübeck, Visby, Riga, Dorpat, Novgorod), skippers and sailors, council envoys and messengers, clerics (Dorpat, Riga, Rome on appeal), pilgrims, journeymen on their wander, Harju peasants coming to market `attested` types [14][16].
- **Novgorod:** twice-yearly convoys (*Winterfahrer* and *Sommerfahrer*); sea leg Reval - Neva - Ladoga - Volkhov; land alternatives via Narva or Dorpat-Pskov `attested` [14]. In April the winter travellers are back and the summer convoy is forming [14].
- **Sea:** Reval to Lübeck took from about **4 weeks** to, in winter, **4 months** `attested` (Hanse historiography) [22]. The Gulf ice breaks up in April; the first sailings are risky; ships winter at Reval [14][17].
- **Land:** winter was the season of travel and peasant trade, when frozen bogs made direct sledge roads; spring thaw (April) is the **worst** time for land travel `attested` (Kreem 2011; Põltsam-Jürjo 2014) [1][23]. Overland Riga is about a week, Dorpat 3-4 days `plausible composite`.
- **On an April day in town:** a few merchants are away (Lübeck, Gotland, Novgorod), a few foreign guests lodge with burghers, ship crews wait for open water, peasants arrive at dawn and leave before the gates close, messengers ride to the Order and to Dorpat. After 23 April the land roads close to normal traffic (uprising) [14].

## Production hooks

- **Dev:** add a `social` layer to the citizen timetables: evening venue by status/trade/ethnicity, Saturday bath, Sunday mass-and-visit day, night bell at 21:00, feast evenings to 23:00; keep positions a pure function of (date, hour). Venues are places compiled from census households (beer-shops) and plan buildings (guild houses, town hall cellar).
- **Map:** designate existing plan houses as the two guild houses on Pikk and as beer-shops; add a dressing kit (bush on a pole, benches, barrels) at their doors; one suburb krug on the Viru road; a harbour beer booth.
- **Art:** venue dressing props and two interiors (diele beer-shop, guild hall with trestle tables) - each needs its own task and `assets/SOURCES.csv` rows (asset freeze P0-040).
- **Character / Dialogue:** innkeeper, alewife, guild alderman, watchman, bath keeper get venue lines; drunk-after-the-bell bark for the watch.
- **Quest:** the beer-shop is the natural rumour hub for *Bitter Brew*; the night bell is a stealth timer.
