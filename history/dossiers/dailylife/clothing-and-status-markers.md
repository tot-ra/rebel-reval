# Clothing and status markers (Spring 1343 Reval)

## Brief for Art / Character

You are dressing **April–May 1343 Lower Town Reval** — a **Hanseatic port under Danish rule** with a **German merchant elite**, a **large Estonian labour force**, **hill knights and clergy on Toompea**, and **Order brothers** arriving after late April. **No Reval tailor's invoice or wardrobe inventory survives for 1343**; every silhouette below is built from **Nordic textile finds**, **German manuscript comparanda**, and **population-law context**, each labelled.

**Ship these decisions:**

1. **Base male kit (all tiers):** linen **shirt** (*Hemd*) and wool **braies** (*Bruch*), then wool **hose** (*Hose*) tied to a waist belt, then a wool **tunic** (*Rock*, *Kirtel*) — hem length and fabric quality signal rank [1][2][3]. **No trousers, no Renaissance doublet, no printed cotton.**
2. **German burgher / councillor:** mid-calf to knee tunic in **fine fulled wool** or **scarlet** (*scharlach*); **contrasting hose**; **fur collar or lining** on wealth scale; **leather belt** with **pouch, keys, knife**; **turned shoes** or **ankle boots**; short **hood** (*Gugel*) or **chaperon** in cold rain [`dailylife.clothing-and-status-markers.01`] `plausible composite` [1][4].
3. **Master craftsman (Kalev):** **knee-length tunic** — shorter than merchant, longer than servant; **heavy wool** in **undyed grey-brown** or **woad-blue**; **leather apron** and **rolled sleeves** at forge [`dailylife.clothing-and-status-markers.02`]; **iron-stained** forearms; **belt** with **small tools**; **no fur trim** unless feast scene `plausible composite` [2][5].
4. **Estonian townsman / labourer:** **shorter tunic** (knee or above); **coarser home-spun wool**; **wooden shoes** (*Holzschuh*) or **patched leather**; **headscarf** (women) or **bare head / simple cap** (men); **no guild feast regalia** [`dailylife.clothing-and-status-markers.03`] `plausible composite` [6][7].
5. **Apprentice / servant (Mart):** **hand-me-down** tunic one size large; **no belt knife** unless errand scene; **wooden shoes**; sleeps in **wool blanket** not fur coverlet `plausible composite` [2][8].
6. **Women — burgher wife (Aita tier):** **long wool gown** (*Kleid*) to ankle; **linen coif** or **barbette** under **veil** for church; **wool mantle** pinned at breast; **keys at belt** signal household authority [`dailylife.clothing-and-status-markers.05`] `plausible composite` [1][9].
7. **Women — Estonian alewife / market seller:** **shorter gown** or **tunic-skirt**; **apron**; **headscarf** always; **no fur**; carries **jug or basket** not seal-purse `plausible composite` [6][7].
8. **Vassal knight / Danish hill retainer:** **mail hauberk** under **surcoat**; **sword belt**; **heraldry absent or Danish lion** — not burgher pouch dress [`people.estonian-and-german-populations.04`] `plausible composite` [10].
9. **Livonian Order knight (post–late April):** **white mantle/surcoat** with **black cross** [`dailylife.clothing-and-status-markers.06`]; **bearded**; mail harness under surcoat per [`arms-and-armour-livonia-1340s.md`](../military/arms-and-armour-livonia-1340s.md) — **1348 marshal seal** is nearest attested Livonian visual [11][12].
10. **Dominican friar:** **black hooded habit** (*cappa*); **white scapular**; **sandals**; **no belt pouch** [`dailylife.clothing-and-status-markers.07`] `plausible composite` [13].
11. **Clergy — parish priest:** **surplice** over **tunic** for mass; **street dress** = dark wool tunic like middling burgher with **tonsure** visible `plausible composite` [13].
12. **Watch / militia overlay:** **padded jack** (*Waffenrock*) over tunic; **iron cap**; **crossbow** or **spear** — kit from [`watch-duty-and-town-defence.md`](../military/watch-duty-and-town-defence.md); **does not replace** undertunic [14].
13. **Never mix without narrative reason:** council chain on Estonian labourer; Order cross on rebel peasant; **fur-trim merchant gown** on forge apprentice; **bareheaded noblewoman** in church procession; **plate harness** on burgher watchman.

## Findings

### Materials and colours available in 1343 Reval

| Material | Who wears it | Source / trade | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Undyed grey-brown wool** | Labourers, smiths, many Estonians | Local sheep; home spinning | plausible composite [2][6] |
| **Dyed wool** (woad blue, madder red, weld yellow) | Burghers, guild feast dress | Hanse import + local dyers | plausible composite [4][15] |
| **Scarlet** (*scharlach* — fine fulled wool, not modern scarlet dye name only) | Wealthy merchants, some councillors | Lübeck/Riga trade | plausible composite [4] |
| **Fur trim** (marten, squirrel, lamb) | German elite; rare on Estonians in town | Novgorod/Baltic fur trade | plausible composite [4][16] |
| **Linen** | Shirts, coifs, veils, altar linen | Local + import | plausible composite [2] |
| **Leather** | Belts, shoes, aprons, scabbards | Local tanner craft | plausible composite [2][5] |
| **Imported silk** | Extreme elite only — council feast, church vestments | Hanse luxury | plausible composite [17] |

**Spring 1343 palette:** April mud and smoke mean **wool shows wear** — patched elbows, sun-bleached shoulders, **forge soot** on smiths. **Post–23 April siege:** same clothes, **more tears**, **no new feast dress** until trade resumes `plausible composite`.

**Nordic archaeological comparandum:** Bocksten Man (mid-14th-c. Sweden) and Herjolfsnes tunics (Greenland, 14th c.) confirm **wool tunic + separate hose + hood** construction for Baltic-adjacent dress [`dailylife.clothing-and-status-markers.04`] — **not proof of Reval urban fashion**, but **best surviving northern European civilian cut** [2][3][19].

| Tier | Gown / tunic | Head | Outer layer | Markers | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| **Cistercian nun** | **Black/white habit**; wimple | Veiled | None in public | Rosary | plausible composite [13] |
| **Estonian market woman** | Tunic-skirt, apron | Scarf | Shawl | Basket | plausible composite [6][7] |
| **Noblewoman (Toompea)** | Long gown, fur, jewellery | Veil or barbette | Mantle | **No town keys** | plausible composite [1][10] |

**Women's hemlines:** manuscript evidence shows **long gowns for elite women** and **shorter practical skirts for workers** by mid-14th c. [1][9]. **No corset, no farthingale.**

### Guild, office, and feast dress

- **St Canute / St Olaf feast days:** masters wear **best tunic**, **hood with liripipe**, **guild pin or badge** — **exact 1343 insignia not attested**; use **plain hood + fur collar** as `plausible composite` [5][21].
- **Council office:** **chain of office** and **seal pouch** — **councillor only** [`people.estonian-and-german-populations`](../people/estonian-and-german-populations.md) [22].
- **Watch arming:** **padded jack** over **unchanged street tunic** — militia do not strip to shirt [14].
- **Easter (13 Apr):** **finer dress** for church; **Lent plainness** ended — **meat-coloured trim** (red/brown) plausible on burghers `plausible composite` [23].

### Markers that must never be mixed

| Marker | Permitted on | Forbidden on (without flag) |
|---|---|---|
| Council chain / seal pouch | Sitting councillor, Vogt clerk | Kalev, Mart, Estonian hauler |
| Great Guild hall livery | **Absent in 1343** — do not use | anyone |
| Black cross white mantle | Order knight | burgher, rebel, smith |
| Fur-trimmed scarlet | German merchant elite | apprentice, Estonian labourer |
| Leather forge apron | Smith, metalworker | councillor in session |
| Wooden shoes | Labourer, servant, poor Estonian | wealthy merchant |
| Mail hauberk | Knight, armed militia watch | market scene default |
| Tonsure | Clergy | lay townsfolk |
| Guild feast hood (liripipe) | Master at feast | child apprentice at anvil |

### Regional context: Danish Estonia, Hanseatic Reval, Livonian Order

- **Danish hill (Toompea):** dress skews **German knightly** and **clerical**; **not Hanse burgher** [10][22].
- **Lower Town Hanse:** **Lübeck-law burgher dress lane** — tunic, hose, hood, pouch — shared with Riga, Visby, Lübeck comparanda [4][24].
- **Estonian Undeutsch:** **same garment types**, **coarser wool**, **fewer dyes**, **wooden shoes** — **not a different century** [6][7].
- **Order arrival (late April):** introduces **white/black cross silhouette** distinct from **Danish vassal** and **town militia** [11][12].
