# Food and drink (Spring 1343 Reval)

You are feeding **April–May 1343 Lower Town Reval** — a **Hanseatic port under Danish rule**, one week after **Easter (13 April Julian)** and through **St George's Night (23 April)** and the **May siege**.

**Ship these decisions:**

1. **Staples:** **Rye bread** and **beer** are the daily calorie base for almost everyone; **fish** peaks in Lent and remains common after Easter; **meat, eggs, and dairy** return for observant burghers from **Easter Sunday** onward [`liturgical-calendar-spring-1343.md`](../religion/liturgical-calendar-spring-1343.md) `plausible composite` [1][2][3].
2. **Status tiers differ in quality, not in skipping bread and beer.** A **German merchant** eats **white or fine rye loaves, city beer, imported herring, butter, and occasional meat**; an **Estonian haulier** eats **coarse rye, thin beer, onions, salt fish, and rare meat**; a **Dominican friar** eats **fish, legumes, bread, and monastery beer** under mendicant rules `plausible composite` [2][4][5][6].
3. **Brewing is a German civic-monopoly hypothesis, not a dated 1343 Reval rule.** Later Tallinn ordinances support a `plausible composite` citizen-brewer seam, while a secondary source reports Estonian beer-excise collection without a dated AWB/Denkelbuch line; see [`reval-brewery-ordinances-1340s.md`](../economy/reval-brewery-ordinances-1340s.md) [7][8]. **Dominican priory** runs its **own brewery** east of the market [`churches-and-religious-houses.md`](../religion/churches-and-religious-houses.md) [9].
4. **Two-meal rhythm:** **Dinner (*Mittagessen*)** = main cooked meal **late morning to midday**; **supper (*Abendessen*)** = lighter **evening** bread, beer, and leftovers. **Breakfast** is **working-class practical**, morally suspect for gentry `plausible composite` [10][11].
5. **April–May scarcity:** **Last year's grain** still feeds the town, but **spring sowing** and **wet fields** mean **no fresh harvest**; **pre-harvest tension** before St George's Night — rye and beer prices are **nominal-normal** until **23 April** then **×1.25–1.5** when hinterland carts stop [`coinage-prices-and-measures.md`](../economy/coinage-prices-and-measures.md) [12][13].
6. **Kitchen location:** Cooking in **rear yard or living bay** at **open hearth** or **hooded fire**; **beer and grain in stone cellars**; **smokehouses** at fishing shore and some merchant yards `plausible composite` [14][15].
7. **Do not show:** Coffee, tea, potatoes, refined sugar bowls, Renaissance fork etiquette, **artig** coins at the tavern, or **abundant fresh meat** in every labourer's hand in late April.

**Regional arc:** **Danish crown** levies grain; **town council** regulates brewers and market weights; **Great Guild** merchants profit on **imported herring and salt**; **Estonians** visible as **excise collectors and hauliers**, not as **brewing patricians** [7][8][12].

### Bread

- **Rye dominates** Baltic and Hanseatic bread — **coarse rye** for labourers, **fine triangular rye (*schonroggen*)** and **wheat wedges (*Semmeln*, *Wecke*)** for elites `plausible composite` from Livonian documentary comparanda [2][3].
- **Town bakers (*Beckh*)** are a **regulated craft** — Mendel Hausbuch comparanda show **master baker with peel and oven** [`dailylife.food-and-drink.02`] `plausible composite` [21].
- **Loaf price:** **1–2 pfennig** nominal for rye bread in Hanse tables — apply siege multiplier after 23 April [12].
- **Portable food:** Labourers carry **bread + knife** to gate, harbour, or wall walk; bread doubles as **plate** for cheese or fish `plausible composite` [10][11].
- **No attested 1343 Reval bakery street name** in reviewed dossiers — bakers likely **mixed into craft quarters** near market (gap).

### Beer and brewing

- **Beer is food:** Hanseatic **daily drink** for adults and many adolescents; **thin beer (*Stopbier*)** cheaper than bread per calorie `plausible composite` [3][12][22].
- **Hopped beer** standard in Hanse towns by **early 14th c.** — Lübeck, Hamburg, Bremen export model; Reval **imports beer culture** through German settlers `plausible composite` [22][23].
- **Town brewers:** German-citizen brewing and household/tavern sale are a Hanseatic reconstruction. **1450s Tallinn ordinances** are later comparanda, not 1343 law; the Estonian collector role is a datedness gap in the local record [`../economy/reval-brewery-ordinances-1340s.md`](../economy/reval-brewery-ordinances-1340s.md) [7][8].
- **Dominican brewery:** Priory **east of market** produces **beer for friary and sale** — **four beer types** in later descriptions; **1343 operational** `plausible composite` [9].
- **Monastic vs civic:** Friary beer **not** under council guild brewer rules — **sanctuary seam** for tavern competition `plausible composite` [9].
- **Prices:** Thin beer **~1 pfennig per ~1.8 L** (Hamburg 1462 comparandum); tavern quart **2–4 pfennig** [12].
- **Props:** **Malt sacks**, **keg stacks**, **brew kettle** in rear yard — already in slice map contract [`brewery_keg_stack`, `malt_sack_pile`] (project).

### Meal times and commensality

- **Two-meal system:** **Dinner** = principal hot meal **9 a.m.–noon** (earlier for labourers, later for merchants) `plausible composite` [10][11].
- **Supper:** **Sunset band** — bread, cheese, beer, cold fish or pottage remnants `plausible composite` [10].
- **Breakfast (*Morgenimbiss*):** **Labourers, apprentices, women at home** — bread and beer; **councillors avoid** unless travelling `plausible composite` [10][11].
- **Communal eating:** **Guild and feast meals** at **long tables** in guild hall or Diele; **family** eats in **living bay** behind workshop [`burgher-house-plan.md`](../architecture/burgher-house-plan.md) [14].
- **Tavern:** **Evening drinking** associated with **morals risk** in church discourse — valid for **Bitter Brew** tone `plausible composite` [26].

### Spring scarcity (April–May 1343)

- **Pre-harvest gap:** **Last year's rye and barley** in **cellars and granaries**; **fields green but empty** — no July grain `plausible composite` [13][20].
- **April prices:** Nominal bread and beer per [`coinage-prices-and-measures.md`](../economy/coinage-prices-and-measures.md); **psychological scarcity** more than famine inside walls **before 23 April** `plausible composite` [12][13].
- **After St George's Night:** **Hinterland cart traffic fractures** — **grain, beer, charcoal** spike **25–50 %**; **queue at Viru Gate** for sacks `plausible composite` [12][28].
- **Fish supply:** **Harbour and roadstead** keep moving under siege tension — **fish does not vanish** when grain tightens [`hanseatic-trade-and-season.md`](../economy/hanseatic-trade-and-season.md) [28].
- **Danish grain levy (11 May 1343):** Crown **extraordinary levy** on grain — **political food stress** beat `attested` event pattern [29].
- **Manor countryside:** Rebels and peasants face **sharper hunger** than walled burghers — **do not equalise diets** across wall line [20].

### Storage and preservation

- **Grain:** **Cellars, loft bins, merchant granaries** near market; **moisture risk** in wet April [`burgher-house-plan.md`](../architecture/burgher-house-plan.md) [14].
- **Beer:** **Cool cellar** — **stone cellars** standard in merchant houses [14].
- **Fish:** **Salt herring barrels**, **smoke-dried fish** from Kalamaja; **Dominican fish trade** intermediates bulk `plausible composite` [9][16].
- **Meat:** **Salt pork and beef** for elite stores; **fresh slaughter** mainly **feast and elite** `plausible composite` [2].
- **Butter and cheese:** **Spring dairy** resumes after Easter; **limited before Lent ends** [4].
- **Vegetables:** **Onions and dried peas** year-round; **fresh cabbage and spring greens** in **May** [`spring-climate-and-living-world.md`](../nature/spring-climate-and-living-world.md) [13].
