# Law, courts, and punishment (Spring 1343)

## Brief for Quest / Narrative / Dialogue

You are writing **Lower Town justice under Lübeck law**, not a modern police procedural. April–May 1343 Reval punishes through **fines, public shame, banishment, and hanging** — decided by a **Vogt** at first instance and the **sitting council** (*Rat*) on appeal. The bishop, viceroy, and manor lords have **their own forums**; putting the wrong defendant before the wrong bench is a quest seam, not a paperwork error.

**Ship these decisions:**

1. **Court ladder (inside walls):** **Vogt** presides first instance → losing party may **Schelte** (cry appeal) to the **Rat** as upper bench → timely appeal to **Lübeck** remains **plausible composite** for ordinary civil disputes, not April siege emergencies [1][2][3].
2. **Proof system:** Disputes are settled by **written instruments**, **witnesses**, and **oath-helping** (*Eideshelfer* / compurgation) — the accused swears with a prescribed number of oath-helpers who attest to his credibility, not to the facts [4][5]. Full trial-by-combat is **declining** but not extinct in German-law tradition **plausible composite** [6].
3. **Written law in force:** The **1282 Reval codex** of Lübeck law (168 articles, Low German with Latin introduction) is the city's working statute book; an earlier Latin codex arrived **1257** [7][8]. Council craft *Schragen* for individual trades begin **1363** — not yet the primary forge rulebook in 1343 [9].
4. **Public shame venue:** A **market-square pillory** (*Schandpfahl*) is attested from **1337** at the forum centre — usable in 1343 scenes [10]. The **Town Hall arcade pillar pillory** is **post-1404**; do not chain anyone to hall columns in April 1343 [11][12].
5. **Executions:** Capital punishment is normally **outside the walls** on a gallows or execution ground **plausible composite** [13][14]. One **legendary priest beheading** inside the walls is **folklore**, not procedural norm [15].
6. **Who is tried where:** Burgher brawls, theft, market fraud, perjury → **Vogt/Rat**. Marriage, clergy discipline, tithe disputes → **bishop's court**. Peasant offences on a manor → **vassal lord**, not council. Knights on Toompea → **feudal/Danish forum**, not Vogt, without a political crisis [16].
7. **Siege overlay (April–May):** Council courts may continue for **internal order** (curfew breaches, hoarding, riot) while **military justice** for rebels stays **extramural** — Order troops do not replace the Vogt inside walls before 16 May [16][17].
8. **Estonian townsfolk:** May be defendants and witnesses in burgher courts if resident **plausible composite**; they do not sit on the bench [18]. Rural serfs dragged from Harju are tried in **manor courts** unless they have gained urban freedom [16][19].

### Court structure and procedure

| Stage | Officer / body | Jurisdiction | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| First instance | **Vogt** (*voget*) | Lower criminal and civil bench under Lübeck law | plausible composite [1][2][3] |
| Appeal | **Rat** (sitting council) | Upper bench; co-opted merchant councillors | attested structure [2][3] |
| Further appeal | **Lübeck Rat** | Hanseatic mother-city court if **Schelte** lodged in time | plausible composite [2][20] |
| Ecclesiastical | **Bishop of Reval** / parish clergy | Marriage, morals, tithes, clergy | attested framework [16] |
| Feudal | **Viceroy / vassal** | Toompea, manors, knightly immunity | attested [16] |

**Procedure (plausible composite from Lübeck-law tradition, not 1515 Ratsurteilsbuch verbatim):**

1. **Summons** — Stadtschreiber or Vogt's officer delivers written citation; default risks contumacy fine [2][3].
2. **Pleading** — Parties state claim and defence; written deeds (*urkunde*) weighed heavily in merchant disputes [2][7].
3. **Proof** — Documentary evidence preferred; failing that, **witnesses**; failing that, **oath with oath-helpers** (*Eideshelfer*) whose number scales with offence gravity and defendant status [4][5].
4. **Settlement pressure** — Council may order parties to reconcile before formal judgment **plausible composite** from later Reval practice [2].
5. **Judgment** — Vogt pronounces; appeal window opens with **Schelte** cry to the Rat [2][3].
6. **Enforcement** — Fines to Kämmerer; corporal punishments executed publicly; banishment proclaimed at market [2][13].

Confidence: court ladder **plausible composite** from Kämpf on Reval courts and Lübeck-law historiography [1][2][3]; detailed 1343 docket **absent** — no case file survives for spring 1343.

### Oath-helping and evidence

- **Eideshelfer** (oath-helpers) swear to the **credibility** of the defendant's oath, not to witnessing the crime — standard medieval compurgation [4][5].
- Number of helpers rises with offence seriousness and social rank of the injured party **plausible composite** [4].
- **Written deeds** (*des Rates urkunde*, merchant contracts, guild letters) outweigh oral testimony in Hanseatic commercial suits **plausible composite** [2][7].
- **False testimony** carries the heaviest procedural penalty in the transmitted Lübeck article set: **60 shillings** and **never testify again** — attested in Stadtrecht transmission [24].
- Relic-oath and sacred oath forms appear in German-law manuscripts **plausible composite** for high-stakes denials [`power.law-courts-and-punishment.03`] [6].

Confidence: compurgation framework **attested** as medieval norm [4][5]; exact helper counts for Reval 1343 **not attested**.

## Production hooks

- **Quest:** **Wrong forum** — drag an Estonian servant to the Vogt while the manor lord claims serf jurisdiction. **Perjury trap** — merchant case fails when a witness is disqualified under Art. 50 precedent. **Pillory scene** — repeat market fraudster chained at forum centre (1337+ attested fixture) while crowd mocks [`power.law-courts-and-punishment.04`]. **Siege curfew** — night gate breach fine during April watch; council needs the revenue. **After 16 May** — Order banner on Toompea but Vogt still sentences a brawling smith in the diele.
- **Narrative:** Henning (watch) delivers curfew violators to the Vogt, not the castle. Jürgen (merchant) produces a **Low German urkunde** and six oath-helpers. Kaja's kin outside the walls are **not** in council jurisdiction. Kalev's forge dispute splits: *Amt* elders for quality, Vogt for fire risk.
- **Dialogue:** *"vor deme richte"* (before the court); *"Eideshelfer"* (oath-helpers); *"Schelte"* (appeal cry); *"an den Schandpfahl"* (to the pillory); *"des bisscops gericht"* vs *"des vogeten richten"* (bishop's vs Vogt's court).
- **Dev / systems:** `offence_id` → `{forum, fine_marks, pillory_bool, banishment_days, capital_bool}` lookup; `proof_mode: {deed, witness, oath_helpers}`; `jurisdiction` flag from jurisdictions dossier; siege boolean tightens curfew offence weight.
