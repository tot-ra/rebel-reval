# AWB sanitation, bath-income, and street-language clauses (1340-1343)

## Brief for Quest / Narrative / Dialogue / Dev

The accessible Arbusow edition of the *Ältestes Wittschopbuch* gives useful bath-property and civic-delivery comparators, but it does **not** supply a confirmed 1340-1343 *Badpacht*, manure fine, gutter fine, or street-cleaning tariff. AWB 553 is a wood-and-delivery obligation attached to three *stupa* sites, not a bath rent or sanitation penalty. Preserve the printed abbreviation `4 mc. den.`; this dossier does not expand it to `4 denarii`.

**Ship these decisions:**

1. Use AWB 553 as an `attested` local comparator for recurring municipal bath fuel supply: three *stupa* owe the city annual Michaelmas payments for wood and for wood being delivered. Do not turn it into a rent, fine, or harbour due [1].
2. Use AWB 526 as an `attested` bath-property income comparator only: an annual mark is secured in the hereditary property or *stupa* of the fraternity of St Olaf. The entry does not name an operator lease, *Badpacht*, or municipal rent [1].
3. Treat ordinary AWB *census* and *redditus* language as property annuity or ground-rent context unless a clause explicitly identifies a bath operator or civic service. It is not automatically a street-cleaning charge [1].
4. Keep `fine_schilling` and `bath_rent_pfennig` unset for attested 1343 content. Any playable amount must be labelled `plausible composite` and sourced to a stated comparandum, not AWB [2][3].
5. AWB 539's *via ... gaanc* is a shared passage/access clause, not a confirmed *Gasse* fine or street-rent term. Do not use it as sanitation enforcement evidence [1].

| **539** | **9 Mar. 1342** (*anno eodem ante letare*); exact date supplied by the edition's marginal gloss. | pp. 80-81; MDZ canvases 96-97 | A shared *via* is to remain high and wide enough for one vehicle and wagon to enter and leave; the text calls it *gaanc* | Access and passage obligation, not a toll, fine, or confirmed *Gasse* sanitation term | attested [1] |

The printed AWB text reads `4 mc. den.` in AWB 553. The abbreviation is reproduced rather than silently normalised: the present evidence does not justify choosing between a mark-denarius expansion and another editorial explanation. The harbour dossier's prose expansion to “4 denarii” therefore requires a separate editorial reconciliation and is not adopted here [1][4].

### Bounded negative search

The review covered the 1340-1343 AWB band around entries 500, 504/508, 523-528, 537-553, and 564-575 in the accessible printed scan/OCR, with variant-aware searches for `Badpacht`, *pacht*, *stupa*, *Mist*, *Gasse/gass*, *via/gaanc*, *strat/platea*, *census/redditus*, *mulcta*, and *fina*. No confirmed clause in that reviewed material names:

- a *Badpacht* or other bath-operator rent;
- a fixed manure, night-soil, gutter, or street-dumping fine; or
- a *Gasse* rent/penalty or municipal street-cleaning tariff.

This is a **gap in the reviewed edition/search**, not proof that no such rule existed in Reval. The positive *stupa* entries show that baths and bath property occur in the register, but they do not convert an annuity or wood delivery into a sanitation ordinance [1].

## Production hooks

- **Quest / Narrative:** A council clerk may demand wood delivery at Michaelmas or dispute whether a bath site's annual obligation has been met. Do not frame the dispute as a documented dung-fine tariff or operator rent.
- **Dialogue:** Retain *stupa*, *census*, *redditus*, *pro lignis advectendis*, and the unexpanded `mc. den.` when a literate clerk quotes the register. Treat *Badpacht* as an unconfirmed research term for this date band.
- **Dev:** Keep `bath_wood_due` as a site-specific annual obligation with `source: awb_553`; keep `bath_rent` and sanitation-fine amounts null unless a later source is explicitly labelled `plausible composite`.
- **Map / Art:** Three bath-site references can support civic fuel logistics, but AWB 553 does not establish exact footprints, drains, carts, or a dedicated sanitation facility.
