# Revaler Kodex 1282: Cm 6 article map and folio evidence

## Brief for Quest / Narrative / Dialogue / Dev

Use this dossier to distinguish the **Reval 1282 sequence of 168 articles** from the longer
Lübeck and Bardewik transmission. The public evidence supports the following production rules:

1. The Reval witness is **TLA Best. 230 Verz. 1, Cm 6**, an original dated 1282; the catalogue does
   not expose its folio images or article-level folio coordinates [1][2][3].
2. Bunge's 1844 public-domain edition prints the Reval text on **pp. 40-71**. Parenthetical
   numbers in the printed headings are Bardewik/Lübeck comparative numbers, not Reval article
   numbers [4].
3. Kala's 1998 edition is the controlling modern transcription and translation, pp. 35-69 for
   the text, with commentary on pp. 5-31; the exact article-to-Cm 6-folio collation remains open
   until the physical or image witness is read [1][3].
4. The exposed Reval text contains council authority, civic assembly, night public order,
   councillor protection, expulsion, and *Burspraken* material. It does **not** expose a Reval
   article numbered 179 or a clean article-level arms checklist [4][5].
5. Do not quote Bardewik Art. 179 as “Reval Art. 179”. In 1343 production, label that clause a
   Bardewik/Lübeck comparator and keep its transfer to Reval as **plausible composite** [5][6].
6. Until a folio read lands, cite the manuscript as `TLA Cm 6, folio not exposed in public source`,
   and cite the printed anchor separately, for example `Bunge 1844, p. 57; Kala 1998, pp. 35-69`.

## Findings

### Witness, edition, and numbering discipline

The catalogue describes the 1282 Revaler Kodex as a Low German copy of Lübeck law with a Latin
prologue, consisting of 168 articles. It identifies Cm 6 as the original from 1282 and Cm 10 as a
near-contemporary everyday copy with exposed folios 7r-37r [1]. This is **attested [1][2]**.

Kala's 1998 edition is listed as the modern transcription and Estonian translation, with the
German/Low German text on pp. 35-69 and the introduction/commentary on pp. 5-31 [1][3]. This is
**attested [1]**. Bunge's 1844 edition is an earlier public-domain witness to the same Reval text;
its scanned pages 40-71 contain the prologue and articles 1-168 [4]. This is **attested [4]**.

Bardewik's 1294 codex reorganised the older material into thematic groups and expanded the
transmitted Lübeck corpus. Heidelberg's project explicitly warns that the Bardewik ordering is a
systematisation and that earlier manuscripts do not preserve a fixed article sequence [5].
Therefore a parenthetical number in Bunge is a comparative Bardewik/Lübeck number, not a folio
number and not a second Reval article number. This is **attested [4][5]**.

The crosswalk is **edition-attested [4]** but **folio-incomplete [1][2][3]**. The printed article
numbers and parenthetical parallels must be checked against Kala's transcription before any exact
Low German quotation is shipped in dialogue.

### What the exposed 1-168 sequence does not prove

No reviewed public text supplies a Reval article number 179. Bardewik Art. 179 may be a useful
Lübeck comparator for a “defend the city rather than serve on an external campaign” principle, but
it is outside the 1282 Reval sequence and must remain **plausible composite [5][6]** when applied to
Reval in 1343. The current source chain does not justify writing “Reval Art. 179” [1][4][5].

No reviewed article in the exposed Bunge pages is a clean, numbered Reval equipment checklist
requiring sword, jack, iron cap, spear, or crossbow. Those arms tiers belong to the separate watch
and militia comparanda, not to a directly quoted Cm 6 article. This is an **attested absence in the
reviewed edition [4]**; the 1343 arms custom remains **plausible composite [6]** pending a full Kala
and Cm 6 collation.

The source also does not expose a fixed Reval 1343 curfew fine. Article 69 supports a night-order
scene, but the amount and enforcement routine should remain `fine_marks: null` until an AWB or
council-ordinance folio supplies it. This is **attested evidence boundary [4][6]**.

## Production hooks

- **Quest:** A council crier calls a morning city-need assembly under Reval Art. 34; a night
  traveller is detained under Art. 69; an NPC tries to bribe a Ratman under Art. 104; an assault on
  a councillor in service triggers the Art. 90 fine split; expulsion under Art. 92 is a severe,
  council-controlled outcome.
- **Narrative:** The Stadtschreiber can hold the 1282 codex as the town's written authority while
  the crier reads *Burspraken*. A viceroy or sergeant may cite a Bardewik comparator, but the text
  should not call it a Reval article 179.
- **Dialogue:** Keep exact Low German quotations to the Kala-checked wording. The safe exposed
  formula is `dat de ratman settet, dat scholen de ratman richten` (Bardewik comparative wording,
  printed under Reval Art. 28) [4][5]. Use `Bursprake`, `Ratman`, `Voget`, and `vor deme richte`
  as source-backed register terms, while marking reconstructed pronunciation separately.
- **Dev / systems:** Store legal references as `{reval_article, bardewik_parallel, printed_anchor,
  cm6_folio, confidence}`. For this dossier, `cm6_folio` is `null` for every row until the archival
  pass lands. Do not allow a Bardewik article number greater than 168 to serialize as a Reval article
  number. Keep curfew fine amounts nullable.
- **Exclusions:** Do not add a 1282 Reval sword-and-crossbow checklist, a fixed curfew pfennig
  amount, or a Reval Art. 179 citation from this dossier. Each would exceed the exposed evidence.
