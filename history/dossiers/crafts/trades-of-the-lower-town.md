# Trades of the Lower Town (Spring 1343 Reval)

## Brief for Map / Art / Character / Quest

Lower Town craft life should read as a network of small household workshops, not a modern industrial quarter or a row of purpose-built guild halls. The evidence confirms craft fraternities (*innige*) by about 1335, while individual council-issued craft ordinances are first documented from 1363; therefore trade placement and equipment below use a mixture of attested town fabric and labelled Hanseatic composites [1].

**Ship these decisions:**

1. Put baker, brewer, cobbler, tanner, cooper, carpenter, and butcher in ordinary *boda* or diele-front plots, with rear yards, sheds, cellars, and shared service lanes rather than monumental guild buildings [1][2].
2. Bakers and brewers need heat, water, fuel, storage, and customer-facing sale space. Keep their ovens, kettles, malt, flour, and casks visible, but do not make every tavern a brewery [3].
3. Cobbler, cooper, and carpenter work is bench-and-yard craft: leather, staves, hoops, timber, shavings, and customers' containers should be more visible than finished luxury goods [1][2].
4. Tanning and slaughter are wet, smelly trades. Place vats, hides, bark, drains, blocks, and offal handling at rear or marginal plots; do not put vats or slaughter waste on the forum paving [4].
5. The cooper's craft has an evidence-bounded extramural presence: published property surveys record two cooper gardens before the Coastal Gate, alongside a carter garden. This is an aggregate pattern, not a named 1343 shop address [6].
6. A craftsperson may work in the city without having the same legal standing as a German burgher master. Do not convert later total guild exclusions into an absolute April 1343 rule; the pre-uprising Estonian membership seam remains open [1].
7. Use later guild allocations only as a cautious grouping aid: bakers and shoemakers with St Canute, butchers, carpenters, and coopers with St Olaf are a retrospective composite, not a dated 1343 roster [1].

## Findings

### Workshop pattern and status

Lower Town plots commonly combine a street-facing diele or *boda*, a rear living room, cellar, yard, shed, privy, and access to a back lane. This makes a household workshop with domestic production the safe default for ordinary trades [2] **attested typology; exact trade allocation plausible composite**. The surviving record is much stronger for houses, guild umbrellas, and later ordinances than for named 1343 shop addresses; absence of an address is a gap, not proof that a trade was absent [1][2].

### Trade grouping and guild caution

The St Canute and St Olaf umbrellas, individual *Amt* fraternities, and merchant Kindergilde are the documented framework for organising craft identity, but the source does not provide a complete April 1343 roll for these seven trades [1]. Later allocations place bakers, shoemakers, and smiths under St Canute and butchers, carpenters, and coopers under St Olaf; use that division only as a **plausible composite** for dialogue colour or quest factions, never as a dated legal roster [1]. Tanner and brewer placement remains especially open in the reviewed material; they should be treated as household or civic trades without a hard umbrella assignment.

Guild membership, workshop tenancy, and burgher citizenship must remain separate variables. An Estonian worker can be a visible craftsperson or assistant without automatically owning a full frontage plot or holding master privileges; conversely, a German master is not required to work in a guild hall. The pre-uprising Estonian presence in St Canute recorded by Zür Mülen prevents an absolute ethnic ban from being authored for April 1343 [1].

### Nuisance, fire, and circulation

Heat trades need fire-safe handling, while wet and dirty trades need drains, yards, and waste routes. The available legal material gives council authority over public orders and neighbour-path obligations, but it does not supply a verified 1343 tariff for each trade's nuisance [4]. Therefore use the following as production-safe composites rather than quoted ordinance text:

| Problem | Appropriate response | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Baker or brewer ash and heat | Rear service zone, fuel stack, controlled hearth, neighbour complaint if ash blocks a lane | `plausible composite` [2][4] |
| Tanner liquor and hide waste | Wet-margin or rear-yard pits, controlled drainage, no discharge into market paving | `plausible composite` [4][5] |
| Butcher or fish offal | Covered tubs or waste route to edge pits; no slaughter on the forum throat | `plausible composite` [3][4] |
| Cooper and carpenter shavings | Fenced work yard, swept frontage, bundles kept clear of fire paths | `plausible composite` [2][4] |
| Barrel, stave, and timber deliveries | Shared lane and gate circulation; use carts as traffic, not a permanent toll booth | `plausible composite` [5][6] |

## Production hooks

- **Map:** Seed mixed craft plots on 7–11 m frontages with a diele or *boda*, rear yard, shed, and service-lane access [2][5]. Put bakers and brewers near market/customer routes, cobblers on ordinary frontages, coopers near Coastal Gate/harbour logistics, carpenters in yards, and tanners or slaughter work at wet or marginal edges. These are placement weights, not attested shop coordinates.
- **Art:** Generate one compact kit per trade: baker oven/peel/flour sacks; brewer kettle/malt/casks; cobbler bench/lasts/leather; tanner vats/hides/bark; cooper shavehorse/staves/hoops; carpenter trestles/boards/adze; butcher block/hooks/tubs. Keep material wear, soot, damp, hide staining, shavings, and offal containers local to the trade.
- **Character:** Use *Becker*, *Brawer*, *Schuster*, *Lohgerber*, *Böttcher/Püttsepp*, *Zimmermann*, and *Fleischer* as Low German trade labels. A master owns or leases a workshop; journeymen and apprentices work under household authority; Estonian assistants can be present without an automatic burgher or guild vote [1][2].
- **Quest / Narrative:** Build conflicts around a baker's ash pile, brewer's water/fuel access, tanner's smell and drainage, cooper barrel delivery, carpenter timber obstruction, cobbler debt, or butcher offal citation. Cite council sanitation authority rather than inventing a trade-specific 1343 fine [4].
- **Dialogue:** Market speech may use *Brot*, *Bier*, *Schuhe*, *Leder*, *Fass*, *Holz*, and *Fleisch* alongside Estonian loan and occupational terms. Keep *Amt* and umbrella-guild references cautious: a character can say “our fraternity” without naming a later charter as current law [1].
- **Dev / systems:** Represent a workshop with `{trade, heat, water, waste, storage, frontage}` tags. Use `waste_profile: dry|wet|organic`, `service_access: rear|lane|edge`, and `guild_affiliation: unknown|kanute|olaf` with confidence metadata. Do not make guild affiliation a prerequisite for every craft interaction until the missing 1343 ordinance evidence is resolved [1][4].
