# Schmiede *Amt* rules and penalties before 1363 (Reval)

## Brief for Quest / Dev / Dialogue

This dossier answers a narrow question: what can a forge quest safely treat as a craft rule in Reval before the first surviving council-issued Ämter ordinances around 1363? The archive boundary matters more than a complete-looking code. The reviewed evidence confirms craft fraternities (*innige*) around 1335 and later places the smiths (*Schmiede*) under St. Kanuti, but it does **not** preserve a pre-1363 Reval smith *Schragen* or a dated table of smith fines [1][2].

**Ship these decisions:**

1. Treat a 1343 Schmiede *Amt* as a working fraternity with quality, admission, dues, feast, and fire-safety expectations, but do not show a written council *Schragen* on the forge wall [1][2].
2. Put ordinary workmanship disputes before *Amt* elders first; put a spreading fire, public-order breach, market violence, or perjury before the Vogt/Rat under the 1282 Lübeck-law framework [3][4].
3. Use the three offence-to-penalty pairs in the table below for forge quests. Their amounts are either general Lübeck-law figures or deliberately unset; none is an attested pre-1363 Schmiede tariff [3][4].
4. A masterpiece (*Meisterstück*) and master admission are a wider German craft composite, not a documented Reval smith requirement for 1343. Do not make Kalev quote a surviving local test [5].
5. Keep Estonian membership socially and legally tense, not absolutely impossible before the uprising: the pre-uprising St. Kanuti presence is reported, while later exclusions must not be backdated [1][2].

## Findings

### Evidence boundary and chronology

| Question | Finding | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Were craft fraternities present? | The Reval guild study reports the first *innige* around **1335**. | attested [1] |
| When did council-issued Ämter rules begin? | The same study says the Ämter received their ordinances from the Rat **from 1363**. | attested for the chronology [1] |
| Was there a pre-1363 Schmiede *Schragen*? | No text or archival folio was located in this pass. The surviving evidence is a gap, not evidence that no local custom existed. | evidence gap [1][2] |
| Where did smiths belong? | A retrospective allocation places bakers, tailors, shoemakers, smiths, and goldsmiths in St. Kanuti around 1500. It is a useful institutional comparator, not a dated 1343 roster. | plausible composite [1][2] |
| Was a masterpiece required? | Apprentice-to-journeyman-to-master progression and a guild-approved masterpiece are wider German craft norms. A Reval Schmiede test before 1363 is not attested here. | plausible composite [5] |

### Tallinn archive and catalogue pass (2026-08-07)

The official Rahvusarhiiv **Pärgamendid** catalogue was queried with institution `TLA`, date bounds `1340` to `1363`, and each of the title terms `Schmiede`, `Schmied`, `Smed`, `Schragen`, and `innige`. Each query returned **No results found**; the result pages are reproducible through the query form, for example [Schmied search](https://www.ra.ee/apps/pargamendid/index.php/en/parchment/search?institution=TLA&title=Schmied&start_year=1340&end_year=1363&q=1) and [Schragen search](https://www.ra.ee/apps/pargamendid/index.php/en/parchment/search?institution=TLA&title=Schragen&start_year=1340&end_year=1363&q=1). The same catalogue's bounded search identifies the relevant Denkelbuch record as **TLA.230.1.Aa2**, *Ältestes Denkelbuch des Revaler Rats*, 1333-1374: [RA catalogue record](https://www.ra.ee/apps/pargamendid/index.php/en/parchment/search?institution=TLA&refcode=TLA.230.1.Aa2&start_year=1340&end_year=1363&q=1).

The ILC city-book catalogue independently describes this volume as one 48-page band with notes on council elections, punishments, and judgments: [Denkelbuch catalogue](https://www.stadtbuecher.de/en/stadtbuecher/estland/kreis-harju/reval-talinn/aeltestes-denkelbuch-des-revaler-rats/). Its image link redirects to the RA.ee VAU login, so this pass could not inspect the manuscript folios or quote original wording. The result is therefore a **bounded negative catalogue check**, not proof that no smith entry exists in the book. The AWB (*Altere Verzeichnisse*) and Denkelbuch folio text likewise remain unreviewed behind the archive access boundary. No local 1340-1363 Schmiede offence, remedy, fine, or masterpiece condition was located; all local penalty amounts remain **unset**.

### Craft authority versus town authority

| Problem | First plausible forum | Escalation | 1343 handling |
|---|---|---|---|
| Soft edge, cold shut, wrong weight, or failed commission | Schmiede *Amt* elders | Vogt/Rat if the dispute becomes fraud, assault, or public market disorder | Use an *Amt* remedy or an unset fine; do not invent a tariff [2][3] |
| Forge fire left burning or sparks endanger neighbours | Watch / council order | Vogt under Rat authority | Fire control is a Hanseatic composite; the general council penalty route is usable, but the amount remains unset [3][4] |
| False statement during an inspection or court case | Vogt/Rat | Appeal to Rat under Lübeck-law procedure | The transmitted false-witness penalty is 60 schillings and permanent witness disqualification; applying it to a Reval 1343 smith dispute is a composite [3][4] |
| Brawl at a market or customer handover | Vogt/Rat | Council judgment | The transmitted market-peace figure is 3 marks silver, with two-thirds to the city and one-third to the court; Reval application is a composite [3][4] |

The distinction is important for Kalev: an *Amt* can reject work or discipline a member, while the Rat controls public order and the Vogt hears the town case. A later council *Schragen* must not be used to erase this 1343 jurisdiction seam [2][3].

### Offence-to-penalty pairs for forge quests

These are the minimum three pairs requested by the task. The first two are craft-specific applications of general town-law or guild practice; the third is a craft quality sanction. The exact *Amt* sums remain open.

| Forge offence | Consequence usable in a quest | Source basis and confidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Leaving a hot forge or sparks uncontrolled after the evening bell**, causing a neighbour complaint or fire-watch intervention | Watch/council summons; fine or detention until payment, with a separate neighbour-loss claim if damage is proved. **Amount: unset.** | Fire banking and hot-trade discipline are Hanseatic composites; Rat legislation and Vogt enforcement are grounded in the Lübeck-law framework. `plausible composite` for 1343 Reval [2][3][4] |
| **Lying about a blade's test result or hiding a defect during an *Amt* inspection**, then repeating the lie before the town bench | **60 schillings and permanent disqualification from testimony** under the transmitted Lübeck false-witness article. Applying that article to a forge inspection is `plausible composite`; the penalty itself is attested in the transmitted law [3][4]. | Lübeck Stadtrecht Art. 50, Hamburg transcription; Reval 1282 codex and local enforcement require archival collation [3][4] |
| **Selling or handing over a disputed commission and starting a brawl at the market** | **3 marks silver**, divided two-thirds to the city and one-third to the court under the transmitted market-peace article; the *Amt* may separately demand rework or suspend recognition. Reval 1343 application is `plausible composite`. | Lübeck Stadtrecht Art. 91 and the Reval court ladder; no pre-1363 smith tariff survives [3][4] |
| **Repeatedly delivering soft, underweight, or visibly cold-shut work** | *Amt* elders reject the piece, require rework or restitution, and may impose a fraternity fine or suspend access to recognised sales. **Amount: unset.** | Quality inspection and restricted craft admission are wider guild practice, not a surviving Reval Schragen clause. `plausible composite` [2][5] |

Do not convert the table into a fixed numeric “smith fine list”. The 60-schilling and 3-mark figures belong to transmitted Lübeck law, while the *Amt* remedies and fire amount are intentionally unpriced. This is the historically safer way to provide quest outcomes while the archival gap remains open [1][3][4].

### Admission, masterpiece, and social standing

- A *Lehrling* is plausibly bound to a master's household; *Lossprechung* leads to *Geselle* status; a master normally requires recognition, workshop rights, and a masterpiece in wider German practice [5].
- For 1343 Reval, use a **demonstration commission** or elder inspection as a narrative composite, not as an attested three-object test. A “masterpiece” can be a character's ambition, but its required steel grade, dimensions, and fee must stay invented unless labelled so [5].
- The pre-uprising Estonian presence in St. Kanuti reported by the guild study keeps a total ethnic ban out of scope. A character can be a skilled Estonian smith with contested burgher or *Amt* standing; later restrictions are not automatically April 1343 law [1][2].

## Production hooks

- **Quest / Narrative:** Use a night-fire complaint, a hidden soft edge, a false inspection oath, or a market handover brawl. Let the player choose between *Amt* rework, restitution, a Vogt hearing, or an appeal to the Rat. Never name a 1343 “Schmiede Schragen” as a surviving document.
- **Dev / systems:** Store `forum: amt|vogt|rat`, `penalty_amount: null|60_schilling|3_mark`, `remedy: rework|restitution|fine|detention|witness_bar`, and `confidence: attested|plausible_composite`. Keep fire-risk and quality failures separate from public-order offences.
- **Dialogue:** Safe terms are *Schmiede*, *Amt*, *innige*, *Meister*, *Geselle*, *Lehrling*, *broke* (fine), *Rat*, and *Vogt*. A clerk should call the 60-schilling result Lübeck law or an old law article, not a local smith charter.
- **Character:** Mart may be a bound *Lehrling* who cannot vote in *Amt* matters. Kalev's authority can come from skill and a working household forge while his burgher and fraternity status remains contested [1][5].
- **Art:** Show a spoken or wax-sealed inspection custom, a ledger, rejected work, and a town summons. Do not show a monumental guild hall or a numbered pre-1363 ordinance board in the smithy [2].
