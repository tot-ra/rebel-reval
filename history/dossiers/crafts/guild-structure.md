# Guild structure (Spring 1343)

## Brief for Dev / Quest / Character

You are staging **craft regulation in Danish Reval one month before the uprising**, not the fully codified guild world of the 15th century. April 1343 has **two craft umbrella guilds** (St Canute and St Olaf), **trade Ämter** (*innige*, first attested **~1335**), and a **merchant association** later called the Great Guild — but **no Great Guild hall, no Black Heads brotherhood in writing, and no council-issued craft Schragen until 1363** [1][2][3][4].

**Ship these decisions:**

1. **Three layers, not one "guild":** (a) **Kindergilde** — wealthy long-distance merchants, council-co-opted elite; renamed *Große Gilde* only from the **15th c.** [1][2]. (b) **St Canute** (*Kanutigilde*) and **St Olaf** (*Olaigilde*) — craft umbrellas with chapels, mutual aid, and political voice against the council from **mid-14th c.** [1][5]. (c) **Ämter / Zünfte** — individual trades (smiths, bakers, coopers…) with their own fraternities inside an umbrella [1][3].
2. **Where Kalev's smithy sits:** The **Schmiede** (blacksmith) *Amt* belongs under **St Canute** by the **~1500** allocation and is treated here as **already there in 1343** — `plausible composite` from the 1335 Ämter list and later guild books, not a dated 1343 smith charter [1][3][6]. **Do not** place smiths in St Olaf (butchers, stonemasons, carpenters, coopers by ~1500) [1].
3. **Master, journeyman, apprentice:** Standard Hanseatic progression — **Lehrling** bound to a master household; **Lossprechung** to **Geselle** (waged, may travel); **Meister** after guild-approved **Meisterstück** and workshop licence [7][8]. Durations are **not attested for Reval 1343** — use **4–7 year apprenticeship**, **2+ journeyman years** as `plausible composite` from wider German practice [7][8].
4. **Citizenship gate:** Full guild membership normally requires **Reval burgher status** (*Bürgerrecht*). Council co-optation draws from **Kindergilde merchants** [1][2]. **Estonians** could hold craft skill but **burgership and elite Ämter were German-dominated**; hard exclusion tightens **late 14th–16th c.** [9][10]. **Critical 1343 seam:** Zür Mülen records **Estonian members of St Canute who left after the uprising and founded a separate guild** — so **some Estonian craft membership was possible before St George's Night**, then breaks [1][11].
5. **Council vs guild authority:** Trade Ämter received **formal ordinances from the Rat from 1363** [1][3]. In **April 1343** council still sets **fire, market, and militia rules**; craft umbrellas negotiate **quality, membership, and feast obligations** — exact smith fines are **not attested** for 1343 (`plausible composite` from Lübeck-law towns) [3][12].
6. **Obligations a forge-owner faces:** Attend umbrella **feast and funeral masses**; pay **Ämter dues**; maintain **apprentice contract**; supply **watch arms** as a household master [`watch-duty-and-town-defence.md`](../military/watch-duty-and-town-defence.md); **no selling finished blades to non-burgesses** without market scrutiny — composite Hanseatic norm [8][12].
7. **Do not show:** Great Guild hall (1407–10), St Olaf guild hall rebuild (15th c.), Black Heads house (1400 charter / 1517 purchase), Brotherhood procession regalia, or rigid 16th-c. Estonian ban as if already absolute in April [2][4][13][14].

## Findings

### Umbrella guilds present in 1343

| Body | Patron / character | First documentary anchor | April 1343 status | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Kindergilde** (later *Große Gilde*) | Merchant elite | Mid-14th c. political weight; name *Große Gilde* from **15th c.** [1][2] | **Active** — wealthiest traders; council recruitment pool | attested association; 1343 label **Kindergilde** `plausible composite` [1] |
| **St Canute** (*Kanutigilde*) | St Knud Lavard | House possession **1326**; origins 13th c. [5][15] | **Active** — "finer" crafts, mostly German | attested |
| **St Olaf** (*Olaigilde*) | St Olaf | House possession **1341** [1] | **Active** — simpler trades; more Estonian presence in later tradition [16] | attested body; ethnic mix in 1343 **uncertain** |
| **Great Guild** (*Suurgild*) as named hall | — | Building **1407–10**; foundation often dated **~1363** [2][4] | **Absent as institution name** — use Kindergilde for merchants | attested anachronism if shown |
| **Black Heads** (*Schwarzhäupter*) | St Maurice | First charter **1400** [13] | **Absent** — legend ties to 1343 defence, not documents | attested absence |
| **Great Guild hall** | — | Built **1407–1410** [4] | **Absent** | attested |

St Canute and St Olaf were **originally mixed merchant–craft associations**; merchants migrated to the Kindergilde / Great Guild line over the 14th–15th centuries [5][15].

### Trade Ämter (Zünfte) and the smith

- Individual crafts appear as **innige** (fraternities) **from ~1335** in Reval sources [1][3].
- **Council ordinances** (*Schragen vom Rat*) for Ämter begin **1363** — twenty years after the game date [1][3].
- **~1500 allocation** (retrospective evidence, use with caution): St Canute held bakers, tailors, shoemakers, **smiths**, goldsmiths; St Olaf held butchers, stonemasons, carpenters, coopers, furriers, saddlers, hemp-spinners, painters [1].
- **First extant cooper charter: 1435** — shows how late some Ämter ordinances survive [17].
- **Smith *Amt* in St Canute for 1343** is `plausible composite`: consistent with 1335 Ämter list, Harju smith-street identity [`lower-town-street-plan.md`](../topography/lower-town-street-plan.md), and absence of any source placing Schmiede under St Olaf [1][3][18].

### Apprentice – journeyman – master progression

| Stage | German term | Rights / duties | Reval 1343 evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Apprentice | *Lehrling* | Lives in master's household; labour for training, board, lodging; father/guardian contract (*Aufdingen*) | Hanseatic norm `plausible composite` [7][8] |
| Release | *Lossprechung* / *Freisprechung* | Ends binding; may not yet open shop | `plausible composite` [8] |
| Journeyman | *Geselle* | Waged work; **Wanderjahre** common in closed trades from 14th c. | `plausible composite` [7][8] |
| Master | *Meister* | Own workshop; take apprentices; vote in *Amt* matters | Requires guild acceptance + masterpiece — `plausible composite`; Reval smith masterpiece rules **not attested** before later centuries [7][8][17] |

**Mart (16, apprentice)** fits the *Lehrling* tier. **Kalev (master smith)** must be read against citizenship: an **Estonian master without burgher rights** is a **legal tension** the narrative can exploit — not a documented 1343 norm [9][11].

### Estonians, Germans, and membership barriers

- **St Canute** was **mostly German**; Estonian membership **restricted especially 1508–1698** per encyclopaedic summary — but that is **later tightening** [15].
- Washington Baltic survey: guilds excluded non-Germans; Estonians barred from **citizenship** until **1787**, accomplished by tying guild entry to citizenship **by end of 14th c.** in Livonian towns [9].
- **Johansen & Zür Mülen:** Estonian members of **St Canute** **withdrew after the 1343 uprising** and formed a **separate guild** [1][11] — implies **pre-uprising Estonian presence in St Canute was real**, not hypothetical.
- **St Olaf** later reputation as **"lower trades" with more Estonians** [16] — usable as **social colour** for 1343, not proof every Estonian smith belonged there.
- **Transport trades** (carters, porters), often **undeutsch**, entered St Olaf only from the **16th c.** [1] — **absent** from formal guild rolls in April 1343.

### Mutual obligations and political role

From **mid-14th c.**, Kindergilde + St Canute + St Olaf **represented the city community (*Stadtgemeinde*) against the council** on administration and defence questions [1]. This matters in **siege April–May**: craft masters are **burgher militia**, not a separate guild army [19].

**Spiritual welfare:** Umbrella guilds funded **masses, burials, and poor tables** (*Tafelgilden* linked to Kindergilde and St Canute) [1][2].

**Quality control:** Ämter inspected work, limited competition, set training rules — standard Hanseatic guild functions `plausible composite` [9][8].

### What is absent or anachronistic for April 1343

| Element | Status |
|---|---|
| Great Guild hall interior | **Absent** (1407+) [4] |
| Black Heads as formal body | **Absent** (1400+) [13] |
| Council-published smith Schragen | **Absent** (from 1363) [1] |
| Cooper masterpiece three-barrel test | **Absent** (charter 1435) [17] |
| St Olaf absorbing all "simple" trades | **Partial** — process continues to 17th c. [1][16] |

## Production hooks

- **Dev / systems:** Model **three IDs** — `umbrella: kanute|olaf|kinder`, `amt: schmiede|baker|…`, `rank: lehrling|geselle|meister`. Gate `meister` shop ownership on `burgher:true`. Flag `estonian_guild_member` as **pre-uprising rare**, **post-uprising schism** event. Craft **quality violations** → *Amt* fine (composite) vs **council fire breach** → Vogt.
- **Quest:** **Schism foreshadow** — Estonian smiths asked to swear St Canute feast oath before St George's Night. **Foreign journeyman** without Lossprechung papers. **Kindergilde merchant** demands council seat while Kalev's *Amt* disputes market stall rent. **Illegal blade sale** to country rebels — council jurisdiction, not *Amt* alone.
- **Character:** **German master smith** — St Canute brother, burgher, watch arms, guild feast obligations. **Kalev (invented Estonian master)** — skilled but **burgher/guild standing ambiguous**; exploit Zür Mülen schism [11]. **Mart** — bound apprentice; cannot join *Amt* votes.
- **Art:** Smith workshop is **modest *boda* or diele-front forge**, not guild hall [`burgher-house-plan.md`](../architecture/burgher-house-plan.md). Guild **ceremonial** scenes use **church-adjacent chapels**, long tables, patron banners — not the 1407 Great Guild hall [`crafts.guild-structure.03`]. St Canute / St Olaf **meeting houses** on Pikk are **timber–stone medieval cores** under later skins [`crafts.guild-structure.02`][`crafts.guild-structure.04`].
- **Dialogue:** *Kanutigilde*, *Olaigilde*, *Kindergilde* (not *Suurgild* in 1343 speech); *Schmiedeamt* / *des smedes amt*; *geselle*, *lehrling*, *meister*; *innung* / *zunft* interchangeable in Low German register `plausible composite`.
