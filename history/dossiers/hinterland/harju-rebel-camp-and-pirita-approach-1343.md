# Harju rebel camp and Pirita approach (Spring 1343)

## Brief for Map / Narrative / Quest / Art

This is a bounded placement pass for the eastern Reval approach, not a claim that a surviving source locates the rebel kings' camp.

1. Treat the **Viru - Iru - Pirita - inner Harju** line as a long-lived movement corridor and a `plausible composite` route, not a surveyed 1343 road [1][2][3].
2. The **23 April signal fire in Harria is attested**, but the hill is unnamed. Any exact signal-hill anchor is `invented` until a source identifies it [1][4].
3. A bridge or ford at Pirita in 1343 is **unknown**. A runtime timber bridge may be used as a reversible `invented` crossing, never as an attested historical object [2][3].
4. Place `world.rebel_kings` as an authored camp in the eastern Harju corridor, on a meadow or field edge with access toward Reval. Do not label the selected plot as the historical signal hill [1][4].
5. Keep `world.harju` as a compact rural cluster with open field strips and a primary approach path; do not promote an LCD tax unit into a measured hamlet plan [1].
6. Iru is a defensible archaeological landscape anchor, but the reviewed record does not establish an Iru *hiis*, a rebel camp, or an exact 1343 settlement footprint [5][6].
7. Pirita's later Bridgettine convent is absent from Spring 1343; the later convent plot remains open river meadow in this period [2][3].
8. April-May presentation should read as muddy, working countryside with ploughing, meadow, reeds and sparse farm compounds, not as a finished park or urban suburb [1][7].

## Findings

### 1. Event geography and the missing signal hill

The St George's Night uprising began on 23 April 1343 with a signal fire on a hill in Harria in the project's attested campaign spine [1][4]. The reviewed local dossier and timeline do not name the hill, provide coordinates, or connect it to Iru, Pirita, a particular village, or the later rebel camp [1][4]. The event is therefore safe to use as an eastern-Harju ignition beat, while an exact hill is an `invented` gameplay anchor [1][4].

The Four Kings and the siege of Reval belong to the attested uprising tradition, but the chronicle-scale numbers are rhetorical and do not identify a camp footprint or daily headquarters [1]. A camp that supports authored scenes is a `plausible composite` when it stays within the eastern Harju movement corridor and does not claim a source-backed coordinate [1][4].

### 2. Viru - Iru - Pirita corridor

The existing Harju research identifies the Viru Gate - Iru - Pirita line as the main eastern approach toward Reval, with a working bracket of 5-12 km from inner camps to the walls and 1-3 hours on foot [1]. Those distance and walking figures are a production composite, not a measured 1343 itinerary [1].

Pirita River geography is attested as a long-lived settlement and movement area, and Iru Linnapära / Iru settlement provides an archaeological anchor near Reval [2][5]. This supports a river-valley route wrapper and a readable approach from Reval toward Harju, but it does not establish the medieval road's exact alignment, a camp on the river, or a bridge at the later convent site [2][3][5].

The inactive `viru_gate_foreland` prototype therefore remains useful as a reversible Pirita crossing reference. Its project translation uses a south-to-north river, wet meadow, reed and alder banks, and a timber bridge; the report explicitly classifies the road, bridge and farm boundaries as reconstruction rather than a measured 1343 plan [2].

### 3. Bridge, ford and river-bank evidence

The accessible Kloostri 16 archaeological report records natural river sand, reed impressions, clay and river mud, with early traces of movement in the 14th-16th centuries but no building predating the later riverside farmstead on the excavated plot [2][3]. This evidence supports an open wet bank and restrained route dressing, not a dense settlement or a documented bridgehead [2][3].

No reviewed source in the local pass identifies a 1343 Pirita bridge, ford, ferry tariff, bridge keeper, or exact crossing point [1][2][3]. The absence is an `unknown` evidence boundary, not proof that no crossing existed [1][2]. For production, choose one reversible crossing primitive for navigation and retain `crossing_status: invented_reconstruction`; do not write "the 1343 bridge" in UI, dialogue, or canon [2][3].

The Padise research is relevant as a separate western Harju road and monastery case, not as evidence for Pirita. Local AVE material describes the Padise complex and road context, but it does not identify a Pirita crossing or move Padise's route geometry to the eastern approach [8].

### 4. Camp placement and named hamlet boundary

The Harju dossier supports clustered villages, open strips, and a small number of rural compounds, while warning that the Liber Census Daniae records tax units rather than physical street plans [1]. A named playable hamlet near Iru or Pirita therefore remains unresolved; selecting one for a game route is a `plausible composite` or `invented` stable label, not an attested 1343 locality assignment [1].

For `world.rebel_kings`, the least misleading placement is a temporary camp on a muddy meadow or field edge within the eastern Harju corridor, close enough to support a short approach toward Reval but not rendered as a permanent fortified town [1][2]. The camp's council fire, supply shelters, sentry ring and paths are gameplay composition derived from the event role, not archaeological claims about a specific plot [1][4].

For `world.harju`, use the existing rural contract: a compact farm cluster, a primary packed-earth spine, field or fallow strips, a well or work yard, and a manor or other higher-status node only where the map source already requires it [1]. Keep the camp and village as separate stable nodes so the travel graph does not imply that the rebel command camp was the ordinary village centre [1].

### 5. Sacred-landscape boundary

Iru and nearby eastern-Harju sites can function as archaeological landscape candidates, but the dedicated sacred-place pass found no source that makes Iru a documented 1343 *hiis* [5]. A grove, offering place, or believer's sacred reading may appear as `folklore` or `plausible composite`; it must not be promoted to an attested grove polygon or used to prove the camp's location [5][6].

The sacred-grove route may therefore connect to Harju as a candidate landscape layer, while the camp route remains a separate movement and campaign layer [5]. This separation prevents a later or folkloric sacred-place reading from silently becoming evidence for the signal hill, bridge, or rebel headquarters [5][6].

### 6. April-May 1343 ground and faction seam

The seasonal contract for Harju calls for mud season, leaf-out, lambing, last stored grain and urgent spring field work [1][7]. These are regional production conditions rather than a plot-specific crop survey [1][7]. The uprising was not a pure peasant-only event in the reviewed synthesis: the coalition included multiple rural statuses reacting to taxation and vassal extraction [1]. Camp dressing should therefore avoid a single uniform costume or a fantasy army encampment [1].

Reval's Hanseatic town, Danish administration and the Livonian Order form the political seam around the uprising, while the rural route is not governed as a Lower Town street [1]. Use those factions for dialogue and quest pressure, but keep the eastern approach visually rural and preserve the later Order intervention as a campaign-state change rather than anachronistic permanent Order occupation [1][4].

## Production hooks

- **Map:** Keep `world.harju` on the existing compact-cluster contract: one primary packed-earth approach, open strips and meadow, sparse farm compounds, and no measured hamlet geometry inferred from LCD tax data [1].
- **Map:** Use `world.rebel_kings` as a temporary command-camp wrapper in the eastern Harju corridor. The authored plot may be near the route to Iru/Pirita for traversal readability, but its coordinate is `plausible composite`, not an attested camp or signal hill [1][2][4].
- **Map:** Preserve `viru_gate_foreland` as the stable Pirita prototype ID. A timber bridge is allowed only as a reversible `invented_reconstruction`; the river, wet banks and open meadow are the safer evidence-led reads [2][3].
- **Map / Dev:** Store evidence metadata separately from gameplay placement: `corridor_status: plausible_composite`, `signal_hill_status: invented`, `pirita_crossing_status: unknown`, `hamlet_status: unresolved`, and `hiis_status: candidate_only` [1][2][5].
- **Art:** Build low log farm masses, trampled earth, field margins, reeds, alder/willow wet-bank vegetation, rope/fence work and restrained camp shelters. Avoid a stone convent, a later Pirita church, a permanent fortress, or a monumental signal tower in 1343 [2][3].
- **Quest / Narrative:** Let the player choose how to interpret a signal fire, road report, or suspected crossing without claiming that the source names the hill. A courier can follow the eastern corridor; a wrong turn or blocked ford is authored uncertainty, not a historical bridge fact [1][2][4].
- **Quest / Narrative:** Keep the sacred grove as a separate candidate route. A local believer may use `folklore` language for Iru or another site, while the map metadata retains `hiis_attested: false` [5].
- **Dev / systems:** Travel costs may use the existing 1-3 hour Viru-Iru-Pirita bracket and mud-season modifiers, but must remain tunable production values rather than documentary timings [1].
- **Dev / systems:** Do not activate or rewrite P5 content in this research pass. P5-002/P5-003 should consume the IDs and evidence fields above through their own allowed implementation tasks [9].
