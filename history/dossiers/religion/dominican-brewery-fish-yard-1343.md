# Dominican south service yard (Spring 1343)

## Brief for Map, Art, and Narrative

The south service yard is a **bounded reconstruction inside the Dominican St Catherine close**, not a claim that a measured 1343 cadastral plan survives. Place it on the **east / south-east side of the usable Dominican church**, between the church's southern service edge and the east curtain-side lane. The yard supports the friary's documented fish-and-beer economy without turning it into a public harbour or a later guild quarter.

**Ship these decisions:**

1. Use one labelled authoring polygon, `dominican_south_service_yard`, attached to the eastern precinct boundary in [`ecclesiastical-precinct-boundaries-1343.md`](./ecclesiastical-precinct-boundaries-1343.md). The polygon is a production envelope, not a survey parcel.
2. Put the **brewery / mash and cask zone** nearest the kitchen or service threshold, with smoke, water, malt sacks, a kettle, and casks. The institutional brewery is supported; its exact room and yard wall are not.
3. Put the **stable / cart zone** on the quieter southern edge, with a small timber lean-to, fodder, dung management, and a gate toward the service lane. Treat the footprint as typological, not excavated.
4. Put a **fish receiving and sorting point**, not a stone quay, on the eastern or south-eastern edge. Use baskets, barrels, a low timber landing or wet-ground transfer spot, and carts; keep boats and a formal harbour outside the close.
5. Treat the three structures separately: brewery = **plausible composite, medium-high**; stable = **plausible composite, medium**; fish transfer / wharf = **plausible composite, low-medium**. No source reviewed gives an exact 1343 footprint for any one of them.
6. Preserve the precinct seam: the yard remains inside Dominican church jurisdiction, while carts approach through controlled service access at the Vene / Katariina käik / Müürivahe belt. Do not route a public street through the close.
7. Omit later St Catherine expansions, Black Heads patronage, a stone quay, a fish market hall, and a named modern street label as if any were documented in April 1343.

## Findings

### Evidence boundary

The primary spatial dossier places the Dominican built core between **Vene on the west and the city curtain on the east**, with the church in the southern part and the cloister north of it [1]. Its south boundary explicitly allows a **brewery / stable yard south of the east wing**, but labels that arrangement a `plausible composite` rather than an archaeological measurement [1]. The companion institutional dossier describes the 1343 friary as operational, with preaching, school, brewery, and fish intermediation, while warning that the large late-medieval church footprint lies in the future [2].

Kühnert's 1926 study, as cited by the heritage summary, is the plan and phasing source for the Dominican close and its church-to-cloister relationship [3]. It is useful for the **relative placement of church, claustral ranges, and service side**, but it is not a surviving April 1343 service-yard cadastral survey. The exact yard boundary must therefore remain labelled reconstruction [1][3].

The AVE 2016/17 surveillance by Kraut and Nurk covers the Viru, Vana turg, and Kuninga streets. In the relevant eastern approach it records the archaeological limits of the street-side context and the poor survival / unclear dating of some occupation layers east of Vene; it does **not** excavate or prove a Dominican south-yard plan [4]. Use it to constrain the lane and southern approach treatment, not as direct evidence for a brewery, stable, or quay.

### Evidence audit (R-060a)

The plan artifact requested by this row has been located, but it does **not** upgrade the yard to measured 1343 geometry. The University of Tartu Library ADA record identifies a plan image titled **“Tallinn. All-linn. Dominiiklaste kloostri territooriumi hoonestus 1926.a-l. E. Kühnerti ja O. Grohmanni joonis”** (item `10062/34977`, catalogued 1924; the record's verso description says *Allgemeiner Situationsplan des Klosterareals und seiner Bebauung i. J. 1926*). The record supplies the A-94-2236 TIFF/JPEG plan files, but dates the depicted built-up state to 1926 rather than April 1343 [13]. It is therefore usable for relative church / cloister / east-side service orientation, not as a 1343 cadastral survey or a safe source for transferring later wall lines into the game.

The AVE surveillance report is a second negative check. Kraut and Nurk describe the Viru / Vana turu / Müürivahe street works on pp. 181-184, including disturbed modern services and only sporadic, unclear-dated earlier layers east of Vene; the medieval gate and an inner limestone wall along Müürivahe are discussed on pp. 186-187. None of those observations identifies a Dominican service-yard wall, gate, drain, fish-transfer surface, or brewery/stable footprint [4]. **Measurement verdict:** no source-backed metre values can safely be assigned to SY-1..SY-4. The existing **18-24 m north-south depth x 24-32 m east-west span** remains a production envelope derived from the ~58 x 95 m parent close, not a measured yard. The requested upgrade from composite anchors to partial-attested geometry is therefore **not warranted**. This is an evidence result, not a claim that no service yard existed. A future upgrade needs a plan with a 1343 phase legend or a dated excavation/property record that distinguishes the east/south service range from later monastery fabric.

### Authoring polygon and anchors

The following is a local authoring frame derived from the labelled precinct, not a GIS coordinate claim:

- **Parent envelope:** the approximately **58 m east-west by 95 m north-south** Dominican built-core rectangle in [1]. Its west edge is the Vene-side frontage; its east edge is the curtain / Müürivahe side. The church occupies the southern part and the cloister lies north.
- **South-yard envelope:** reserve a compact **18-24 m north-south service depth** and **24-32 m east-west working span** at the church's east / south-east service side. Keep the envelope inside the parent close and outside the nave walls. These dimensions are bounded production targets, not measured archaeology.
- **Labelled corners:** `SY-1` = south-east church/service threshold; `SY-2` = east curtain-side service-lane turn; `SY-3` = north-east edge below the east service wing; `SY-4` = inner yard return at the church's east wall. Connect `SY-1 -> SY-2 -> SY-3 -> SY-4` as one irregular polygon, with the southern edge opening toward the south service lane.

### Vene archaeology and access constraint

Vene is a useful **relative route anchor**, not proof that the modern street name was used in April 1343. The back-lanes dossier places the Dominican close between the Vene feeder and Müürivahe, with Katariina käik following the west claustral range [5]. This makes the east/south service yard legible as a **restricted institutional back-of-house space** reached from the lane network, while keeping the fish landing distinct from the open-water harbour below the Coastal Gate [5][11].

AVE 2016/17 confirms that archaeology around the Viru / Vana turg / Müürivahe approaches is heavily disturbed and that some earlier occupation layers are black humus or otherwise hard to date [4]. That negative evidence is important: do not turn a nearby paving or wall observation into a named Dominican yard structure. Use the report to keep the service approach earth, pebble, or limited rubble, with no blanket late-medieval paving [4][5].

### Fish-transfer point verdict

The reviewed evidence does **not** isolate the April 1343 hand-off point among the three live alternatives:

- **Inside the south service yard:** remains the safest production location, but only as a **low-medium plausible composite**. The institutional dossier attests Dominican fish intermediation [2], while the precinct dossier places a brewery / stable service yard south of the east wing as a bounded reconstruction [1]. Neither source identifies a fish-processing feature, dated paving, drain, wall, or inventory tied to that yard.
- **At a priory gate:** remains operationally possible because the close had controlled service access from the lane network [5]. It is **not attested** as the receiving point: no reviewed source names a gate, gate-side fish stall, or dated delivery record connecting a consignment to the Dominican entrance.
- **At a separate market-side point:** remains an economic alternative, not a location verdict. The harbour dossier places the main merchant landing below the Coastal Gate and the fishing dossier places small-boat processing at the extramural Kalamaja shore [11][12]. Neither source connects either interface to the Dominican fish intermediation, and the evidence does not support a public fish market inside or beside the close.

The negative archaeological evidence is material to this decision. Kraut and Nurk's AVE 2016/17 surveillance reports extensive modern utility disturbance around the Viru / Vana turg / Müürivahe approaches; north of Viru Street east of Vene it found only sporadic earlier activity in a black-humus layer of unclear date, while the south side preserved only scarce earlier structural traces (AVE 2016, pp. 182-184) [4]. The report documents water-system and possible fortification remains near Müürivahe, but does **not** identify a Dominican plot, service gate, fish-transfer surface, or market-side receiving installation. This is a limit on inference, not evidence that no transfer occurred.

**Verdict for April 1343:** retain `fish_transfer` inside `dominican_south_service_yard` as a **low-medium plausible composite** and keep the exact hand-off point unresolved. Author a restricted wet-ground or short timber receiving edge that can be reached from the service side, but do not promote it to an attested priory gate, public fish market, stone quay, or named harbour facility. A future upgrade requires a dated property, excavation, plan, or economic record that distinguishes one of the three alternatives.

### What the yard is not

- It is **not** a second public harbour. The main merchant landing belongs below the Coastal Gate; small-boat fishing belongs at the north-western Kalamaja shore [11][12].
- It is **not** a proven stone fish quay. A short timber transfer edge or wet-ground cart exchange is the maximum safe visual treatment [11][12].
- It is **not** the later eight-bay Dominican church, a guild workshop quarter, or a Black Heads patronage yard [2].
- It is **not** a public fish market. The fish is received, sorted, salted or moved onward under friary control; public sale belongs to a separate market/economy decision [8][12].

## Production hooks

- **Map:** Add one `service_yard` polygon named `dominican_south_service_yard` inside the Dominican precinct. Keep it attached to the east/south-east church edge and the curtain-side service lane. Subdivide visually into `brewery_service`, `stable_cart`, and `fish_transfer` zones, but do not encode those subdivisions as surveyed parcels. Mark the polygon `confidence: composite` and `jurisdiction: church_seam`.
- **Art:** Brewery = soot-darkened limestone or timber service wall, copper/brass kettle, malt sacks, paddle, casks, water tubs, and damp spent-mash ground [6][8]. Stable = low timber shelter, fodder, cart shafts, manure basket, and worn threshold. Fish transfer = herring baskets, split-fish rack, salt barrel, rope, wet planks, and one handcart; no stone quay, crane, or large boat [9][11].
- **Character:** Dominican lay brother or hired worker handles brewing, carting, and fish receiving; a friar may supervise accounts or distribution. Estonian labourers can appear as service workers without a claim of a dated Dominican payroll or guild status [2][6].
- **Quest / Narrative:** A fish cart arrives through the restricted service opening; a brewer disputes water or fuel access; a stable hand clears dung before a procession; a friar argues that a fish consignment belongs to the priory rather than a burgher buyer. Treat each as scene-ready composite, not a quoted 1343 legal case.
- **Dialogue:** Use generic Low German trade vocabulary such as *Bier*, *Fass*, *Hering*, and *Hof* cautiously. Do not call the yard a modern "fish market" or name a later lane as an attested 1343 street label.
- **Dev / systems:** Suggested metadata: `{id: dominican_south_service_yard, kind: service_yard, jurisdiction: church_seam, confidence: plausible_composite, access: restricted, zones: [brewery_service, stable_cart, fish_transfer]}`. Keep exact structure coordinates in the map authoring layer, not as historical facts in dialogue.
