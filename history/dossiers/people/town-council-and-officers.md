# Town council and officers (Spring 1343)

## Brief for Character / Narrative / Quest

You are populating **Reval’s self-governing Hanseatic council** (*Rat*), not the Danish castle administration on Toompea. In April–May 1343 the Lower Town council is the **corporate voice of wealthy German merchants**; it co-signs appeals to the Livonian Order but does **not** command the Danish garrison or the rebel army.

**Ship these decisions:**

1. **Membership pool:** Only adult male **German burghers** of merchant or wealthy craft standing. Estonians, unfree labourers, and transient foreigners are **outside** the council; they may appear as petitioners, defendants, or riot crowd — never as sitting councillors unless explicitly labelled `invented` [1][2][3].
2. **Size and chairs:** The full council numbers roughly **19–25 Ratsherrn** (*raehärrad*), led by **two Bürgermeister** (burgomasters). The later four-burgomaster bench is **post-1343** — do not seat four mayors in 1343 scenes [4][5].
3. **Rotation:** Half the council sits as the **sitzender Rat** (active bench); the other half is the **alter Rat** (resting year). The annual swap falls on **1 August** (*Petri ad vincula*). In spring 1343 both benches exist; only the sitting half holds daily sessions [4][6].
4. **Election mechanics:** New councillors are **co-opted by the sitting council**, not voted in by townsfolk. The annual election assembly is held **20 December** (St Thomas’s Day) inside the Town Hall with doors closed; results are proclaimed from the hall windows to the square [5][7]. **Do not stage a popular election.**
5. **Meeting place:** Council meets in the **limestone Town Hall** (*consistorium*, first attested 1322) on the south side of the market square — single-storey, no tower, no arcade in April 1343 [8][9].
6. **One attested burgomaster near 1343:** **Dietrich (Didericus) Zierenberg**, merchant dynasty, died **6 December 1345** in Reval; treat as a sitting or recently sitting burgomaster in spring 1343 unless a scene needs a generic officer [10][11].
7. **Corporate actions in 1343:** The council acts as a body in crisis diplomacy — e.g. **27 October 1343** joint declaration with Bishop Oluf, clergy, and Danish vassals requesting Order help (*DD* III:1, no. 376); **21 May 1343** Reval named as party to an armistice with Swedish envoys (*DD* III:1, no. 324) [12][13]. During the **late-April siege** chronicle tradition has burghers appealing to the Order for military aid [14].
8. **Named 1333 cohort (use cautiously):** The Denkelbuch / Johansen *Ratsliste* preserves the **1333** intake — fourteen councillors plus four men believed to be burgomasters (Kersebom, Osenbrygghe, de Lapide, Bremen). Any still alive in 1343 may remain on the bench, but **1343 names are not individually attested** in the sources reviewed here [15][16].
9. **Officers to cast:** **Kämmerer** (chamberlain-treasurer, often drawn from *alter Rat*), **Stadtschreiber** (city scribe — professional writer, not a councillor), hall servants, and the **town seal** keeper. Lower criminal court sits under a **Vogt**; the council is the **upper/appeal bench** under Lübeck law [17][18][19].
10. **Invented councillor names** for crowd scenes must be flagged `invented` in design docs and must not duplicate attested 1333 or Zierenberg names unless intentionally portraying those figures.

## Findings

### Legal foundation and Hanseatic identity

- Reval received **Lübeck law** from King Eric IV (Eric Ploughpenny) in **1248**; the council is attested as *consilium consulum civitatis* in that privilege [1][20].
- Explicit **ius Lubicense** confirmation and the first codex arrived **1255–1257**; the council’s **earliest known seal use is 1257** [21][22].
- Hanseatic membership from **1285** shapes the council’s Novgorod-trade priorities; spring 1343 trade tension with Danish castellan **Konrad Preen** over Russian routes is documented in a **1343/1344** Dorpat–Lübeck letter [23].

### Council actions specific to the uprising window

- **23 April 1343:** Bishop Oluf and Livonian knighthood report the “heathen” rising — council not named, but town is already in crisis posture [12].
- **Late April – May 1343:** Besieged burghers seek **Livonian Order** military aid (chronicle tradition) [14].
- **11 May 1343:** Danish vice-castellan and knights receipt a **grain levy** from the **town** for the crown during the rebellion [13].
- **16 May 1343:** Danish **vassals** (not the town council document) transfer **Reval and Wesenberg castles** to the Order — distinct from Lower Town self-rule [12].
- **21 May 1343:** **“Staden Reval”** joins armistice negotiations alongside Danish king’s councillors in Estonia [12].
- **27 October 1343:** **“Staden Revals borgmestre og rådmænd”** jointly petition the Livonian Order with bishop, monasteries, and vassals [12].

### Danish, Order, and council seams (preview for R-008)

- **Toompea:** Danish viceroy/castellan and knights — **not** the merchant council [24].
- **Lower Town:** Council holds **burgher law, market, walls, and urban justice** inside Lübeck framework [3][24].
- **1346 sale** to the Order changes **Toompea** overlordship; council privileges are **confirmed to continue** — spring 1343 is still **Danish-overlord / Hanseatic-self-rule** [25].

## Production hooks

- **Art:** Two burgomasters in **dark merchant wool** with fur-trimmed civic dress (no crown insignia); councillors in matching sober Hanseatic dress; **town seal** and wax on table [`people.town-council-and-officers.01`]; hall interior as low stone room with benches, not baroque panelling [`people.town-council-and-officers.03` comparandum]; proclamation scene from **upper hall windows** onto market square [`people.town-council-and-officers.02`].
- **Map:** Council chamber in **Town Hall** south of market; route from hall to **Viru Gate** for messengers; separate **Toompea** castle track for Danish letters.
- **Character:** Dietrich Zierenberg as senior burgomaster face; generic councillor age **40–60**, male, German, merchant-backed; scribe younger professional; Estonians as **petitioners outside** the bench.
- **Quest / Narrative:** Friction — council must fund **grain levy** (11 May) while negotiating **Order protection**; armistice talks (21 May) vs rebel siege morale; October petition shows council **accepting Order political cover**; co-optation explains why **no popular rebel sits on council**.
- **Dialogue:** Address **“Herre Bürgermeister”** / **“Herren Rat”**; Low German civic register; proclamations open **“To alle burger der stadt Revel”** (plausible composite formula) [3][17].
