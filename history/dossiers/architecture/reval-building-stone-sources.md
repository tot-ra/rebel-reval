# Reval building stone sources: Lasnamäe quarries, lime, moat spoil and fieldstone (Spring 1343)

## Brief for Map, Art and Quest

Medieval Reval is a limestone town. The Toompea plateau quarry is already covered in
[`toompea-castle-and-upper-town.md`](./toompea-castle-and-upper-town.md#limestone-quarry-and-natural-plateau-edge-map-boundary-evidence).
This dossier covers the other sources of building material for the Lower Town: the quarries on the
Lasnamäe klint east of the town, lime burning, moat excavation, and fieldstone. Main finding: a town
quarry on the Lasnamäe klint rim in 1343 is well supported as a **plausible composite**. Medieval
quarrying there is **attested** in general (street-name and geological tradition), but the first
written forms of the hill's name date from **1370-1372**, after the game year. No 1343 record names a
quarry, its owner or its workers.

**Ship these decisions:**

1. **One working quarry, on the klint rim, beside the road that climbs the klint toward Narva.** Put it
   about **2.3 km east-south-east of the Viru Gate**, at the modern Sikupilli / Kivimurru area. It is a
   shallow open cut into the plateau top, not a deep industrial pit: a stepped face a few metres high,
   loose spoil heaps, stacked slabs. `plausible composite` (place from street-name tradition, form from
   period-rule simplification) [1][2][3].
2. **Under the period rule, use the simplified later form.** The 1370-1372 name forms and the later
   North and South Quarries (*Põhjamurd*, *Lõunamurd*) prove use of this rim later in the century and
   beyond, but its 1343 state is not documented. Show a smaller, younger version of the later cut. Do
   not show the deep 19th-20th-century South Quarry, which is now the Pae park lake [3][4].
3. **The quarry belongs to the town.** The land of Lasnamäe was town pasture and meadow from the Danish
   period. A council-run stone pit fits that, but no 1343 lease or council entry is known, so ownership
   is `plausible composite` [6].
4. **The lime kiln stands at the quarry.** A low limestone shaft kiln built half into a slope and fired
   with wood is the standard Estonian form. Lime burning in Estonia begins in the 13th century. Kilns
   at Lasnamäe are attested only from the 18th century, so the 1343 kiln is `plausible composite` [6][7].
5. **Stone travels by ox or horse cart in the open season and by sledge in winter.** Limestone slabs
   go down the klint road, across the Härjapea valley at the stone-bridge crossing, and in through
   the Viru Gate. `plausible composite` [9][10].
6. **Workers are Estonian peasants and town labourers.** No stone-breaker or mason name survives for
   1343. A brotherhood of stonemasons by 1340 appears only in a non-scholarly secondary text and is
   **not** used. Stonemasons sit under St Olaf's guild only by the c. 1500 allocation [11][12].

## Period-rule decisions

- **Excluded (dated later than 1343):** the 1370-1372 name records as evidence of the 1343 quarry, the
  deep North and South Quarry pits, the Pae park lake, the 18th-century peat-fired kilns and their
  surviving walls, Peter I's Kadriorg works, the lighthouses (1806+), the 19th-century naval barracks,
  the 1902 street grid, the Laagna road cutting.
- **Simplified later form (1343 state unknown):** a small open cut on the klint rim with one wood-fired
  kiln, a slaking pit, spoil heaps and a track to the Tartu road. The cut is shallow because the hard
  top beds were taken first and the upper layers were opened only around 1900 [3].
- **Uncertain and kept neutral:** who owned and ran the quarry; whether a treadwheel crane was used.

## Treadwheel crane and lifting

The user brief asks for a timber treadwheel crane at the cut face. Treadwheel cranes are attested on
large 13th-14th-century Hanseatic church and harbour sites, and the repo already shows a crane and
hoist on the St Mary's and St Olaf's building sites (CANON). For a **shallow** rim quarry, slabs are
levered out with bars and wedges and loaded straight onto carts or sledges, so a treadwheel crane at
the cut is **not needed**. Decision: use a simple **shear-legs (two-pole) hoist with a hand windlass**
at the face, `plausible composite`. A treadwheel crane at the quarry would be `invented` and is not
recommended.

## Labour and seasons

- Breaking and carting stone is heavy unskilled work. Estonian peasants of the town's villages and
  poor town labourers are the plausible workforce; carting for the town was a common servitude
  obligation (see [`awb-fuhr-servitude-clauses-1340-1343.md`](../economy/awb-fuhr-servitude-clauses-1340-1343.md)).
  `plausible composite`.
- Spring 1343 (April-May): the quarry works, the kiln smokes, carts go down the klint road.
  `plausible composite`. A modern local tradition (the 1930s Jüriöö park) places the 23 April signal
  fire on Lasnamäe [8]; CANON keeps the signal hill **unnamed** ("a hilltop in Harju County"), so the
  quarry must not be presented as the signal hill.
- Winter: frozen ground favours sledges over carts; kilns idle in deep frost. `plausible composite`.

## Quest hooks (all `invented`)

1. **Quarry tools commission (forge loop):** the council's quarry overseer orders wedges, crowbars and
   pick heads from Kalev's forge; worn tools return for re-steeling. Fits the commission → modification
   loop.
2. **Stone-cart contraband:** carts that come down from the klint pass the Viru Gate with little search;
   in Act 2-3 weapons or messages ride under limestone slabs.
3. **Spirit of the klint:** the cut wounds an old place on the stony grassland. In spirit sight a
   ground spirit (*maa-alused*, see `docs/BESTIARY/cards/maa_alused.md`) objects to the new face. A
   spirit-dialogue duel, not a fight. `folklore`-based, `invented` instance.
