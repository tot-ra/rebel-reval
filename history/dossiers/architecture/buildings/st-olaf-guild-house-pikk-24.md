# St Olaf's guild house, Pikk 24

## Brief for Map and Art

The St Olaf guild (*Olaigilde*) is first named in **1341** and **already had its own house on this plot in the 14th century**; the surviving building was rebuilt in **1419-22** as two gabled houses, a small fore-hall with the image of St Olaf in the gable and a large two-aisled hall with six star vaults [1][2]. The Blackheads, who later took over the complex and next door Pikk 26, only appear from 1399 and bought their house in 1517 [2]. So in April 1343 the guild meets in an **earlier, smaller house** of unknown form on the Pikk 24 plot.

Build it as a **simplified bespoke** guild house: one gabled house with a hall on the ground floor and a yard warehouse toward Pühavaimu street, plainer than St Canute's. The seamless city already sends St Olaf's tradesmen here in the evening ([Social life](../../../docs/SYSTEMS/SOCIAL_LIFE.md)); this dossier gives the house its 1343 shape.

## 1343 status

**Standing (attested by century, plausible for April 1343).** The guild exists two years before the uprising and holds a house on this plot in the 14th century [1][2]. The game shows it as a simplified merchant-type house; the vaulted hall is later and is excluded.

## Function in 1343

The umbrella guild of the simpler trades: butchers, stonemasons, carpenters, coopers, furriers, saddlers (c. 1500 allocation, used with caution) [3]; later tradition gives it more Estonian members, which makes it the natural ground for Estonian-German tension in April 1343 as social colour, not as proof [3]. Drinking evenings, feasts, burials and masses.

## Exterior

Plain gabled house, likely more timber than St Canute's; a painted image of St Olaf (axe) over the door.

**Leave out:** the 1419-22 fore-hall and the two-aisled star-vaulted hall with octagonal pillars, the Blackheads House renaissance facade (1531-32 hall, Arent Passer's 1597 front), the 18th-century owners' plaques, the 1834 rustication [1][2].
