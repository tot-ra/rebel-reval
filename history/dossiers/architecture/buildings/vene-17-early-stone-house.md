# Vene 17: the early stone house (Tallinn City Museum plot)

## Brief for Map and Art

The plot at **Vene 17** (today the Tallinn City Museum) holds the clearest surviving trace of a private stone house that probably stood in April 1343: a **small, probably one-room stone house of the first half of the 14th century**, preserved as the oldest part of the cellar storey under the later dornse [1]. Everything visible above ground today is 15th-century and later (portal wall, diele with mantel chimney dated 1506, portals of 1513) and must be left out [1].

Build it as a **simplified bespoke house**: a low, square, one- or two-storey limestone block (the "stone chamber" type) standing back from or beside the street line, with a timber street-front house or yard building beside it and an open drip gap to the neighbour [2]. No high stepped gable, no hoist row, no carved portal.

## 1343 status

**Standing (plausible composite).** The register dates the oldest building part to "probably the first half of the 14th century" [1]. That window includes April 1343 but does not prove it; the first written mention of buildings is twenty years later (1363). Under the period rule (unknown exact state: use a simplified form of the documented building) the small stone house may be shown; the later merchant house may not.

## Function in 1343

Unknown owner. The type is the merchant's or craftsman's **fire-safe stone chamber** (Latin *domus lapidea*, *hereditas lapidea*): the stone part of a plot where goods, money and documents were kept, while the family often lived in a timber house in front [2]. Vaga notes that before the second quarter of the 15th century the store building was usually **separate** from the dwelling, the store of stone and the dwelling often of timber [2]. Use it as a merchant household on the Vene street run between the market and St Catherine's.

No measured 1343 plan exists; the room count comes from the register phrase "one room" and from Vaga's description of the early plot pattern [1][2].

## Exterior

- Coursed limestone rubble, lime-washed or left grey; small rectangular or pointed openings with plank shutters; a plank door.
- Steep roof, shingle or board; no stepped gable [2].
- The timber front house: log or plank, limewash optional, overhang of no more than "two feet" over the street (a late-14th-century rule) [2].

**Leave out:** the 15th-century ashlar portal wall, the profiled double window, the diele with the 1506 mantel chimney pillar and the 1513 portal, the baroque stair and all painted ceilings [1].

## Decoration and interior

Plain: chests, a strongbox, scales, a lamp niche. Do not copy City Museum displays. Domestic props per [burgher-house-plan](../burgher-house-plan.md).

## Open questions

- Exact date of the stone house (no dendro date published in the register).
- Whether the 1343 street front was timber or a second stone house.
