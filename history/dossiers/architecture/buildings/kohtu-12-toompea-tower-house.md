# Kohtu 12: a vassal tower house on Toompea

## Brief for Map and Art

**Kohtu 12** is a long baroque noble house on the north edge of the Toompea plateau. The register records that its oldest parts are **probably 14th-century**, and that **square-plan massive walls in the north part point to a tower-like original building** [1]. That is the fortified house type the project already expects on the Great Castle plateau: *curiae* of the Harju-Viru vassals, "fortified town houses, not Lower Town diele-dornse merchants" [2].

Build it as a **simplified bespoke tower house**: a square limestone tower of three or four storeys with thick walls, small openings, an entrance at first-floor level by a timber stair, a walled yard with timber stable and hall buildings. It gives the vassal belt one recognisable, evidence-based silhouette; the other vassal plots stay generic.

## 1343 status

**Predecessor, plausible.** "Probably 14th century" does not fix the decade. The plateau was densely built by vassals from the late 13th century [2], so a tower house here in April 1343 is plausible; under the period rule a simplified form of the documented core may be shown. Record it as `plausible composite` in the manifest.

## Function in 1343

A Danish vassal family's town residence on the crown's hill: refuge and status seat, close to the cathedral and the bishop's court. In spring 1343 the vassals are the political actors who hand Reval castle to the Order on 16 May [2]. The owner is not known; the game may assign a vassal family from [Toompea castle and upper town](../toompea-castle-and-upper-town.md) as `invented` with a note.

## Exterior

- Coursed limestone, thick walls, few small openings, a corner latrine projection is possible.
- Steep roof; no baroque cornice, no regular window rows, no wall anchors [1].

**Leave out:** the baroque two-storey palace front, regular framed windows, wall anchors, the 19th-century south extension and classicist interiors, the carriage house in the forecourt [1].

## Decoration and interior

Lime-washed hall, painted beams, heraldic shields of a vassal family, benches, trestle table, chests. Composite after Livonian noble houses; no primary description survives [2].
