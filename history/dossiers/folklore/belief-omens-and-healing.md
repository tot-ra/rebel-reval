# Belief, omens, and healing (Spring 1343)

## Brief for Narrative, Dialogue, and Quest

You are writing **lived belief in April–May 1343 Harjumaa** — not a museum of 19th-century folklore collections and **not** a tidy pagan-versus-Christian binary.

**Ship these decisions:**

1. **Evidence tiers:** Only **Tharapita/Taarapita**, **sacred groves (*hiis* / *lucus sanctus*)**, and **continued idol-offering after baptism** are **`attested`** in pre-1343 written sources [1][2][3]. Named beings (*kratt*, *näkk*, *haldjas*, *luupainaja*, etc.), charms, and most omens are **`folklore`** — genuine oral layers, first collected centuries later; present them **in-world as real to Estonian believers**, keep scholarly dating in this dossier only [4][5].
2. **Geography split:** **Rural Harju** = full animist toolkit (groves, field spirits, water beings, death-omens). **Lower Town Reval** = **baptized labour + secret practice** at wells, hearths, and back lanes; **German burghers and clergy** read the same events as **heresy, fraud, or peasant superstition** [`estonian-and-german-populations.md`](../people/estonian-and-german-populations.md) [6][7].
3. **Sacred groves:** Treat **hiis** as **hill-and-forest sanctuary** with **tree, stone, and spring nodes** — offerings of **bread, wool, yarn, wax** attested only from **18th-c. ethnography projected backward** as **`plausible composite`**; the **1220 Tharapita grove** and **1241 LCD *lucus sanctus*** are the **`attested`** anchors [1][2][8].
4. **Healers:** **Baptized midwives and cunning-women (*tark*, *nõid*)** blend **Latin prayer, herb lore, and charm-song** — **`plausible composite`** for Ellen/Aita-type characters; **no attested 1343 Reval witch trial** survives — persecution is **framework fear**, not a dated docket [7][9].
5. **Iron and thresholds:** **Scissors, nails, or iron at doors** against nightmare spirits and **smith-forged amulets** are **`folklore`** with **wide Baltic-Finnic distribution**; a **smith as spirit-warder** fits the forge fantasy but is **`plausible composite`**, not chronicle-sourced [4][10].
6. **Spring omens:** **St George's Night (23 Apr)** carries **mobilisation fire on a hill** — **`attested`** chronicle event, **not** a documented folk-omen custom [11]. **Thunder, fire-tails, death-doubles (*mardus*)**, and **lake-spirit riddles** are **`folklore`** usable as **ambient dread** before and during the rising [4][12].
7. **Christian overlap:** **Lent/Easter (13 Apr)** constrains food and work [`liturgical-calendar-spring-1343.md`](../religion/liturgical-calendar-spring-1343.md); **folk spring rites** may continue **outside church hours** in **groves and manor yards** — same act, three readings (sacred / criminal / diabolical) [6][13].
8. **Do not import:** Kreutzwald **literary pantheon** (Vanemuine as "god of song"), **Ülemiste Elder** market-square ritual (later Tallinn legend), **19th-c. national Taara movement**, or **named quest seeds F-1…F-12** from the lore compendium without **`invented`** flag [14].

### Healing, charms, and cunning practice

**Who heals:**

| Practitioner | Setting | Tools | Legal risk | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Parish clergy** | Church, sick-bed | Latin prayer, holy water, relic touch | Low inside Christian frame | attested framework [13] |
| **Dominican friar** | Priory, town | Confession, preaching against superstition | Defines **forbidden** practice | attested framework [13] |
| **Baptized midwife (*ämma*)** | Manor or lane house | Herbs, birth prayers, **regilaul** charm | Grey — needed, suspect | plausible composite [6][9] |
| **Cunning-woman (*tark*, *nõid*)** | Grove edge, sauna | Charms, finding thieves, curse (*lendva*) | High if denounced | folklore + composite [4][14] |
| **Smith** | Forge | Iron nails, scissors, amulets | Respected craft; charm sale **invented** unless flagged | composite [10] |

**Charm types (all `folklore` unless noted):**

- **Herb poultice and dietary cure** — aligns with **Tacuinum/leech-book** Latin medicine among burghers; Estonian names **`plausible composite`** [16].
- **Spoken charm against bleeding, fever, nightmare** — parallels German/English leech-book charms; **no Estonian text dated 1343** [16][17].
- **Offering at spring or tree hollow** — bread, yarn, wax; **`plausible composite`** from 18th-c. Hupel describing **surviving custom** [8].
- **Singing illness into a stone or water** — regilaul healing genre; **`folklore`** [4][14].

**Christian-pagan overlap rules:**

1. **Same act, three readings:** Estonian labourer leaves **first bread** at threshold → **haldjas respect**; Aita → **household luck**; Dominican → **idolatry**.
2. **Baptism does not erase belief** — LCD and chronicles assume **reversion**; 1343 countryside rising rides **economic grievance + sacred grove networks**, not pure pagan restoration [`harju-village-and-manor.md`](../hinterland/harju-village-and-manor.md) [2][11].
3. **Lent/Easter** forbids certain foods; **folk healer** may still treat with **herbs not classified as fast-breaking** — dispute space for dialogue [13].
4. **Clergy weapon:** Accusation of **pact with kratt or näkk** converts folklore neighbour into **capital crime** — Order-adjacent justice [`law-courts-and-punishment.md`](../power/law-courts-and-punishment.md) **`plausible composite`** [7].
