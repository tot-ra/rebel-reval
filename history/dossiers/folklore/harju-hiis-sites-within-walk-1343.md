# Harju hiis and *lucus sanctus* candidates within a day's walk of Reval (1343)

## Brief for Map / Narrative / Quest

Use this as a **candidate layer**, not a catalogue of proven pagan temples. The origin is
Raekoja plats in central Reval, approximately 59.4373543 N, 24.7452110 E. A day's walk is
set here at roughly **25-35 km on a usable dry-land route**. Distances are working route
brackets for the 1343 map, not measured medieval roads [6].

**Ship these decisions:**

1. **Textual anchor:** LCD 1241 records a *lucus sanctus* near Wærkæla, but the place-name
   identification is disputed. Harju Verkla is a reduced-confidence candidate; Viru Võrkla is
   the comparison that is clearly outside this walk [1].
2. **Archaeological sites are not automatically *hiis* sites.** Iru, Rebala/Maardu, Saha,
   Jägala, and Keila are archaeological or landscape candidates whose monuments can support
   sacred-place geography, but the label must remain **archaeological candidate** unless a
   source explicitly supplies a sacred-place tradition [2][3].
3. **Best in-range map cluster:** Iru is the strongest near-Reval archaeological anchor;
   Rebala/Maardu and Saha form the most usable eastern landscape band; Jägala is a boundary
   case. Keep Keila as a western comparator and do not force it into the core one-day ring.
4. **No exact grove polygon:** use a point or small search area around the documented monument
   or modern place-name. Do not draw a precise 1343 sanctuary boundary from a modern register
   point [2][3].
5. **Production labels:** `attested` means the text or monument record itself is attested;
   `folklore` means a later sacred-place tradition; `plausible composite` means the map or
   route interpretation is reconstructed. Archaeological existence alone does not upgrade
   the *hiis* claim.

## Method and evidence boundary

The 1220 Tharapita grove and the 1241 *lucus sanctus* are the secure medieval textual
anchors discussed by Jonuks. The LCD *lucus* entry is not a list of all sacred groves, and
its place-name reading is contested [1]. Valk's distinction between sacred natural places,
folklore memory, and archaeological interpretation is applied here as a guardrail: a stone,
hillfort, cemetery, or settlement is evidence of past activity, not proof of a grove cult
[2].

The route origin and map links are modern orientation aids. Coordinates for Iru and Jägala
are map anchors supplied by OpenStreetMap nodes, while the broad Rebala point and place-name
search links are orientation anchors only. They do not claim that the archaeological feature
occupied the exact modern pin in 1343 [5].

### 1. Iru linnamägi and Iru settlement

**Map anchor:** [OSM Iru linnamägi node 633513567](https://www.openstreetmap.org/node/633513567)
(59.4556498 N, 24.9028435 E). The Estonian cultural heritage register separately lists
Iru Linnapära and the Iru settlement site; the register record is archaeological, not a
sacred-grove record [4].

Iru is the most practical near-Reval anchor for a map layer because it is close enough to
be reached and returned from the walls in a long day, and it sits on the eastern approach
used by the hinterland dossier. The hillfort/settlement combination supports a defensible
**archaeological landscape** node. It does not demonstrate that Iru was a *hiis*, that a
lucus stood there in 1343, or that a surviving tree/stone cult was attached to the fort [2][4].

**Use:** `archaeological_candidate`, `route_reachable: high`, `hiis_attested: false`.
A narrative may let a local believer call the hill sacred, but that dialogue is a
`plausible composite` or `folklore` layer, not a historical site label.

### 2. Rebala-Maardu archaeological landscape

**Map anchor:** [Rebala place and protected-landscape search](https://www.openstreetmap.org/search?query=Rebala%2C%20Estonia),
approximately 59.4402346 N, 25.1340663 E for broad orientation only. Use the official
[Muinsuskaitseamet protected-landscape page](https://www.muinsuskaitseamet.ee/kultuuriparand-eestis/malestised/muinsuskaitsealad)
for the protected-landscape frame, not the modern museum pin as an excavation coordinate [3].

Rebala/Maardu is useful as a **landscape bundle**: the protected area brings archaeological
monuments and settlement geography into one eastern Harju band. That makes it a strong
candidate zone for a map quest involving old graves, field margins, stones, and wooded
ridges. The official protected status attests heritage value and landscape management; it
does not identify a single 1343 *hiis* or prove that every monument belonged to one cult
place [3].

**Use:** mark the zone `archaeological_landscape`, with monument sub-points only when a
register entry supplies them. Any grove, offering, or taboo scene is a `plausible composite`
based on the general sacred-natural-place framework, not an attested Rebala ritual [1][2].

### 3. Saha cult-place candidate

**Map anchor:** [OSM Saha search](https://www.openstreetmap.org/search?query=Saha%2C%20Harju%20County%2C%20Estonia).
Use the village and register search as a map-usable locality anchor; do not promote the
search centroid to an exact archaeological coordinate [4][5].

Saha belongs in the candidate set because the locality is associated with an archaeological
monument cluster and a strong sacred-place reading in modern heritage and folklore
contexts. The evidence is not sufficient here to collapse a cult stone, chapel setting,
place-name tradition, or nearby settlement into one medieval *hiis*. Keep the candidate
name deliberately broad until a monument-specific register record or archaeological
publication is collated [2][4].

**Use:** `cult_place_candidate`, `hiis_status: plausible composite`, `exact_monument_point:
unknown`. A Saha scene can show a remembered sacred edge outside the village, but it must
not be presented as a directly documented 1343 grove.

### 4. Jägala linnamägi and Jõesuu

**Map anchor:** [OSM Jägala linnamägi node 650043580](https://www.openstreetmap.org/node/650043580)
(59.4660453 N, 25.1596916 E). The heritage register search returns the Jägala hillfort
and associated settlement records as archaeological monuments [4].

Jägala is a useful **outer-ring** candidate because the hillfort and river-mouth settlement
make a legible archaeological landscape. The estimated 30-37 km dry-land route places it
at or beyond a single day's walk from the Reval walls. It should therefore be reachable by
a staged expedition, boat-assisted approach, or an intentionally generous day estimate,
not silently treated as an ordinary walk [6]. Nothing in the cited register record upgrades
Jägala to a *hiis* [2][4].

**Use:** `archaeological_candidate`, `route_reachable: boundary`, `hiis_attested: false`.
The river and hill may carry believer speech labelled `folklore` or `plausible composite`;
the fortification and settlement remain archaeological evidence.

### 5. Keila western comparator

**Map anchor:** [OSM Keila archaeological search](https://www.openstreetmap.org/search?query=Keila%20archaeological%20site%2C%20Estonia).
Treat this as a locality search, not as a claim that a specific Keila hillfort has been
identified. The working route bracket is 29-36 km, and both the route and the exact monument
identity require a dedicated western-Harju pass [5][6].

Keila is retained because it tests the western edge of the one-day definition and prevents
the eastern candidate cluster from being mistaken for a complete Harju sacred geography.
At present it is a **comparative archaeological landscape candidate**, not an attested
*lucus sanctus*. Do not place a precise grove, cult stone, or hillfort marker until a
monument-specific source is cited [2][4].

**Use:** keep outside the default `walk_1343_core` layer; expose it as
`walk_1343_boundary_comparator` with `hiis_status: gap`.

### 6. Harju Verkla and the Wærkæla problem

**Map anchor:** [OSM Harju Verkla search](https://www.openstreetmap.org/search?query=Verkla%2C%20Harju%20County%2C%20Estonia).
This search link is intentionally used instead of an exact archaeological coordinate.

Jonuks records LCD 1241's *lucus sanctus* near Wærkæla and explains that the identification
is disputed between Harju Verkla and Viru Võrkla [1]. The **attested** part is the medieval
textual anchor and its wording; the assignment to Harju Verkla is a **reconstructed,
reduced-confidence** geography. Keep it in the dossier because a Harju reading could matter
to the one-day ring, but do not give it the same status as a confirmed local monument.

The Viru Võrkla comparison is about 137-170 km from central Reval and is explicitly
**out of range**. It is retained only to make the identification dispute visible. A route
from Reval to Harju Verkla is not fixed here because the place-name ambiguity prevents a
responsible route claim [1][6].

**Use:** `textual_lucus_anchor: attested`, `site_identification: disputed`,
`harju_route: unresolved`, `viru_comparison: out_of_range`. No exact grove polygon.

## Production hooks

- **Map:** Create a `hiis_candidate` layer with separate fields `evidence_class`,
  `hiis_status`, `route_bracket_km`, `coordinate_precision`, and `source_ids`. Use Iru as
  the near-Reval anchor, Rebala/Maardu and Saha as the eastern band, and Jägala/Keila as
  boundary cases. Keep Harju Verkla as a disputed text marker and Viru Võrkla hidden from
  the normal walk layer.
- **Narrative / Quest:** A local can call an archaeological hill, stone, or spring *hiis*
  in a `folklore` or `plausible composite` scene. The researcher-facing UI and quest
  metadata must retain the distinction between a sacred-place belief and an attested
  archaeological description.
- **Art:** Use a wooded hill, field edge, spring, or stone only as a regional composite.
  Do not depict a monumental pagan temple, priesthood, or uniform offering installation;
  those are not established by this evidence pass [1][2].
- **Dev:** Reject exact 1343 sanctuary polygons when `coordinate_precision` is
  `locality_only` or `modern_orientation`. Route status should distinguish `core`,
  `boundary`, `unresolved`, and `out_of_range`.

## Reference plates

No licensed visual evidence was found in this pass for a documented 1343 Harju *hiis* or
for any of the candidate sites in a form that would safely show their medieval appearance.
Modern register photographs and museum imagery should remain link-only until date, licence,
and evidentiary use are checked. The map anchors above are evidence-navigation links, not
reference plates.
