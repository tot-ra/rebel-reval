# Writing supports and record-keeping — Reval, spring 1343

## Brief for Art / Dev

You are dressing a **pre-paper, pre-print, mark-and-tally town**. There is no printed book, no paper
stack, no upright spine-out shelf, no glass inkwell anywhere in 1343 Reval. Ship these decisions:

1. **Default support is parchment** (calf/sheep membrane), not paper. Off-white to yellow-cream,
   uneven thickness, wavy cockled edges, visible follicle speckle on the hair side, prick-holes and
   faint ruling lines, scraped patches where text was erased with a knife — `attested` for Livonian
   charter and codex practice [1][2].
2. **Paper is a risky prop for 1343.** Rag paper reaches Hanse chanceries in the second half of the
   14th c.; Reval's paper town-books belong to the later 1300s. Do not model loose white paper
   sheets. If a paper document must exist, make it a single imported merchant letter and label it
   `plausible composite` in the asset row — see [Open questions](#open-questions) R-101.
3. **Everyday running accounts are wax tablets and tally sticks, not books.** A wooden diptych with
   a recessed dark-wax field, written with a pointed metal stylus and erased with its spatulate end,
   is the working notebook of clerks and merchants. Notched split tally sticks are the receipt
   system between parties who cannot read — including smiths, carters and bakers [3][4].
4. **A craftsman's "ledger" is not a codex.** For Kalev, use a **limp binding** (`Kopert`): loose
   parchment quires held in a soft leather wrapper with a long flap and a thong tie. This is why
   individual leaves can be altered or torn out in `the-makers-mark.md` — a board-bound codex would
   not behave that way.
5. **Board-bound codices are institutional.** Oak boards, leather cover, brass clasps and corner
   bosses, laid **flat** on a lectern, in a chest or on a shelf. The quotable civic example is the
   **1282 Revaler Kodex** (TLA Best. 230, Cm 6), 168 Low German articles with a Latin prologue [5][6].
6. **Charters carry pendant seals.** Single membrane, horizontal fold (`plica`), seal of natural,
   red or green wax on a parchment tag or hemp/silk cord. Seals hang, they are not stamped flat onto
   the face of a craft document [1][7].
7. **Tools:** goose quill, penknife, horn inkwell (iron-gall ink, brown-black), red minium for
   rubrics, lead plummet, awl and ruler for pricking, pumice, sealing wax, brass or ring seal matrix.
   Sand-shakers, pens with metal nibs, pencils and envelopes are anachronisms.
8. **Marks stand in for signatures.** House marks, merchant marks and smith marks scratched or
   stamped are the identity layer for the illiterate majority; this is the same mark logic the
   Maker's Mark quest runs on [8].

## Findings

### Supports — what one actually wrote on

| Support | Material | 1343 Reval use | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Parchment sheet / bifolium** | calf, sheep, goat membrane, lime-processed | charters, town books, quires for accounts | attested [1][2] |
| **Parchment codex** | quires sewn on cords, oak boards, leather, brass clasps | statute book, chancery registers, liturgical books | attested (Cm 6 ms.) [5][6] |
| **Limp binding (`Kopert`)** | leather/parchment wrapper, flap, thong tie | merchant and craft accounts, school texts | plausible composite (Hanse binding norm) [2] |
| **Wax tablet (diptych/polyptych)** | lime/beech boards, beeswax + soot/resin fill, stylus | drafts, daily reckoning, message slips | plausible composite for 1343; the Tallinn tablet holdings need a dated folio check [3] |
| **Tally stick (split tally)** | hazel/willow, notched, split into two halves | debts, deliveries, wages, charcoal and iron loads | plausible composite (north-European craft norm) [4] |
| **Rotulus (roll)** | membranes sewn end to end, rolled | account rolls, transport/storage of charters | plausible composite; more English than Livonian [1] |
| **Rag paper** | imported linen-rag paper | **avoid** for 1343; later-14th-c. Hanse practice | attested gap [1] |
| **Wax-sealed folded letter** | parchment, folded, tied, sealed | Rat correspondence, courier notes | attested by seal practice [1][7] |

### Appearance rules that make the props read as period, not fantasy

- **Colour:** parchment is not white. Hair side yellower and speckled, flesh side paler and smoother;
  the sheet cockles and never lies dead flat. Ink is brown-black iron gall, faded at the tops of
  strokes; rubrics and paragraph marks are red minium — `attested` for surviving Livonian material [1][2].
- **Layout:** ruled lines and prick-holes are visible before text; text sits in one or two justified
  blocks with wide margins; no title page, no page numbers, no printed regularity [2].
- **Storage:** books lie **flat**, stacked or fore-edge outward, in chests, cupboards (`armarium`)
  or on lectern shelves. Upright spine-out shelving with lettered spines is post-1500 — a hard
  anachronism ban for every Reval interior [2].
- **Chaining:** chained books belong mostly to later-medieval libraries; a chain on the Rat's statute
  book is `plausible composite` at best, so keep it to the council chamber and never in a smithy [2].
- **Wear:** greasy corners on the leaves a clerk turns, wax spatter, knife-scraped patches, torn
  stub edges where a leaf was removed, a stylus-polished shine along the tablet wax, notch clusters
  worn smooth on old tallies.

### Who writes, and in what language

| Actor | Support they use | Language | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Town clerk / chancery | codex registers, charters, wax tablets for drafts | Middle Low German; Latin for solemn charters | attested [5][6] |
| Merchant / factor | limp account book, wax tablet, tally, sealed letters | Middle Low German | plausible composite [2] |
| Clergy (Dominicans, parish) | liturgical and school codices, parchment | Latin | attested [9] |
| Craftsman (Kalev) | limp quire ledger kept by someone literate, tallies, own smith mark | marks and Low German notes | plausible composite [8] |
| Estonian labourers, carters | tallies and marks only | Estonian spoken, unwritten | plausible composite [8] |

## Production hooks

- **Art:** palette — parchment `#E4D8B8` hair / `#F0E6CC` flesh, ink `#241C14`, rubric `#8E2C1E`,
  cover leather `#4A3123`, oak boards `#77492D`, pale limewood `#C9B189`, tablet wax `#2C2A20`,
  seal wax red `#7E2B22` and green `#3B5A34`, iron `#464F52`, brass `#8A6B2C`, hemp cord `#B6A483`.
  Sizes to model: codex 320×220×90 mm; limp ledger 300×200×40 mm plus flap; loose quire 280×200 mm,
  8 leaves; sheet 300×210 mm; charter 420×260 mm with Ø55 mm pendant seal on a 120 mm cord; wax
  tablet 180×100×12 mm per board; tally sticks 220×18×18 mm in bundles of five to seven; rolled
  rotulus Ø45×300 mm; horn inkwell Ø40×70 mm; quill 240 mm; lectern 900 mm tall, 400×300 mm top at
  15–20°.
- **Map:** writing props belong to the smithy ledger nook, the Town Hall petition desk and scribe
  archive, the harbour clerk's stall, and church/convent interiors. Nothing writing-related belongs
  in Estonian labourer housing or field props.
- **Quest / Narrative:** the alter/destroy branch is physically possible only because leaves are
  loose in a limp wrapper; a tally stick gives a second, independent record that can contradict the
  ledger, and a wax tablet can be smoothed flat to erase a draft without trace.
- **Dialogue:** terms of art — `pergament`, `wastafel` (wax tablet), `kerfstock` / tally, `breff`
  (letter), `ingesegel` (seal), `stadschriver` (town clerk), `merk` (mark).
- **Dev:** three ledger states (`preserved`, `altered`, `destroyed`) can map to three mesh variants:
  intact quire, quire with a scraped leaf, quire with a torn stub.
