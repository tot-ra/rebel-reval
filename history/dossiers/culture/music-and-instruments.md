# Music and instruments (Spring 1343)

## Brief for Art, Dialogue, and Dev

You are scoring **audible Reval in April–May 1343**. Sound is **sparse, functional, and socially coded** — not a continuous street soundscape and **not** a formal town-musician corps on every corner.

**Ship these decisions:**

1. **Church default:** Parish and cathedral worship is **Latin plainchant** (unison or simple organum in skilled houses) — **no congregational hymn-singing in Estonian**, no Lutheran chorales, no post-1433 large organs in Lower Town parishes [`churches-and-religious-houses.md`](../religion/churches-and-religious-houses.md) [1][2][3].
2. **Organ verdict:** An **organist at a Tallinn church is attested 1341**; treat **Dome church or St Nicholas** as the only plausible fixed organ site — **small positive/portative scale**, not a festival instrument filling the market square [4][5]. **St Olaf, Holy Spirit, Dominican church: no attested organ in 1343** [3][6].
3. **Town pipers verdict:** **No attested Reval council-employed Stadtpfeifer corps before the late 14th century.** The **piper bode** (*Piperbude*) at Kinga/Teenri is **first named 1479**; Nottbeck's **3–4 pipers + 2 trumpeters** description belongs to a **later weak tradition**, not April 1343 [7][8][9]. Secular wind players at **weddings, guild feasts, and taverns** are **plausible composite** from Hanseatic practice — **itinerant Spielleute**, not uniformed council musicians [10][11].
4. **Watch and alarm:** **Parish bells** mark hours and curfew; **trumpet at muster** is Hanseatic **plausible composite**, not attested by name for Reval 1343 [`watch-duty-and-town-defence.md`](../military/watch-duty-and-town-defence.md) [12][13]. **Do not** place daily tower fanfares from a paid Ratsmusik ensemble.
5. **Tavern and market layer:** **Pipe-and-tabor, shawm, fiddle, and drum** fit Dominican brewery yards, merchant diele feasts, and harbour inns — label **`plausible composite`**; no named Reval tavern musician survives for 1343 [8][14][15].
6. **Estonian runic song (*regilaul*):** **Rural and manor-yard tradition**, not parish-nave or council-hall performance. Meter and alliterative form are **scholarly reconstructions of deep oral layers** — usable for **hinterland and labour scenes only**, tagged **`folklore`** / **`plausible composite`**, never as attested 1343 town repertoire [16][17][18].
7. **Absent in April 1343:** Great Guild **musicians' balcony** (guild hall **1407–17**), Black Heads ceremonial music, **collegium musicum**, printed part-books, **lute-song art music**, regular **hourly tower music** (17th-c. Leipzig pattern), Estonian-language church singing [7][8][19].
8. **Campaign sound:** Easter week (**13 April**) adds **passion bells and processional chant**; **St George's Night (23 April)** is **not** a documented civic music event — rural mobilisation may carry **horn shouts**, not organised town bands [20][21].

### Church music

- **Liturgical core:** Reval's German-speaking parishes and the Dominican house follow the **Western liturgical year** in **Latin** [`liturgical-calendar-spring-1343.md`](../religion/liturgical-calendar-spring-1343.md). Musical delivery is **chant** on feasts from **Palm Sunday (6 Apr)** through **Easter (13 Apr)** and **Ascension (22 May)** [2][20]. **attested** calendar; musical performance detail **plausible composite** from Livonian cathedral practice [22].
- **Notation:** **12th-century unison hymns in neume notation** survive among Tallinn City Archives holdings — evidence that **musical literacy existed in town churches** before 1343, not that every parish had a trained schola [1]. **attested**
- **Organ:** The **first Tallinn organist reference dates to 1341** [4]. The **1329 Helme/Paistu organ-destruction story** in Hermann von Wartberge is **probably not reliable** for actual organs in rural churches [5]. **St Nicholas** receives a **new organ only after the 1433 fire** [3]. For April 1343: **at most one small organ on Toompea or at St Nicholas** — never a filling-the-nave instrument. **plausible composite** [3][4][5]
- **Congregational singing:** Burghers **listen**; they do not **lead** vernacular hymnody. Estonian labourers in town **may know** Latin responses by rote; **Estonian-language church song is not attested** [2][18]. **attested** framework / **gap**
- **Dominican layer:** Friars preach with **musical accent** in sermons; their **brewery and school** are plausible venues for **student song and informal polyphony** — not documented by name in 1343 [2][22]. **plausible composite**

### Secular and civic music

- **Town-employed musicians:** Hillar Saha's archival survey allows **musicians on council payroll by the late 14th century** (four to six *moosekandid* in later records) [8]. **Piperbude** at Raekoja plats edge is **1479** [8][9]. **Nottbeck/Neumann 1904** describes a chapel of **3–4 pipers and 2 trumpeters** but frames Reval's art-music culture as **weak** — treat as **15th–16th c. horizon**, not 1343 staffing [7]. **attested negative** for formal corps in April 1343
- **Stadtpfeifer institution:** The term **Stadtpfeifer** enters official documents **mid-14th century in Germany generally** [10]; **Lübeck Ratsmusik** ordinances are **1404+** [11]. Transfer to Reval 1343 as an **established guilded corps** is **invented** without AWB evidence. **plausible composite** only for **occasional hired Spielleute**
- **Where secular music happens:** **Merchant wedding feasts** in diele halls, **Dominican brewery yard**, **harbour taverns** near the Coastal Gate, **private council banquets** — all **`plausible composite`** [8][14]. **Market-square busking** is **not attested**; use sparingly and label.
- **Instrument set (Hanseatic comparandum):** **Shawm** (*schalmei*), **pipe and tabor**, **flute**, **fiddle**, **drum** — attested in **16th-c. Tallinn musician inventories** and **late-medieval stone/iconography** as the **local secular bundle**; projected back to 1343 as **`plausible composite`** [8][14][15]. **Trumpet** reserved for **ceremony and alarm** in many towns — overlap with watch practice [`watch-duty-and-town-defence.md`](../military/watch-duty-and-town-defence.md) [10][12]
- **Dance:** **Estampie** and **round dances** with **pipe-and-tabor** are **Western 13th–14th c. norm** at burgher feasts [15]. **No attested Reval dance ordinance** for 1343 — do not script named dances.

### Estonian runic song (*regilaul*)

- **Genre:** Alliterative **Kalevala-metre** song — work songs, laments, wedding choruses, epic fragments — shared across **Baltic Finnic** peoples [16][17].
- **Evidence problem:** **Systematic collection begins in the 19th century**; no **1343 text or melody** survives [16][17]. Folklore.ee stresses that **layer dating inside the corpus is hypothetical** [17].
- **1343 usability:** **Rural Estonian rebels, manor field gangs, and travellers on Harju roads** may carry **call-and-response song** — **`folklore`** for specific plots, **`plausible composite`** for **genre presence** [16][18][21]. **Never** in **St Nicholas nave**, **council chamber**, or **Order ceremony** without explicit fiction label.
- **Language note:** Some scholars link *regi-* to **Middle Low German** *rege* (row/verse) — the **town German layer did not sing regilaul** [`estonian-and-german-populations.md`](../people/estonian-and-german-populations.md) [18]. **plausible composite**

## Production hooks

- **Art:** Church scenes = **open mouths, no instruments** in nave; clergy in choir stalls; **one portable organ** maximum at cathedral Easter Mass [`culture.music-and-instruments.01`](../../reference/culture/music-and-instruments/culture.music-and-instruments.01.jpg). Tavern = **shawm or pipe-and-tabor duo**, bench drinkers, **no lute-song circle** [`culture.music-and-instruments.02`](../../reference/culture/music-and-instruments/culture.music-and-instruments.02.jpg). Burgher feast = **fiddle + drum** at trestle table [`culture.music-and-instruments.03`](../../reference/culture/music-and-instruments/culture.music-and-instruments.03.jpg). Rural night = **women in line, arms linked, mouth open on long syllables** — **folklore** colour only [`culture.music-and-instruments.04`](../../reference/culture/music-and-instruments/culture.music-and-instruments.04.jpg). Watch/muster = **bell tower + optional straight trumpet** [`culture.music-and-instruments.05`](../../reference/culture/music-and-instruments/culture.music-and-instruments.05.jpg).
- **Map:** Audio zones: `{liturgical}` parish polygons; `{tavern}` harbour and Dominican brewery; `{rural_folklore}` outside walls only; `{silent_curfew}` all lanes after evening bell. **No** `town_piper_post` marker — institution absent.
- **Character:** **Cantor / parish clerk** (Latin chant); **unnamed Spielmann** (itinerant, low status); **bell-ringer** (guild or parish servant); **Estonian field singer** (hinterland scenes). **Do not** spawn uniformed **Stadtpfeifer** in council livery.
- **Quest / Narrative:** Smuggle message during **Good Friday quiet**; overhear **feast music** proving a merchant wedding; **regilaul** on the Harju road as **cultural marker** separating Estonian rebels from German council space; **false alarm** when curfew bell mistaken for assault bell.
- **Dialogue:** Church = **Latin phrases** only; tavern = **Low German calls** to musicians (*spille man*, *hor mir up* — **`plausible composite`**); Estonian = **single-line regilaul fragment** without translation in UI if tagged folklore.
- **Dev / systems:** `ambient_music_profile` per district: `church_silent`, `church_feast`, `tavern_pipe`, `market_sparse`, `curfew_none`, `siege_alarm`. **No** hourly `stadtpfeifer_fanfare` scheduler before **1400** flag.
