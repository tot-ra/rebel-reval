# Festivals, games, and public life (Spring 1343 Reval)

## Brief for Art, Map, Quest, Dialogue, and Dev

Stage public life as **short, calendar-bound bursts around the forum, parish churches, guild houses, taverns, and gates**, not as a permanent festival market. The playable window is post-Easter, but the late-April siege changes what public gatherings can safely do.

**Ship these decisions:**

1. **Eight usable event windows:** Palm Sunday (6 Apr), Easter Sunday and Monday (13-14 Apr), St George's feast and uprising night (23 Apr), St Mark (25 Apr), Rogation processions (19-21 May), Ascension (22 May), and ordinary Sundays throughout the window. Dates are Julian and follow the liturgical dossier [1].
2. **Forum first:** Raekoja plats is the attested civic market from 1313. The weekly market weekday for 1340-1343 is not yet attested; do not invent a named Tuesday or Saturday market [2][3].
3. **Processions are the safest public spectacle:** Palm Sunday, Rogationtide, and Ascension can route church, forum, and gate as `plausible composite`; no exact 1343 route survives [1][4].
4. **Drinking is ordinary social infrastructure:** beer, bread, tavern talk, guild meals, and post-Easter feasting are visible; do not turn Reval into a carnival or show a modern public house [5][6].
5. **Games stay portable and local:** dice, tables, chess, simple ball play, and informal contests fit tavern, yard, and feast settings as `plausible composite`; a named Reval tournament is not attested [7][8].
6. **Archery or crossbow contests are a regional transfer only:** fourteenth-century French and Flemish towns hosted urban shooting competitions, but no Reval 1343 competition is documented [9].
7. **Punishment is public theatre:** the forum pillory is attested from 1337; market fraud, perjury, banishment, and a crowd's reaction can be staged there. Executions remain normally outside the walls [10][11].
8. **After 23 April:** siege alarms, ration queues, proclamations, and watch musters displace leisure. A busy harbour beside a tense or thinly supplied forum is historically legible [12].

## Findings

### Calendar anchors and event windows

All dates in this dossier use the Julian calendar used by the campaign. The liturgical calendar is the authority for movable feasts; exact civic attendance and street routes are labelled separately where the Reval record is thin [1].

| Date or season | Public event | Scene use | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **6 April, Palm Sunday** | Parish Palm Sunday observance, likely including a palm or branch procession | Branches, clergy, bells, temporarily cleared church approach; keep stalls sparse | Feast date attested; street procession plausible composite [1][4] |
| **13 April, Easter Sunday** | Easter Mass and end of Lent; communal feast resumes | White cloth, bells, meat and dairy returning to tables, household and guild meals | Date and liturgical feast attested; street-scale feast composite [1][5] |
| **14 April, Easter Monday** | Holy-day continuation with restricted routine trade | Quiet forum morning, visiting, leftovers, parish traffic; no invented market fair | Date attested; trading restriction plausible composite [1][3] |
| **23 April, St George's feast / uprising night** | Parish observance overlaps the documented rural rising and siege opening | Morning Mass and banners can contrast with night beacons, gate anxiety, and rebel reports | Feast and campaign anchor attested; civic music/procession not attested [1][12] |
| **25 April, St Mark** | Minor fixed feast within the siege week | A church calendar cue or clerk's date, not a large civic festival | Calendar date attested; public prominence uncertain [1] |
| **19-21 May, Rogationtide** | Three days traditionally marked by litanies and boundary perambulation | Small outdoor processions, parish banners, fields or gate-edge prayers; route is fictionalised | Rogation dates and practice attested in Western tradition; Reval route plausible composite [1][4] |
| **22 May, Ascension Thursday** | Major holy day with worship and likely processional movement | Bells, clergy, temporary quiet, crowd crossing the forum or church close; siege may shorten the route | Date attested; local street route plausible composite [1][4] |
| **Ordinary Sundays: 6, 13, 20, 27 Apr; 4, 11, 18, 25 May** | Weekly holy-day rhythm and reduced routine trading | Fewer commercial stalls, family visits, church traffic, watch staffing; 11 May also carries the Kanavere campaign beat | Calendar dates attested; exact Sunday market rule composite [1][3][12] |
| **Mid-April to late May** | Post-Lent market restart and summer convoy preparation | Carts, harbour labour, merchant meals, weighing, and talk of supplies; after 23 Apr interior traffic fractures | Seasonal trade phase plausible composite, siege disruption attested [5][12] |
| **After 23 April, siege weeks** | Proclamations, ration queues, watch musters, and emergency gatherings replace leisure | A crowd can form around a levy, gate closure, alarm bell, or council notice without becoming a festival | Campaign pressure attested; crowd choreography plausible composite [10][12] |

The table supplies more than the six datable or seasonally placed backdrops requested by production. It does **not** imply that each date had a town-wide procession, fair, or day off. A holy day can be socially visible through bells, food, church traffic, and altered work even when no civic spectacle is attested [1][3].

### Markets, fairs, and the limits of the record

- **Raekoja plats / forum** is the attested central civic market from 1313. The east-side Vanaturu kael is a traffic throat and cart staging lane, not a second 1343 market square [2][3].
- The **weekly market weekday** in Reval for 1340-1343 remains an open archival question. Use generic `market_open` scenes or a dated feast-day exception, not a hard-coded Tuesday, Saturday, or Sunday market [3].
- Medieval fairs commonly joined commerce with entertainment, but the surviving Reval evidence in this tick does not name a spring fair, fair charter, or licensed troupe for April-May 1343. A small cluster of peddlers, food sellers, musicians, and dice players at the forum is a **plausible composite**, not an attested annual fair [2][13].
- **Holy-day trading restrictions** belong to the calendar layer. Sundays, Easter Monday, Ascension, and Pentecost should thin routine trade; a siege emergency can override the normal rhythm without erasing the religious observance [1][3].
- Before 23 April, market scenes can show cart queues, scales, bread, beer, fish, and imported goods. After 23 April, the harbour may remain active while inland grain, livestock, charcoal, and labour flows become unreliable [5][12].

### Processions, parish space, and civic visibility

- **Palm Sunday:** branches, bells, clergy, and parishioners moving through a church approach are a safe visual composite. Do not identify the branch species as an attested Reval custom; willow or local spring greenery is an art choice [1][4].
- **Rogationtide:** the three-day sequence of litanies and boundary walking is a strong seasonal event because it makes the parish claim space outside the nave. The exact Lower Town route is unknown; use a short church-to-gate or church-to-field edge loop and mark it `plausible composite` [1][4].
- **Ascension:** a major feast can produce bells, clergy, and public movement, but the active siege may keep processions close to the parish precinct. Do not force a full city circuit [1][4][12].
- **St George's feast:** the date is an attested campaign anchor, but there is no evidence here for a formal civic procession or a permanent town band. A morning parish service and an evening rural signal layer should remain separate [1][12].
- Processions should pass **existing civic and religious landmarks** rather than create festival-only architecture: forum edge, parish threshold, Dominican precinct, gate approach, and guild chapel or meeting house [1][2][14].

### Drinking, feasting, and public sociability

- Beer is a daily food-drink across status groups; city and household brewing, tavern sale, and Dominican beer belong to ordinary economic life rather than an exceptional festival [5][6].
- **Easter 13-14 April** is the clearest communal feast beat: meat, eggs, dairy, better bread, and household or guild tables return after Lent. The dossier does not claim a town-wide banquet [1][5].
- Tavern and brewery-yard scenes can include mixed-status conversation, games, hired musicians, arguments, and information exchange. A public inn with stable rooms and a broad travelling clientele is a later-medieval comparative model, not a specific 1343 Reval building [6].
- Drinking scenes should show **wooden or ceramic cups, bread, beer, barrels, benches, and shared tables**. Strong wine is an elite or imported accent, not the default drink of labourers [5].
- During the siege, tavern sociability narrows: prices, grain, absent carts, watch duty, and rumours replace leisurely festival talk. Alcohol does not make the crisis disappear [5][12].

### Games and sport

The evidence supports a distinction between **portable pastime** and **organised civic sport**:

| Activity | Where it fits | 1343 Reval verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Dice | Tavern bench, merchant diele, guard room, yard | Plausible composite; no named local set or ordinance in the reviewed record [7] |
| Tables / chess-like board play | Merchant or guild table, quiet tavern corner | Plausible composite from medieval game culture; avoid claiming a Reval rulebook [7][8] |
| Simple ball or throwing games | Yard, green margin, youth or labour downtime | Plausible composite only; keep it informal and out of the forum's main traffic [8] |
| Wrestling or strength contest | Feast yard or rural gathering | Plausible composite for a local crowd, not an attested Reval annual event [8] |
| Archery / crossbow shooting | Militia practice or a festal contest | Regional urban comparandum; fourteenth-century French and Flemish competition is attested, Reval 1343 event is not [9] |
| Joust or knightly tournament | Toompea or elite visitors only if a plot requires it | Do not use as ordinary Lower Town public life; no 1343 Reval tournament is attested [8][12] |

Use games as **social separators**: an apprentice loses dice money, a merchant protects a board game, watchmen throw dice during a quiet shift, or children imitate a militia drill. Do not build a standalone minigame premise from a supposed Reval tournament without a new evidence pass [7][8].

### Punishment as spectacle

- The **forum pillory / Schandpfahl** is attested in Tallinn from 1337, so public shame at the market centre is valid in Spring 1343. A short crowd scene can show a market fraudster, perjurer, or repeat brawler under watch [10].
- The audience is not passive decoration. Medieval urban punishment could involve public participation, mockery, recognition, and argument; keep the crowd mixed and let guild, neighbour, servant, and child reactions differ [10][15].
- Fines, banishment proclamations, church penance, and detention are more useful recurring public events than executions. Hanging belongs normally outside the walls; do not turn the forum into a permanent gallows [10].
- The Town Hall arcade pillar pillory is post-1404 and must not be used. Place the 1343 shame scene at the open forum, not on a later arcade column [10].
- During the siege, a public punishment competes with ration queues, proclamations, and alarm. The council can use the same forum as market, legal, and political theatre, but the scene should make the conflict legible [10][12].

## Production hooks

- **Art:** Build event dressing as reversible overlays: branch bundles and parish banners for Palm Sunday; white cloth, bread, meat, and bells for Easter; small processional crosses and litanies for Rogation; Ascension bells and restrained church traffic; beer cups, shared trenchers, dice, and board pieces for tavern life; a timber forum pillory for the 1337+ public-shame fixture. Keep St George's feast banner and rural beacon dressing separate from an invented civic parade [1][5][10].
- **Map:** Route processions through existing church thresholds, forum edge, and gate approaches. Reserve a clear forum sightline for market, proclamation, pillory, and ration-queue variants. Treat the Vanaturu kael as cart circulation, not festival plaza. Do not author a fixed weekly market weekday until R-050 is resolved [2][3].
- **Character:** Give event roles to parish clerk, bell-ringer, brewer, tavern keeper, guild master, apprentice, peddler, watchman, market officer, penitent, and processional parishioner. Status reads through who joins a guild table, who can afford strong beer, and who is exposed at the pillory [5][6][10].
- **Quest / Narrative:** Use `easter_feast`, `st_georges_feast`, `rogation_procession`, `ascension_holy_day`, `market_pillory`, `ration_queue`, and `watch_muster` as event beats. A missing cart during the post-23 April supply break is a stronger conflict than an invented tournament [1][10][12].
- **Dialogue:** Low German calendar words include *Oster*, *Sankt Jürgen* / *Georgi*, *Himmelfahrt*, and *Markt*. Tavern lines should turn on beer, bread, debt, dice, prices, and rumours. Public shame can use *Schandpfahl* and *vor deme richte* from the law dossier [5][10].
- **Dev / systems:** Represent event overlays with `{date, event_id, venue, public_visibility, trade_modifier, procession_route, siege_override}`. Keep `market_weekday` unset until R-050. Set `festival_density` high only during dated feast windows; after 23 April raise `watch_and_supply_pressure` and lower `leisure_activity` rather than deleting all tavern life [1][3][12].

## Open questions

- **Reval weekly market weekday in 1340-1343** - resolve through AWB and council ordinances before setting `market_weekday` in systems or dialogue; candidate **R-050**.
- **1343 Rogation procession route** - identify any parish boundary or perambulation evidence before converting the composite route into a map contract.
- **Named Reval taverns or public inns before 1343** - search AWB, tax, and property records; do not promote a later inn name into campaign canon.
- **Local game finds and rules** - archaeological pass on dice, board pieces, and gaming contexts from 13th-14th-century Tallinn.
- **Reval archery or crossbow competition** - search guild, militia, and feast records; current shooting evidence is a French/Flemish comparative pattern only.
- **Forum pillory coordinates and hardware** - reconcile the 1337 attestation with the 1343 forum polygon and archaeological open-ground evidence.
