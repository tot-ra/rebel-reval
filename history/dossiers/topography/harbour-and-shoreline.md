# Harbour and shoreline (Spring 1343)

## Brief for Map and Art

You are modelling **Reval's wet north margin and roadstead**, not the modern engineered port basin, cruise quays, or 16th-century Fat Margaret silhouette. In April 1343 the **sea stood farther inland** than today; much of Lootsi, Sadama, and the D-terminal zone was **shallow water, marsh, or river delta** [1][2][3].

**Ship these decisions:**

1. **Two harbour zones:** (a) **merchant landing** immediately below the **Coastal Gate** at the foot of **Pikk** — low wet cargo ground, short timber jetties, roadstead beyond; (b) **fishing shore** at **Kalamaja / Kalarand** outside the northwest wall — small boats, net yards, not a second stone quay city [4][5][6].
2. **1343 shoreline vs modern:** Do **not** copy today's straight quay line. Medieval shore was **irregular**: sand ridges, mud, reed fringe, and **fill only from the 19th century onward** at Lootsi [1][2]. Viru-hotel / Narva Street margin was **wet or underwater** in the 13th–14th centuries [7].
3. **Coastal Gate descent:** Gate sits **5–8 m above historical harbour ground** on sandstone cliff; **~100 m** from first medieval gate to open water in the 14th c. (much shorter than today) [8][9][10]. Show **steep path or ramp**, not a flat plaza to the sea.
4. **No Fat Margaret, no foregate barbican, no drawbridge complex** at the Coastal Gate in April 1343 — rectangular gate tower **plausible 1311–1340**; written *Strandporte* only **1359** [9][11]. Stolting watchtower at cliff edge is **~1400** replacement of an earlier small rectangular harbour guard point [9].
5. **Quay practice:** **Broken shoreline + timber/rubble landings**, not a continuous dressed-stone quay [4][12]. **One reversible treadwheel crane** at the merchant yard is **plausible composite** (Hanseatic norm by 14th c.; no Reval-specific crane attestation) [13][14].
6. **Roadstead:** Deep-draft **cogs** anchor or ground in **open water** offshore; cargo moved by **lighters and carts** through shallow margin [1][4][12]. Do not moor four full-size cogs to four long stone piers simultaneously [4].
7. **Härjapea mouth:** River entered the sea **east of the walled merchant landing**, at the **Lootsi / Sadama delta** — wrecks and wet sediments confirm active shallow channel into the 14th c. [1][2][15]. Do not show a broad navigable river mouth under the Coastal Gate itself.
8. **Sand Gate (*Väike Rannavärav*):** Northeast **beach / small-boat** exit on the mid-14th-c. circuit — minor gate to wet shore, not the main cargo landing [16].
9. **Ship types:** **Cogs and cog-like cargo hulls** dominate Hanseatic bulk trade; **small open boats** at jetties; **Scandinavian-influenced hull variants** attested in local wrecks — do not paste one "Bremen cog" silhouette on every vessel [1][17][18].
10. **April 1343 season:** **Ice break-up** and first coastal sailings are **plausible** on the Gulf of Finland by late April–May; harbour mud, cold wind, and **limited deep-water access** remain — label exact ice-off date **unknown** [19][20].

## Findings

### Shoreline displacement: 1343 vs modern coast

Post-glacial **land uplift** and **artificial fill** moved the coast seaward. Künnapuu & Raukas note that in the **13th–14th centuries the sea reached almost as far as the present Viru hotel** and the Narva Street area was **presumably under water** [7]. Holocene shore-displacement work confirms **heavy later removal** of coastal forms by harbours and industry [3].

Archaeology at **Lootsi 8** places a **14th-c. wreck** on the **foreshore of the Härjapea mouth** — today **~1.5 m below urban ground**, inland from the modern harbour basin [1][2]. The medieval **main port lay beside the old town** in the **Mere puiestee / Sadama** zone; the Lootsi wreck grounded in **shallow sandy bottom** after drifting from roadstead or harbour [1][2]. **Lootsi Street became dry land only in the 19th century** (first half), after warehouse and railway fill [1][2].

|---|---|---|---|
| Below Coastal Gate | Cliff descent to wet cargo flats | Paved apron, museums, later barbicans | attested relief [9][10] |
| Pikk north tail | Merchant landing approach | Same alignment, filled shore | plausible composite [4][21] |

### Merchant harbour and roadstead

**Primary cargo interface** is the **low ground north of the wall below the Coastal Gate**, reached by **Pikk** [4][21]. Reppo & Kadakas: gate on **sandstone rise 5–8 m above historical harbour ground**; **probable gate tower 1311–1340**; first written *Strandporte* **1359** [9]. Estonian heritage summary: first medieval **Suur Rannavärav** was **~100 m from sea to gate** — four-storey rectangular gate **~14.4 m** high, **not** the surviving 16th-c. barbican [8][10].

**Quay infrastructure (1343-safe band):**

| Element | Verdict for April 1343 | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Continuous stone quay | **Absent** as default | plausible composite [4][12] |
| Timber/rubble short jetties | **Present** | plausible composite [4][12] |
| Plank warehouses / open sheds | **Present** | attested type by 19th-c. predecessors on site [1] |
| Treadwheel harbour crane | **One yard crane plausible** | plausible composite [13][14] |
| Ropewalk / cordage ground | **Present** near merchant strips | plausible composite Hanseatic port [4] |
| Permanent stone pier forest | **Avoid** unless labelled reconstruction | not attested [4][12] |

**Roadstead:** Large vessels (**Lootsi cog ~24.5 × 9 m** — among largest N European medieval hulls) operated in Reval waters in the **14th-c. third quarter** [1][17]. Wreck sank in **shallow sand**, bow higher than stern, likely **blown off moorings** [1][2]. **Peeter wreck** (~800 m NE, Pikksilma) and **Roio et al. Kadriorg find** (east of Hanseatic town, second quarter 14th c.) show **multiple grounding zones** — not one narrow dock [1][15][22].

### Härjapea river mouth

The **Härjapea** (buried under central Tallinn today) reached the sea through a **delta at Lootsi / Sadama**, with **shifting sand ridges** [1][2]. Medieval harbour activity and wrecks cluster **west-northwest of Lootsi wreck site** (~800 m) and along the **shallow coastal strip toward Kadriorg** [1][2]. **Do not** route a wide navigable river under Pikk Street to the Coastal Gate — the gate opens to **filled foreshore and cliff descent**, not a river channel [9][10].

Harju Gate suburb archaeology: prehistoric/medieval **road toward harbour at Härjapea mouth** from Karja Spring area — wet ground limited early farming [23]. Supports **east–west spine linking inland springs, town, and delta**.

### Fishing shore: Kalamaja and Sand Gate

**Kalamaja** ("fish houses") is a **strong bounded reconstruction** for a **northwestern fishing/settlement fringe** outside the wall — official visitor material describes a **medieval fishing village**; **Kalarand fishermen first commonly cited 1352** (nine years after game date) [5][6]. For April 1343: show **small boats, net drying, smoke/salt sheds**, low timber huts — **not** a formal second Hanseatic quay [4][5].

**Sand Gate (*Väike Rannavärav*):** minor **northeast beach / boat** gate on mid-14th-c. plan [16] — links wet shore to wall circuit; distinct from Coastal Gate merchant landing.

**Off-limits for 1343 harbour pair:** **Pirita convent** (initiative ~1400; building after 1417) [4]; **Kose river mouth** as principal Reval fishing harbour — not established in reviewed material [4].

### Ship types calling at Reval

| Type | Role | 1343 verdict | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cog / cog-like cargo ship** | Bulk Hanseatic trade, timber, tile, salt | **Present** | attested wrecks [1][17][18] |
| **Small open boat / lighter** | Jetty transfer, fishing | **Present** | plausible composite [4][12] |
| **Scandinavian-influenced cargo hull** | Open-sea trade variant | **Present** — local wrecks show non-standard cog anatomy | attested debate [17][18] |
| **Hulk / two-masted transition types** | Later Baltic trade | **Uncertain** in 1343 Reval specifically | plausible composite only [18] |
| **Novgorod river boats** | Eastern trade connection | **Plausible** via Hanseatic eastern route — hull form **unknown** at Reval | plausible composite [20] |
| **Warships / Order patrol craft** | Military corridor | **Present** in 1343 crisis — separate from merchant landing | attested campaign [24] |

**Lootsi cog notes for art:** clinker-built hull, **single mast**, square sail, **compass** finds (late-14th-c. Baltic norm) [1][17]; dendro on similar finds may post-date strict 1343 — use as **third-quarter 14th-c. comparandum**, not April-1343 launch date [17][18].

### Ice, season, and April–May 1343 trade moment

Gulf of Finland **ice cover** typically breaks **late winter–spring**; exact 1343 ice-off day is **not recorded**. **Plausible composite:** coastal lanes **opening in late April–May**, with **mud, cold rain, and shallow-grounding risk** persisting [19][20]. Merchant activity in **April 1343** can show **last winter goods, early spring sailings, and preparation for summer Novgorod convoys** — but **no attested fleet schedule** for that exact month [20].

When the **countryside rises** (St George's Night, 23 April 1343), **harbour labour and overland cart traffic** may **continue under siege tension** while **interior supply chains fracture** — see [`../economy/hanseatic-trade-and-season.md`](../economy/hanseatic-trade-and-season.md) (R-015 deliverable) [24].

### Regional context

- **Danish Estonia:** Crown on Toompea; harbour tolls and royal interest in trade revenue — specifics at Reval **thin** in this tick [24].
- **Hanseatic Reval:** Member of Hanseatic trade network; **cogs, seals, and eastern-route staples** define the merchant landing look [17][20].
- **Livonian Order:** Extramural military pressure in April 1343; **harbour not Order-administered** inside the wall — defence at **Coastal Gate cliff** matters for scenes [9][24].
