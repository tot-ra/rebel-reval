# Kalamaja / Kalarand fishing shore (Spring 1343)

## Brief for Map and Art

You are dressing the **northwestern fishing fringe outside the wall**, not the merchant landing below the Coastal Gate and not a second Hanseatic stone quay [`harbour-and-shoreline.md`](./harbour-and-shoreline.md) [1][2].

**Ship these decisions:**

1. **Location:** **Kalamaja / Kalarand** sits **northwest of the walled Lower Town** on wet beach and low foreshore, reached from inside by the **Sand Gate** (*Väike Rannavärav*) beach exit — distinct from the **Coastal Gate** cargo descent [1][3][4].
2. **April 1343 certainty band:** Fishing activity on this shore is **plausible composite**; **named fishermen at Kalarand are first commonly cited 1352** (nine years after game date). **Kalamaja as a toponym is attested 1374** [5][6][7]. Show a **working shore**, not a dense chartered suburb.
3. **Built footprint:** **Low timber huts**, **net yards**, **smoke/salt sheds**, **boat-repair ground**, **short beach landings** — **no** stone warehouse rows, **no** treadwheel crane, **no** continuous quay [1][2][8].
4. **Population:** Predominantly **Estonian fishermen** with **Swedish and Finnish** presence; **German burghers** buy fish at the wall/market, they do not live here in numbers [6][9]. Status markers tie to R-026 [`estonian-and-german-populations.md`](../people/estonian-and-german-populations.md).
5. **Boats:** **Small open clinker boats** (roughly **5–10 m**), **oared or small sail**; **not** cogs. **6–8 working boats** on shore is a **plausible composite** density for a pre-1352 fringe [1][10][11].
6. **Net-yard layout:** **Three fenced yards** with **pole racks**, **splitting tables**, **rope coils**; nets dried **horizontal on poles** or **hung in open sheds** [8][12][13].
7. **Fish processing:** **Splitting, smoking, salting** in separate small sheds; **salt** is imported Hanseatic stock, not locally mined [`hanseatic-trade-and-season.md`](../economy/hanseatic-trade-and-season.md) [14][15]. **Spring herring** is the dominant Gulf catch band; **drying** preferred for lean spring fish, **salting** increasing through the century [15].
8. **Surface:** **Sand, mud, reed fringe**, scattered **grass**; **no** paved quay. Shoreline **closer to the wall** than today due to uplift and later fill [1][16].
9. **Off-limits:** **Pirita convent** (~1400 initiative); **19th–20th-c. wooden workers' housing**; **railway/industrial Kalamaja**; treating this as the **main Hanseatic cargo harbour** [1][17].
10. **April 1343 moment:** Ice **recently broken**; cold wind, wet sand, **first spring net mending**; fish still reaches town but **countryside uprising** may interrupt overland cart supply without erasing shore work [`spring-climate-and-living-world.md`](../nature/spring-climate-and-living-world.md) [18][19].

## Findings

### Toponym, attestation, and what 1343 may claim

| Claim | Verdict for April 1343 | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Fishing shore northwest of wall | **Present** — bounded reconstruction | plausible composite [1][2][5] |
| Name *Kalamaja* ("fish house/camp") | **Function likely; name not yet attested** (first written **1374**) | attested 1374; pre-1374 use **plausible composite** [5][7] |
| *Kalarand* fishermen named in records | **Activity plausible; names from 1352** | attested 1352; 1343 names **unknown** [6] |
| Medieval fishing village outside wall | **Present as shore settlement type** | plausible composite [5][8] |
| Dense suburb with own church | **Absent** — St Mary's Kalamaja is **15th-c. mid** | attested later [6] |
| Own council (*Fischerherr*, *oldermann*) | **Absent in 1343** — Kalamaja self-government is **later** | attested 15th c.+ [6] |

Tallinn city heritage summary: the area was used by **coastal village fishermen** from **prehistory**; written sources name **fishermen from 1352**; main residents were **Estonians, Swedes, and Finns** with occupations including **boat masters (*mündrik*)**, craftsmen, fishermen, and carters [6]. Paul Johansen (cited in city history) argued for **two villages** within later Kalamaja — a **looser western Estonian-style** strip and a **tighter eastern Swedish-style** cluster — but that structure is **not datable to 1343** [6].

**Danish Estonia / Hanseatic Reval:** The fishing shore lies **outside burgher jurisdiction** but **inside the economic orbit** of the walled town — fish is **sold into Lower Town markets** and Dominican **fish-and-beer** trade [`churches-and-religious-houses.md`](../religion/churches-and-religious-houses.md) [9][14]. **Livonian Order** interest is **military** (coastal approach), not harbour administration [1].

### Built footprint and yard functions

Archaeology and heritage consensus support a **low-density extramural shore settlement** by the 14th century, without a measured 1343 plot plan [1][8]. For map authoring (`reval_harbor_east`), the project report specifies [1]:

| Zone | Elements | 1343 verdict | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Beach landings** | 3 short timber decks/beach ramps | **Present** | plausible composite [1] |
| **Moored boats** | 6 small working boats on wet sand | **Present** | plausible composite [1][10] |
| **Net yards (×3)** | Pole racks, fences, `fishing_nets`, splitting tables | **Present** | plausible composite [8][12] |
| **Smoke shed** | Timber smoke rack, low roof | **Present** | plausible composite [8][15] |
| **Salt shed** | Imported salt pile, barrels | **Present** | plausible composite [14][15] |
| **Boatwright ground** | Timber stack, cart, repair tools | **Present** | plausible composite [1][10] |
| **Fisher huts** | 2–4 single-room timber dwellings | **Present** | plausible composite [8] |
| **Stone quay / crane** | Continuous harbour works | **Absent** | attested absent for this shore [1][2] |
| **Market hall** | Permanent covered fish market | **Absent** — first country fish market **15th c.** | attested later [6] |

**Sand Gate link:** Minor **northeast beach gate** on the mid-14th-c. wall circuit gives wall-side access for carts and small boats; it is **not** the main cargo gate [`walls-gates-towers.md`](./walls-gates-towers.md) [3][4].

### Boat types and fishing gear

| Type | Size / form | Role at Kalarand | 1343 verdict | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Open clinker boat** | ~5–10 m, oar + optional small sail | Inshore herring, net fishing | **Present** | plausible composite [10][11] |
| **Lighter / ship's boat** | Ship-built small craft | Transfer from roadstead — rare here | **Marginal** | plausible composite [2] |
| **Cog / cargo hull** | 20 m+ | **Not beached** at fishing shore | **Absent** at this interface | attested [2][16] |
| **Sewn or hybrid Baltic hull** | Wreck typology debate | Possible local build tradition | **Uncertain** | plausible composite [16] |

Nordic and Baltic small craft were **clinker-built** with rivets or treenails; UNESCO and wreck literature treat this as the **default coastal fishing tradition** around the Baltic [10][11]. No **Reval-specific** 1343 fishing-boat wreck is published; use **comparanda**, not a single canonical hull [16].

**Gear:** **Hemp/flax nets** with stone or lead weights; **longlines** plausible but less visible in art dressing [12][13]. **Boat masters (*mündrik*)** commanded larger boats within the community — a **status step above** ordinary net fishermen [6].

### Fish species, processing, and trade link

| Product | Spring 1343 role | Processing on shore | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Baltic herring** | Dominant Gulf catch | Split, smoke, salt | plausible composite [14][15] |
| **Pike, bream, perch** | Local consumption + some export | **Drying** common pre-salt | plausible composite [15] |
| **Salted barrel herring** | Town/Lent market | Salt shed; barrels from town cooperage | plausible composite [14] |

Before mid-century **Hanseatic salt** availability improved preservation economics; for **April 1343** show **both drying racks and emerging salt piles** — not a fully industrial saltery [`hanseatic-trade-and-season.md`](../economy/hanseatic-trade-and-season.md) [14][15]. Local herring export through Reval is **minor vs Novgorod bulk** but **visible at the fishing shore** [14].

### Shore character vs merchant harbour

| Feature | Merchant landing (Coastal Gate) | Kalamaja fishing shore |
|---|---|---|
| Shore surface | Mud flats, short jetties, cliff descent | Open beach, reed margin |
| Vessels | Cogs in roadstead; lighters at jetties | Small boats on sand |
| Buildings | Cargo sheds, ropewalk, crane marker | Huts, net yards, smoke/salt sheds |
| Labour | Mixed harbour workers + merchants | Estonian/Swedish/Finnish fishers |
| Stone works | Rubble/timber landings only | **None** |

## Production hooks

- **Map:** Author `reval_harbor_east` as **northwest extramural beach** with **three net-yard clusters**, **three walkable timber beach decks**, **6–8 boat props** on wet sand, **reed/scrub margin** to playable edge; link cart path toward **Sand Gate**, not Viru [1][3]. Keep **water impassable** except deck tips [`docs/reports/reval_harbour_1343_research.md`](../../../docs/reports/reval_harbour_1343_research.md).
- **Art:** **Hut massing:** low timber, tarred plank or split log, **no** high stone gables — plate [`topography.kalamaja-fishing-shore-1343.01`](../../reference/topography/kalamaja-fishing-shore-1343/topography.kalamaja-fishing-shore-1343.01.jpg) (Walraversijde comparandum, label **1465 Flanders**). **Nets:** horizontal pole racks — plate **02** and manuscript **04**. **Boats:** small open **clinker** hulls, **not** cog silhouettes — plate **03**. **Fish work:** splitting table, smoke rack, salt pile props per [`MAP_AUTHORING.md`](../../../docs/MAP_AUTHORING.md) district-life kit [1].
- **Character:** **Estonian fisher** in wool layers, tarred boots, knife at belt; **Swedish/Finnish** boat master with slightly richer cap/cloak; **no** burgher fur-trim or guild insignia on shore workers [6][9].
- **Quest / Narrative:** Smuggle through **Sand Gate** at dawn; **net-yard theft** during St George's unrest; **Order patrol** watching beach approach; **Dominican buyer** haggling over Lent herring price [9][14].
- **Dialogue:** Estonian shore terms deferred to R-024; Low German *Fisch*, *Hering*, *Rauchfisch* for town-facing speech; avoid modern harbour admin vocabulary [14].
