# Pikk and Lai frontage materials (1340s)

## Brief for Map and Art

You are texturing **street-facing building skins** on the two main merchant spines, not assigning interior plans (see [`burgher-house-plan.md`](../architecture/burgher-house-plan.md)).

**Critical limitation:** surviving 1340s Reval property books (*Alter Wirtbuch*, mortgage entries, tax rolls) name **stone houses** (*lapideam domum*) and **timber houses** but **almost never give street addresses** until the late 1340s [1][2]. There is **no attested house-by-house cadastre for Pikk or Lai in April 1343**. This dossier gives **ward-level material bands** and **two worked blocks** derived from archaeology, building history, and synthesis — every plot-level call is labelled.

**Ship these decisions:**

1. **Default 1343 frontage on both spines:** **timber-dominant street faces** (log, plank, or lime-plastered timber) with **stone cellars, party walls, and rear *Hinterhaus*** already common on wealthy strips — attested pattern for the 14th century, not the 15th-century stone-skin default [3][4][5].
2. **Do not stone-skin every gable.** Full limestone Gothic street facades spread **more widely in the 15th century**; April 1343 is the **transition zone** [3][5].
3. **Pikk Block A (market east toward St Olaf):** ~**55–70% timber front**, ~**25–40% mixed or stone front** on affluent plots near the Town Hall and guild quarter; rear stone storehouses on deep plots. Tile on stone masses only [6][7].
4. **Pikk Block B (harbour descent):** ~**60–75% timber front** with **stone cellars and warehouses** behind; hoist beams on affluent merchants regardless of skin material [3][8].
5. **Lai Block A (market south, NUKU-quarter archaeology zone):** ~**70–85% timber front** in April 1343 — mid-14th-c. round-log auxiliary buildings are attested; **modest stone street house only from the fourth quarter / end of the 14th century** on this quarter [4][9].
6. **Lai Block B (St Olaf end / Three Brothers strip):** ~**50–65% mixed front** — stone merchant fabric accumulates here across the late 14th c., but surviving unified Gothic rows are **15th c. and later skin** [3][10][11].
7. **Roof cover tracks material:** tile clusters on stone civic/merchant masses; shingle and thatch on timber fronts [6].
8. **Surviving tourist houses** (Three Sisters, Three Brothers, Lai 23) are **typology comparanda only** — their present stone Gothic skin is mostly post-1343 [10][11][12].

## Findings

### What the 1340s records actually say

Reval's city archives hold one of the richer Hanseatic record collections [1]. Fourteenth-century survivals include land and mortgage books, city accounts, customs books, and property-tax rolls [1].

| Record type | Material evidence | Location precision | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| AWB mortgage entries 1334–1346 | Stone houses, granaries, stalls, orchards | **No street** in oldest book [1][2] | attested material terms; location **unknown** [1][2] |
| AWB 1349 annuity | *lapideam domum* opposite Dominican priory, St Olaf parish | **First located stone house** near 1343 in reviewed sample [2] | attested [2] |
| NUKU quarter plot record 1372 | Timber house given to shoemaker | Lai-quarter plot, **29 years after 1343** [9] | attested; supports timber persistence on Lai [9] |
| Building-history chains (Pikk 52, 55, etc.) | Original wooden houses replaced 15th c. | Address-specific but **post-1343** [10] | attested for later phases; 1343 inference **plausible composite** [10] |

**Bottom line:** property records confirm **both timber and stone houses existed in the 1340s** but **cannot** be mapped to individual Pikk/Lai frontages for Spring 1343 [1][2]. Any street-level map is a **labelled composite**.

### AWB street-name pass (1340-1343 window; edition-local)

A full-volume OCR pass on the public-domain Arbusow/BSB MDZ digitisation (240 canvases, manifest `bsb00149661`) searched for `picken`, `langen`, `Pikk`, `Lai`, `laien`, `karri*`, and related Latin street forms. **No AWB entry in the published edition names Pikk or Lai** (or obvious MLG/Latin equivalents such as *platea longa* / *platea picken*) in any register year [14].

Within the **1340-1343** entry band, location formulas that **do** appear are landmark- or function-based, not the modern merchant-spine names:

| AWB no. | Date / sequence | Printed page; MDZ canvas | Location formula | Relevance to Pikk/Lai | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **508** | **1341** | p. 78; canvas 91 | `areas duarum institricarum bodarum iuxta consistorium proxime sitarum` | Forum / council-house adjacency, not a named Pikk/Lai frontage | attested [14] |
| **521** | **1341** | pp. 72-73; canvases 89, 93 | `hereditas in platea fabrorum in opposito domini Hunoldi de Ostinchusen sita` | Smiths' plate near a named burgher; useful guild-quarter comparandum, **not** evidence that *platea fabrorum* equals Pikk or Lai | attested [14] |
| **553** | **1342 register sequence** (before AWB 554, 3 Dec. 1342) | p. 83; canvas 99 | `stupa sub longo monte` | Bath-site landmark; **do not** equate *longo monte* with the Estonian street name Pikk without separate onomastic evidence | attested clause; street identification **gap** [14] |
| **569** | **1343** | p. 86; canvas 102 | `hereditatem retro consistorium sitam` | Rear plot behind the council house; forum-edge hook only | attested [14] |

Earlier but still pre-1343 comparanda outside the narrow window include AWB **302** (`platea dicta dummestrate`, hoist-street / Rataskaevu anchor; see [`back-lanes-east-of-pikk.md`](./back-lanes-east-of-pikk.md)) and AWB **497** (continuation on canvas 89: `platea fabrorum in opposito domini Hunoldi de Ostinchusen`) [14].

**Production read:** Map and art must keep Pikk/Lai as **modern spine labels** backed by later onomastics and archaeology, not AWB house-by-house addressing. Ward-level timber/stone bands in this dossier remain valid; do not promote AWB *platea fabrorum* rows into plot tags on Pikk Block A/B without a separate transfer argument.

### Citywide material rule (1343-safe)

Medieval Heritage's synthesis for Reval states that in the **14th century front street houses were mostly wood**, while **square-plan stone houses** often stood behind them on the same plot; stone street frontage spread more widely in the **15th century** [3]. The project audit agrees: **no defensible citywide wood-to-stone percentage** — vary by wealth and ward [6].

Archaeology in the **NUKU inner courtyard** (Lai-quarter, western Lower Town) adds timing:

- **Mid-14th century:** round-log auxiliary building (3.4 × 2.5 m), dirt floor, no hearth — service structure, not main dwelling [4][9].
- **Fourth quarter / end of 14th century:** modest limestone house (4.4 m wide), lime-mortar bound, wooden floors [4][9].
- Soil layers with chips/manure dated **mid-14th to fourth quarter 14th century** associated with timber construction [9].

For **April 1343** on Lai: timber front and service buildings are the **safest default**; stone street house on this archaeology zone is **not yet attested** [4][9].

### Worked block — Pikk Block A (Raekoja plats east frontage)

Segment: south side of market eastward toward St Olaf, ~8–12 plots. Aligns with [`lower-town-street-plan.md`](./lower-town-street-plan.md) Block B and [`raekoja-plats-extents-1343.md`](./raekoja-plats-extents-1343.md) south frontage.

| Plot position | Street front material | Rear / cellar | Roof | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Adjacent to Town Hall (wealthy) | Mixed: stone ground floor with timber upper, or timber front over stone cellar | Stone cellar, possible stone *Hinterhaus* | Tile on stone mass | plausible composite [3][7][13] |
| Mid-block merchant (standard) | Timber front, limewashed plank or log | Stone cellar neck; stone rear storehouse common | Shingle/tile mix | plausible composite [3][6] |
| Near St Olaf / guild zone | Timber front dominant | Timber yard sheds; stone party walls | Shingle | plausible composite [3][8] |
| *Boda* renter plots | Small timber two-room front | Minimal yard | Thatch/shingle | attested type [3] |

**Pikk 55** (within this zone): limestone outer walls **likely 14th century**, diele-dornse layout discernible — use as **upper-bound stone-front example** for one wealthy plot, not the block default [10]. **Pikk 52** plots first mentioned **late 14th / early 15th century** with **original wooden houses** — confirms timber origin on this strip before stone replacement [10].

**Art/map hook:** Shoulder-to-shoulder gables with **visual mix** — mostly pale limewashed timber, occasional grey limestone front on 1–2 plots per ~10-plot run.

### Worked block — Lai Block A (market south, west of NUKU quarter)

Segment: Lai parallel to market south frontage through the quarter bordered by Lai, Nunne, Suur-Kloostri, Väike-Kloostri [9].

| Plot position | Street front material | Rear / cellar | Roof | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Market-adjacent merchant | Timber front over stone cellar | Stone rear house emerging on wealthy plots | Shingle/tile mix | plausible composite [3][4] |
| Mid-quarter standard | **Timber front** (log/plank) | Timber service buildings; wattle yard fences | Shingle/thatch | attested archaeology for mid-14c timber [4][9] |

**April 1343 verdict for this block:** **no attested stone street house** in the NUKU archaeology zone; assign **≥70% timber fronts** [4][9].

### Worked block — Lai Block B (St Olaf end / Three Brothers strip)

Segment: Lai east toward St Olaf, including Lai 38–42 (Three Brothers) and Lai 23 [3][10].

| Plot position | Street front material | Rear / cellar | Roof | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Three Brothers strip (Lai 38–42) | Mixed to stone — **late 14th c. accumulation** | Courtyard wings stone and timber composite | Tile on stone masses | plausible composite; present skin **15th c.+** [3][10][12] |
| Lai 23 | Stone Gothic core, portal **second half 15th c.** | Courtyard ensemble multi-phase | Tile | attested later fabric; 1343 **plausible composite** stone merchant [10] |
| Lai 5 rear reference | Stone front house | **Timber service buildings** in yard | Mixed | attested rear-yard timber [12] |

**April 1343 verdict:** more stone than Block A, but still **mixed** — do not render the full Three Brothers stone row as uniform 1343 skin [10][12].

### Regional power context (why material mix matters)

- **Danish crown / Hanseatic council:** no decree mandating stone fronts in 1343; material choice follows **wealth, fire risk, and plot geology** [3][6].
- **Livonian Order:** extramural; does not set Lower Town facade rules in April 1343 [6].
- **1343 siege:** no licence to show post-war stone-rebuild fabric; the mix described here is **pre-uprising** [6].

## Production hooks

- **Map:** Apply material tags per plot in the four blocks above: `front_timber`, `front_mixed`, `front_stone` (stone ≤2 per 10-plot run on Pikk A and Lai B only). Cellars: `stone_cellar` on ≥50% of merchant plots on both spines. Roof: ward bands from audit — Pikk/Lai merchant rows tile **10–25%** of roof area [6]. Use plate [`topography.pikk-lai-frontage-materials-1340s.04`](../../reference/topography/pikk-lai-frontage-materials-1340s/topography.pikk-lai-frontage-materials-1340s.04.jpg) for gable rhythm, not facade date.
- **Art:** Timber fronts: horizontal log or plank with limewash, **smaller openings**, tar-dark shingle roof. Stone fronts: grey limestone, pointed-arch ground openings only, **no rich blind niches** as default [3][6]. Mixed: stone ground storey + timber upper (late-1340s wealthy). Rear yards: timber sheds even when front is stone — plate [`.05`](../../reference/topography/pikk-lai-frontage-materials-1340s/topography.pikk-lai-frontage-materials-1340s.05.jpg). Late-campaign stone rows: plates [`.01`](../../reference/topography/pikk-lai-frontage-materials-1340s/topography.pikk-lai-frontage-materials-1340s.01.jpg)–[`.03`](../../reference/topography/pikk-lai-frontage-materials-1340s/topography.pikk-lai-frontage-materials-1340s.03.jpg) labelled post-1343.
- **Character:** Wealth displays on **goods, hoist gear, and cellar capacity**, not universal stone skin [3][8].
- **Quest / Narrative:** Fire risk on timber-front rows; council pressure to rebuild in stone after fires — **post-1343 trope**, do not stage as April 1343 ordinance [3].
- **Dialogue:** *Steinhus* / stone house vs *Holzhaus* / timber house as property-law terms in mortgage speech [1][2].
- **Dev / systems:** Plot material roll: Pikk A `timber 0.62, mixed 0.28, stone 0.10`; Pikk B `timber 0.70, mixed 0.22, stone 0.08`; Lai A `timber 0.78, mixed 0.18, stone 0.04`; Lai B `timber 0.55, mixed 0.30, stone 0.15` — all **plausible composite** weights, not measured 1343 data [3][4][6][9].

## Open questions

- **Resolved (edition-local negative, 2026-08-28):** the published Arbusow/MDZ AWB contains **no** explicit Pikk/Lai street name in 1340-1343 entries; landmark formulas (*consistorium*, *platea fabrorum*, *longo monte*) remain the usable locators. A TLA manuscript folio collation could still surface spellings absent from the 1888 print/OCR [14].
- **Dendrochronology** for surviving Pikk/Lai houses with 14th-c. cores — would tighten which plots can carry `front_stone` in Act 2.
- **Fire-loss chronology** on Pikk vs Lai before 1400 — would explain acceleration of stone fronts.
