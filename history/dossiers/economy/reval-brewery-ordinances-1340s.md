# Reval brewery ordinances, beer excise, and Estonian collectors (1340–1343)

## Brief for Research / Canon / Quest

The requested 1340–1343 Reval evidence does **not** currently establish a citizen-only brewing rule, a dated beer excise, or a formally named Estonian beer-collector office. The dossier therefore preserves a useful negative finding rather than back-projecting later Tallinn brewing ordinances or an undated secondary description into Spring 1343.

**Ship these decisions:**

1. **No three-line ordinance or excise proof was located.** The reviewed AWB and accessible council evidence supplies dated property, debt, and civic-rights entries, not a 1340–1343 *Brauerschragen*, *Biersteuer*, *cervisia*, or brewer-registration line [1][2][3].
2. **Do not present citizen-only brewing as attested in 1343.** A later Tallinn ordinance tradition can support a `plausible composite` brewing monopoly only when labelled as later comparandum; it cannot fill this source gap [4].
3. **Do not cast an Estonian collector as a documented 1343 office-holder.** Johansen and von zur Mühlen's secondary account preserves an Estonian beer-excise collection role, but the reviewed summary gives no 1340–1343 date, named collector, rate, office title, or AWB/Denkelbuch line [5].
4. **Beer remains an ordinary production and diet element.** Beer, malt, kegs, cellar storage, and brewery labour are usable as period-appropriate economic and visual context; their presence does not prove a council excise [6][7].
5. **The Bitter Brew conflict must be labelled.** An Estonian collector confronting a German brewer is a `plausible composite` or `invented` quest device pending a folio-level source, never an `attested` 1343 ordinance scene.

### Archival access audit (2026-08-10)

The official Rahvusarhiiv catalogue identifies `TLA.230.1.Aa2` as a digitized **48-page** German-language Denkelbuch unit covering **1333–1374**, with catalogue metadata about council elections, punishments, and judgments. The public DGS permalink for the unit redirects to VAU authentication before the manuscript image can be read. No 1340–1343 folio/page, original wording, beer-excise rate, collector name, or office can therefore be reported from this pass [9][10].

The public BSB/MDZ IIIF manifest for Arbusow's **1888 printed** *Ältestes Wittschopbuch* remains usable for the separate AWB publication and its OCR, but it is not a folio-level surrogate for Denkelbuch `TLA.230.1.Aa2`. The result is an **access blocker**, not negative evidence about the contents of the Denkelbuch. R-451 remains blocked until an authenticated VAU/DGS review or a lawful archive-provided scan/transcription preserves provenance and folio/page identifiers [1][9][10].

### AWB OCR collation (2026-08-11)

A bounded OCR pass was run against the public BSB/MDZ digitisation of Arbusow's printed AWB, covering printed pp. 74–87 and AWB nos. 500–580 (MDZ canvases 90–103). This span contains the 1340–1343 entries used here; canvas 104 / printed p. 88 was checked only as the 1344 boundary, where AWB 577 begins on 3 January 1344. The OCR is hOCR XHTML, so tags were stripped, whitespace normalized, and adjacent canvases inspected before classifying a token as absent [1].

The search families were `Bier` / `bire` / `cervisia` / `cerevisia`, `Brau*` / `Brauer` / `Brauerschragen`, `accisa` / `excisa` / `collect*` / `vectigal` / `tribut*` / `telon` / `thelon` / `Zoll` / `Steuer` / `tax`, plus `Fass`, `doli*`, `cupa`, `malz` / `malt`, and collector terms. No relevant beer, brewing, excise, or collector hit was found in this AWB span. The apparent OCR fragments `tol` on pp. 74–75 and 83 are parts of unrelated words, and `cupa` on p. 80 occurs in the phrase `cupa ... intrare et exire` about a property access clause; neither supports a beer duty or brewery reading [1].

### Later and indirect material that must stay labelled

- Bunge's identifiable *Brauerschragen* material is from **1485 or later**, not 1340–1343. It can illustrate a later regulatory vocabulary, but cannot be used as Spring 1343 law [4].
- The secondary account of Estonian beer-excise collectors is valuable for a social-role hypothesis, but the accessible citation does not supply an AWB number, Denkelbuch folio, date, rate, or office name for this window [5].
- Existing food dossiers' brewing props and diet claims remain usable as `plausible composite` context. They must not be upgraded into a dated fiscal system by adding a collector NPC, tax post, or quoted rate without a new source [6][7].

## Production hooks

- **Quest / Narrative:** `Bitter Brew` may stage a brewer-versus-collector dispute only with an explicit `plausible_composite` or `invented` tag. The conflict can turn on debt, quality, access, or ethnic suspicion without claiming a verified beer tariff. A future archival hit should replace the tag, not silently reinterpret it.
- **Character:** A German citizen brewer and an Estonian haulier, tavern worker, or tax-related messenger are period-compatible composites. Do not give the Estonian character a documented council office, badge, rate book, or legal monopoly title in 1343.
- **Dialogue:** Use *Bier*, *Öl*, *Fass*, *Brauer*, and *Brauerei* as cautious MLG vocabulary. Do not have a clerk quote *Biersteuer*, *cervisia*, a *Brauerschragen*, or a pfennig rate as attested 1343 law. If the quest uses a collector, subtitle the uncertainty through rumour or an invented local arrangement.
- **Art:** Malt sacks, wooden kegs, a boiling kettle, cellar neck, and tavern cups are valid brewery-life props from the food-and-drink brief; no tax booth, official collector livery, or ordinance tablet is supported by this pass [6].
- **Map:** Allow a brewery yard and cellar-service route, but set `beer_excise_post: null` and `brewery_ordinance_1343: unknown` until a folio read supplies a stable civic location.
- **Dev / systems:** Store `beer_excise_attested_1340_1343: false`; keep any quest tax amount under `confidence: plausible_composite` or `invented`; do not expose it as a historical price table.
