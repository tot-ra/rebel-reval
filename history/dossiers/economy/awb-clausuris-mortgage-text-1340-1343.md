# AWB *clausuris* and yard-access language (AWB 554-580; 1342-1344 boundary)

## Brief for Quest / Dev / Map / Dialogue

A direct OCR pass through the public-domain MDZ scan of Arbusow's AWB edition was run against entries 554-580, the requested register band rather than a falsely uniform 1340-1343 date block. The pass finds mortgage and hereditary-property language in entries dated 1342 and 1343, plus the boundary entries 577-580 dated 1344, but **does not find the noun *clausuris* in AWB 554-580**. The exact *clausura/clausuris* attestations begin later in the same register and are useful only as a lexical control, not as Spring 1343 dating evidence.

**Ship these decisions:**

1. Keep `clausuris` as a **later AWB enclosure term**, not an attested 1343 legal phrase. The earliest direct hits located here are AWB 716/717 (7 April 1348), 926 (20 March 1355), and 1004 (9 February 1358) [1].
2. Use 1343 property language that is actually present: *hereditas*, *area hereditatis*, *curia*, *murus*, and *retro consistorium* for hereditary plots, courtyard access, walls, and rear-yard positioning [1].
3. Do not make a 1343 clerk quote *clausuris* or turn the term into a fixed cart toll, gate fee, or municipal enclosure ordinance. The target-year mortgage entries remain financial/security records, not a tariff schedule [1][2].
4. Treat the negative result as **edition-local and entry-bounded**: it is established for the published Arbusow/MDZ pages and OCR covering AWB 554-580, not for every AWB entry, every manuscript witness, or an uncollated TLA folio [1].
5. Keep the dossier `partial` pending Canon Keeper review and, if the exact manuscript wording is required, a TLA/Arbusow folio collation of the same entries.

## Findings

### Method and source boundary

The scan is the public-domain MDZ digitisation of L. Arbusow (ed.), *Das älteste Wittschopbuch der Stadt Reval (1312-1360)*, Reval 1888. MDZ exposes page OCR and IIIF search; the printed page numbers below are the book page, followed by the MDZ canvas number. OCR was checked against the IIIF image coordinates for each quoted term. The OCR is searchable, but it is not a substitute for a TLA manuscript collation; uncertain readings are marked rather than silently normalised [1].

### Later lexical control for *clausura*

The term is directly attested in the same printed edition, but outside the 1340-1343 target window:

| AWB no. | Date / printed page | Verbatim Latin excerpt | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| **716** | 1348, printed p. 116; MDZ canvas 132 | `... se quinque clausuras, que Engelberto de Werve pertinebant et duas bodas institorum ... pro 45 mc. arg. ratione pignoris obligasse ...` | Direct mortgage/security use, but five years after the target window. |
| **717** | 1348, printed p. 116; MDZ canvas 132 | `... dimidiam suam hereditatem ... et dimidiam suam lapideam domum et duas penesticales bodas ... pro 45 mc. arg. ratione pignoris obligasse ...` | The paired entry describes hereditary property, a stone house, and two shoemaker workshops; the index associates the preceding *clausuras* with this entry group. |
| **926** | 1355, printed p. 143; MDZ canvas 177 | `... tertiam partem de tribus clausuris, videlicet uno cellario et duabus lapideis domibus ...` | The register glosses three enclosures as one cellar and two stone houses. This is a strong semantic control for the later term, not a 1343 citation. |
| **1004** | 1358, printed p. 176; MDZ canvas 192 | `... in suam lapideam domum cum omnibus suis clausuris in opposito fratrum predicatorum sitam ...` | *Clausuris* groups the appurtenant enclosures of a stone house opposite the Dominicans. Again, later than 1343. |

The printed index confirms `clausura` at AWB 716, 803, 805, 926, and 1004; it does not list AWB 755. The direct text of AWB 755 (1349, printed p. 125) is an annuity in a hereditary property and does not contain *clausura* [1]. This corrects the broader inherited note that had grouped 755 with the enclosure attestations.

### Negative result for the target window

The target-year entries show the property-security grammar around the requested term: *obligatus* / *ratione pignoris*, *hereditas*, *area hereditatis*, *curia*, *murus*, and rear positioning. They do **not** provide a direct *clausuris* quotation in AWB 554-580, including the 1343 entries and the 1344 boundary entries included in the requested band [1]. The safest historical statement is therefore: enclosure and yard boundaries are an attested property concern in the register, while the exact plural noun is first located here in later entries. `attested` applies to the quoted clauses; `partial` / `gap` applies to transferring the later lexical form back into 1343 or from the printed edition into the TLA manuscript [1][2].

## Production hooks

- **Quest / Narrative:** A 1343 creditor can cite *hereditas* and *ratio pignoris*; a neighbour can point to a *murus*, *curia*, or a window right. Do not put *clausuris* in a dated 1343 line unless dialogue labels it as later register vocabulary or a deliberate reconstruction.
- **Map:** Use a rear hereditary plot beside a wall or behind a civic landmark as `plausible bounded reconstruction` from the attested `iuxta murum` and `retro consistorium` formulas. Preserve the existing Coastal Gate garden evidence as a separate secondary pattern; this dossier does not add a measured polygon.
- **Dialogue:** Retain Latin/MLG terms *hereditas*, *area*, *curia*, *murus*, *pignus*, and *clausura* only as a later/control term. A clerk may explain that the record names property and security, not a wheel toll.
- **Dev / systems:** Keep `fuhr_servitude_attested: false` and `cart_toll_pfennig: null` from the parent contract. If a future property-clause model needs an enclosure token, mark `clausura` as `later_awb_lexicon` until a 1340-1343 archival folio confirms it.
- **Art:** A wall, courtyard window, rear plot, and attached workshop are supported by the target-year clauses; no specific 1343 fence height, gate design, or enclosure count is established here.
