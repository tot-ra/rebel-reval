# Names, address, and oaths (Spring 1343)

## Brief for Dialogue / Character / Narrative

You are writing **speech in a tri-lingual port town** where **Low German is the civic and commercial default**, **Latin is the church and charter register**, and **Estonian is the labour and hinterland tongue**. April–May 1343 Reval is **not** bilingual signage — most written surfaces are **Latin or Low German**; Estonian is **spoken**, rarely **recorded** in town documents.

**Ship these decisions:**

1. **Register split:** Council, merchants, Vogt, guild masters, and forge contracts → **Middle Low German (MLG)**. Liturgy, indulgence letters, Danish royal charters → **Latin formulae** (characters may quote fragments, not lecture in Ciceronian prose). Estonian labourers, herdsmen, rebel camps, manor yards → **Estonian** (use sparingly in subtitles; no modern Estonian slang) [1][2][3].
2. **Naming — German burghers:** Forename + **hereditary or occupational byname** (*de Lapide*, *Osenbrygghe*, *Zierenberg*). Patronymic *-sone* / *-dochter* appears but **stable surnames dominate merchant families** by 1343 [4][5][6]. Hypocoristics (*Henne*, *Clawes*, *Tydeke*) are **normal spoken forms** — use full Latinised forenames only in **Latin charters** [7][8].
3. **Naming — Estonians:** **Single Christian or traditional forename** + optional **patronymic** (*…pockke* / *…poeg*, "son of …") or **farm/village locator** in German records [9][10][11]. **No hereditary family name** for peasants in 1343 — do not give Kalev a documentary surname unless flagged `invented` [9].
4. **Address ladder (Low German):** **Herr** + name or office for any male burgher you respect; **Bürgermeister** / **Ratsherr** for council; **Meister** for guild masters; **junker** only for **knights and noble vassals**, not merchants [12][13][14]. **Frau** / **wîf** for married women of standing; **maget** for unmarried women in courtly/Lübeck comparandum [15]. Servants and labour: **geselle**, **knecht**, **dirne** (maid) — status-marked, not insults by default [16].
5. **Greetings:** Daytime **"Gode dagh"** / **"Guden dag"**; evening **"Gode avend"**; departure **"Gode reise"** — **plausible composite** from MLG greeting patterns [17]. Market haggling opens with **"Wat kostet dat?"** not High German *Was kostet das?* [1].
6. **Oaths — legal:** Court oaths invoke **God, saints, relics**; perjury is **theological crime**, not modern profanity [`power.law-courts-and-punishment.md`](../power/law-courts-and-punishment.md) [18][19]. Formula: **"bi Gode"** / **"so wahr mir God helpe"** — **`plausible composite`** from Sachsenspiegel and Lübeck-law tradition [20][21].
7. **Oaths — anger:** Workshop and street frustration uses **divine names** (*Gotts lichnam*, *verflucht*) and **honour insults** (*hundes fot*, *valsche hore*) — **plausible composite** from 14th–15th c. Germanic practice; **no modern scatological register** [22][23]. False oath in anger still **frightens** witnesses — blasphemy ordinances exist **`plausible composite`** [24].
8. **Do not use:** High German standard (*Sie*, *Herr Doktor*), Early Modern English (*thee/thou* as default), Russian unless a **Novgorod trader** scene, Swedish unless a **named envoy** after 18 May, or **post-Reformation** oath forms [1][25].

MLG was the **Hanseatic lingua franca** from c. 1300 and **parallel to Latin for deeds and diplomacy** [1][2]. Reval's **non-standardised** orthography — same word may appear **Clawes**, **Claues**, **Nikolaus** in one generation [7][8].

### Name stock — usable without `invented` label

**German male forenames (documentary frequency high in Baltic MLG):** Johannes, Henricus/Hinrik, Theodoricus/Tyde, Nicolaus/Clawes, Hermann, Winandus, Dietrich, Rotcherus, Ricbodo, Heyno, Apollonius [7][8][30].

**German female forenames (Riga debt-book comparandum 1286–1352):** Mechtild, Sophia, Walburgis, Yda, Greta [32].

**Estonian male forenames (traditional + Christian layer):** Mart (Marten), Hindrik, Töll, Lembit, Ain — **last two more folklore-heavy for 1343** [10][34]. Use **Marten**, **Hinrick**, **Jüri** (George — ties to St George's Night) with **`plausible composite`** label [34].

**Estonian female forenames:** Liis, Mari, Anna — **sparse 14th-c. attestation** [10].

**Offices as address substitutes:** **Bürgermeister**, **Ratsherr**, **Schöffe**, **Vogt**, **Kämmerer**, **Stadtschreiber**, **Meister** (guild), **Bruder** (Dominican friar), **Herre** (knight/vassal) [12][14][30].

**Frau** for burgher wives; **dirne** for maid — not automatically pejorative [15][16]. **Junker** misapplied to a merchant is a **deliberate insult** [14].

### Oaths, vows, and curses

**Legal oath (Vogt/Rat):** Swearer raises hand on **gospels or relics**; helpers (*Eideshelfer*) attest credibility, not facts [`power.law-courts-and-punishment.md`](../power/law-courts-and-punishment.md) [`language.names-address-and-oaths.02`](../../reference/language/names-address-and-oaths/language.names-address-and-oaths.02.jpg) [18][19][20].

| Type | Example MLG | Context | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Simple truth oath | **So wahr mir God helpe** | Court, guild inquiry | plausible composite [20][21] |
| Relic oath | **Bi des hilligen Cristes bein** | High-stakes denial | plausible composite [19][20] |
| Charter closing | **In getüge desses segels** | Town seal on proclamation | plausible composite [27] |
| Forge bargain | **Bi minem ere** | Handshake deal | plausible composite |
| Angry blasphemy | **Gotts lichnam!** / **Verflucht!** | Stubbed toe, betrayal | plausible composite [22][24] |
| Honour curse | **Du valsche hore!** | Brawl — may draw Vogt | plausible composite [23] |
| Estonian oath | **Jumala säästku** (God save) | Rural — **modernised spelling for UI** | folklore / composite [34] |

Medieval oaths were **performative and sacred** — a false oath **imperils the soul** [19][22]. **Scatological swearing** as modern default is **anachronistic** for 1343; divine-name invocation carries the weight [22][23].

## Production hooks

- **Dialogue:** Bind NPC locale to register — `{mlg_burgher}`, `{mlg_labour}`, `{estonian}`, `{latin_clergy}`, `{latin_formula}`. Address table above is the **lookup**; never mix **Junker** onto a merchant. Subtitle policy: **Estonian lines get Estonian text + gloss once**; Latin only as **short formula** (*In nomine Patris*).
- **Character:** Name generator pools — **German:** forename list + byname list (toponymic, occupational); **Estonian:** forename + optional patronymic, **no inherited surname**. Flag every name from **1333 list** if used as sitting councillor. **Zierenberg** is the only **near-attested burgomaster surname** for 1343 [31].
- **Narrative:** Treaty scene (21 May) — **Latin header**, MLG oral summary for crowd; **Stadtschreiber** reads **Datum Revalie**. Court scene — oath on book [`language.names-address-and-oaths.02`](../../reference/language/names-address-and-oaths/language.names-address-and-oaths.02.jpg). Forge — **Meister** address and **bi minem ere** handshake.
- **Quest:** Insult escalation ladder: **schalk** (verbal) → **brawl** → **Vogt** → **Eideshelfer** trial. False oath in guild hall triggers **Amt** fine **`plausible composite`** [`guild-structure.md`](../crafts/guild-structure.md).
- **Art / UI:** Chirography on props - **MLG blackletter** on shop signs; **Latin gothic** on church and seal [`language.names-address-and-oaths.01`](../../reference/language/names-address-and-oaths/language.names-address-and-oaths.01.jpg).
