# Reval Rebel: Top 100 World-Building Sound Effects

## Scope and decisions

- Requested deliverable: 100 high-value sound effects with ready-to-copy generation prompts. Every prompt explicitly prohibits music.
- Setting: medieval Baltic Reval and its surrounding countryside, with the campaign beginning in spring 1343. Prioritize Kalev's smithy, Lower Town, Viru Gate, harbor, church/monastery spaces, rural routes, and night pressure.
- This is a proposed production shortlist, not a claim that the sounds are missing, generated, historically authenticated, or connected to runtime events.
- Prompts are in English for direct use in a sound-effects generator. No audio generation is part of this deliverable.
- Numbers are stable catalog references grouped by use, not a strict cross-category ranking. Generate **P0 first**, then **P1**, then **P2**; within a tier, follow catalog order. Priorities favor reusable world layers and frequent interactions over unique cinematic effects.
- **P0** = core identity and frequent gameplay feedback. **P1** = broad location/action coverage. **P2** = situational enrichment and authored fantasy.
- **NEW** = proposed generation candidate; still check the asset library before production. **AUDIT** = an obvious related asset/library already exists; listen and reuse first, generate only if unsuitable. Existing audio has not been auditioned for this list.
- Scope deliberately excludes soundtrack, musical UI rewards, sung prayer, intelligible dialogue, exotic wildlife, modern machinery, firearms, and unsupported monster encounters. Bell strikes remain physical environmental sounds, never melodies.
- Folklore cues 96-97 preserve uncertainty rather than declaring an objective monster. Cues 98-100 are proposed materials for content-gated magic/rites, not historical claims or a claim that a specific spell is currently shipped.

## Generation and delivery rules

1. **One sound per generation.** A row is an asset family, not a montage. For repeated one-shots generate 4-6 separate takes; for important impacts and footsteps aim for 6-8. Do not ask the model to fit all variants into one clip.
2. **S = one-shot**, **L = loopable bed**, **T = finite texture/event**. Durations below are targets in seconds, not engine limits. Leave full decay on one-shots; no baked fade on loops. If the generator cannot produce a long bed, generate shorter consistent sections and build a loop offline.
3. Keep spatial objects dry and centered, preferably mono. Use stereo for broad environmental beds. Add room reverb, distance filtering, occlusion, footsteps cadence, and movement in-engine rather than baking them into every asset. Explicitly distant beds are exceptions.
4. Prefer lossless WAV masters, 48 kHz / 24-bit when supported, with no clipping or heavy limiting. Exporting compressed generation as WAV does not restore lost fidelity. Keep source files and derive runtime formats according to project policy.
5. Suggested naming: `sfx_<number>_<english_name>_v01.wav`. Keep variants and loops separate. These are proposed filenames, not registered runtime IDs.
6. The phrase **No music, no score, no singing, no instruments.** is included in every prompt. It prohibits musical instruments, not the requested physical ringing of an anvil, bell, or metal tool. Reject melodic drones, cinematic risers, rhythmic backing, or accidental choral layers even if the model ignores the prompt.
7. Layer environments: one quiet bed, a few local emitters, and sparse intermittent details. Do not run all crowd, weather, animal, and work loops continuously. Keep investigation sounds, speech, and combat transients readable.
8. Fauna must match Baltic ecology, location, season, and time of day. Insect activity in the opening cool spring should be restrained, not a summer-night chorus. Verify species calls against existing recordings; do not use AI calls as zoological references.

## First assembly pass

1. Audition AUDIT assets and mark each as reuse, edit, or regenerate. Check source/usage rights before shipping.
2. Generate P0 variants and verify the identity of each physical source by listening, not by filename.
3. Assemble four listening scenes: **Lower Town market**, **Kalev's working smithy**, **harbor**, and **Viru Gate at night**. Use separate beds and local actions rather than an all-in-one scene recording.
4. Generate P1 sounds to extend those scenes to rural travel, domestic life, monasteries, weather changes, and encounters. Adjust animal density and voice walla to scene state.
5. Generate P2 only when its location or authored event needs it. Folklore and magic cues require narrative approval and must not leak into neutral ambience.
6. QA every file for accidental music/speech, artifacts, clipping, cropped attacks/decays, inappropriate modern sources, and mono compatibility. Manually crossfade and audition loops for at least three repeats; prompt wording alone does not guarantee a seamless loop.
