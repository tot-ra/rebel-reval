# Estonian Folklore, Legend & Fairy-Tale Research

**Task:** Folklore research for quest content
**Status:** Research draft for review
**Scope:** A curated, sourced catalog of Estonian myths, legends and fairy tales, mapped to *Reval Rebel*'s existing cast, factions and locations, with quest seeds. Intended as a **`reference`** document, not active scope. Individual seeds reactivate only through README / `TODO.md` tasks, per [ADR 0008](../adr/0008-three-act-campaign-and-faction-scope.md).

Confidence labels follow [`docs/CANON.md`](../CANON.md#confidence-labels): `attested`, `plausible composite`, `folklore`, `invented`.

---

## 1. How to use this document

Estonian folklore is our single richest, most setting-authentic quest quarry: it is native to Harjumaa and the north coast, it predates and resists the Christian/German/Danish order the player is rebelling against, and it is already the spiritual backbone of the [Metsik Cult](../../characters/metsik_cult/README.md) and the [Sacred Grove](../../scenes/world/sacred_grove.md) hingepuu ([`docs/CANON.md` — Hingepuu](../CANON.md#terminology)).

Three ways to draw from it:

1. **Ambient texture** - songs, sayings, weather-omens, and roadside legends NPCs repeat. Cheap, high flavour, no new systems.
2. **Bestiary encounters** - many creatures already exist in [`assets/bestiary/`](../../assets/bestiary/) and the [bestiary README](../../assets/bestiary/README.md); this doc grounds them in real sources and adds candidates.
3. **Full quest lines** - folklore tales restructured as objectives, tied to a named NPC and a real location. See §7-§8.

**Design rule:** In 1343 these beliefs are *lived*, not "mythology." Estonians treat spirits as neighbours; Germans/Danes/clergy treat the same beliefs as heresy or peasant superstition. That tension is the quest engine - the *same* event (a kratt in a barn, a näkk in the harbour) reads as sacred, criminal, or diabolical depending on who you ask.

## 2. Primary sources (real books to mine)

These are the actual published collections the team can cite and pull tales from. All are 19th-century or later compilations of genuine oral tradition; most are public domain.

| Source | Author / Compiler | What it gives us |
|---|---|---|
| **Kalevipoeg** (1857-1861) | Friedrich Reinhold Kreutzwald (from Faehlmann's groundwork) | The national epic. The giant-hero Kalevipoeg, his parents **Kalev** and **Linda**, the founding of the land, journeys to the underworld and to the world's edge. `attested` (as a literary work) / `folklore` (its content). Direct namesake of our PC **Kalev**. |
| **Eesti rahva ennemuistsed jutud** ("Old Tales of the Estonian People", 1866) | Fr. R. Kreutzwald | The canonical Estonian fairy-tale book: *Põhja konn* (The Northern Frog), *Kaval-Ants ja Vanapagan* (Cunning Hans and the Old Devil), *Rehepapp*, *Vaeslapse käsikivi* (The Orphan's Handmill), *Tuhka-Triinu* (Cinderella variant), *Loomade keel* (The Language of Animals). |
| **Eesti Muinasjutud / Esivanemate varandus** and many legend volumes | Matthias Johann Eisen | Vast place-legend and creature-legend corpus: *Vanapagan*, kratt/pisuhänd lore, plague-legends, church- and lake-legends. |
| **Monumenta Estoniae Antiquae / Setu songs**, and the great regilaul archive | Jakob Hurt | Runic song (*regilaul*) tradition; ritual songs, laments, the Seto epic material and the god **Peko**. |
| **Estonian mythology / Liivimaa** studies | Oskar Loorits (*Grundzüge des estnischen Volksglaubens*) | The scholarly backbone for haldjas (guardian spirits), the dead, and the animist worldview. |
| **Chronicon Livoniae** (Henry of Livonia, c. 1229) | Henricus de Lettis | **`attested` period source**: names **Tharapita** ("Taara appears!") as the Oeselians' god born on a Virumaa hill. This is the one deity we can cite from a 1300s-adjacent chronicle - gold for canon safety. |
| **Suur Tõll** (Saaremaa legend cycle) | oral / Eisen collections | The giant hero of Saaremaa; directly usable for our [Saaremaa](../../scenes/events/saaremaa.md) content. |

> Note: the game is set in **1343**, five centuries before these were written down. That is fine and normal - oral tradition long predates its collectors. Present beliefs in-world as living custom; keep the *scholarly* framing (dates, authors) here in the research layer only.

## 3. Cosmology, gods & culture-heroes

Use these for oaths, songs, shrine dedications, and the Metsik Cult's theology. Most are `folklore`; Tharapita is `attested` as a named period deity.

- **Taara / Tharapita** - sky-and-thunder god, worshipped in sacred groves, especially by the Oeselians (Saaremaa). *Attested* by Henry of Livonia. Already referenced in the [Metsik Cult README](../../characters/metsik_cult/README.md). Best "official" pagan god to swear by. Hook: an oath "Taara appears!" (*Tarapita*) as a rebel rallying cry - period-plausible.
- **Uku / Vanaisa / Taevataat** ("Grandfather", "Sky Father") - supreme sky god. Household and harvest blessings invoke him.
- **Vanemuine** - god of song and poetry (a Kreutzwald-era literary systematization, so `folklore`/`invented`-adjacent; flag as later reconstruction). Useful as patron of the *regilaul* tradition Ellen keeps.
- **Pikne / Kõu** - the thunder itself, personified; lightning is his weapon hurled at the devil. Farmers' storm-omens.
- **Kalevipoeg** - giant son of **Kalev** and **Linda**. Ploughed the land, fought the Devil, built towns by hurling stones, died guarding the gates of Hell, prophesied to return and free his people. **The reincarnation-of-the-giant-king motif is already wired into [Urmas Laar](../../characters/rebels/urmas_laar.md), who reads our PC Kalev as the prophesied giant-king.** This is the strongest single folklore-to-character link we have.
- **Suur Tõll** - giant hero-king of Saaremaa; ploughs, throws boulders, defends islanders, beheaded but his head speaks. Pairs with our [Saaremaa](../../scenes/events/saaremaa.md) and [Maasilinna/Pöide](../../scenes/world/) island content.
- **Peko** - Seto fertility/crop god (far south-east; use only if we ever reach Setomaa). A wax effigy kept in a barn - mechanically echoes the Metsik straw effigy.
- **Vanapagan** ("Old Heathen"/"Old Devil") - the dim, strong giant-devil of the tales, endlessly outwitted by the farmhand **Kaval-Ants**. Already our top-level antagonist archetype in the [bestiary README](../../assets/bestiary/README.md). Not the Christian Satan - a folkloric strongman/landlord figure, which lets us satirize the German lords through him.

## 5. Reval / Harjumaa place-legends (highest priority)

These are set **exactly where the player is**. They are the most valuable material in this doc because they turn real landmarks into quests.

### 5.1 The Elder of Lake Ülemiste (**Ülemiste vanake**) — `folklore`
The old man (or lake-spirit) of Lake Ülemiste comes to the city wall each year, usually near St. Catherine's / autumn, and asks the guard: *"Is Tallinn finished yet?"* If anyone ever answers **"yes, the city is complete,"** the lake will rise and drown Reval. The guard must always answer that much is still unbuilt. Already in canon as an existing belief: [`TOURIST_LANDMARKS.md:212`](../TOURIST_LANDMARKS.md) - *"home of the Ülemiste Elder legend... the lake spirit grants the city water."* The lake is also the **Battle of Sõjamäe** shore (May 14, 1343) - the *end* of the mainland rising happens on the water of a spirit who could drown the city. **This is our single best folklore set-piece.**

### 5.2 Linda, Toompea and the tears of Ülemiste — `folklore` (Kalevipoeg)
When Kalev died, his widow **Linda** heaped stones over his grave; the mound became **Toompea** (Domberg, the upper town - the seat of the Danish viceroy the player is rebelling against). Carrying one last great stone, exhausted, she dropped it and wept; her tears formed **Lake Ülemiste**, and the dropped boulder is the **Iru "Grandmother" stone (Linda kivi)**. So the oppressor's fortress-hill is literally the tomb of the native hero-father, and the city's water is a grieving mother's tears. **This subtext alone can carry the whole Toompea storyline.**

### 5.3 Other usable local/near legends
- **The Devil's Wedding at Lake Ülemiste** and the many "devil builds/wrecks the church" legends attached to St. Olaf's and stone churches - use for St. Olaf's ([`st_olafs_church.md`](../../scenes/reval_north/st_olafs_church.md)): the master builder whose name was "Olev," who fell from the spire and a toad and snake crawled from his mouth (a real Tallinn legend).
- **Kalevipoeg's stone-throws** - erratic boulders across Harjumaa are the giant's cast stones or the Devil's dropped load. Roadside flavour and "sacred stone" offering-sites (cf. [Sacred Grove](../../scenes/world/sacred_grove.md)).
- **Sacred groves & offering-stones (hiis)** near villages - the Metsik Cult's practice; the [pagan shrine](../../scenes/reval_east/the_pagan_shrine.md) (currently archived) and [Sacred Grove](../../scenes/world/sacred_grove.md) hingepuu.
- **Suur Tõll of Saaremaa** - for the [Saaremaa](../../scenes/events/saaremaa.md) / island content: the islanders' own giant defender, killed by crusaders, whose severed head promises to rise again "when war comes to Saaremaa" - a ready-made island-rebellion prophecy paralleling the Kalevipoeg one on the mainland.

## 6. Fairy tales with quest structure (Kreutzwald, 1866)

Fairy tales give us *plots*, not just monsters. Each below is a genuine tale reduced to a quest skeleton.

| Tale | Kernel | Quest skeleton |
|---|---|---|
| **Kaval-Ants ja Vanapagan** (Cunning Hans & the Old Devil) | A poor farmhand wins wagers against the strong-but-stupid devil (who can eat more, mow more, throw further) by trickery. | A "contest" quest vs. a brutish overseer/Brute (bestiary): win by wit not strength - eating match, a rigged stone-throw, a "whistle louder" trick. Reward: freedom of a village from a levy. Reskins the German lord as Vanapagan. |
| **Rehepapp** | The barn-keeper and his kratt steal from the manor to keep peasants alive. | Multi-step heist against a Hanseatic/Order granary; build or feed a **kratt** to haul the loot; keep it busy or it turns on you (fail-state). Direct rebel-economy quest. |
| **Põhja konn** (The Northern Frog) | A hero with a magic ring slays the man-eating giant frog. | Boss quest already stubbed as [`põhja_konn.md`](../../assets/bestiary/põhja_konn.md); the "ring" = a forge-crafted relic, tying to Kalev's smithing. |
| **Vaeslapse käsikivi** (The Orphan's Handmill) | An abused orphan is aided by the dead mother's spirit / a magic hand-mill that grinds salt or gold. | Ties to **kodukäija/haldjas** ancestor-aid; a mill that won't stop (why the sea is salt) - a cursed-object quest in a village mill. |
| **Loomade keel** (The Language of Animals) | A man gains the power to understand animals; must never reveal it. | An investigation quest where overheard animals (our [30-bird catalog!](../FLORA_FAUNA.md#bird-model-ledger-3030)) leak secrets; ties folklore to the ambient-fauna system (P0-105). |
| **Tuhka-Triinu** (Ash-Triinu, Cinderella) | Mistreated girl, dead-mother's tree/bird helpers, recognition. | Social side-quest in a merchant household; the birch/rowan on the mother's grave as helper-spirit. |
| **Libahunt** tale-type | A woman is forced into wolf-form and back; jealousy and false wives. | Werewolf-territory quest ([`assets/bestiary`](../../assets/bestiary/README.md)); the "monster" is a wronged human - a persuade-vs-kill choice, our signature branch. |
