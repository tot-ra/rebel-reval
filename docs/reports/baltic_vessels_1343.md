# Baltic vessels for Spring 1343 Reval

Recorded: 2026-09-26
Board: **R-952** / CO-05
Scope: dimensions and mechanics for the CO-06 fleet. Not art direction (ADR 0018).

This report is the evidence gate for `docs/tasks/coast/CO-06_vessel_asset_fleet.md`.
It does not add assets or runtime code.

## Confidence labels

`docs/CANON.md` is the vocabulary used here:

- **`attested`**: wreck measurement, contemporary written name, or established excavation publication
- **`plausible composite`**: a 1343 gameplay type built from dated Baltic comparanda, with the gap named
- **`invented`**: a number or feature with no source (must not appear as fact)
- **`folklore`**: not used for hull geometry

CO-05 also asked for attested / bounded reconstruction / reconstructed / speculative.
Map those as: attested = attested; bounded reconstruction and reconstructed = plausible composite;
speculative = invented, or **unknown** when the field is left empty.

An uncited number is not allowed. If a source does not give the field, the card says **unknown**.

## How CO-06 should read this

1. Do not paste one Bremen-cog silhouette on every hull. Local wrecks already show two sizes and mixed
   carvel-bottom / clinker-side anatomy ([1], [2]).
2. Roadstead cogs stay scenery. Working craft at jetties and on Kalamaja sand are the small and mid
   boats ([3], [4]).
3. Prefer the **Peeter** hull as the 1343-contemporary Reval cog. Treat **Lootsi** as a large
   third-quarter-14th-c. comparandum: its timber was felled around 1360 ([1], [5], [6]).
4. Fishing and ferry sizes have **no published Reval wreck**. Use the cited comparanda and keep
   the unknown fields empty rather than inventing plank counts.

## V1. Hanseatic cog (roadstead cargo)

### Type and role

Bulk Hanseatic carrier for timber, grain, salt, and cloth. In Reval it stands in **open roadstead**
and is lightered; it is not boarded and is not beached at Kalamaja ([3], [4]).

Two local wrecks fix the type in Reval waters:

- **Peeter** (Kadriorg / Pikksilma, excavated 2015): wreck of a cog, pottery of the second quarter
  of the 14th century, oak dendro *terminus post quem* 1296 against the East Pomeranian chronology ([2]).
  This is the **best 1343-contemporary** Reval hull.
- **Lootsi 8** (found 2022): 24.5 x 9 m cargo hull `attested` [1] with cog-like anatomy, in use around
  the mid-14th century; museum dating places the felling around 1360 ([5], [6]). Use as the
  **large end** of the type, not as an April 1343 launch date.

### Dimensions

| Measure | Peeter (Reval, 1343-safe) | Lootsi (large comparandum) | Bremen cog 1380 (North Sea comparandum) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Length overall | **18.08 m** wreck max [2] `attested` | **24.5 m** [1] `attested` (post-1343 build) | **23.23 m** [11] `attested` (1380) |
| Beam | **6.64 m** wreck max [2] `attested` | **9 m** [1] `attested`; MaSS also lists 8.6 m [12] | **7.62 m** [11] `attested` |
| Depth / side height | Port **3.05 m**, starboard **2.11 m** as preserved [2] `attested` | Stern preserved height about **4 m** [6] `attested` | Midships to wash-strake **4.26 m**; incl. stem **7.23 m** [11] `attested` |
| Draught | **unknown** | **unknown** | Light **c. 1.25 m**; loaded **c. 2.25 m** [11] `attested` for Bremen only |
| Freeboard | **unknown** | **unknown** | Loaded remaining freeboard **c. 2 m** on the Bremen reconstruction [14] `plausible composite` for Bremen only |
| Keel | Plank keel preserved entire length [2] `attested` | Flat keel **17.94 m** long, 19-20 cm high, midships width about 56 cm [1] `attested` | Length on keel **15.60 m** [11] `attested` |

Do not average these three ships into one "standard cog". CO-06 should ship **one Peeter-scale**
roadstead cog and, if a second silhouette is needed, a **Lootsi-scale** large visitor, labelled as
the later large type.

### Construction

Peeter and Lootsi share the cog-like recipe: **flush (carvel) bottom**, **clinker sides**, straight
stem and stern posts, moss caulking under wooden laths and sintels, frames treenailed ([1], [2]).

Peeter specifics `attested` [2]:

- Overlapped side planks clenched with double-bent nails
- Bottom and side seams caulked with Hook-moss (*Drepanocladus aduncus*, *Warnstorfia fluitans*)
  retained by oak laths and sintels
- Oak from East Pomerania (or east of it); one pine sample
- Keelson, inner planking, large knees, and crossbeams preserved

Lootsi specifics `attested` [1]:

- Bottom planks about 50 cm wide and 8 cm thick, trenail-fastened to frames
- Bottom-based building (temporary clamp holes later pegged)
- Side planks clenched with iron double-clenched nails
- Caulking: moss and animal hair; Vlierman Type III sintels (widely 1350-1400)
- 62 frames preserved; floor timbers 20-25 cm wide, 15-20 cm high; limber holes each side of the keel
- Keelson 14.96 m; maststep block 274 x 60 x 31.5 cm with a drainage hole
- Five through-beams with hemi-conical fairings (fenders) on the protruding ends
- Sternpost plus false stern with **metal gudgeons for a rudder**
- No fixed hearth; portable rack inferred from a charcoal stripe; peat blocks 15-20 cm

Bremen 1380 is the same family (carvel bottom, clinker sides, frames added after the hull) [11], [15].
It is a Weser find, not a Reval ship.

Tar and paint: **unknown** on the Reval wrecks. Do not invent a colour scheme here.

### Rig

| Field | Evidence | Label |
|---|---|---|
| Mast count | Single maststep on Lootsi [1]; Peeter keelson and mast base [16]; Lübeck seal silhouette [4] | `attested` as single-masted |
| Mast position | Lootsi maststep sits in the thickened midships keelson (frames 7-46) [1]. Exact fraction of LOA is **unknown** | position `unknown`; single mast `attested` |
| Mast height | **unknown** on Reval wrecks. Bremen reconstruction **c. 21 m** [11] | Bremen only, `attested` as reconstruction table |
| Yard length | **unknown** on Reval wrecks. Replica tables for Bremen-class ships give about **14.6-18 m** [17] | `plausible composite` only as a Bremen-class band |
| Sail | One square sail. Bremen table **c. 200 m²** [11]; Ubena replica listing **150-200 m²** [18] | Reval sail area `unknown` |
| Sheets / braces / halyards / shrouds | Period seals show a single square on one yard. Count of shrouds and exact brace layout on a 1343 Reval cog is **unknown**. Replica handling uses sheets, braces, halyard, and reefing lines [18], [19] | gear *presence* `plausible composite`; counts `unknown` |

### Oars

Not a pulling boat. Harbour sweeps, if any, are **unknown** on Peeter and Lootsi. Do not add a
full bank of oars to V1.

### Fittings

| Fitting | 1343 verdict | Label |
|---|---|---|
| Rudder | **Stern rudder** on Lootsi (gudgeons on the false stern) [1]. Peeter has a straight stern post in the cog pattern [2]. This is the period of the side-to-stern transition; Reval's excavated large hulls already show the stern hang. | `attested` for Lootsi; Peeter hang **unknown** in the published extract |
| Tiller | Expected with a stern rudder; not separately measured | `plausible composite` |
| Anchor | **unknown** (not published from these wrecks in the sources used here) | unknown |
| Bailer | **unknown** | unknown |
| Mooring bitts | **unknown**. Lootsi through-beam fairings imply harbour contact [1] | bitts unknown |
| Cargo gear | Lightering to beach and cart, not four stone piers [3] | `plausible composite` |
| Compass | Dry compass found on Lootsi [5]. The hull is the 1360s ship, so this is **not** an April 1343 attestation | `attested` on Lootsi; do not put it on a 1343 Peeter-scale cog without a label |

### What we do not know

Exact 1343 sail area, mast height, yard, shroud count, draught, crew size, paint, and whether
Peeter carried a side rudder, a stern rudder, or both during its life. Dendro on Lootsi is after
the game date. Typology debate (cog vs a larger transitional hull) is open on Lootsi [6]; keep
the gameplay name "cog" as the historical class, not as a claim that every feature matches
Heinsius's checklist.

## V2. Small inshore clinker fishing boat

### Type and role

Open herring and net boat on the Kalamaja / Kalarand sand. Oars first, optional small square
or spritsail. Not a cog. Six working boats on that shore are a project composite, not a census
([4]).

No published **Reval** 1343 fishing wreck exists ([4]). Nordic clinker boatbuilding is the
default coastal tradition around the Baltic and is inscribed by UNESCO as a living craft;
that inventory describes open boats commonly **5-10 m** long [7].

### Dimensions

| Measure | Value | Label |
|---|---|---|
| Length overall | **5-7 m** as the small end of the UNESCO 5-10 m open-boat band [7] | `plausible composite` (tradition range, not a Reval wreck) |
| Beam | **unknown**. Do not invent a ratio | unknown |
| Depth, draught, freeboard | **unknown** | unknown |

Do not use Skuldelev 6 (11.2 x 2.5 m, draught 0.5 m, c. 1030) as a 1343 Reval measurement [20].
It is three centuries early and belongs under V3 as a distant ancestor, not as this card's numbers.

### Construction

Clinker planks, iron rivets or treenails, tarred seams. Hair or moss caulking is both attested
on larger Baltic hulls ([1], [2]) and normal in the living Nordic tradition [7]. Plank count,
frame spacing, keel profile, and paint for a 1343 Kalamaja boat are **unknown**.

### Rig

Optional small square or a simple settee/sprit. Mast position, height, yard, and sail area are
**unknown**. Many such boats are rowed only.

### Oars

A few pairs (typically two to three per side in the living tradition) [7] `plausible composite`.
Exact number, length, and thole versus oarport arrangement at Reval are **unknown**. Stowed
along the gunwale or in the bottom when beached.

### Fittings

Side rudder or steering oar is the conservative small-boat default; a stern rudder on a 6 m
open boat would need a source and does not have one here. Anchor, bailer, and bitts: **unknown**.
Fish and net handling is by hand and by pole racks on shore ([4]).

### What we do not know

Every numeric field except the 5-10 m tradition band. A 1343 Reval fishing wreck would retire
this card's guesswork.

## V3. Larger inshore working boat (second fishing size)

### Type and role

The Kalamaja *mündrik* (boat master) boat: a heavier open or half-decked clinker craft for
longer inshore work and for carrying a bigger net or a shore-to-town load ([4]). Still not a cog.

The nearest **dated** Baltic inshore hull in the sources used here is **Gedesby** (Bøtø Nor,
Falster): entirely clinker-built oak, C14 in the last quarter of the 13th or first quarter of
the 14th century, used in shallow local waters and a ferry harbour ([8]). It is a southern Danish
trader / local carrier, **not** a Reval fisher. Using its size for a Kalamaja working boat is
`plausible composite`.

### Dimensions

| Measure | Gedesby wreck | Label |
|---|---|---|
| Length overall | **12.5 m** (site publication) / **12.6 m** (MaSS) [8] | `attested` for Gedesby; Reval application `plausible composite` |
| Beam | **about 5 m** / **5.2 m** [8] | same |
| Midships height | **1.60 m** [8] | `attested` for Gedesby |
| Draught | **unknown** (hull adapted to a shallow nor; replica sailing is reported but not used as a number here) | unknown |
| Displacement | MaSS lists **13 t (7 last)** [8] | `attested` as catalogue figure for Gedesby only |

Skuldelev 6 (11.2 x 2.5 m, draught 0.5 m, 14 original oar stations, sail 26.5 m², c. 1030) [20]
shows that a dedicated fishing hull in the Nordic tradition can sit near 11 m, but it is too
early to copy as 1343 fact.

### Construction

Gedesby `attested` [8]:

- Clinker oak, **15** iron-clenched strakes each side
- Square iron clench nails
- Caulking of animal hair and cloth scraps
- Keel partly sawn (an early use of sawn planks in the north); strakes cleft with an axe
- Lime-bast rope among the rigging remains
- Stem hole interpreted as a towing / hauling point across sandbars

Reval plank count and fastening mix remain **unknown**. Do not require 15 strakes on the
Kalamaja boat just because Gedesby has them.

### Rig

Gedesby preserved rope but not a reconstructed sail plan in the sources used here. A single
small square on a light mast is the conservative Baltic default (`plausible composite`).
Mast fraction, height, yard, and area: **unknown**.

### Oars

Present for harbour and calm work. Count and length: **unknown**.

### Fittings

Towing hole in the stem [8] is useful for beach work. Rudder type on Gedesby is not taken as
a Reval rule. Anchor, bailer, bitts: **unknown**.

### What we do not know

Whether any 1343 Kalamaja boat actually reached 12 m. If CO-06 needs a safer local size, stay
inside the UNESCO 5-10 m band [7] and treat Gedesby as the **ceiling**, not the target.

## V4. Open rowing / ferry boat

### Type and role

Short-hop craft at the Coastal Gate timber landings and for a strait or river-mouth crossing:
people, a barrel, a net, a carcass. Oars are primary. A small sail is optional.

No Reval ferry wreck is published in the sources used here. Gedesby itself sat in a ferry
harbour ([8]), but its 12.5 m x 5 m hull is already the V3 ceiling. V4 should read **smaller
and slimmer** than V3.

### Dimensions

| Measure | Value | Label |
|---|---|---|
| Length overall | **6-8 m** inside the UNESCO open-boat band [7], kept below Gedesby [8] | `plausible composite` |
| Beam, depth, draught, freeboard | **unknown** | unknown |

### Construction

Open clinker, few strakes, light frames. Plank count, spacing, keel, caulking mix, tar, and
paint: **unknown**.

### Rig

Usually none. If a sail is shown, it is a small square, mast stepped well forward, area
**unknown**.

### Oars

Four to six oars is the living-tradition default for a boat of this size [7] `plausible composite`.
Length and thole geometry: **unknown**. Shipped (blades inboard, looms on the gunwale or
thwarts) when coming alongside a deck.

### Fittings

Steering oar or a tiny side rudder. Stern rudder on a 7 m open ferry is **not** supported.
Anchor, bailer, bitts: **unknown**. No fish davit, no windlass.

### What we do not know

Every number except the tradition band. Do not copy a 19th-c. *praam*.

## V5. Baltic lighter / Estonian *lodi* (lodja-type)

### Type and role

Shallow, beamy cargo barge that takes a cog's load across the wet margin to a beach or
short timber landing ([3], [21]). The Estonian name is ***lodi*** (plural *lodjad*); Russian
*lodja* / *lodya* is a broader class word, not one hull ([9], [22]).

Hanseatic eastern-route writing in this repo already sends cog cargo onto **Russian river
boats (*lodja*)** at the Neva / Ladoga / Volkhov transfer [21]. At Reval the same *job*
(lightering) is needed because the roadstead is shallow ([3]). That does not prove a Peipsi
*lodi* was moored under the Coastal Gate in April 1343.

Estonian handbook tradition dates the Peipsi-Emajõgi *lodi* from the **14th century** and
describes a short, wide, shoal-draft sailing barge that lasted, with little change of
concept, into the 20th century ([9]). The surviving measured hulls and the Tartu
reconstruction **Jõmmu** (launched 2006) are modern or late-modern ([9], [23]). The Kuru
wreck published in AVE 2018 is identified with the 1909 barge *OAT* (23.91 x 13.28 m) [23].
**Do not use those 20th-c. numbers as 1343 fact.**

### Dimensions

| Measure | Ethnographic / reconstruction band | 1343 Reval |
|---|---|---|
| Length overall | Typical handbook *lodi* **10-15 m**; large examples **18-24 m** [9] | **unknown**. If a lighter is built, stay at the **small** 10-15 m handbook band and label it `plausible composite` |
| Beam | Typical **6-7 m**; large **10-13 m** [9] | unknown as a 1343 wreck; handbook beam is `plausible composite` only |
| Draught | Handbook: can move in water a little under **1 m**. Jõmmu listed draught **0.8 m** [9] | 1343 draught `unknown`; shoal draft as a *property* is `plausible composite` |
| Mast height / sail | Handbook mast **14 m** above water, sail about **100 m²**; Jõmmu repeats those figures [9] | `plausible composite` for the *type*, not a 1343 measurement |

Aleshkovsky's 11th-c. Novgorod logboat (max 6.75 x 0.90 x 0.55 m) [24] is a different object.
Sorokin's *Slavia* (9.4 x 2.7 m, draught 0.4-0.5 m, 10 clinker strakes, mast 7.5 m, sail 5 x 4 m,
eight oars) [25] is a **2002 reconstruction** of an "Old Russian" boat, not a 1343 Reval wreck.

### Construction

Handbook *lodi*: very beamy, oval plan, shoal draft, built for Peipsi chop and Emajõgi bends
([9]). Clinker versus carvel on a 14th-c. example is **unknown**. Fastening, frame spacing,
keel, caulking, tar, and paint: **unknown**.

### Rig

One (sometimes later two) mast(s) and a large square. Brace, sheet, and shroud counts:
**unknown**. Jõmmu's diesel and passenger fittings are **rejected** for 1343.

### Oars / poles

Quant and sweep for river and harbour. Number and length: **unknown**.

### Fittings

Later *lodi* have a deckhouse and, in the 20th century, a steering **wheel** [23]. For 1343
use a **tiller**, not a wheel. Anchor, bailer, bitts: **unknown**. Cargo is barrels and
sacks over the side onto carts ([21]).

### What we do not know

Whether a Peipsi-style *lodi* visited Reval at all in 1343, or whether lightering used
ordinary open boats (V3/V4) only. Prefer a **beamy open lighter** over a 20th-c. deckhouse
barge if the type stays uncertain.

## V6. Saaremaa strait craft

### Type and role

Open clinker boat for the Väinameri, the Little Strait, and the ferry landing on
`world.saaremaa`. It must beach, take a few people or a cart-load, and live in shallow
water. It is not a roadstead cog and not an inland *haabjas* logboat.

No 1343 Saaremaa hull is published in the sources used here. Viking-Age **Salme I** is a
small sail-less clinker boat suited to the Väinameri; **Salme II** is reconstructed at
**17-17.5 x 3 m** with a suggested draught **0.5-0.9 m** ([10]). Those burials are
**8th-9th century**. They prove a long island tradition of open clinker boats. They are
**not** 1343 measurements.

Medieval harbour-site work on Saaremaa (Tornimäe abandoned or reduced to small boats by
the 12th century; later stone pier near Uuemõisa / taxation centre c. 1300) shows
**small-boat** use in the high Middle Ages ([26]). That supports an open strait craft.
It does not give a length.

### Dimensions

All 1343 numbers are **unknown**. CO-06 should author toward **V2/V4 scale** (open, beachable,
low freeboard), not toward Salme II's 17 m war-boat and not toward a cog.

### Construction

Clinker, Nordic / East Baltic rivet tradition. Plank count, frames, keel (Salme II left a
vertical-keel stain [10], which is a Viking-Age observation only), caulking, tar, paint:
**unknown** for 1343.

### Rig

A small square is plausible for a strait crossing. Mast geometry: **unknown**. Salme I is
described as sail-less ([10]); do not force that on 1343.

### Oars

Primary in calms and in the strait. Count and length: **unknown**.

### Fittings

Steering oar or side rudder. Stern rudder is possible by 1343 on larger craft (see V1) but
is **not** attested on an island open boat here. Anchor, bailer, bitts: **unknown**.

### What we do not know

The entire card, except that the 1343 island crossing is a **small open clinker boat**
(`plausible composite`) and that Salme / *haabjas* / 19th-c. *juha* and *lootsik* are the
wrong century or the wrong waters.

## 1343 rigging behaviour (for CO-07)

This section is mechanics and vocabulary, not a second art pass. Where the wrecks are silent,
the source is Bremen-class replica practice or the living Nordic clinker tradition. Those
are `plausible composite` unless marked otherwise.

### Square sail on a single yard (V1, and the small squares on V2-V6)

- **Set.** Halyard lifts the yard. Sheets (clews) and braces (yard arms) trim the sail.
  Replica cog handling uses several people for one sail; braces also spill or gather the
  cloth ([18], [19]). Shroud and stay counts on a 1343 Reval cog are **unknown**.
- **Brace.** The yard is rotated so the sail takes wind from aft or on the quarter. Bremen
  replica notes: best with wind **diagonally from astern**; about **6 knots** in those
  trials; wind on the beam is usable but the hull makes **leeway**; a loaded cog is slow
  on the helm ([15]). Those speeds are replica observations, not Lootsi logbook figures.
- **Luff.** If the yard is not braced and the sail comes too close to the wind, the luff
  shivers and the ship loses drive. That is ordinary square-sail behaviour (`plausible composite`).
  It is the motion CO-07 should show before a brace or a sheet ease, not a modern jib luff.
- **Reef.** Reduce area by tying reef points and lowering the yard a short way. Reefing
  lines are attested on cog *replicas* [19]. Whether a 1343 Reval yard had one, two, or no
  reef bands is **unknown**. Do not invent a patented reefing boom.
- **Furl.** Yard lowered or left aloft; canvas bunched to the yard with gaskets. Exact
  gasket count is **unknown**. A furled square hangs in a long bundle under the yard, not
  like a rolled bermudan mainsail.
- **What the sail must not do.** It must not invert through the mast, fly as a lateen, or
  set as a gaff. See the reject list.

### Oars (V2, V3, V4, V6)

- **Shipped.** Blades inboard, looms on thwarts or gunwale, used when coming alongside a
  deck or when sailing. CO-07 should treat stowed oars as **zero blade motion** except
  hull rock.
- **Stowed on the beach.** Oars out of the water, often along the landward side. Tholes
  stay on the boat.
- **Rowing.** Short stroke, sitting or standing depending on boat; the living Nordic
  tradition is the analogue [7]. Stroke rate and oar length at Reval are **unknown**.
- **V1.** No regular oar bank.

### Mooring and surge

Cogs in the roadstead ride at anchor or on a short warp to a lighter. Jetty boats use a
bow line and a stern line to timber piles. Line sag and hull surge are gameplay (CO-07);
period hardware (bitts, cleats) is **unknown** in the wreck publications used here.

## Reject list

Features that must **not** appear on a 1343 Reval or Saaremaa hull in this pack, unless a
later task cites a new primary source and changes CANON.

| Feature | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| Gaff, boom-and-gaff, or Bermuda mainsail | Post-medieval fore-and-aft rigs |
| Lateen as the main cog sail | Mediterranean; not the Hanseatic square |
| Two- or three-masted *kraweel* / caravel rig | Later Baltic transition; Lisa von Lübeck is the wrong century [19] |
| Steering **wheel** | Later *lodi* and post-medieval ships [23] |
| Ratlines on the shrouds as a climbing ladder | Not supported for these 1343 hulls |
| Full carvel (flush) sides on the cog | Reval wrecks are carvel **bottom**, clinker **sides** [1], [2] |
| Four long stone piers with four cogs made fast | Explicitly rejected by the harbour reconstruction [3] |
| Boardable deep-water cog | Gameplay decision [3] |
| Fat Margaret / 16th-c. harbour silhouette as a docked backdrop | Post-1343 [3] |
| Pirita convent boats, 15th-c. Kalamaja church boats | Post-1343 [3], [4] |
| *Haabjas* as the Saaremaa strait ferry | Inland aspen logboat, wrong waters |
| 19th-20th-c. *juha*, *lootsik*, *Jõmmu* deckhouse, diesel, wheel | Wrong century [9] |
| Viljo wreck (Kadriorg, dendro after 1487, 14 x 3.5 m preserved) as a 1343 type | Two centuries late [2] |
| Kuru / *OAT* 23.91 x 13.28 m as a medieval *lodi* | Built 1909 [23] |
| Salme I/II lengths as 1343 Saaremaa ships | 8th-9th c. [10] |
| Skuldelev 6 numbers as 1343 Kalamaja fact | c. 1030 [20] |
| Compass as a 1343 Peeter-scale prop | Lootsi find on a c. 1360 hull [5] |
| Warship castles, three masts, Hamburg arms on the sail | 19th-c. history painting, not a wreck [15] |
