# The Brotherhood of Blackheads (candidate seat)

Status: implemented as content (task **R-CITIZENS-001**). Confidence: `plausible composite`. Ledger id: `blackheads` (`faction.blackheads`, an **events-only candidate seat** via P4-045, see [`faction_blackheads_candidate.json`](../../data/faction_blackheads_candidate.json)). It is not a ninth launch faction; the launch ledger stays at eight. Scope: the unmarried-merchants' circle as it lives among Reval's residents in the week before St George's Night, 1343. The census block at the foot is generated; the hinterland estimates are not part of it. Out of scope: any promotion to the launch table (needs an ADR), and everything later than 1343.

A period warning before anything else. The famous Blackheads' house on Pikk and the 1400 charter belong to a later century and **do not exist** in this game. In April 1343 there is only a loose fellowship of unmarried merchants and their clerks, which its members already call "the Blackheads" among themselves, in the way a boys' club names itself before it has a roof. It has no hall, no banner in public, no written rule and no patron saint's altar of its own. Everything below is a plausible composite built on that thin base.

## What they want and what they cost

The launch-style summary: they want a seat at the table when the council changes, and a city that answers to its merchants rather than to a castle or a foreign war chest. The shadow is that people and routes become entries in a ledger. For an ordinary Reval resident this is the clerk who is polite to the cooper and then, three weeks later, knows exactly which stave-cart is late and who owes whom. The Brotherhood is not feared like the Order or hated like the Crown's reeve. It is *priced in*: a lodging found, a debt eased, a letter written, and a favour remembered at the next quarter-day.

The cost lands on the junior and the unmarried. A clerk who takes a night's lodging or a small loan from the circle finds that the loan is also a leash, and that a spoken sentence at supper can become a line in someone else's memory. For the wider town the cost is that some harbour arrangements are promised twice, and the second buyer finds out.

## Why people join

The shared root is simple and hard: an unmarried man in a Hanseatic town has no vote, no stall and no standing until he has a house, a wife and burgher rights, and all four cost money he does not have. The council is made of married masters. The circle is the waiting room that wants to become a hall.

The branches differ.

- **[Arend van Lemego](../people/lower_town/arend_van_lemego.md)** acts from ambition and a notary's son's neatness. He wants burgher rights and a dowry, and he recruits with small loans.
- **[Kersten Corte](../people/lower_town/kersten_corte.md)** acts from debt and shame. Arend gave him a night's lodging in a cold week, and he resents being treated as a debtor while he hopes for a place at table.
- **[Winand Lutke](../people/lower_town/winand_lutke.md)** acts from fear of dying a clerk in a rented bed; he holds the circle's small purse in a hollowed book.
- **[Reimar Witte](../people/lower_town/reimar_witte.md)** acts from a wish to be trusted by two masters at once, and has not yet decided whether he can be. He has attended suppers without a vow.
- **[Wessel van Hamelen](../people/lower_town/wessel_van_hamelen.md)** acts from boredom turned into romance. Eighteen, unable to whisper, he thinks himself a spy in a ballad.
- **[Johannes van Verden](../people/lower_town/johannes_van_verden.md)** acts from protection. He is married with eight children, owes 40 marks, and sells what he hears at the quay to keep the debt quiet.
- **[Ludolf van Munster](../people/lower_town/ludolf_van_munster.md)** acts from patience and control. He guards the unmarried seat he has waited for and runs the circle's letters through the back lane.
- **[Mart Weaver](../../CHARACTERS/mart_weaver.md)** (promoted cast, not in the census) acts from craft solidarity: protection and access for Estonian artisans in exchange for his quiet use of the circle's credit.

Nobody on this list joined for a slogan. Faith plays almost no part; love plays a small one, mostly in the form of an imagined wife.

## Who belongs

The census puts **7 residents** in the Brotherhood, 0.2% of 4,247. All seven are German, all male, all in Lower Town, and all adults between 18 and 35 (five aged 15-29, two aged 30-44). Five are merchant's clerks and two are Fernhändler; five are sympathisers and two are informers. None holds an office. The streets are Pikk (three), Lai, Nunne, Rataskaevu and Dunkri.

That small, narrow shape is the realistic one. The circle is a fellowship of unmarried men of one class and one language in a town where about 36% of residents are German and only a fraction of those are young, unwed and in merchant households. A circle that recruited women, Estonians or craftsmen in numbers would be some other body. Most members leave through marriage, which is why the faction stays tiny and why its seniors are men who have waited a long time. The seven carded here are the whole census-visible circle; the cast adds [Johann von Minden](../../CHARACTERS/johann_von_minden.md), [Hinrik the Cartographer](../../CHARACTERS/hinrik_cartographer.md) and Mart Weaver, who stand outside the census numbers.

For gameplay a balanced faction mix means this: the Brotherhood is one thread among many, not a crowd. Its few faces must be met often enough to matter (counting rooms, the quay, after Mass), and its influence must reach through other factions' people, such as carters, weavers and clerks. A street that feels Blackhead-heavy is a design error.

## Where they are

All seven live on or near the merchants' spine of Lower Town.

- **[Pikk](../ledger/lower_town/pikk.md):** the van Minden counting room, where Reimar and Wessel work under one roof, and Johannes van Verden's house with its rented shed.
- **[Lai](../ledger/lower_town/lai.md):** Arend in the van Deventer house.
- **[Dunkri](../ledger/lower_town/dunkri.md):** Kersten, lodged in Heyno's counting-room loft.
- **[Rataskaevu](../ledger/lower_town/rataskaevu.md):** Ludolf's house, yard and stable, with a back lane he uses after curfew.
- **[Nunne](../ledger/lower_town/nunne.md):** Winand, a lodger who pays rent in copying.

Meetings happen **after Mass at St Nicholas** ([Niguliste](../ledger/lower_town/niguliste.md)), on the church steps and in the nave, where nothing can be repeated, and at small suppers behind counting rooms. Money and messages cross the **[Coastal Gate quay](../ledger/lower_town/coastal_gate_quay.md)** in Wessel's satchel; Johannes listens there. Harju-road cartage carries Ludolf's private notes. There is no meeting house.

## Organisation and secrecy

There are no ranks, only **seniority that nobody names**. Winand Lutke is senior in the clerks' circle, and Ludolf van Munster is above him; Johannes is Ludolf's ears. Reimar and Wessel are watched by both. The rule is that the senior men never speak in the same room as the juniors.

Signs are small: a clerk's silence at the right moment, an unasked-for lodging, a Latin phrase dropped into Low German. Money moves as favours and quarter-day credit. The purse is one hollowed book. A message moves as a second copy of a master's letter, a note in cartage, or a spoken line on the church steps.

Cells are loose pairs: Arend recruits, Kersten copies, Wessel carries, Winand counts. Most pairs suspect each other without proof. A Kalev commission (a banner, a hinge, a locked strongroom chest) could reveal a letter copied twice, a debt note held in a senior hand, a purse book, or a back-lane route. It could not reveal a roll of names, because none exists.

## Resources and economy

Money is small and soft: a few marks in the purse, loans of a few öre to young clerks, rent paid in copying, silver weighed on the counting-room scale. What the circle really owns is information: who is late, who is in debt, whose master fears the grain levy. Wessel has already passed the names of three councillors who fear it.

Food and arms do not matter to them; they have no store of either. Their needs from the city are quiet quays, honest weights and a council that stays slow. What they take is trust: a clerk's access to a master's letters, a master's goodwill, a neighbour's gossip. Their favours are lodgings, letters, introductions and an eased debt.

## Relations with other factions

- **[Hanseatic guilds](./hanseatic.md):** parent and rival. The circle lives inside the Hansa's houses and wants its seats. It does not oppose the Hansa; it wants to inherit it faster.
- **[Danish Crown](./danish_crown.md):** enmity of interest and cooperation by debt. A crown clerk, Niels Skjalm, owes Ludolf 18 marks, and the circle tolerates the Crown only while it keeps the quay open.
- **[Livonian Order](./livonian_order.md):** no ties before the rising. A few members already fear it as a new master with new customs.
- **Black Cloaks:** wary tolerance. Mart Weaver shelters artisans through the circle's credit, but the clerks fear that a rising closes the gates and the quay.
- **Harju Kings:** the circle watches the grain and the cartage, and fears the levy more than the rebels. It sells nothing to them, but Harju-road carters hear things.
- **Church and others:** Latin schooling from St Catherine's Dominicans and parish masters gives the clerks their hand. Pskov and Novgorod merchants are customers, not allies.

## Tensions inside

1. **Care or use.** Johann von Minden treats people and routes as assets; Mart Weaver and Hinrik the Cartographer want a map and a network that protect users. Wessel sides with whoever makes him feel important.
2. **Master or circle.** Reimar must choose between Winand van Minden's house and the circle, and has not decided. Wessel risks being sent back to Hameln. Kersten copies his master's letters twice and hides the second copy.
3. **Senior and junior over the purse.** Winand Lutke holds the purse and knows who has paid; Arend lends small sums to bind clerks and resents any reckoning of it. Johannes owes 40 marks and is both the circle's ears and its weakest link.

## Spring 1343 timeline

- **Before 23 April:** Easter was 13 April. Suppers continue. Wessel passes councillors' names, Ludolf's cartage carries notes, and Arend recruits one more clerk with a small loan. Johannes is questioned about gossip and stays quiet.
- **The night:** members stay in their counting rooms; Reimar guards the strongroom key; Wessel runs letters to the quay until the gates close. Ludolf uses the back lane. Nobody takes up arms.
- **The siege weeks:** the circle splits on whether to protect cargo or to bargain with the Order. Johann von Minden's contracts, Hinrik's harbour charts and Mart Weaver's shelter become events in the candidate ledger.

## Outside the walls

Estimates here are plausible composite and **outside the census**. A dozen or so unmarried factors and clerks across Harju, tied to Ludolf's cartage along the Harju road, and perhaps another dozen contacts in Visby and Lübeck houses. No rural base, no militia, no hall.

## Game hooks

**Ledger events** (candidate seat, events only): `ledger.blackheads.johann_contract_favour`, `ledger.blackheads.hinrik_harbour_chart`, `ledger.blackheads.mart_weaver_shield`. Standing rises for honest delivery, a banner or hinge commission kept quiet, and a copied letter returned. It falls for exposing a clerk's debt, breaking a seal, or a loud bargain.

**Bark ideas:** "Nothing I say is repeatable." "Mind the quay price." "A clerk remembers; a master forgets."

**Quest seeds:**
- A hinge for Reimar's strongroom that must not be known to the van Minden house.
- A second copy of a letter that Kersten must retrieve from the loft beam before Heyno finds it.
- A cartage note from Ludolf to Taniel on the Harju road.
- Johannes's shed, rented to a tallow-boiler, hides a ledger.

**Reputation reactions:** at high standing, clerks offer lodging and quay tallies; at low, doors close and a master hears about Kalev's debts.

**What a faction-tied resident says to Kalev:**
- At **-3** (Arend): "I have no letters for a smith. Mind your own accounts, and I will mind mine."
- At **+3** (Arend): "Come by after Mass. A man who keeps silence like you is worth a loan, and a loan is only a loan."
