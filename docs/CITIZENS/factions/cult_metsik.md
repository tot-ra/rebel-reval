# The Cult of Metsik

Status: implemented as content (task **R-CITIZENS-001**). Confidence: `plausible composite`. Ledger id: `cult_metsik` (`faction.cult_of_metsik` in the cast promotion table). Scope: the faction as it lives among Reval's residents in the week before St George's Night, 1343; the census block at the foot is generated, the hinterland estimates are not part of it. Out of scope: any reconstruction of a pre-Christian church (there is no such thing in the sources), supernatural proof, and any ninth faction.

## What they want and what they cost

The launch table gives the Cult one want, "the old ways, the sacred groves, the old gods", and one shadow, "would burn the new world whole to regrow the old". In April 1343 this is not an organisation with a creed. It is a web of households that leave bread under thresholds, tie a knot in the first thread of a net, hang a rowan twig over a stable door and carry salt to a stump, and that have decided, quietly, to protect each other when the priest, the bailiff or the Danish levy comes for the grove. Metsik is the forest's keeper, called by hearth-names; nobody in town claims to have seen him.

Ordinary people meet the Cult as a favour, a debt and a silence. A herb bundle from a back door, a charm-knot in a spinner's basket, hemp from the grove at a fair price. The price is a small thing asked later: an overheard word, a jug that passes, a door left unbarred. For the town it is also a risk. The Church calls it superstition and can fine or flog for it; the Hansa sees only a smuggling net it cannot read. The shadow is that the youngest and angriest members hear the groves being felled on the manors and think fire is a cure.

## Why people join

The shared root is loss of a place. A felled grove, a dead child, a manor that bound a family, a priest's scolding over a pinch of flour: each member lost something the old rites held, and found nobody else who would say its name aloud. The branches differ.

- **Grief.** [Kadri Pärtli tütar](../people/lower_town/kadri_partli_tutar.md), a maid on Müürivahe, saw a manor-lord fell the grove where her mother left offerings. She now leads a small circle from a Hanseatic kitchen. [Ell](../people/harju_road/ell.md), a miller's wife on the Harju road, spins by day and walks out to an oak with her dead daughter Marta's name.
- **Debt.** [Elisabet Hinricku tütar](../people/kalarand/elisabet_hinricku_tutar.md), a net-maker on the beach, had her mother's debt to the hemp-seller paid by a woman from the grove in 1339 and has been given a rung in a ladder she never asked to climb.
- **Hunger and a small kindness.** [Mari](../people/harju_road/mari.md), the ostler at the Harju road yard, took barley from the grove when her master cut her wage, and repays it with carters' gossip. [Priidik](../people/kalarand/priidik.md), a porter, was fed broth when he lay sick and now carries salt and a crust to a stump past the Sand Gate at each new moon.
- **Faith, sincerely held.** [Anna Everti tütar](../people/lower_town/anna_everti_tutar.md), a spinner on Kuninga, was taught the old charms by Ell, is devout at Mass and at the grove, and would do a large favour for either.
- **Ambition.** [Albert](../people/lower_town/albert.md), an Estonian merchant on Börsi käik, wants a seat in the Kindergilde before his son is mocked for his name, and sells the Hansa's cellar talk to the grove as a way of being needed by both.
- **Inheritance.** [Jüri Marteni poeg](../people/lower_town/juri_marteni_poeg.md), a sixteen-year-old potter's boy, keeps his grandfather's grove-oaths in his mouth and a twig under his shirt; for him the cell is the only place he is trusted.
- **Habit and usefulness.** [Mari Eerika tütar](../people/lower_town/mari_eerika_tutar.md), a servant on Nunne, sells dried herbs and beeswax by the back door and puts the first spoon of ale under the threshold for a stump in a field.
- **Belonging, young.** [Magdalena Pärtli tütar](../people/viru_road/magdalena_partli_tutar.md), fourteen, a spinner on the Viru road, is in the same cell as Mari the ostler because her mother's circle is the only company that treats her as grown.
- **Watching.** [Judit Madise tütar](../people/lower_town/judit_madise_tutar.md), 69, a retired craftsman on Müürivahe, informs because she sits at a window all day and nobody else wants what she sees.

Nobody on this list calls it a cult. They say they "keep it", and mean the threshold, the first cup and the grove.

## Who belongs

The census gives 110 residents, 2.6% of 4,247. That is small, and it should be: a faction of the poor, hidden from a council and a parish, would not be large, and the census holds the other 74% of the town unaffiliated by design. The shape of the roster is the shape of a secret.

- **Roles.** 39 secret sympathisers and 20 open sympathisers make over half the roster: people who leave the bread and say nothing. 17 secret cell members, 13 informers and 10 couriers are the working web. Only 8 are `active` and exactly one is `core`; two are coerced or dependent (Elisabet is one). Kadri is `active`, not core, and the core member is not carded. The leadership is thinner than the web by design.
- **Sex and age.** 64 women to 46 men, against 49% women in the town, because the trades that carry messages and enter kitchens are women's: 21 spinners, 18 maids and 15 household servants lead the trade list. 52 are aged 15 to 29, 30 aged 30 to 44, 18 aged 45 to 59, 8 aged 60 and over, and 2 children. The old keep the rites; the young carry them.
- **Trades.** Beyond spinners and servants: day labourers, retired craftsmen, stable hands and ostlers, washerwomen, fishermen, two retail traders, two long-distance merchants (Albert is one), two smiths and two nail-smiths, merchant's clerks and dairy-women. They are trades that enter every house, yard and gate.
- **Ethnic mix.** 100 Estonian and 10 Finnish. No Germans appear, because a German burgher has a council and a parish to lose; the Finnish ten are fishers and haulage hands from the beach who share the forest tradition of the north shore.
- **Place.** 87 in Lower Town, 7 on the fishing beach, 5 each on the Viru and Harju roads, 4 on Toompea, 2 on the cattle-road farmsteads (see below).

A balanced faction mix for play means the Cult is felt everywhere and seen nowhere. Kalev meets it in kitchens, yards and the beach long before he meets anyone who says the word, and with 110 residents the player cannot clear it by finding a leader.

## Where they are

The densest streets are Pühavaimu (8), the Western coast road on the fishing beach (7), Müürivahe (7), Lai (6), the Harju road (5), the Viru road (5), Olevimägi (4), Pikk (3), Laboratooriumi (3) and Börsi käik (3). Müürivahe and Lai are service streets behind great houses, so the Cult sits in the kitchens of the houses that tax it.

- **Meeting places.** Never one house. Back doors on Müürivahe and Vana-Posti; the herb-wife's kitchen; the shingle at Kalarand where nets dry; the Harju road yard by the Smiths' Gate; the wall-side lanes the night-watch skips. After Mass at St Olaf, in the porch, two women may exchange a sentence nobody could repeat.
- **Routes.** Out through the Sand Gate to a tended stump on the road; out the Smiths' Gate to an oak on the Harju side; along the Western coast road to the shingle. The grove paths lie beyond the census.
- **Toompea.** Four residents work in houses on the hill, in kitchens and laundries, and hear the Danish talk.

## Organisation and secrecy

There is no hierarchy a Rat clerk could draw. There are cells of two or three, each member knowing one other and suspecting a third. Albert, Mari and Anna each suspect the other two are "the third"; none asks. Kadri leads her circle without being called leader; Elisabet holds a rung above Jüri, though neither would say so; neither speaks to the other in front of others.

- **Signs.** A knot in the first thread, a rowan twig over a door, bread or a first cup at a threshold, the greeting "Metsik hoiab" (the wood keeps) answered "as it is". Jugs from the potters carry marks. Nothing is written; few members can read.
- **Messages.** Spinners' baskets, washerwomen's loads, servants at the market, carters' talk. Couriers carry charm-knots and spoken phrases, never paper. An informer repeats what she has heard twice.
- **Money.** Almost none: hemp, barley, wax, a thimble of mead, a paid debt. A little silver by weight from Albert for the grove.
- **Who knows whom.** Most members know two others. Only Kadri's circle and the one core member see across cells.
- **What a Kalev commission could reveal.** Knots on a spearhead sleeve, a jug mark, a rowan twig in a stable: any commission with a household link can turn up a cell's habit. Reveal it to the Rat or the priest and Kalev burns a threshold; keep it and he gains a favour.

## Resources and economy

The Cult is poor and runs on goods and favours. Hemp for net-makers, barley in a bad winter, dried herbs and wax for the sick. In return it takes news: what the carters say at the Harju road yard, what the Hansa's cellars hear, where the watch walks. It needs the town to stay open, not to close its gates, and for the Danish levy not to fell the manor groves; it takes risks with a servant's job, a porter's credit and a wall-side cellar. It does not hold arms in town. Members who fall behind are not threatened, but they notice who has stopped greeting them. In spring scarcity after Lent a sack of barley is a stronger bond than any oath.

## Relations with other factions

- **Black Cloaks:** useful neighbours. The smiths and artisans share Müürivahe's wall-side lanes and quietly share news; [Martin of the Black Cloaks](../../CHARACTERS/martin_black_cloaks.md) values the Cult's eyes and distrusts its faith.
- **Harju Kings:** kin by blood and grievance, since most members come from manors being bound. The Cult would lend a courier and a charm but not a man for a killing.
- **Hanseatic guilds:** the Cult lives in their kitchens, takes their gossip through Albert and fears their credit book. Open enmity is impossible.
- **Danish Crown:** the felling of manor groves and the levy make it the villain; a Toompea servant is the Cult's best ear on the hill. See [Konrad Preen](../../CHARACTERS/konrad_preen.md).
- **Church:** tolerated when it does not look. Members go to Mass at St Olaf and the Holy Spirit, and the parish priest sometimes guesses.
- **Livonian Order:** an unknown. Some members believe, falsely, that it will back the Estonians.
- **Pskov and Novgorod; Vitalienbrüder:** little contact; the beach may deal with a harbour pedlar.
- **Promoted cast:** [Ellen](../../CHARACTERS/ellen.md) is the moral voice of the old ways, refusing a pure ancestral cause; [Aita](../../CHARACTERS/aita.md) shares herb-lore with her; [Mart](../../CHARACTERS/mart.md) wants a heroic past the Cult will not give him. Kalev's story touches the Cult through them.

## Tensions inside

- **Guard the rites or fight for them.** Ell and Mari the ostler want the rites kept quiet and the grove tended; Kadri holds the same course out of grief, while younger members like Anna, who would do a large favour for the grove, hear the felling of manor oaks as a call. The shadow in the launch table lives here.
- **Belief or bargain.** Albert and Mari the ostler inform for wax, barley and a dry roof, not for Metsik. Anna and Priidik, who act from faith and debt of kindness, find it unclean. Kadri has noticed Albert's silences.
- **Debt and coercion.** Elisabet never asked to be a rung. She resists large favours and resents the grove that paid her mother's debt; Made Lauri tütar, the maid on Vene who fed Priidik and recruited him, thinks debt is the only honest bond.

## Spring 1343 timeline

- **Before 23 April.** Rumours that the Danes will fell the grove before summer move through the spinners; some say the priest will burn every herb bundle after Easter (both false). Kadri's circle keeps bread out; Priidik waits for the new moon.
- **The Night.** Most stay indoors with doors barred and bread at the threshold. Priidik goes to the stump to pray. Couriers pass messages from the Harju road to the beach; Ell walks out to her oak. Few join the rising openly; a handful of the young take part.
- **The siege weeks.** Gates close and carters stop; the web goes quiet. Some inform the Black Cloaks of watch movements, while others hide a runaway. Mari, who refuses large favours, will not hide one. Whoever is caught is a priest's matter, not a Rat's.

## Outside the walls

Not part of the census; plausible-composite estimates only. The Cult is stronger in the hinterland than in town: perhaps 300 to 600 people across the Harju manors and coast villages keep some rite at a grove, spring or stump, mostly unorganised. The grove paths run to the oak on the Harju road, the stump past the Sand Gate and the hills around Rapla, where Kadri's mother left offerings. Felled groves on the manors are the engine of recruitment. The cattle-road farmsteads (2 residents) are the nearest link between town and country. Estimates stay estimates.

## Game hooks

- **Ledger events that raise standing:** returning a stolen charm-knot; sparing a threshold when a priest searches; asking for a herb and paying fairly; keeping silent about a cell. **Lower it:** reporting a rowan twig or stump to the priest or the Rat; felling a tree for commission work; mocking a first cup.
- **Barks:** "Metsik hoiab." "The first cup is not yours." "Bread for the threshold." "I only carry the jug."
- **Quest seeds:**
  - A potter's jug with an odd mark comes to the forge for a rim; who does it belong to? ([Jüri Marteni poeg](../people/lower_town/juri_marteni_poeg.md))
  - Priidik asks Kalev to shoe a handcart for a trip past the Sand Gate and not to ask why.
  - Albert's wax is blessed at a grove first; a Hansa factor asks Kalev to check the weights.
  - Kadri's sapling goes missing from under her bed.
- **Reputation reactions:** at high standing, doors open and herb bundles appear; at low standing, thresholds are closed and the street does not greet him.
- **Standing -3:** a faction-tied resident such as Mari Eerika tütar says, "Take it and go. The first cup is not yours." and keeps her back to him.
- **Standing +3:** the same resident says, "Ära karda. Come to the back door; the first cup was for you."
