# The Church (parish and orders)

Status: implemented as content (task **R-CITIZENS-001**). Confidence: `plausible composite`. Ledger id: `church`. Scope: the 98 clergy, religious and church servants of the census, plus an estimate of the hinterland (outside the census). **The Church is an affinity for the census, not one of the eight launch factions.** It has no row in the launch table of the README, no ledger standing of its own and no quest line; it exists so that the 2.3% of residents who live under a rule, a bell and a prior are not scattered into other camps. No April 1343 list of Reval's clergy survives; every figure is a composite.

## What they want and what they cost

The launch table has no want or shadow for the Church, so this page borrows from the eight factions that do. Each of them reaches the Church sideways. The Hanseatic guilds want a quiet, profitable Reval and pay for masses for that purpose. The Danish Crown wants its rule blessed from the cathedral on Toompea. The Livonian Order wants one faith and one law, and the friars suspect it would have them preach only the one. The Harju Kings and the Black Cloaks want freedom, and the Estonian-speaking friars hear their confessions. The Cult of Metsik wants the old ways, and the sextons and lay brothers keep half of them without saying so. The Church itself wants what a house of prayer always wants: to keep the offices said, the poor fed and the lamps lit, and to be left the right to refuse.

Its cost is the other half of the bargain. A tithe, a mass-stipend, a burial fee, a wax candle at a price. The clergy refuse to be taken for a faction, so they ask every faction to leave them out, and every faction takes something from them: a letter carried, a name confessed, a loft lent, a sermon bent. The shadow is complicity by silence. To an ordinary person on the street the Church is the bell that says when to work, the porch where a child is baptised in haste, the bread dole after a bad winter and the one place a bound peasant may stand next to a burgher at the same mass. Few people think of it as a camp until a priest refuses to say the words.

## Why people join

The shared root is vocation by necessity. Most of these people entered by a door someone else opened: a third son with no shop, a village boy who walked into town with a loaf, a daughter with no dowry. The branch is what each did after.

- **Faith and duty.** [Tilman van Munster](../people/lower_town/tilman_van_munster.md), a Dominican of 66 with a failed knee, preaches to the Estonians in their own tongue and has refused to preach obedience since he saw a man flogged at the friary gate in 1319.
- **Debt.** [Lambert van Dortmund](../people/lower_town/lambert_van_dortmund.md), 28 and widowed, priest of St Olaf's, buys cheap Novgorod wax through the gate by night and owes the smugglers for it.
- **Grief.** [Wessel van Brunswik](../people/lower_town/wessel_van_brunswik.md), resident priest of St Nicholas, was given to the Church as a third son and keeps a dead woman's name in the obit book under a false initial. [Tyde Sasse](../people/lower_town/tyde_sasse.md) of the Holy Spirit house prays each year for a daughter whose grave has no marker.
- **Care.** [Rotcher Sasse](../people/lower_town/rotcher_sasse.md), the friary's infirmarian, learns the Estonian Our Father from his patients one phrase at a time so that he can pray beside them.
- **Money and order.** [Arnold van van Lubeke](../people/lower_town/arnold_van_van_lubeke.md), the friary's market friar, weighs everything twice and has covered an eight-mark shortfall from his own sales of candle ends. [Johannes van Hamelen](../people/toompea/johannes_van_hamelen.md), a vicar choral who has never had a stall of his own, counts the masses he is owed and sells marginal songs on the side.
- **Belonging.** [Eerik Aino poeg](../people/lower_town/eerik_aino_poeg.md), a lay brother of 58, walked in from a manor that took his father's flock in 1306 and never left the well; he feeds the carp pond a pinch of the first catch, a custom from his village.
- **Protection.** [Rein Peetri poeg](../people/toompea/rein_peetri_poeg.md), sexton of the cathedral, took minor orders so that the chapter would pay him a wage, and keeps a notched stick of every grave-fee the vicars skim. [Tõnis Kaspari poeg](../people/lower_town/tonis_kaspari_poeg.md) at St Olaf's holds a spare tower key under a stone.
- **Charity against rule.** [Winand Wulf](../people/toompea/winand_wulf.md), senior canon, walks bread down a back lane after curfew to a widow under the wall and counts it as the chapter's debt.
- **Ambition and pride.** [Marquard van Wismar](../people/toompea/marquard_van_wismar.md), a canon in charge of the cathedral fabric, wants the north roof finished and talks too freely over supper to servants of the Danish officers.

## Who belongs

The census puts 98 residents in the Church, 2.3% of 4,247, all of them role `core`. There is no sympathiser ring around them. Anyone who is only friendly to the Church is simply a parishioner, which is nearly everyone, and parishioners do not get an affinity tag. The roster is made from fixed institutional rosters: 24 friars and 8 lay brothers of St Catherine's, 22 nuns, 8 lay sisters and 2 chaplains of St Michael's, 12 canons and 8 vicars choral of the cathedral chapter, plus parish priests, chaplains and sextons at St Olaf's, St Nicholas and the Holy Spirit house. The 20 hospital inmates are not in this count; only their chaplain is. Servants of the institutions are counted in their households, not here.

The mix is unlike the secular factions. It is 68 men and 30 women, and the women are all in the nunnery. It is middle-aged: 49 residents are 30-44, 33 are 45-59, 10 are 60 or over and only 6 are under 30, because orders recruit young and lose people to fever and famine. By ethnicity 77 are German, 20 Estonian and 1 Russian. The single Russian is [Dmitri Stepanovich](../people/lower_town/dmitri_stepanovich.md), an Orthodox priest of the Novgorod merchants' court on Vene; he is here because a priest counts as clergy, and he is the clearest bridge to the `pskov_novgorod` faction.

The size is realistic for the place. A town of three to four thousand under Lübeck law supported two or three parish churches, a friary, a nunnery, a hospital and a cathedral chapter on the hill, and a ratio of roughly one cleric or religious for every forty residents is in line with the model's own estimate. For gameplay, a roster this small means that the Church is not a bloc to be recruited. Its members are scattered across every street through confession, funerals and baptisms. A balanced faction mix on a street therefore includes at least one church voice that belongs to nobody else, and Kalev's standing with them follows from how he treats the dead, the sick and the hungry rather than from contracts.

## Where they are

- **Katariina käik, 32 residents:** the Dominican friary of St Catherine, at the end of the lane that bears its name ([ledger](../ledger/lower_town/katariina_kaik.md)). Friars walk from there to the market, to the Vene end of the harbour road and to Estonian houses.
- **Väike-Kloostri, 32 residents:** the Cistercian nunnery of St Michael ([ledger](../ledger/lower_town/vaike_kloostri.md)), with its chaplains. Nuns are enclosed; their lay sisters and servants go to market.
- **Kiriku plats, 22 residents:** the cathedral chapter of St Mary on Toompea ([ledger](../ledger/toompea/kiriku_plats.md)). Canons, vicars choral and the sexton live on the hill, close to the viceroy.
- **Oleviste and Niguliste, 5 each:** the parish houses of St Olaf (harbour, Estonians, Scandinavians) and St Nicholas (merchants) ([Oleviste ledger](../ledger/lower_town/oleviste.md), [Niguliste ledger](../ledger/lower_town/niguliste.md)).
- **Pühavaimu, 1:** the chaplain of the Holy Spirit hospital for the poor ([ledger](../ledger/lower_town/puhavaimu.md)).
- **Vene, 1:** Dmitri Stepanovich at the Novgorod court ([ledger](../ledger/lower_town/vene.md)).

So 76 of the 98 live in Lower Town and 22 on Toompea. The routes are bells and processions: the friary to St Olaf on feast days, the cathedral to the hill gates on funerals, the parish priests to the gate for sailors dying at the Coastal Gate. Meeting places are porches and sacristies, the cloister, the hospital hall, and the lane between the friary and the market.

## Organisation and secrecy

The Church has a visible organisation and no secret one. Orders are strict by rule: the Dominican prior and sub-prior, the cellarer and the infirmarian; the nunnery's abbess or prioress and her chaplains; the chapter's dean and canons, who vote; parish priests who answer to the bishop and the chapter. 

Secrecy comes from the confessional and the sacristy rather than from signs. There are no passwords. There is a sacred silence that other factions learn to use: a man who tells a priest where he hid a spear has given it to no one else, and the priest will not give it up, but he may change how he preaches on Sunday. Money moves as alms, wills and mass-stipends, which no one audits closely. Who knows whom: sextons know every key, vicars know every canon's habits, friars know the Estonian lanes, chaplains know the wills. Several members hold a private secret that is not political (a bastard in a ditch, a skimmed fee, a loaf stolen for a widow). A Kalev commission could reveal that a candle, a bell clapper, a hinge or a chest-lock for a church carries someone's request under it: the bell-rope for a rising, the spare key that opens a tower.

## Resources and economy

The Church holds land and rents, the best masonry in town, wax, candles, bread, ale, bell-metal and, above all, letters and Latin. The cathedral chapter lives on prebends and grain rents from Harju estates; the friary on alms and a garden; the nunnery on dowries, a fish pond and weaving; the hospital on bequests; parish priests on stipends, fees and what they can sell at the porch.

They need the city for alms, burials, grain in a dear spring and protection from levies. They take tithes, fees, bequests and land that merchants leave them when they die. Arms: none, by rule. A friar or canon carries a knife for bread and a staff for the road. What the Church trades in is favour: the lime Marquard lends to a builder, the wax Lambert sells cheap, the bread Winand carries, the Estonian catechism Tilman wants to leave behind.

## Relations with other factions

- **Hanseatic guilds:** the Church's richest donors and most demanding patrons. St Nicholas is their church; the friary gets alms and quiet in return.
- **Danish Crown:** the cathedral chapter lives beside the viceroy and sometimes pays for it. Marquard mistrusts the Danish officers and says so; Winand shuts his eyes to the levies.
- **Livonian Order:** no resident presence yet. Before the rising it is only an idea of what a stricter Church could be. After the rising it will arrive with its own chaplains.
- **Black Cloaks:** divided. St Olaf's is a meeting ground; the Dominicans are suspect to some, loved by others.
- **Harju Kings:** the Estonian friars and sextons come from the same villages; they hear the bound peasants' complaints and have no way to help.
- **Cult of Metsik:** tolerated in practice. Eerik's pond offering and Tilman's pagan-edged proverbs are not reported to the prior.
- **Pskov / Novgorod:** Dmitri's Orthodox chapel is allowed under Hanseatic practice; contact is polite and wary.

## Tensions inside

- **Rule against mercy.** Winand Wulf, Rein Peetri poeg and Lutke van Munster pull toward practical charity that bends the chapter's rules; the vicars who skim grave fees, whom Rein keeps on his notched stick, pull the other way. Winand will not report Rein's tally; Rein will not report Winand's bread.
- **Friars against parish.** Tilman van Munster's Estonian sermons are a quarrel with the St Olaf parish, where Lambert van Dortmund wants the Estonian half of his flock but fears the friary's rival hold on them. The older Dominicans mutter at Arnold van van Lubeke's market dealings.
- **Hill against town.** Marquard van Wismar and Johannes van Hamelen live on the chapter's rents and speak the viceroy's language; Wessel van Brunswik and Tyde Sasse serve a merchant town that resents Danish levies. A shared sorrow, a child baptised in haste by a lay hand, binds Wessel, Tyde and Lambert, and each hides it from the others.

## Spring 1343 timeline

- **Before 23 April.** Easter was on the 13th and the Lent just ended left stores low. Priests hear more confessions than usual and carry rumours to nobody. Friars buy rye ahead; the nuns' lay sisters shop quietly; the cathedral prepares for St George's feast on the hill. Gates close early twice. Tilman preaches on the second Sunday and the Estonians stay after the sermon to talk of the grain levy.
- **The night of 23 April.** Church doors are open. Some priests ring bells in alarm, others stay in choir. Sextons hold the keys to every tower and no one is certain who will ask for them. Hospital beds fill. Clergy do not carry weapons and sanctuary is the one law they can still invoke.
- **The siege weeks.** The friary becomes a hospital and a grain store; the nunnery shelters women and children; the cathedral chapter splits between the viceroy and the town. Bells are rung to count and to warn. The Order enters after the rising and brings its own priests; the old parish clergy must choose how to meet them.

## Outside the walls

Plausible-composite estimate, outside the census: Harju's rural parishes hold perhaps 40 to 60 priests and chaplains, most of them Estonian-speaking German or Estonian-born, plus lay sextons. Padise abbey and its monks, Cistercian and close to the Harju roads, add a few dozen more. Clergy there are less secure than in town: a manor lord can starve a village priest, a bound peasant can confess to him and a rising can burn his church. They are the Church's strongest link to the Harju Kings and its most exposed members. None of them is counted in the 98.

## Game hooks

- **Raise standing:** deliver hinges, locks or a bell clapper at honest weight; spare the church's well or its sanctuary; bring grain or wax to the hospital; protect a priest during the night. A commission that leaves a church better made and hides nothing in it counts most.
- **Lower standing:** forge arms in a church's name; sabotage a bell; betray a confession or a sexton's key; take sanctuary and bring weapons into it.
- **Barks:** "A bell does not choose a side, smith." "Come after Vespers, I have counted the loaves." "Keep your penance light and your debts lighter."
- **Quest seeds:** a cracked bell clapper at St Olaf's that Tõnis has hidden a spare key beside; a missing eight marks in the friary cellar that Arnold wants found without the prior knowing; a widow's hovel under the hill wall that needs a door before Winand's secret is found.
- **Reputation reactions:** with Hanseatic standing high, St Nicholas speaks warmly; with the Black Cloaks high, Dominicans speak carefully; with the Danish Crown high, the cathedral canons invite Kalev to Toompea.
- **Standing -3:** a faction-tied resident turns the key in the door: "The church is not a forge. Take your hammer elsewhere."
- **Standing +3:** the same resident leaves the sacristy door open: "There is a bench and a loaf. Say what you came to say and I will not say it again."
