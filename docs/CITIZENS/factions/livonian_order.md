# Livonian Order

Status: implemented as content (task **R-CITIZENS-001**). Confidence: `plausible composite`. Ledger id: `livonian_order`. Scope: the Order's network of residents inside the walls and immediate suburbs of Reval in the week before St George's Night 1343, plus a clearly marked estimate of its reach outside. Out of scope: the Order's later garrison and field army, which arrive after the rising and are overlays, not census rows. See [`CENSUS.md`](../CENSUS.md) and the [faction index](./README.md).

## What they want and what they cost

The launch table gives the Order a clean want, "a pious land under one faith and one law", and a dark shadow: order becomes fanaticism, and massacre becomes policy. In April 1343 the want is mostly a promise. The Order holds no house, tower or gate in Reval. Its strength lies in Riga, Fellin and Wenden, and in the rumour that when the Danish viceroy on Toompea ([Konrad Preen](../../CHARACTERS/konrad_preen.md)) finally fails, someone with discipline will be needed.

On the street the Order means two things. To the frightened, it is the end of grain levies by the Danes and of robbers on the Tartu road. To the wary, it is the hand that will take over the levies and keep them. Most townspeople have never met a knight; they know a wool-factor, a grey-coated man after Mass, a rumour. The Order's cost is paid first in secrets (what you said in the tap-room), and later in obedience. Estonians are not part of its picture of a pious land except as tenants, and everyone in Reval who is not German knows it.

## Why people join

The shared root is **fear of disorder and of owing**. The Danish crown is indebted and distant, the council is cautious, Estonian bands are rumoured to be gathering, and the old remember the troubles of 1313-1325. The Order offers to be the one thing that stays solid. Its members differ in what moves them from that fear into action.

- **Debt:** [Arend van Bocholt](../people/lower_town/arend_van_bocholt.md), a seventeen-year-old clerk on Lai, reports to a Riga agent because the agent covered his dead father's bad debt. He wants his father's name cleared and has told himself that small items do no harm.
- **Dependence:** [Johan Kniphof](../people/lower_town/johan_kniphof.md), fifteen, a cellar hand on Rataskaevu, repeats tap-room talk to a man in grey because a wine-importer who deals with Order stewards pays his mother's rent in Riga.
- **Money:** [Sibbe van Luneborch](../people/lower_town/sibbe_van_luneborch.md), a spinner on Lai, sells her neighbours' small talk for silver pfennigs, saving for a Latin schoolmaster for her son, and hides it from her husband.
- **Admiration:** [Lambrecht van Minden](../people/lower_town/lambrecht_van_minden.md), a young merchant on Lai, met Order knights at Riga in 1340 and wants a quiet, ordered Reval; he has dined with an agent twice and promised nothing. His wife [Sibbe Hovesche](../people/lower_town/sibbe_hovesche.md) works in the Hanseatic circle, not his.
- **Kinship and loyalty to a lord:** [Johan Rotermund](../people/toompea/johan_rotermund.md), 67, a vassal knight on Toom-Rüütli, lives by the Order's old law of service and holds land by it. He is the nearest thing to a household lord in the census.
- **Habit and gossip:** [Elisabet Lunge](../people/toompea/elisabet_lunge.md), a Danish-born spinner on Rutu, is the go-between who found Sibbe the work, and carries wool news up and down the hill. [Gyse van Lemego](../people/toompea/gyse_van_lemego.md), 63, a spinner on Rahukohtu, passes what she hears across the market.
- **Livelihood:** [Hildebrand van Hildensem](../people/toompea/hildebrand_van_hildensem.md), a market gardener on Kohtu, sells to the hill and counts the Order's silver as steadier than the crown's.
- **Old loyalty:** [Evert Rode](../people/lower_town/evert_rode.md), 72, a retired craftsman on Raekoja, remembers the wars and trusts men who keep their word to the day. In his household [Ermelin](../people/lower_town/ermelin.md), a maid of 29, sympathises because her master does, and is quieter about it than he is.

## Who belongs

Twenty-three residents, 0.5% of the census: a thin network, not a party. That is realistic. The Order has no town house, no chapter and no brethren in Reval before the rising, so membership here means being useful to an outsider.

- **Roles:** one core, five active, thirteen sympathisers, four coerced or dependent. The core is the market gardener Tilman van Zwolle at the foot of the castle hill, a man who knows exactly whom he serves.
- **Sex and age:** fifteen women and eight men; eight are 60 or older. This is an older, female-weighted faction because the work is listening, and spinners, midwives and elder widows hear more than merchants. The youngest (Arend, Johan Kniphof) are in it by debt, not belief.
- **Trades:** seven spinners, six retired craftsmen, two market gardeners, a midwife, a shoemaker, a maid, a household servant, a clerk, an apprentice, a Fernhändler and one vassal knight. Almost no one is armed.
- **Ethnicity:** twenty Germans and three Danes. There are no Estonians, on purpose: the Order's pitch is German law and German faith, and Estonians read it correctly.
- **Place:** nine in Lower Town, nine on Toompea, three in the vassal yards below the castle, one in the Viru road suburb and one among the cattle-road farmsteads.

**For gameplay:** a balanced mix means the Order is felt as information more than force. At the start Kalev can meet it only as a clerk's slip, a spinner's lie, a merchant's invitation. Standing should move through whispers first. Troops come later, so a player who treats every Order resident as a soldier gets the faction wrong.

## Where they are

- **Lai** (three members): the merchant street. Lambrecht's house, Sibbe van Luneborch's doorstep and Arend's counting-room sit within a few doors of each other, with Mass at St Nicholas and St Olaf. See the [Lai ledger](../ledger/lower_town/lai.md).
- **Raekoja** (two): Evert Rode's household by the town hall, which is the best place in Lower Town to overhear council business.
- **Rataskaevu**: the well-street tap-room where Johan Kniphof listens; the well itself is the neighbourhood's news exchange.
- **Pikk and Sauna** hold one member each; **Olevimägi** and **Kiriku põik** one each on the hill's edge; one household sits on the **Tartu road** among the cattle-road farmsteads and one on the **Viru road** suburb.
- **Toompea:** Toom-Kooli (three), Toom-Rüütli (two), Rutu, Rahukohtu and Kohtu, around the cathedral of St Mary and the Danish castle. The three on the Western castle road live in the vassal yards.

The routes that matter run uphill and downhill: the **Pikk jalg** and the **Western castle road** between the market and the hill. The gates that count are the hill gates and the **Viru** and **Cattle** (Karja) gates, where road news arrives first.

## Organisation and secrecy

There are no ranks and no chapter. The structure is a chain of three links.

1. **A contact on the road:** a Riga wool-factor, a wine-importer's agent, a man in a grey coat who meets informants after Mass.
2. **A go-between in town:** Elisabet Lunge on Toompea and the core member Tilman van Zwolle gather what the street sells.
3. **The street:** spinners, servants, a clerk, a boy.

Messages travel as yarn orders, wool prices, the list of who buys and who sells; nothing is written down that a bailiff could read, except through clerks like Arend. Money moves as a few silver pfennigs by weight, never in a single sum. Few members know each other: Sibbe knows Elisabet, Arend knows only his agent, Johan Kniphof knows only a face in grey. A shared sign is the plain greeting "may your thread run even".

A Kalev commission can expose this. A cask, a wool contract or a lock-fitting delivered to a member's house will show the same Riga hand in two unrelated places, or show who carries names uphill. Kalev should be able to follow the chain to its ends or to break it at its weakest link, the two boys.

## Resources and economy

The Order's resources are not in the town. It needs three things from Reval: **intelligence** (who can be bought, which houses hold grain and iron, how the watch is paid), **neutral factors** in the market, and **a quiet place** for envoys. It takes these cheaply: a few schillings a month, rent paid in Riga, a debt forgiven. Wool and cloth are the natural cover; a Riga wool-factor buying yarn for Order mills is credible to every spinner. Food and arms are not supplied locally. If the siege comes, the network matters less than what the market can feed a garrison; the Order's local men are consumers, not suppliers, and every sack of rye it takes raises Fear.

## Relations with other factions

- **Danish Crown:** formal respect, quiet contempt. Elisabet and Rotermund cross Toompea daily and are tolerated because the viceroy cannot afford to quarrel.
- **Hanseatic guilds:** the council trades with the Order's towns and does not want it inside the walls. Lambrecht and his wife are a miniature of the split.
- **Black Cloaks:** enemies. Their rebels see Order informers as the first thing to burn; [Martin of the Black Cloaks](../../CHARACTERS/martin_black_cloaks.md) would name them if he could.
- **Harju Kings:** open enmity, since the Order's pitch is to be the lord of the manors they rise against.
- **Cult of Metsik:** deep hostility; the Order's faith leaves no room for groves.
- **Pskov / Novgorod:** rivals who want a weakened Order; [Anisia of Novgorod](../../CHARACTERS/anisia_of_novgorod.md) is watched.
- **Vitalienbrüder:** ignored; they are nuisance, not threat.
- **Church:** shared language and rites; the Dominicans are sympathetic to order without being the Order's men.

## Tensions inside

- **Silver or conviction:** Sibbe van Luneborch and Elisabet Lunge sell talk; Johan Rotermund and Tilman van Zwolle hold that service is a duty, not trade. The first group would stop if the pay did.
- **Quiet or acting now:** Lambrecht wants discipline brought to Reval; Evert Rode would rather wait for it. Hildebrand wants no violence near his gardens.
- **Coerced or willing:** Arend and Johan Kniphof begin to suspect they are not alone and that the price keeps rising. If either breaks, the whole chain is open.

## Spring 1343 timeline

- **Before 23 April:** Easter (13 April) has just passed. Agents ask for longer reports; Sibbe and Arend are asked for more, faster. Lambrecht sends his eldest child to her grandmother and visits the gates more often.
- **St George's Night:** the rising catches the network by surprise. Members shut doors; Rotermund keeps to Toompea. Nobody acts for the Order itself.
- **Siege weeks:** Order envoys arrive from Riga and agents become useful. Informants are exposed, hidden or hunted; the cost falls first on the coerced.

## Outside the walls

Estimates only, a `plausible composite`, not census rows. The Order's land reaches Harju mostly through vassals and allies, and its real armed force is in Livonia proper. In April 1343 perhaps a dozen to twenty vassal families in Harju hold by Order-aligned tenure; its marshal's men would take weeks to arrive. Support among Estonian peasants is effectively nil, and every manor lord who fears his own tenants is a potential ally.

## Game hooks

**Standing events.** Raise: returning a lost letter without reading it; refusing a rebel request to burn a house; a clean commission to a vassal knight. Lower: exposing an informant; arming the rebels; leaving a coerced boy to be beaten. A `ledger.bell_and_chain.fracture_exposed` style discovery should cost Order standing, as in the Living City page.

**Barks.** "Mind your tongue, the wool has ears." "Ask Elisabet, she knows what a hank costs on the hill." "A calm town is a quiet debt."

**Quest seeds.**
1. A hidden message in a cask delivered to Rataskaevu.
2. A clerk begs Kalev to forge a bill that clears his father's debt.
3. A knight's old mail on Toompea needs mending, and the man who asks is watched.

**Reputation reactions.** At standing **-3** a faction-tied resident turns away: "Not at my door, smith. I know what you did on Lai." At **+3** one lowers her voice: "A friend at the right ear. Come when the bells are done, and bring nothing written."
