# Harju Kings

Status: implemented as content (task **R-CITIZENS-001**). Confidence: `plausible composite`. Ledger id: `harju_kings`. Scope: the town sympathisers, couriers and informers of the rural rising, as they live inside the walls and the nearest suburbs in the week before St George's Night 1343; the hinterland host is estimated under "Outside the walls" and is not part of the census.

## What they want and what they cost

The launch table gives the Harju Kings freedom as their want and a slide from liberation into indiscriminate slaughter as their shadow. In town those words turn into smaller things. The want is a mother brought off a manor for six marks the bailiff will not name, a nephew not bound, a reeve who stops asking for a drover's licence. The shadow is a rising that does not distinguish the manor bailiff from the cooper's family who happen to have a German name.

To ordinary people the Kings are a rumour with a cost. To an Estonian maid they are the people who ask nothing for a year and then ask one thing. To a German householder they are the reason the Rat has doubled the gate watch and why the cook is watched at market. Nobody in the lanes calls them a faction; they say "the Harju folk" and lower the voice. The Kings are not the Black Cloaks: those are urban smiths and artisans who want a different city, while the Kings want the land back and are using the city as a road.

## Why people join

The root grievance is the same in nearly every household: a bound strip, a flogging, a levy, a mother left behind. Since the 1325 binding of the manors, a runaway in town is property that has not yet been collected. The branch differs for every person.

- **Fear.** [Kaspar](../people/kalarand/kaspar.md), the harbour pilot, ran from a flogging at seventeen. He passes ship names and the Danish guard-boat's sleeping hours to the circle because they know what he is, and resents every word.
- **Debt.** [Lauri Lembitu poeg](../people/lower_town/lauri_lembitu_poeg.md), the clerk, had his school fee paid by an old man and cannot stand being a debtor, so he carries words he never writes down.
- **Love.** [Ell Tanieli tütar](../people/lower_town/ell_tanieli_tutar.md) runs word between the lane and the Harju villages in a baby's cradle because her mother is still bound near Kose.
- **Ambition and the wish to matter.** [Triin Aino tütar](../people/harju_road/triin_aino_tutar.md), fifteen, wants to be the girl who carried the word that counted on the night it mattered.
- **Faith and memory.** [Evert Marteni poeg](../people/harju_road/evert_marteni_poeg.md), the old boat-builder, keeps a pebble in a wall crack for each manor he hears has been bound, and is afraid of the fighting he quietly cheers.
- **Protection of kin.** [Taniel Kaspari poeg](../people/lower_town/taniel_kaspari_poeg.md), the netmaker, carries knot-signs he does not want because the men holding his kin's land on the Harju road ask him to.
- **Grief turned to stubbornness.** [Veronika](../people/harju_road/veronika.md), the drover, has lost two nephews to the binding, the second this Lent, and walks word between manors inside her head with the herd.
- **Silver and a free day.** [Tuulikki Lassentytär](../people/lower_town/tuulikki_lassentytar.md), a Finnish maid, listens at a Hanseatic table to buy her mother a cow.
- **Small loyalties and quiet shame.** [Kornel Antsu poeg](../people/lower_town/kornel_antsu_poeg.md) hides runaways in a woodpile and hides his father's bondage under a servant's name; [Katrin Villemi tütar](../people/lower_town/katrin_villemi_tutar.md) hides notes in a basket lining because her brother was flogged.
- **Duty without a story.** [Helena](../people/kalarand/helena.md) shouts across the lane and hides the most; her skeins carry knotted cords that [Paul Tõlli poeg](../people/lower_town/paul_tolli_poeg.md), a Vene street merchant, reads. [Ede Eerika tütar](../people/lower_town/ede_eerika_tutar.md) and [Elisabet Madise tütar](../people/lower_town/elisabet_madise_tutar.md) complete the Vene and Müürivahe thread.

## Who belongs

The census holds 64 Harju King residents, 1.5% of 4,247. That is small, and it is right: this is the faction of a countryside, whose strength is in villages that the census does not count. Inside the walls it needs only eyes, feet and a few safe lofts.

By district, 44 live in Lower Town, 7 on the fishing beach, 6 on the Viru road, 5 on the Harju road, 1 on Toompea and 1 on the cattle-road farmsteads. By role, most are hidden: 18 secret sympathisers and 12 secret cell members, 8 informers and 7 couriers, only 4 core and 3 active. There are only 2 coerced or dependent. Ten are open sympathisers, which means they say what they think in a kitchen.

The mix is striking. Women are 41 of 64, and the age curve is young (28 aged 15 to 29, only 7 over 60, 3 children). The trades explain it: 12 maids, 10 spinners, 9 household servants and 3 cooks. These are the people who walk the town with a basket, a skein or a jug and are not stopped. Estonians are 61 of 64, three are Finnish; there are no Germans, which is the faction's weakness and its safety, since no German household can be sure of its own maid.

For gameplay the balance means the Kings are best met through errands and kitchens, not at a table. The player will find them among servants and carters, and a standing change reaches the lane faster than the guild hall.

## Where they are

The densest street is the Western coast road on the fishing beach (7), followed by the Viru road (6), the Harju road (5) and Sauna street (5). In Lower Town they sit in the merchant streets where Estonians serve: Lai (4), Suur-Karja (4), Vene (4), Müürivahe (3), Pikk (3), Dunkri (2), with single members on Nunne and Lühike jalg.

Meeting places are all ordinary. The fishing beach and the harbour quay, where Kaspar passes his word. The Smiths' (Harju) Gate and the bench by the Harju road door, where carters stop. The well at the market, where a Finnish-speaking woman first found Tuulikki. The door of St Olaf, where Estonians stand at the back on Sundays, and where a netmaker hears news. The shambles, where a drover has a reason to be. Routes run from the Harju Gate along Harju and Suur-Karja into Lower Town, and from the beach along the Western coast road to the Coastal Gate quay and Pikk.

## Organisation and secrecy

There are no ranks the street could name. A **core** member (Ell) knows three or four others. A **cell** is a household and its neighbours: a maid, a clerk, a spinner and an old man. A **courier** knows one hand-off and nothing beyond it. Between cells, one person is usually a bridge: Kaspar for the water, Paul for the cords, Taniel for the net.

Signs are made of work. A knotted cord inside a skein (Helena), a knot-sign on a fishing net (Taniel), a list recited word for word (Triin), a message in the lining of a basket twice a month (Katrin), a cradle (Ell), a drover's count of the herd (Veronika). Money moves as small favours, school fees, caulking, a pfennig for a loft. Nobody writes a list.

A Kalev commission could reveal this in layers. Delivering a spearhead to a Pikk house shows a basket. Following a spinner shows a loft. A cord shows a merchant who reads it. A good player learns that the Kings are a web of debts and kin, not a hierarchy, and that cutting any thread hurts a servant before it hurts a king. The promoted courier [Kaja](../../CHARACTERS/kaja.md) crosses the Black Cloaks and the Kings, and [Mart](../../CHARACTERS/mart.md)'s passed-on spearheads show how the threads touch.

## Resources and economy

The Kings have almost no silver. A maid earns a pfennig a week and board. Their economy is favours, kin and information. From the city they take food in small amounts, a bed for a runaway, a ship's name, a gate-captain's habit and the time of the Danish guard-boat's watch. From the countryside they bring grain stored against the spring gap, scythes, spears and billhooks, and men who have never held a crossbow. They have no arms inside the walls; spearheads must come from forges, rejected or traded, and that is where Kalev's trade is dangerous.

If the city is searched, the Kings lose almost nothing and the servants lose everything.

## Relations with other factions

- **Black Cloaks:** allies of convenience. They share smiths and a grievance but want different things, and [Martin](../../CHARACTERS/martin_black_cloaks.md) and his cell distrust the pace of the countryside.
- **Cult of Metsik:** tolerated and often kin. The old ways feed the Kings' courage; the Kings' leaders ([Lembit Helme](../../CHARACTERS/lembit.md) among them) keep the grove and the church apart in public.
- **Danish Crown:** enemy. Its Vogts bind the manors and [Konrad Preen](../../CHARACTERS/konrad_preen.md) holds the hill.
- **Livonian Order:** a future enemy and a danger to the Kings' own villages; no one in town yet knows how much.
- **Hanseatic guilds:** the employers of the maids and the buyers of the cattle. The Kings hate the law and spare the households.
- **Pskov / Novgorod:** distant friends at best; rumours of a carter's offer are common, a promise is not.
- **Vitalienbrüder:** unwanted. Raiders do not help a land rising.

## Tensions inside

Three splits run through the page.

**Pace.** [Lembit Helme](../../CHARACTERS/lembit.md) has written to city contacts that the rural host may move before the gates are ready and has not told every king. On the other side, Triin and Lauri want the night to come soon; Evert and Taniel want it never.

**Money against faith.** Kornel wants to buy free his mother for six marks and would take silver from anyone, including a merchant; Veronika holds that freedom cannot be paid for to the manor that took it.

**The informers' shame.** Kaspar and Tuulikki carry word and resent it; Ell and Helena do not. Kaspar has told only one man which guard-boat sleeps, and Tuulikki does not want to learn that the Hanseatic house she serves is kind.

## Spring 1343 timeline

**Before 23 April.** The week after Easter is the busiest of the year. Triin's lists lengthen, Helena's cords double, Ell's messages double, Kaspar watches the roads for a Danish boat, and Veronika walks the manors twice. Strangers appear at the Harju Gate.

**The night.** The couriers become guides. Kaspar's one piece of knowledge matters, Katrin and Lauri carry the final word to merchants' kitchens, and the Harju road houses close their shutters.

**The siege weeks.** Town sympathisers split: some go home, some leave through the Harju Gate for the host, a few are searched, a few are hanged on suspicion. The old, such as Evert and Taniel, can do nothing but wait.

## Outside the walls

Outside the census, plausible-composite estimates only. The Harju host is probably a few thousand men and women across a dozen or so manor districts, mostly farmers with spears and billhooks, led by four elected kings. The Kings' real strength is knowledge of roads and the sympathy of the clerics at village churches. Their weakness is grain and iron; they have neither in quantity.

## Game hooks

**Ledger events that raise standing:** carrying a message without reading it; refusing to name a runaway; paying a servant's debt; a rejected spearhead that reaches the right hands. **That lower it:** revealing a courier; reporting a woodpile; selling a muster list; an unwarranted killing during the rising (the shadow).

**Barks.** "The herd knows the road." "No one tells the Harju road anything, and it hears everything."

**Quest seeds.**
- A skein with a cord that should not be read.
- A runaway in a woodpile and a bailiff's man at the gate.
- A net whose knots say something new.
- Six marks for a mother.
- A pilot who wants a guard-boat to sleep.

**Reputation reactions.** At Harju Kings standing 2 or higher in Lower Town, the existing reputation moment `reputation.harju_kings_trusted` fires and the street cheers. At negative standing, doors close and children are called in.

**Ell at -3:** "You ask a maid where the lane goes? Ask the Danes, they have the boots." **Ell at +3:** "Hush. The baby is asleep. There is a loft on Lühike jalg that nobody counts."
