# The Hanseatic guilds

Status: implemented as content (task **R-CITIZENS-001**). Confidence: `plausible composite`. Ledger id: `hanseatic` (`faction.hanseatic_guilds` in the cast promotion table). Scope: the faction as it lives among Reval's residents in the week before St George's Night, 1343; the census block at the foot is generated, the hinterland estimates are not part of it. Out of scope: Lübeck's own politics and any ninth faction.

## What they want and what they cost

The launch table gives the Hansa one want, "trade above all; a profitable, quiet Reval", and one shadow, "lives and traditions priced like cargo". On the street that means the council of Lübeck law, the weighers, the quay, and the credit that keeps a cooper in staves. A quiet town pays its debts. A town on fire does not, so the Hansa is first against the Crown's levy (it taxes cargo), first against the Order (it would bring a new master and new customs), and first against the rebels (they close gates).

Ordinary people meet it as three things: wages paid in weighed silver, a credit book that can be called in, and a long memory. A fair factor on the quay who "took the sailor's side before the bailiff" is the Hansa's face for [Detlev Starke](../people/lower_town/detlev_starke.md). The same book is its grip. The Hansa does not hate Estonians; it prices them. A porter's day, a maid's year and a carpenter's house surety are all lines in someone's account, and the shadow is that nobody asks what a line is worth to the person written in it.

## Why people join

The shared root is exposure. A cargo, a loan, a surety, a wage or a dead father's debt ties each member to a house that can lose everything in one bad season. What differs is the branch.

- **Duty to the Rat.** [Tilman van Lubeke](../people/lower_town/tilman_van_lubeke.md), a stooped old councillor on Pikk, belongs because the council is his life, though a secret debt to a tallow-boiler and a Russian trader means he can be leaned on.
- **Dread of owing.** [Dietrich Zierenberg](../people/lower_town/dietrich_zierenberg.md), the burgomaster, hates debt yet has been bound by one small kindness; a maid returned a lost purse at the Coastal Gate and later asked him to listen.
- **Gratitude.** Detlev Starke, a sailor who cannot read, was defended by a factor and has kept the Hansa's ears ever since; [Erik Jönsson](../people/lower_town/erik_jonsson.md), a Swedish errand-man, was given a winter bed at fifteen and carries three passwords by rhyme.
- **Debt.** [Hildebrand Wulf](../people/lower_town/hildebrand_wulf.md), a skipper, owes a Lübeck loan due by Michaelmas; [Ulrika Torgilsdotter](../people/lower_town/ulrika_torgilsdotter.md), a maid on Pikk, pays her dead father's debt in whispers at funerals.
- **Ambition.** [Tideman van Wismar](../people/lower_town/tideman_van_wismar.md), a clerk, wants a factor's stool and has taken two recruiters to get it.
- **Marginal belonging.** [Conrad van Stralesund](../people/lower_town/conrad_van_stralesund.md), a clerk raised by an Estonian nurse, wants to be taken for a German by Germans and fears for the nurse's village under the Danish levy.
- **Protection.** [Lauri Villemi poeg](../people/lower_town/lauri_villemi_poeg.md), an Estonian carpenter, reports what the watch hears because a German timber factor stood surety for his Pikk house.
- **The post.** [Madis Jaani poeg](../people/lower_town/madis_jaani_poeg.md), gatekeeper of the Short Hill Gate, is dependent on the council and says so; his loyalty is to the door.

Nobody on this list joined for a slogan, and most would be offended to be called a Hanseatic. They would say they work for a house.

## Who belongs

The census gives 434 residents, 10.2% of 4,247. Only 83 are `core`, 142 `active`, 145 `sympathiser`, 57 `dependent`, plus a handful of watch members with no role, one courier ([Hinrick Kristjani poeg](../people/lower_town/hinrick_kristjani_poeg.md), a carpenter of the Estonian night watch) and one informer (Lauri Villemi poeg). A faction that is a third of its membership merely sympathetic is how a trading body really is: most merchants want the quiet, few want the risk.

Men are 265 and women 169 (39%, below the city's 49%): the councillors, skippers and clerks are male, but the faction leans on maids, spinners and cooks, the women who see every back door. Children are only 30 because membership is mostly of adults and heads of house; the 15-29 band (147) is the clerks, apprentices and maids. Germans are 321 (74%), Estonians 56, Swedes 48, with a few Finns, Danes and two Russians. The council is closed to anyone who is not a German burgher, so that is the shape of its core; the Estonians and Swedes are the hands that carry its business. Typical trades: Fernhändler (47), spinner (51), merchant's clerk (36), maid (37), Krämer (26), sailor (12).

The size is realistic. Reval is a small Hanseatic outstation; a few dozen merchant households, their servants and the weaving women who depend on them make the circle. For play, a balanced mix means the faction is neither an army nor a handful: Kalev meets it in every house of Pikk and Lai, mostly as employers and landlords, rarely as conspirators. Pushing it down hurts the quay; pushing it up costs the street.

## Where they are

Lower Town holds 384 of the 434 and the weight is on the merchant streets: Lai (60), Pikk (47), Vene (23), Dunkri (20), the Coastal Gate quay (16), Müürivahe (15), Suur-Karja (15), Raekoja (14), Olevimägi (11) and Katariina käik (11). Toompea has 25, mostly crown clerks and vassals who depend on credit ([Ricbod van Minden](../people/toompea/ricbod_van_minden.md), a crown clerk; [Lambert Sasse](../people/toompea/lambert_sasse.md), a vassal knight). Smaller clusters sit at the fishing beach (7), the Viru road (8), the cattle-road farmsteads (4), and the vassal yards below the castle (5).

Meeting places are ordinary on purpose: the St Nicholas church door after Mass, where [Erik Jönsson](../people/lower_town/erik_jonsson.md) pauses; the weighers at Raekoja; the salt loft on Harju; the Coastal Gate quay stalls and inns; the Rataskaevu well, where servants from three houses fill pails and trade talk. Routes run from Munga to Pikk (shoes and hides), from the quay to the warehouse yards on Lai, and from the gates to the council. A member goes where a house sends them.

## Organisation and secrecy

There is a council face and a circle behind it. The council face is the Rat, the guilds and the Stadtschreiber [Gerhard van Bremen](../people/lower_town/gerhard_van_bremen.md). The circle is a web of cells of two, each with a third unnamed, that began at funerals where the same whisper was heard. Dietrich Zierenberg and Madis Jaani poeg are one pair; Conrad van Stralesund and [Jüri Lauri poeg](../people/lower_town/juri_lauri_poeg.md), a cooper, are another; Detlev Starke and Ulrika Torgilsdotter a third. They nod in church and never speak.

Messages are memorised by those who cannot read (Detlev, Erik) and written by those who can (clerks such as Tideman). Passwords are rhymes. Money moves as favour: a bed, a surety, a freight, a loan settled at quarter-days, not as bribes. Who knows whom: a pair knows each other; the clerk Conrad knows the third in his cell and tells nobody; Gottschalk van Deventer is Conrad's superior in the circle though they never speak in company.

A Kalev commission could reveal a door key lent without a question, a letter copied twice with a changed line, a cargo held back as a lever (Hildebrand Wulf), a postern opened once to a rider who signed no book, or the dull fact that the housemaid who delivers his ironwork is also the one who listens.

## Resources and economy

Money is silver by weight and accounts in marks, öre and örtug; a clerk earns about four marks a year with board, a skipper's voyage pays in öre, a merchant like Dietrich draws perhaps 150 marks from cloth, wax and salt. The guilds hold credit, storehouses, ships and the Rat's seal. Food flows through them (rye, herring, salt) and Lent has just ended, so stores are thin. Arms are few: a household's boar-spear, a quay-side crossbow, the watch's jacks; the Hansa buys protection with silver rather than with iron, which is why Kalev's forge matters.

The faction needs open roads, honest weighers, quiet gates and a council that does not split. It takes interest, tolls, labour, silence, and from the fishing beach and the Viru road, cheap hands.

## Relations with other factions

- **Danish Crown:** a bargain of convenience. The Crown protects trade on Toompea and borrows from it; the Hansa resents the levy and the viceroy [Konrad Preen](../../CHARACTERS/konrad_preen.md).
- **Livonian Order:** feared as a new master, courted as a buyer; the Hansa does not want the Order's law.
- **Black Cloaks:** enemies of the quiet. Their man in the story is [Martin](../../CHARACTERS/martin_black_cloaks.md) closes gates; the Hansa would rather buy the smiths than fight them.
- **Harju Kings:** a danger to the grain trade and the road, but the cheap grain they free is welcome.
- **Cult of Metsik:** tolerated as long as it keeps to the groves and does not interrupt a market day.
- **Pskov and Novgorod:** the best customers and rivals; the Novgorod court under Hanseatic practice is guest and competitor ([Anisia of Novgorod](../../CHARACTERS/anisia_of_novgorod.md)).
- **Vitalienbrüder:** the quay's nightmare; every skipper pays something to stay clear of them.
- **Church:** St Nicholas is a merchant church; the friars are given alms and quiet.

The active promoted face is the amber merchant [Jürgen Witte](../../CHARACTERS/jurgen.md), who turns instability into debt.

## Tensions inside

1. **Council versus quay.** The old councillors, Tilman van Lubeke and [Wolter van Brunswik](../people/lower_town/wolter_van_brunswik.md), want caution; the quay men, Hildebrand Wulf and Detlev Starke, want the cargo moving and are quicker to use a lever.
2. **Loyalty versus conscience.** Tideman van Wismar serves two recruiters and has changed a line in a copied letter; Ulrika Torgilsdotter dreads being named as the one who carried a message.
3. **German core versus the hands.** Lauri Villemi poeg and [Madis Jaani poeg](../people/lower_town/madis_jaani_poeg.md) are bound by favours; Conrad van Stralesund leads from the margin and craves the centre. If the Rat closes ranks against Estonians, these hands drift.

## Spring 1343 timeline

- **Before 23 April:** levy talk and gate closings. The circle moves more messages; Lauri is asked more than he can safely answer; the Rat hears of unexplained visits from the Vogt's men and doubles the gate book.
- **The night:** the gatekeepers choose which door to shut; the watch splits; the Hansa keeps its warehouses lit and its doors barred, and tries to buy peace with grain and silver.
- **The siege weeks:** the quay empties and the faction bargains for safe conduct; the Rat counts debts; some members run letters to Lübeck; the clerks tally who owes whom.

## Outside the walls

Plausible-composite estimates, not part of the census: perhaps 80-150 people across the harbour hamlets and the Harju-road farmsteads depend on Hanseatic credit (carters, salt-pan hands, fishers selling to quay buyers), and a handful of agents reach Narva road and Lübeck-bound ships. Strength is in the road and the credit, not in arms.

## Game hooks

- **Ledger events that raise standing:** delivering a quay cargo safely; repairing a gate lock; refusing a Black Cloak order. **Lower:** forging for the rebels; exposing a cell; breaking a weigher's seal.
- **Barks:** "Weigh it fair and weigh it twice." "The Rat pays, but the Rat remembers." "Which house are you?"
- **Quest seeds:** a copied letter with a changed line; a held cargo; a gate key lent for one night; a maid who asks Kalev to mend a lock without saying whose.
- **Reputation reactions:** at high standing, factors extend credit and clerks look away; at low standing, doors close and weighers tighten the scale.
- **At -3:** a faction-tied resident says, "I will not be seen speaking to you, smith." **At +3:** "If a door is shut, I know whose key opens it."
