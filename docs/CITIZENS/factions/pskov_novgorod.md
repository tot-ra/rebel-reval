# Pskov and Novgorod emissaries

Status: implemented as content (task **R-CITIZENS-001**). Confidence: `plausible composite`. Ledger id: `pskov_novgorod` (`faction.pskov_novgorod`, one combined launch seat; Pskov and Novgorod are told apart by voice, not by two ledgers). Scope: the faction as it lives among Reval's residents in the week before St George's Night, 1343; the census block at the foot is generated, the hinterland estimates are not part of it. Out of scope: Rus' politics beyond what reaches Reval, and any ninth faction.

## What they want and what they cost

The launch table gives them one want, "opportunity in the chaos; a weakened Order", and one shadow, "any ally is sellable at the right price". On the street that is a quiet, patient interest. Russian guests live in Reval under Hanseatic practice: they trade furs, wax, flax and hemp through the Novgorod court on Vene, change grivny and marks, and pray in their own rite. A weakened Order means the eastern road stays open and Pskov can breathe. A rising that bleeds the Order and the Crown without burning the quay suits them, so they will quietly help it along, and they will just as quietly sell the rebels the moment a better offer arrives.

Ordinary Revalers know them as the Vene Street people: careful, fasting, good with silver, bad at German. Most neighbours like the wax and distrust the habit of silence. To a cooper they are customers; to a council clerk they are a ledger line; to a rebel smith they are rich friends who might be gone by morning. The cost is trust. Whoever takes their silver should assume that someone in the circle will weigh it against a cargo.

## Why people join

The shared root is *belonging by trade and tongue*. Few here are exiles. They are guests whose kin, creditors and graves lie east, and who are always one Rat decision away from losing the right to stay. What differs is the branch.

- Faith and grief: [Stepan Fedorovich](../people/lower_town/stepan_fedorovich.md), the Apteegi cap-maker, has sons buried in Pskov; he will hold a letter, never inform on a neighbour.
- Love of a homeland: [Marfa Mikulichna](../people/lower_town/marfa_mikulichna.md), his booming-voiced wife, would feed a Pskov traveller and keep her mouth shut.
- Longing: [Ulyana Dmitrievna](../people/lower_town/ulyana_dmitrievna.md), eighteen, has never seen Pskov and speaks of it like a shrine. Her grievance is German debts, but her reason is a story told on Tolli.
- Gratitude and loyalty: [Irina Ontsiforovna](../people/lower_town/irina_ontsiforovna.md), the Pikk servant, informs from a debt of gratitude to Novgorod, not to the master she serves.
- Trade and kin: [Gavril Gavrilovich](../people/lower_town/gavril_gavrilovich.md), the moneychanger, has clients and family in both cities; a rumour is cheap, a night's lodging dear, a coded note a risk he takes after curfew.
- Ambition and a bargain: [Fedor Yeremeyevich](../people/lower_town/fedor_yeremeyevich.md), his clerk, has kin in Novgorod and a taste for a good price; he gives a letter or an alibi, never information.
- Inheritance: [Ivan Semyonovich](../people/lower_town/ivan_semyonovich.md), the slow-spoken journeyman, remembers that Novgorod trade built his father's mill; he would carry a message, never fight.
- Discretion as a trade: [Agafya Stepanovna](../people/lower_town/agafya_stepanovna.md), a pale household servant on Apteegi, hears the supper talk of every guest and passes it on to men she names to nobody.
- Quiet sympathy: [Marfa Ontsiforovna](../people/lower_town/marfa_ontsiforovna.md) and [Marfa Yeremeyevna](../people/lower_town/marfa_yeremeyevna.md), a spinner and a baker, give bread and silence; the first would never inform.
- Court duty: [Prokhor Mikulich](../people/lower_town/prokhor_mikulich.md), [Timofei Timofeyevich](../people/lower_town/timofei_timofeyevich.md), [Fedor Gavrilovich](../people/lower_town/fedor_gavrilovich.md) and [Timofei Stepanovich](../people/lower_town/timofei_stepanovich.md) live inside the Novgorod court and serve what it needs: the clerk keeps its books, the merchants keep its goods.
- Hearth and cell: [Praskovya Fedorovna](../people/lower_town/praskovya_fedorovna.md), a dairy-woman on Sauna, is one of five core members, and [Mikula Stepanovich](../people/lower_town/mikula_stepanovich.md), the dyer on the same street, is active; they supply the circle's colour and cream, and the cover of daily errands.
- Protection and age: [Irina Dmitrievna](../people/lower_town/irina_dmitrievna.md), 79, retired craftswoman on Tolli, is the keeper of old stories and the quiet superior of younger women; [Olena Timofeyevna](../people/lower_town/olena_timofeyevna.md), 54, spinner on Tolli, is active in her house.

## Who belongs

The census holds **62 residents, 1.5% of 4,247**. All are Russian, a bigger share than any other group by far but a tiny part of a city whose Russian population is about 3%. That size is plausible. Reval was a trading post on the Novgorod route, not a Russian town; the Novgorod court holds 17 residents, and the rest are spread across merchant households on Vene and Pikk and the workshops and servant quarters that serve them.

The roster is **30 sympathisers, 16 active, 9 informers, 5 core and 2 coerced or dependent**. A faction of a few dozen cannot field fighters, and it does not need to. Its strength is the 9 informers, the 5 who give orders, and the 16 who move goods and letters. The mix is 37 men and 25 women, which is unusually balanced for a trade faction, because the circle depends on household women (servants, spinners, a dairy-woman) who see every guest. By age 19 are 15-29, 28 are 30-44, 11 are 45-59 and 4 are over 60. The core group is thus in its prime, and the old women are their memory.

For gameplay: a Kalev commission among them will almost never meet a swordsman. It meets a servant, a clerk, a scale. The faction's reach is information, credit and lodging, and a balanced mix means the player can pick through trades, ages and sexes to find the leak, rather than finding one obvious villain.

## Where they are

The faction lives where Russian trade lives. **Vene** (19 members) holds the Novgorod merchants' church and court (hh.inst_novgorod_court) and merchant houses with rear courts: see [Vene](../ledger/lower_town/vene.md). **Pikk** (13) holds the moneychanger's household and counting rooms. **Apteegi** (5) has the cap-maker's workshop and its servants. **Lai**, **Laboratooriumi**, **Dunkri** (4 each), **Tolli** and **Sauna** (2 each) and **Voorimehe** (2) hold the old women's doors, the dye-works and the dairy; **Raekoja** has one baker. The **Coastal Gate quay** (3) and the **Viru road** suburb (1) are the water and the land side: sailors and carriers who see cargo before the weighers do.

Meetings do not take place. They happen: after Orthodox services in the guest chapel at the court, at the church door after the week of Easter, at the market, in a spinner's cellar, at the dairy. Messages go by yarn orders, caps, wax and kvass. The court's rear yard is the one place where a stranger can be hidden for a night, and the Pikk back lane is the way out after the curfew bell.

## Organisation and secrecy

There is no hierarchy that a clerk could draw. There are **cells of two or three**, each of which knows one person above and one beside. The packet's own links show the pattern: Prokhor Mikulich and Agafya Stepanovna share a funeral and a whisper and form a cell of two, with a third unnamed; Agafya outranks Yakov Fedorovich, but they never speak in a room with others; Irina Ontsiforovna outranks Timofei Stepanovich in the same way; Irina Dmitrievna outranks Ulyana. Superiors are the least expected people: servants, old women, a dairy-woman.

Signs are domestic: bread and salt at the door, a candle in a window on a Friday, a word of Russian at the right moment, a request for a spindle hook or a steel weight to be filed. Orthodox fasts and prayers are visible; the cell is not. Money moves as credit, not silver: a debt forgiven in Novgorod is paid by a favour in Reval. Letters travel in bales of wax and bundles of yarn, and a sailor carries a verbal word where a letter is too dangerous.

A Kalev commission (a lockbox, a filed weight, a spindle hook, a cup-holder) lets him see a house from the inside. He might see an extra bed in a rear court, a second set of scales, a coded tally, or a servant who is too quiet. The cleverest thing he could learn is *who gives orders*, and it is never the master of the house.

## Resources and economy

Their money is silver by weight and credit between kin. The moneychanger converts marks, öre and grivny at a small stall, and he knows who hoards silver and who is short. The Novgorod court trades wax, furs, flax and hemp for cloth, salt, herring and iron, and the cap-makers and spinners work wool bought from Reval merchants. Food is rye bread, kvass, dairy and fish, with strict fasts that cost the household in Lent and set the calendar of favours.

Arms are almost none. The faction owns no weapons stock; its power is a bed for a night, an alibi, a loan, an introduction. Favours it takes: lodging, a letter carried, a name, a word dropped in German company. It needs from the city a tolerated quay, a Rat that does not ban foreign coin or tax amber, and a Crown that does not expel Russian guests at midsummer, which Stepan Fedorovich fears and Fedor Yeremeyevich is ready to profit from.

## Relations with other factions

- **Hanseatic:** the host and the counterparty. They need the Hansa's quay, weights and courts; the Hansa needs their wax and furs. Tolerated, never trusted.
- **Danish crown:** a landlord with a toll bar. The Crown protects trade and might expel guests at a crisis.
- **Livonian Order:** the old enemy of Pskov. A weaker Order is the faction's single clear want; an Order that wins will ask the Hansa for the court.
- **Black cloaks:** useful friends with no money, sold first if needed. Several members quietly sympathise.
- **Vitalienbruder:** buyers of anything that floats; the sailors on the quay know them and say nothing.

## Tensions inside

1. **Informing against carrying.** Agafya Stepanovna and Irina Ontsiforovna pass names; Stepan Fedorovich, Marfa Ontsiforovna and Fedor Yeremeyevich say plainly that they will carry a thing and not inform. Agafya is the superior who sets the rule for Yakov Fedorovich, and a neighbour's name given to a stranger would split the cap-makers' house.
2. **Pskov against Novgorod.** Pskov sympathisers (Stepan Fedorovich, Ulyana Dmitrievna) are loyal to a city whose people fought the Order. Novgorod's merchants (Prokhor Mikulich, Timofei Timofeyevich, Fedor Gavrilovich) would sell any ally if the price is right. Gavril Gavrilovich sits between them.
3. **Courage against the clerk's price.** Young Ulyana wants a Pskov of songs; Fedor Yeremeyevich tells her a fair price and a fairer one for a friend. Irina Dmitrievna tells the young to say nothing, and the young mistake caution for cowardice.

## Spring 1343 timeline

**Before 23 April.** The court's merchants watch the grain levy talk and the closed gates. Gavril leaves by the back lane after curfew. Agafya listens at supper; Irina Ontsiforovna names visitors; the Apteegi cell checks who has been to the cap-maker's. Households prepare Easter bread and fasts after the Orthodox Pascha.

**The night.** Most members stay indoors. Marfa Ontsiforovna hides the children and prays; Ulyana stays in with the little ones; Fedor Yeremeyevich sells stock at midnight; Irina Ontsiforovna sends a word to the guest chapel. Praskovya and Mikula keep their doors shut. Yakov is at the quay. Some will open a back gate to a hunted man, for a price.

**The siege weeks.** If the rising holds, the court tries to stay neutral and sell to both sides. Prices rise. Informers pass names to whichever buyer asks first. If the Order comes, the Novgorod men count their wax and prepare a route east. The circle's real work, lodging and word-carrying, becomes dangerous and well paid.

## Outside the walls

*Estimates, plausible composite, not in the census.* Along the road to Narva river and the eastern border, a few dozen carriers, boatmen and merchants carry goods between Novgorod, Pskov and Reval, with a handful of resident agents at Dorpat and the Narva crossing. Perhaps 150 to 250 people depend on the trade, but most are not politically active. A Pskov scout or a Novgorod envoy can arrive in a bale of furs; they are the "emissaries" proper and are not in the Lower Town count. [Anisia of Novgorod](../../CHARACTERS/anisia_of_novgorod.md), a caravan girl, belongs to this outside world.

## Game hooks

**Standing up** (+): returning a stolen bale or letter; a filed weight, a lockbox, or a spindle hook for a Pskov house; carrying a word east; protecting a Russian guest from a levy; refusing a German buyer who asks for a name.
**Standing down** (-): informing on a Vene household to the Rat; breaking a cell by naming its superior; a rebel act that brings the Vogt to the court; taking silver from the Order.

**Barks:** "Fair price." "Mind the step." "Master is out." "A cap?" "In Pskov they say..." "Doors." "Yarn, good yarn!"

**Quest seeds.**
1. *The filed weight.* Gavril asks Kalev to file a steel scale weight; it is a tell for a smuggled load.
2. *The cup-holder.* Irina Ontsiforovna wants a steady cup-holder; it hides a note.
3. *The hearth hook.* Marfa Ontsiforovna wants a hook; her cellar has a stranger for the night.
4. *The lockbox.* Fedor Yeremeyevich needs a lockbox for letters; Arend van Bocholt suspects stolen goods.

**At standing -3,** a member says to Kalev: *"I did not say that. Go home, smith."* Agafya says nothing and the door closes.
**At standing +3,** a member says: *"Between friends, there is bread and salt. Come in, and shut the door behind you."* Ulyana asks, *"In Pskov they say you know the road. Do you?"*
