# The Vitalienbrüder

Status: implemented as content (task **R-CITIZENS-001**). Confidence: `plausible composite`; the name is the game's own shorthand, since the famous Vitalien and Likedeeler companies belong to later decades, so the 1343 harbour raiders here are an invented composite of Gotland and Baltic sea-robbers, fences and quay hands. Ledger id: `vitalienbruder` (`faction.vitalienbruder` in the cast promotion table). Scope: the faction among Reval's residents in the week before St George's Night, 1343; no fleet simulation; the census block at the foot is generated.

## What they want and what they cost

The launch table gives the brothers one want, "plunder; chaos is the business model", and one shadow, "no flag, no loyalty, no restraint". On the street that is smaller and stranger than it sounds. Nobody in Reval says "I am a Vitalienbruder". A porter says he knows a man with a boat; a weaver says the cloth came with a bad bill. The brotherhood is a way of moving goods nobody will own up to, and a promise that whoever sells to it will be paid in silver by weight and not asked a second question.

Ordinary people meet it as cheap blades, a spare bolt of cloth, a crate in a cellar and, now and then, a stranger at the quay with a hard way of counting a crowd. The cost comes later: a hold searched at the Coastal Gate, a hangman's rope, a neighbour whose ship did not come home. When the rising begins it will sell to the rebels and the Rat alike, and what it wants of Kalev is a smith's hands: blades without marks, a lock that opens from the wrong side, a hook that holds.

## Why people join

The shared root is a debt to the legal city. Every member is someone the Lübeck-law town pays too little or lends to too tightly: the harbour hand under a merchant's cart-hire, the weaver under a belt-maker's loan, the maid with a wage that never grows. The raiders offer a second ledger. What differs is the branch.

- **Appetite.** [Priidik Mikku poeg](../people/kalarand/priidik_mikku_poeg.md), a laughing gaunt porter on the Western coast road, learned the trade as a deckhand on Gotland cogs and wants "a share of a good haul"; he is the only core member in the census, and keeps a crate under his bed that is not cargo.
- **Debt.** [Bengt Magnusson](../people/lower_town/bengt_magnusson.md), a Swedish cloth-weaver on Sauna, owes twelve marks to a respectable belt-maker and fullers and re-dyes damaged cloth for silver and silence.
- **Ambition.** [Erik Andersson](../people/lower_town/erik_andersson.md), a left-handed knife-smith journeyman on Pikk jalg, wants his own forge and sold six blades to a raider's agent last month behind his master's back.
- **Interest.** [Jaan Alberti poeg](../people/lower_town/jaan_alberti_poeg.md), an Estonian skipper on Olevimägi who reads Latin better than his German customers, hides a locker under his floor and has twice lent raiders an anchorage because a hold with two bills pays twice.
- **Service.** [Anna Gunnarsdotter](../people/lower_town/anna_gunnarsdotter.md), a household servant in Suur-Kloostri, is in the census as a secret sympathiser; a servant hears a merchant's table talk, and what she does with it is her own card's business.
- **Youth and boredom.** Helga Torgilsdotter, nineteen, maid in the shoemaker's house on Nunne ([ledger](../ledger/lower_town/nunne.md)), is the youngest; a sailor's story is the only news she gets that no master chose for her.
- **Belonging without a master.** Gunhild Ulfsdotter, a cook at a Pikk merchant house ([ledger](../ledger/lower_town/pikk.md)), is a sympathiser only; she feeds men who come to the kitchen door, and gets fish, salt and no questions.
- **Drift.** Tilman van Bocholt, a German sailor of twenty-seven in the seasonal harbour pool ([ledger](../ledger/lower_town/coastal_gate_quay.md)), has no house at all in Reval; a berth is a berth, and the brothers pay for one.

## Who belongs

The census gives 8 residents, 0.2% of 4,247, the smallest of the ten affinities, and the roster block below holds the numbers. Six are `secret_sympathiser`, one `sympathiser` and one `core`; the faction has no `active` or `courier` rows because its work is done by people with other jobs. Five are Swedish, two Estonian and one German: far more Swedes than the city's 14%, because the Gotland run, the Swedish quay crews and the Visby ties put them at the water's edge. Five are men and three women, and the ages run from 19 to 46, with a clear middle of four in the 30-44 band.

The trades are those that touch a quay: porter, skipper, sailor, knife-smith, weaver, two cooks and maids. Almost nobody is a master of a rich house. It is realistic that so few can be counted. A Baltic robber network in a town of about 4,000 is a handful of fences and carriers, not an army; the real body of the brotherhood is at sea and on the hinterland coast, and visits Reval in the ice-free months. For play, a balanced mix means Kalev will not meet the brothers as a faction but as one oddity in ten streets: a sailor's boots too good for his purse, a skipper's second bill.

## Where they are

Seven of the eight sit in Lower Town and one on the fishing beach (Kalarand). Their streets are Western coast road, Pikk, Sauna, Olevimägi, Pikk jalg, Nunne, Suur-Kloostri and the Coastal Gate quay, one member each. The spread is the point: no street holds two.

Meeting places are ordinary. The Coastal Gate quay and its seasonal berths and inns are where goods change hands; the Western coast road gives a dock-porter's back door to the beach where small boats land after dark; the lanes under Olevimägi, close to St Olaf's tower, are Jaan's end of town and a seaman's landmark. The Sauna street weaving-shed, with its fulling trough and dye vats, hides anything that can be turned into cloth. Pikk jalg, on the slope toward Toompea, holds a forge that can turn out blades quietly. The cooks and maids are the carriers inside the walls: a kitchen door on Pikk, a back stair in Suur-Kloostri, a yard on Nunne. Routes run quay to beach to loft to kitchen; the Coastal Gate is the choke point, and so the chief fear.

## Organisation and secrecy

There are no ranks, only roles that grow from use. A **fence** takes goods (Bengt, Erik); a **carrier** moves them (Priidik, Gunhild, Helga); a **berth** lends a hold or an anchorage (Jaan, Tilman). Cells are of two or three people who do not need each other's names. Priidik knows more people than any other member of the census; Jaan was recruited by Priidik with a small kindness, and Erik's buyer is a captain's agent whom no one else has seen.

Signs are small and practical: a half-hitch tied into a tally-string, a tarred cuff left turned out, a sealskin cap on a peg, a fish of a certain cut left at a kitchen door. Messages travel by tavern talk and on the tide: a skipper says what the roads are like, and everyone who needs to know hears it. Money moves as weighed silver in a purse, as a debt forgiven, or as a favour in kind, and none of it is entered in a book. A Kalev commission could reveal a tally-string with knots no merchant uses, an unmarked blade pattern that matches Erik's file, a strongbox lock with a second keyhole, or a crate under a porter's bed, and so a path from one fence to another.

## Resources and economy

Silver, salt, grain, timber, plain cloth, tar, ropes and blades are what the brotherhood trades. It takes what it cannot make: knives, anchors, boat hooks, coarse wool and the quiet use of a weaving-shed. It gives back fish, ready silver, a cargo that is not on a manifest, and a promise to look the other way. A porter earns about four marks a year, a good skipper run twenty, a weaver two bolts a month; a few schillings from a raider are a heavy sum at those scales, which is how it recruits.

From the city it needs the harbour: the open gate at dawn, an unwatched anchorage, a customs-man who is busy, a crowd to melt into. It takes more than it gives in the end, because each crate under a floor puts a household within reach of the hangman.

## Relations with other factions

- **Hanseatic guilds:** the chief enemy and chief customer; the Hansa's cargo is what the brothers take and its credit is what many members owe.
- **Danish Crown:** the toll and the castle's justice are an enemy at a distance; [Konrad Preen](../../CHARACTERS/konrad_preen.md)'s men have little sea reach and the brothers are happy to keep it so.
- **Black Cloaks:** tolerated and used; they buy blades and cloth, but the brothers do not share their anger, and the two groups look on each other with contempt. [Martin](../../CHARACTERS/martin_black_cloaks.md) takes what is offered and pays in turn.
- **Harju Kings:** a customer for arms, but a dear one; [Lembit](../../CHARACTERS/lembit.md)'s people want a cause and the brothers want the coin.
- **Livonian Order:** the enemy that has not arrived; after the rising the Order will bring a harbour that does not forgive.
- **Pskov / Novgorod:** peers in the dark trade, since wax and furs ride the same ships.
- **Cult of Metsik:** sailors leave a coin or a fish at old shore stones, and nobody mocks that.
- **Church:** confession, a candle at St Olaf, a chaplain who asks nothing at the door.

## Tensions inside

- **Caution against greed.** [Jaan Alberti poeg](../people/lower_town/jaan_alberti_poeg.md) wants a quiet second ship and fewer searches; [Priidik Mikku poeg](../people/kalarand/priidik_mikku_poeg.md) wants a bigger haul while the town is distracted.
- **Craftsmen against carriers.** [Bengt Magnusson](../people/lower_town/bengt_magnusson.md) and [Erik Andersson](../people/lower_town/erik_andersson.md) have homes, masters and guild fraternities to lose; Tilman has nothing, and sees the townsmen as cowards who live off the work of others.
- **Debt against belonging.** [Anna Gunnarsdotter](../people/lower_town/anna_gunnarsdotter.md) and Helga Torgilsdotter, in rich houses, risk their places for others' goods; Gunhild Ulfsdotter feeds the brothers and would not be called one.

## Spring 1343 timeline

**Before 23 April.** The ice is out and the first spring ships have arrived. Orders for blades and plain blankets rise; Erik sells carefully; Bengt sees restless men at the quay; Jaan finds more searches and fewer cargoes. Priidik counts the hire-lists and hears rumours of a rising.

**The night.** The brothers do not storm anything. Priidik's loft is full, the quay is dark, and Tilman has a berth ready. When the Black Cloaks and the Harju fires start, the brotherhood moves goods and watches the gates.

**The siege weeks.** Gates close and the harbour tightens. The Order's men are not yet in the town, but prices rise and every strongbox is wanted. Bengt hides cloth; Jaan wonders whether to sail; Anna and Helga hear arguments in houses that will not be the same again; Gunhild's kitchen door opens more often after curfew.

## Outside the walls

Outside the census, plausible-composite estimates only: perhaps 30 to 60 seamen on the Gotland and Gulf of Finland coast, of whom a dozen or so use the small landings along the Harju shore (Kopli, Paljassaare and Naissaar are the plausible places) in the ice-free months. They have no fleet, only a few open boats and borrowed cogs.

## Game hooks

- **Ledger events that raise standing:** forging an unmarked blade or hook for the quay; holding a crate without asking; warning a skipper of a search. **Lower:** reporting a locker to the Rat; disabling a harbour lock for the Hansa; naming a fence to a bailiff.
- **Barks:** "Who asked you what is in the crate?" "The tide does not read bills." "Silver by weight, and no talk."
- **Quest seeds:** a skipper asks for a second lock on a hold; a porter's crate must cross the Coastal Gate before dawn; a bolt of cloth has a Visby mark that must be dyed out; a maid begs Kalev to mend a kitchen latch and says nothing of why.
- **Reputation reactions:** at high standing, quay hands step aside and a skipper offers a berth; at low standing, a porter spits in the road and a lodging door shuts.
- **At -3:** a faction-tied resident says, "I do not know you, smith, and neither does the tide." **At +3:** "If you need a hold, a hook or a quiet landing, ask on the quay and say I sent you."
