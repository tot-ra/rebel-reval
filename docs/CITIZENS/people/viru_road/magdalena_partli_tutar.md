## Biography
Born in 1328, she was a quiet child, afraid of the dark and fascinated by the wash-yard's old women. At nine she had a fever that left her squint; the neighbours said she had been touched. At twelve she began spinning for the clothier. Last spring a servant at a Vana-Posti house, Hele Toomase tütar, paid her a mark's worth of kindness by giving her bread during a hungry week, and brought her to a gathering after mass. She now leaves offerings at the grove stone beyond the gate and keeps a rowan twig.

## Work and money
She earns about six schilling a month for the clothier and gives it to her mother. She owns a plait-thread, a pouch of herbs and a rowan twig. A bad month means no wool and no wage.

## Faction and belief
A sympathiser of the Cult of Metsik. Her root is a child's fear of a world that feels owned by others; her branch is wonder, not anger. A small favour she would do, a large one she would hesitate over, and informing is unthinkable. She keeps both Christ and the grove, and does not see a quarrel.

She knows the meeting-places and the signals. She would trade it for nothing. She believes the rumour that a spirit will rise from the lime kiln at midsummer.
