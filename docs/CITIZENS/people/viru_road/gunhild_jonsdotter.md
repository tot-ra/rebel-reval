## Biography
Born in 1290 on the Swedish coast at Nyland, daughter of a fisherman called Jon, she married Sven, a butcher, in 1312 and came to Reval with him in the wars' aftermath. They set up outside the Viru gate where slaughter was allowed. Sven died in the cold of 1338 after a cart-wheel crushed his chest, and she kept the trade rather than sell. She laughed a great deal as a young wife; her husband's death and the loss of three infants took that out of her. Now she keeps the household afloat with beasts bought on credit, and sleeps little.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To pass the block and the yard to someone who will not sell it.
- **Fear:** A bad cattle price or a fever in the herd, which would take a year's profit.
- **Contradiction:** Sharp with lodgers about rent, yet cannot let them go hungry.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She owes a Lower Town merchant three marks and has told no one in the household.

## Work and money
She buys beasts from a Harju farm at a mark or two, sells meat by the pfund at four pfennig, and sells tallow and hides to the tanner. A good month clears three marks; a bad one is a loss. She owns the block, the beam, a handcart and the cleaver. Three lodgers pay a few schilling a month between them.

## Faction and belief
No faction. A widow with a household and a debt cannot afford politics. A small favour she grants, a large one she refuses, informing she does not do. She prays to St Olaf, makes a cross on the first carcass of the week and puts salt on the threshold.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Swedish at home, a few Low German words for weights and prices.
- **Delivery:** A flat, tired murmur; she never raises her voice, and her pauses carry weight.
- **Sample lines:** "A pfund is a pfund." / "I do not shout at meat or men." / *Det är vad det är.* (It is what it is.)
- **Verbal tic:** Ends statements with a short breath.

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows which farms are selling their herds early. She would trade it for a fair price on salt. She believes the rumour that the Rat will fix the price of meat for the summer.
