- Big and heavy-set, hired by the day for lime kilns, quays and barrel yards.
- Palms are split and thick with callus, and he cannot close his hands fully.
- Speaks in a monotone, so jokes and threats sound alike.
- A widower; his wife died in the winter of 1341.

## Biography
Born in 1310 in a Jõelähtme village, he came to town at sixteen to avoid a manor's labour service. He has carried lime for the kiln, salt for the quay and beams for the carpenters. He married a girl from the lane in 1334 and buried her in the frost of 1341, along with the child she carried. Anna the midwife sat with him that night, and he has not forgotten. He took a loft bed in Gunhild's house because the empty room was too quiet.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A steady job on one site, not a new one each day.
- **Fear:** An injury that stops him lifting.
- **Contradiction:** Gentle and slow-moving, yet capable of sudden violence if a child is hurt.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He gives a third of his day's wage to a poor widow in the lane and tells nobody.

## Work and money
He earns three to four pfennig a day on a good week and nothing in a wet one. He pays Gunhild two schilling a month for the loft. His savings are four schilling in a knot of cloth. A bad month is rain that stops the quay.

## Faction and belief
No faction. A day labourer is paid by whoever offers, and he has nothing to gain from a quarrel. A small favour he does; a large one he refuses politely; informing he finds incomprehensible. He crosses himself at the lime-kiln and leaves a pinch of lime on his wife's grave.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian only.
- **Delivery:** Monotone, slow, with no emphasis.
- **Sample lines:** "I carry it. I do not ask what is in it." / "The lime burns the hand; the hand burns the lime." / *Kui makstakse, siis kannan.* (If you pay, I carry.)
- **Verbal tic:** Pauses and nods before answering.

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows which yards are hiring and who pays late. He would trade it for a place at a steady site. He believes the rumour that the lime kilns will be shut for fear of fire.
