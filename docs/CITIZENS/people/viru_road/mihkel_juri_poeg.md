## At a glance
- Brews small beer in a tarred tub and sells it by the can to carters, gate guards and thirsty labourers on Viru road.
- A man doing what is usually a woman's trade: he learned it, and kept it, because the household has four daughters and no other income that sits at home.
- Keeps accounts on a hazel tally-stick with notches only he can read.
- Has noticed the boundary stake in his yard has crept, and has said nothing.

## Biography
Born in 1294 in a fishing hamlet near Kopli, the third son of a net-mender, Jüri, sent at twelve to a brewer-master in Sauna street who needed a boy for the water and the grain. That master, long dead now, trained two apprentices who mattered: Mihkel and, later, Toomas Kauri. The master's widow paid Mihkel in malt and a cough, and he went out on his own in 1320 when a cousin rented him the Viru road plot. In the bad winter of 1341 the stored malt froze and the ale sold at a loss. He kept the household off the Holy Spirit alms by boiling cabbage and not complaining. Now he brews twice a week and watches the gate for the carters.

## Motivation
- **Want:** Three dowry chests for the older girls, and one day a licence to sell inside the walls.
- **Fear:** The ale souring in the week before a market. A fire in the brew-shed.
- **Contradiction:** Generous with a can on credit, yet counts every notch twice at night.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He has measured the stake against the yard's corners and knows exactly how many handspans it has been moved. He is waiting to see if Pärtel's household ever needs a favour.

## Work and money
Mihkel buys malt by the lispfund from a carter on the road and hops from a gate-farm. A good week clears about a mark in pfennig and credit; a bad week clears a few schilling. Eeva's tray trade adds the rest. He owes the carter Jaak Lembitu poeg of [hh.vi.viru_09](../../ledger/viru_road/viru_road.md#hh-vi-viru-09) for two months' malt and has promised him ale in kind. Property is the tub, a copper kettle and the right to the yard. A bad month is a frozen mash, an empty tally and four daughters' bread.

## Faction and belief
No faction. Mihkel sells to everyone at the gate, Danish levy men included, and a tub depends on all of them. If asked for a small favour he would give a free can; for a large one he would hesitate; to inform he would not, because informers drink alone. He keeps the hearth rite of pouring the first cup on the floor for the house spirit and then crosses himself at the church door.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian at home and on the road; a few Low German words for coin and measure.
- **Delivery:** Husky and low, as if a night of shouting had worn the middle off it. He says the price before the greeting.
- **Sample lines:** "Two örtug the can, and the foam is free." / "Pay Friday; I have chalked it already." / *Ära kiirusta, õlu ei põgene.* (Do not hurry, the ale will not run away.)
- **Verbal tic:** Ends sentences with "and so."

## Knowledge and rumours
He hears the carters' news before the Rat does: who is hauling grain out, which manors are quiet. He would trade it for malt at cost. He believes the rumour that the Danish viceroy will put a tax on every tub inside and outside the walls.
