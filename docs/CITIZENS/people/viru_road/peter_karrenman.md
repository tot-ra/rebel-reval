- Carries out the Rat's sentences: hangings, floggings, the breaking wheel, and the clearing of dead beasts.

- Rents a shed to a smith, and the rent is the only transaction in which anyone treats him as an equal.

## Biography
Born in 1309 in Reval, the son of the previous executioner, Peter was raised in the suburb by an Estonian washerwoman, which is why his German carries an Estonian lilt. At fifteen he took his father's sword; at twenty-five he married Gyla, a retailer's daughter whose family needed a husband for her. The crooked shin came from a carthorse in 1335. He is paid a fixed sum by the Rat for each sentence and a tariff for each dead beast. The suburb is the only place that will have him.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To see his sons apprenticed to anything but his own trade.
- **Fear:** A botched stroke before a crowd, and what it would mean for his family.
- **Contradiction:** A gentle man in a bloody trade; he feeds stray dogs.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He prays at the church door for every man he has hanged, naming them quietly.

## Work and money
He gets a fee per sentence, a tariff for dead beasts, and rent. A mark for a hanging, a few schilling for a flogging. He rents a shed to Antti Anttanpoika for four schilling a quarter. A bad month is a quiet one, because the Rat pays less. He owns the sword, a rope-store and the right to the plot.

## Faction and belief
No faction. His office makes him a servant of whoever holds the Rat, and he wants no other loyalty. A small favour he does quietly; a large one he cannot, as he would be hanged; informing is not asked of him. He keeps a rosary he never uses in public.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Middle Low German with a clear Estonian lilt, Estonian with his children.
- **Delivery:** Sing-song and gentle, never loud.
- **Sample lines:** "I do as I am bid." / "The dogs are fed; the rest can wait." / *Jumal halasta.* (God have mercy.)
- **Verbal tic:** Looks at the ground when someone meets his eye.

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows who has been sentenced and for what. He would trade it for a promise to leave his sons alone. He believes the rumour that the Rat will abolish hanging for the wheel.
