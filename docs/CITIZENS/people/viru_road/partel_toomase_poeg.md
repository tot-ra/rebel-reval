## At a glance
- A round-bellied, rosy, bearded carpenter who works with his left hand and talks in a flat voice.
- Supplies the suburb's smiths with ash handles, bellows boards and wood for charcoal.
- Holds back stock when he thinks a debtor is slow, and has done it twice to the same man.
- His father moved a boundary stake between his yard and the alewife's; he knows it.

## Biography
Born in 1279 in a village near Rae, he was apprenticed at ten to a carpenter at the Viru gate, who beat him with a plane. He took over the shop at thirty and married Elsa in 1306. Their son died in 1326 of a lung fever, a loss he does not discuss. His father, long dead, had moved a boundary stake in the yard when the plot was divided; the new man, Mihkel, has noticed. Pärtel prefers to keep the peace and not to dig up old business. Now he works from dawn to dusk, with two daughters and a wife to feed.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To leave his daughters a plot with a clear boundary and a trade for the younger one.
- **Fear:** A sudden fire in the timber yard, or losing the use of his left hand.
- **Contradiction:** Tight-fisted about stock but generous with advice.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He suspects his father moved the stake on purpose, and has never measured it to find out.

## Work and money
Pärtel buys ash and oak from gate-farms and carters, and charcoal-wood by the load from a woodcutter. He sells handles at two pfennig, bellows boards at four schilling and rough planks by the length. A good month clears two marks; a bad one is under half a mark when a smith delays payment. He is owed about a mark by the smiths, and owes the woodcutter Laurents Mihkli poeg nothing, because he pays on delivery.

## Faction and belief
No faction. Pärtel's trade depends on the smiths, the carters and the gate-farms, and he cannot afford a side. For a small favour he would loan a plank; for a large one he would count the cost; to inform he would never, since he would lose customers. He has a carved sun-wheel on the lintel and goes to mass without argument.

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows what every smith in the suburb is ordering and which spear-shafts are in demand. He would trade it for prompt payment. He believes the rumour that the Rat will ban timber sales outside the walls.
