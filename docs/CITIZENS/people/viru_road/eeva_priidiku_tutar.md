## At a glance
- Walks the lanes with a shallow wooden tray on a neck strap, selling pins, needles, thread, salt twists and tallow dips.
- Counts coin by feel: she can tell a Lübeck pfennig from a clipped one in the dark.
- Mother of four girls and wife of an alewife; she is the household's steady silver.
- Has a way of looking past a customer's shoulder, so people feel they are being measured.

## Biography
Born in 1298 in a Lasnamäe village, Eeva came to town at fourteen as a helper to a retail widow who sold small wares from a tray at the Sand gate. That widow taught her to count by touch, to spot a false weight and to keep a smile that costs nothing. She married Mihkel in 1326, at the time a poor man with a tub and a good name. After the first daughter lived she took the tray out again because the beer alone would not pay. Her mother died in the winter of 1341, and she paid for the burial with a month of pfennig. Today she works six days a week and keeps a little hoard sewn into the hem of a winter gown.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A stall at the Lower Town market, with a board and a name, instead of a tray.
- **Fear:** A fever running through the girls; being turned away by a German housewife in front of a neighbour.
- **Contradiction:** Hates haggling, yet loses sleep if she has not made the day's mark.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She skims a few pfennig a week into a hoard Mihkel does not know about. It is for Gertrud's dowry, because Mihkel would spend it on malt.

## Work and money
She buys small wares on credit from a Lower Town merchant's agent and repays weekly. A good week clears three or four schilling; a bad one is a loss of a schilling from damp salt. Mihkel's ale brings a mark on a good week. She owes the agent about a mark and a half and lends two schilling to neighbours at no interest, which she remembers.

## Faction and belief
No faction; a hawker's survival depends on every household's door being open to her, so she keeps her opinions in her tray. A small favour she grants, a large one she refuses politely, and informing is not in her nature because it would end her customers. She wears a juniper sprig on the tray strap against the evil eye and goes to mass at St Olaf.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian everywhere, a few Low German phrases for the trade.
- **Delivery:** Thin and reedy, fast, rising at the end of every price.
- **Sample lines:** "Pins, three for a pfennig, and a needle for the pretty one." / "I never said that; I said it was good salt." / *Hoia käsi taskus, tänav on pime.* (Keep your hand in your pocket, the street is dark.)
- **Verbal tic:** "Mind you," before every price.

## Knowledge and rumours
She hears kitchen talk in a dozen houses: who is pregnant, who is in debt, whose master is selling off wool. She would trade it for a better pitch. She believes that the Order's men will come in with the first spring ships and take the city.
