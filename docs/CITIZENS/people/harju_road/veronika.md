## Biography
Born 1280 in a Harju village, married at seventeen to a drover named Mihkel and walked the herds with him from 1297. The great cattle plague of 1332 killed half the herds and Mihkel with them. She took over his licence and his stick.

She has since lost two nephews to the manor's binding, the second one this Lent. She keeps one sheep, one hen, and a servant, and walks the Harju road twice a week.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To live long enough to see her nephews' children free of the manor.
- **Fear:** The reeve who now asks to see her licence.
- **Contradiction:** Slow, mild, and tired, with a stubbornness that has cost her three fines.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She carries word between two manor villages and the lane, in her head, with the herd.

## Work and money
She drives other men's beasts for a pfennig a head and buys a few of her own. A good drive brings twenty pfennigs; a bad road brings a fine. She owes the maltster a pfund of salt. Ruth the servant is paid in board and a coat at Michaelmas.

## Faction and belief
Harju Kings, secret sympathiser. Her root is the manor's binding of her nephews. Her branch is arithmetic: she counts those taken, and the count is rising. A small favour she will do, a large one if no child is near; informing she will not do. She keeps a bundle of grain at the doorstep for the beasts' saint and prays at St Olaf's.

## Knowledge and rumours
Knows which manor fields are fenced and which villages are bound. Believes, falsely, that the Danes will buy the bound men free.
