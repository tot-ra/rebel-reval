## At a glance
- Stooped, thin, white-haired, with a voice that carries across the lane despite his age.

- Mends wheels no more, yet every carter asks his opinion of a cracked felloe.
- Passes what he hears, never what he sees.

## Biography
Born about 1273 in a Harju village, he walked to Reval at thirty-one with his young son Tõll, after the manor doubled the labour days. He apprenticed late to a Lower Town wheelwright, worked at the Smiths' Gate for thirty years, and in 1313 took a flying spoke over the left eye that left him the crescent scar and a story he tells wrongly on purpose. His wife died in 1329. He stopped work in 1338 when his hands gave out.

Now he mends rope, minds the little ones, and watches the lane.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To live to see the Harju road free of the manor's reeve and the gate toll.
- **Fear:** Dying indoors; his son's neck in a rope for his talk.
- **Contradiction:** He tells the children to keep their mouths shut, and has never done so himself.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He does not give names, only what carters said about levies and watch times; he thinks this makes him honest.

## Work and money
No income; his son feeds him. He earns a few pfennigs a month mending rope and wheel-spokes for neighbours, which pays for his one comfort: a salt herring and a pot of ale at Easter. He owes nobody and is owed a favour by half the carters.

## Faction and belief
Black Cloaks, informer. His root is the 1304 labour doubling. His branch is not hatred but habit: he has always listened and now it matters. He would do a small favour readily, a large one if the children were out of sight; informing is what he does. He prays to St Olaf and tips a drop of ale for the house-spirit.

## Knowledge and rumours
Knows which carts cross the gate and when the watch changes. Believes, falsely, that the Order will arrive before May.
