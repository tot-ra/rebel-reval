## Biography
Born in 1264 in a hamlet near the Harju road, Evert came into the harbour at twelve and served as a caulker's boy. In the wars of 1313 to 1325 he built the rafts that took the sick from the shore. His wife died in 1320; his daughter [Mari](../../people/harju_road/mari.md) took him in. He taught her to knot and her son Andres to row. Andres drowned in 1337; Evert was on the mole when they pulled him out, and has never since gone near the water.

Now he sits on a bench, whittles and listens. Under the bench, in a crack of the wall, is a little bag of pebbles: one for every manor in Harju he has heard has been bound.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To see the great-grandchildren grow past the harbour and the manor.
- **Fear:** Dying with the Harju folk still bound.
- **Contradiction:** He is afraid of fighting and quietly cheers each rumour of it.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He once built a boat for a man who later joined the Kings, and has never told Mari.

## Faction and belief
A secret sympathiser of the Harju Kings because his own father was bound to a manor before he fled to town. A small favour, holding a bundle, he gives; a large one he leaves to the young; informing he will never do. He believes in St Nicholas of sailors and in the sea's patience.

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows tides, boats and who owns which hull. He would trade it for news of the manors. Rumour he believes: that the Danish king will send ships to end the rising.
