## At a glance
- Sturdy, olive-fair woman with a cheerful, sing-song cadence even in German.
- Sells small beer from the doorway; a bush on a pole means a fresh brew.
- Keeps a mental tally of which neighbours owe her for the last two batches.
- Does not know that her husband is anything but a farmer, and has decided that is how it will stay.

## Biography
Born 1312 in a coast village, Berta was sent at twelve to scrub for a brewer in the Lower Town. She learned the mash and the hops, and in 1334 married Olev and took the brewing with her. Her mother Ede came with them. Magdalena was born in 1337.

The winter of 1340 taught her to keep a barrel in reserve and a candle in the window. She brews twice a week, and has outlasted two alewives on the same street.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A licence to brew for the council's feast days.
- **Fear:** A bad batch that sours, a fine from the Vogt.
- **Contradiction:** She is the friendliest woman in the lane and the best-informed on who owes what.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She has seen Olev sneak out after curfew and has decided it is for a woman, which is easier to bear than the truth.

## Work and money
Sells small beer at a pfennig the quart and a stronger ale at two. Malt comes from a maltster at the Cattle gate, hops from the market. A good week adds about two schillings to the household. She owes three mark-weight of silver to the maltster, which she repays at quarter-days.

## Faction and belief
None. She has seen enough levy men to want none of them. A small favour she will give for a pfennig; a large one she would refuse. She would never inform. She leaves a cup of the first brew on the hearth for the house-spirit and also says an Ave.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian at home; Low German for customers, with a sing-song under it.
- **Delivery:** Lilting, friendly, quick, ends phrases on a rise.
- **Sample lines:** "A fresh barrel, tasted this morning." / "You owe me two pfennigs, dear, not that I'm counting." / "Võta, võta, see on hea." (Take it, it's good.)
- **Verbal tic:** "Dear."

## Knowledge and rumours
Knows who drinks too much and who is in debt. Believes, falsely, that Elisabet waters her ale.
