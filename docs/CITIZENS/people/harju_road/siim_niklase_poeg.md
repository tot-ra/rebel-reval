## Biography
Born 1304 on the road, son of a mill-hand named Niklas. He learned grinding at ten from a German miller at the Cattle gate and in 1330 took over a horse-mill shed on Harju road when the owner died of the flux. He married Ell in 1327 and has four living children.

The winter of 1340 taught him that bakers pay late and the poor pay never. He carried Olev's feverish girl to the herb-wife that winter and remembers the night every time he sees her.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To buy the shed outright, not rent it.
- **Fear:** A short harvest and bakers who stop paying.
- **Contradiction:** He guards weight like a priest, and has twice held back sacks to make a point.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He knows Ell leaves the house on certain nights, and has chosen not to ask where.

## Work and money
Takes one measure in sixteen as toll, which he sells. A good week brings about three schillings. Rye comes from the gate-farms; he delivers flour to the town bakers and takes bread in return. The horse costs a mark a year to feed. He owes the horse's seller four schillings.

## Faction and belief
None. His root is bread, not banners. A small favour he gives in flour; a large one never; informing he would do only to protect his children. He lights a candle at St Olaf's and ties a red thread on the horse's collar.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian at home; Low German for baker's weights.
- **Delivery:** High and quick, rising on numbers.
- **Sample lines:** "A sack is a sack, the weight is the weight." / "I sent flour Tuesday, ask anyone." / "Ma ei unusta." (I don't forget.)
- **Verbal tic:** "By weight."

## Knowledge and rumours
Knows how much rye each baker really bakes. Believes, falsely, that the council will fix the milling toll.
