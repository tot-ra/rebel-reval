## At a glance
- Slight, ruddy, left-handed, with a nasal voice that carries across a wash-yard.
- Sells small things she makes from her trade: lye, soap balls, a cure for linen mould.
- Knows whose linen is stained with what, and says nothing.
- Odd: she trained under a master who also trained a Toompea washerwoman, and that connection has never once been used.

## Biography
Made was born in 1307 in a fishing hamlet east of the town and sent at eleven to learn washing from a master on Pikk jalg, a woman whose name is gone but whose method is not: boil, beat, rinse in running water, dry on the wall. The same master later took on Bodil, who went on to wash for the Toompea households.

Made married Tõll in 1325 and has borne five children; the first Margareta died as an infant in 1340, and Simon nearly followed. She learned that winter that the cheapest medicine is a clean sheet.

She now takes in washing from three Lower Town households and a baker, and her days are set by the basin, the line, and the baby.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To set aside two marks for a proper door and shutter before next winter.
- **Fear:** Lye on a child's hands; Simon's cough in the night.
- **Contradiction:** She dislikes owing anyone, yet keeps one old courtesy unclaimed for nine years because collecting it would mean asking.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She knows her father-in-law burns something in the hearth on certain nights; she says nothing, and washes his sleeves.

## Work and money
Charges a pfennig a sheet and two for a gown. A good week brings about nine schillings in work and tallow-soap; she buys potash, tallow and wood-ash from the nearby brickmakers and the butcher on Harju road. Her cheap goods undercut the shops in the town. One mark owed to a Pikk household for last winter's rye; she will pay it off by midsummer if the baby stays well.

## Faction and belief
None. She wants nobody's attention. She would carry a bundle if asked small; she would not hide a man; she would not inform on a neighbour, and would not say why. A churchgoer, she also pours the last wash-water on the doorstep for the house luck, as her mother did.

## Knowledge and rumours
Knows which Lower Town households have fever from the linen they send out. Believes, falsely, that the council will tax the wash-yards at the stream.
