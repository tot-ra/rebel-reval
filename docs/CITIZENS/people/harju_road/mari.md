## At a glance
- She is the woman who takes your horse at the yard by the Smiths' gate and never says more than three words, none of them a question.
- Known for a hand that calms any animal and a face that flushes to the ears when a carter tries to haggle.
- Oddity: an illiterate widow who can recite from memory the loads, names and moods of every wagon that passed the gate since Candlemas.

## Biography
Mari was born in 1287 in a hamlet by the Harju road, daughter of the boat-builder Evert, and came into town at fourteen when the family followed his work to the harbour. She married a carter, Jaan, who died in 1330 when a load of casks slid on the hill below the Smiths' gate. Her daughter Aino died in childbed in 1328 and her son Andres in the autumn of 1337, drowned off the harbour mole; she raised their children, [Triin Aino tütar](../../people/harju_road/triin_aino_tutar.md) and [Madis Andrese poeg](../../people/harju_road/madis_andrese_poeg.md), and keeps her old father, [Evert Marteni poeg](../../people/harju_road/evert_marteni_poeg.md), under the same roof.

The winter of 1338 was the one that made her. The yard's master, a burgher she calls only "the master", cut her wage to meat-scraps; a woman from the grove, who had sat by Andres's death, brought barley and a promise. Mari has been the grove's ear since then. Now she lives in her father's old plot, mucks out the master's stalls, and keeps what she hears.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To keep the shed-room roof dry and the two children fed until Triin is married and Madis has a trade.
- **Fear:** The master turning her out; Evert dying hard in winter; being named as the one who talks.
- **Contradiction:** She hates being treated as a debtor, yet she repays the grove's barley with information because nothing else she owns has value.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She has never told the grove who her father is leaning toward, because she suspects, and does not want to know.

## Work and money
She earns about three öre a month in coin and a bowl of meal at harvest; carters tip her a pfennig for a well-rubbed horse. Rent on the plot is paid in labour to the master, who treats a shed-room as a favour. Tools: a hoof-pick, a currycomb with three teeth missing, a leather bucket. She counts the yard's lamp-oil and the number of farm-hands at [Thomas Overdyk](../../people/lower_town/thomas_overdyk.md)'s stable in her head every night. A bad month is a lame horse she did not catch.

## Faction and belief
Mari is an informer, not a believer in the cult's creed. She goes to the grove because the grove fed her when the church poor-box did not. A small favour she gives gladly; a large one (hiding a runaway) she refuses; informing is the only thing she does, and she does it carefully, never repeating a thing she has not heard twice. She prays at St Olaf and hangs a rowan twig above the stable door.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian only; she understands a little Low German from carters but never answers in it.
- **Delivery:** Low and unhurried, never raised; she speaks to horses at the same pace as to people.
- **Sample lines:** "Leave him with me. He kicks left." / "Three loads for the Viru gate and none for the Smiths'. Odd, that." / "Ma ei tea." (I do not know.)
- **Verbal tic:** Ends statements with "so it goes", flatly.

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows which merchants' carters carry what and which ones are asked to carry nothing. She would trade it for a roof beam or for the promise that Madis is taken as an apprentice. Rumour she believes: that the Danish viceroy has a cart of silver waiting to hire Livonian mercenaries.
