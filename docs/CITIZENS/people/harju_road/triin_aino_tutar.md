## Biography
Triin was born in 1328 on the Harju road, the daughter of Aino, who died in childbed bringing her into the world. Her father is a name nobody says; Mari, her grandmother, raised her. She learned to spin at six, because wool was the one thing the household could afford, and by ten she could out-spin women twice her age. At thirteen she overheard two carters talk of a rising in the manors, and she followed them to a lane by the harbour where a pilot named Kaspar was sitting on an upturned boat; he asked her only whether she could remember a list.

Now she carries lists. Nobody in the household knows, least of all her grandmother. Her great-grandfather [Evert Marteni poeg](../../people/harju_road/evert_marteni_poeg.md) lets her pass in the dark without a word, which she takes for sleep.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To matter; to be the girl who carried the word that mattered on the night it counts.
- **Fear:** Being married off to a farmer of the Harju manors, bound to the soil and a stranger.
- **Contradiction:** She wants freedom for everyone like her but hates the idea of the leaders ordering her about.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** Her errands are paid in coin, a few pfennigs, which she hides under a loose stone behind the hearth.

## Work and money
She spins a hank of fine thread in two days and earns about two pfennigs for it; a good week is a schilling. Wool comes from the weavers on credit and is paid for by the hank. The rest of her coin comes from the errands, which she never mentions. A bad month is a damp fleece that will not take the spindle and a weaver who docks her wage.

## Faction and belief
Triin is active because the manor boys at the market told her what binding means: a lord's name on a boy's neck. A small favour is easy; a large one terrifies and excites her; informing she would never do. She holds to St Olaf's saints and the rowan over the door, nothing deeper.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian only; she hears Low German at the market and mimics it for a laugh.
- **Delivery:** Low and unhurried, even when excited; she speaks in short phrases like a list.
- **Sample lines:** "Two roads: Viru and the shore. Count them." / "Thread first, talk later." / "Ma mäletan." (I remember.)
- **Verbal tic:** Counts on her fingers before answering.

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows the gates by the names of their guards and which of them is bribed with beer. She would trade it for a place on the night's work. Rumour she believes: that the Harju Kings have ten thousand men in the forest.
