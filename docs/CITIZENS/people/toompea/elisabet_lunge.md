## Biography
Born 1307 in a Wiek manor village to a Danish steward's family; her father was dismissed in 1319 for a disputed grain-tally and died poor. She spun for a living in Reval from fifteen, married Niels Rud in 1334 and brought her own spinning custom into his workshop. She bears three children and tends her father-in-law Torben.

Her reason for the Order is her father's dismissal: a crown steward ruined him over eight barrels, and the crown has paid her nothing since for the shame. A Livonian merchant buying thread told her last year that the Order remembers such things.

## Motivation
- **Want:** Her father's name cleared, and her daughters well married.
- **Fear:** The crown learning her sympathies; a bad flax year.
- **Contradiction:** A Danish crown dependant by marriage, an Order friend by memory.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She carries a coin-weight bearing a small cross to a Livonian merchant each month, hidden in a thread bundle.

## Work and money
She earns about 15 marks a year from thread sold at 3 örtug a skein. Flax comes from Harju women at a premium; wool from a Lower Town dealer. She owes 2 marks to the flax-wife and is owed 5 by two knights' households. A bad month is one without orders.

## Faction and belief
Her sympathy for the Order is memory, not hope: she thinks the Order keeps accounts. A small favour she gives (carrying a bundle); a larger one only if her children were safe; informing she would refuse. She keeps St Elisabeth's day and a candle for her father.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Danish at home; Low German at market.
- **Delivery:** Booming, fast, ends each phrase on a rise.
- **Sample lines:** "Three örtug, and the price of flax is God's doing." / "Good morning, Knud; onions up again?" / "My father kept accounts too."
- **Verbal tic:** Says "As I say" before everything.

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows each household's thread orders and so who is expecting a child or a wedding. She would trade it for a name cleared. She believes, falsely, that the Order never taxes the poor.
