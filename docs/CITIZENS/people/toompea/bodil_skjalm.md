## Biography
Born in 1286 in Roskilde, the daughter of a tanner, Bodil did rough laundry at the cathedral from nine and came to Reval with her husband Niels in 1311. She washed only for her own house until 1320, when she went to the Pikk jalg master, the same who had taught young Made of the Harju road, and learned boil, beat and rinse in running water; she still uses the method.

Two things made her. In 1319 she buried a child, and found that work was the only grief that could be done in public. In 1338 she learned of her husband's debt and, saying nothing, took in the castle's linen as well; she now washes for four households. She is the Crown's active hand in the lane because her baskets pass every door.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To clear Niels's debt before a creditor comes to the door.
- **Fear:** A fire in the drying-loft; her children's names on a debt roll.
- **Contradiction:** A woman who says almost nothing and sets down every word like a coin; she hates to be owed, yet quietly lives in debt to an old courtesy.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She knows exactly what the castle linen smells of after a council meeting, and has told the circle twice.

## Work and money
She charges a pfennig a sheet and two for a gown; with the castle's pay she earns ten schillings a week and uses it for the larder and the instalments. She buys lye and tallow from a Harju road washerwoman at a fair price. A rival washerwoman on Kohtu accuses her of undercutting. A bad month is a wet week.

## Faction and belief
Active for the Crown because she was fed by it in the hard years and her husband is its servant. For a small favour she would carry a folded cloth; for a large one, hide a person in a basket; to inform, she already does. She prays to St Anne and puts a pinch of salt in the copper.

## Voice

## Knowledge and rumours
Knows what each household's linen says. She would trade it for a debt cancelled. She believes falsely that the castle well makes cloth yellow.
