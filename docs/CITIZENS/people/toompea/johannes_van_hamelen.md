## Biography
Born in 1291 in Hameln, the son of a ferryman, he learned his plainsong from a village schoolmaster who beat time on his head. He came to Reval in 1320 on a canon's invitation to sing in the new choir. For twenty years he has stood in the lower stalls, hired for each anniversary mass, never given a stall of his own. His bitten nails began the winter his mother died in Hameln and he could not go. Now he sings, drinks a little, and counts the masses he is owed pay for.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A permanent vicar's stall with a house and a share of the grain rents.
- **Fear:** Losing his voice, or his hearing of it.
- **Contradiction:** He is the most tender singer in the choir and the stingiest about his own pennies.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He copies and sells mass-books' marginal songs in secret to a Lower Town merchant's wife.

## Work and money
Each anniversary mass brings about two pfennigs from the family and a loaf. Twelve masses a month make about two schillings. The chapter owes him eleven schillings back pay. A bad month is Lent's end with no endowments.

## Faction and belief
Church, through habit and music. A small favour he does if it costs nothing; a large one he does for a promised stall; informing he refuses. He crosses himself at thresholds and carries a sprig of rowan in his sleeve.

## Voice

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows every anniversary the chapter owes and which families have stopped paying. He would trade it for a stall. He believes, wrongly, that the canons plan to sell the choir books.
