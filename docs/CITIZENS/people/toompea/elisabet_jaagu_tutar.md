## Biography
Born in 1288 at a coastal village near Rotalia, the daughter of Jaak, a fisherman. She came to Reval during the 1313-1319 wars when her village burned and was taken into the castle kitchens as a scullion at twenty-five. She has never left.

Two things made her. In 1322 she nursed the viceroy's infant heir through a fever and was promoted, with the baby's mother's gratitude, to nurse. When the boy grew up and became a squire, she kept her place on the back stairs. And in 1330 she lost her only son to a fever; the castle buried him in the servants' corner of St Mary's yard, and she has been loyal to the walls that hold the grave ever since.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To be buried beside her son and have a mass said every year.
- **Fear:** A new viceroy who brings his own women.
- **Contradiction:** An Estonian peasant's daughter, she is the Crown's most devoted servant, yet sings the old hearth songs to the scullery girls.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She passes the circle's slips to the next person by a loose brick in the pantry wall, and has done so for eight years.

## Work and money
She earns five marks a year plus board and gowns; the pantry keys earn her a small perquisite of candle-ends and meat bones, which she sells to the Lower Town. She has seven marks sewn in her mattress. A bad month is a scolding from the housekeeper and a missed loaf.

## Faction and belief
Core, though she would not say "core". For a small favour she would lose a visitor's name; for a large one, lock the pantry on a command; to inform, she is the one who is informed. She keeps the hearth rite of pouring the first milk on the stone, and goes to mass with an easy conscience.

## Knowledge and rumours
Knows which servants are clever and which are cruel. She would trade it for a say in who is hired. She believes falsely that the old viceroy is still alive in a cell.
