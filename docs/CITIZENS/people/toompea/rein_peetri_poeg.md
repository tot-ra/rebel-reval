## Biography
Born in 1295 in a Harju village, son of the church's bell-boy at a wooden chapel, he was brought to the cathedral at twelve to ring and sweep. He took the tonsure of minor orders as door-keeper in 1320 so that the chapter could pay him a servant's wage. He counts by notches, never letters, and has buried three canons, a viceroy's child and a hundred townsfolk. His wife died in 1336 in the bad winter; he keeps her wooden spoon in his hood. He lives in a cell by the bell loft.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A proper grave plot for himself beside the north wall.
- **Fear:** A bell rope breaking during a lord's funeral.
- **Contradiction:** He is cold about the dead and weeps at a clean cassock.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He keeps a notched stick of every grave-fee skimmed by the vicars.

## Work and money
He earns about eight marks a year plus burial fees, bell money and candle ends. Wax and oil cost the chapter more than his wage. A bad month is one with no funerals.

## Faction and belief
Church by employment and habit, and he also leaves a bit of bread under the churchyard elder for the old dead. A small favour he does at once; a large one for a fee; informing he does honestly if asked by a canon. He crosses himself twice at the north door.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian with villagers and his own people, Middle Low German with canons.
- **Delivery:** Clipped and precise, short sentences, counts aloud.
- **Sample lines:** "Seven candles, not eight." / "The grave is two ells deep, no more." / "Kell on juba löönud."
- **Verbal tic:** "Exactly so."

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows who is buried where and who paid. He would trade a name for a favour. He believes, wrongly, that a wolf-dog haunts the churchyard on feast eves.
