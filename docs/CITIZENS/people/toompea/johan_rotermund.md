## Biography
Born 1276 on a Harju fief, he served King Eric's levies and later Danish banners until 1325, in sieges and skirmishes whose names he drops one by one. The scar came at a ford in 1317 and the nose at a ditch fight in 1321. He married Fenne Gude in 1321, and they have two sons living.

He was promised a better fief for his service and never received it; the steward who promised it died, and the clerk who held the record says no such record exists. Since then he has turned his face toward the Order, which keeps accounts.

## Motivation

## Work and money
His fief yields about 20 marks a year; he spends 25. His wife's spinning and the sons' service make up the gap. He owes 12 marks to a Lübeck merchant against next harvest. A bad month is a missed rent, and a cellar of unsold barrels.

## Faction and belief
His sympathy for the Order is a grievance with a pedigree: a promise unkept by the crown. A small favour he gives easily; a large one, a cellar; informing, never. He keeps St George's day, as a knight should, and has heard the old women talk of oaks.

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows which castle men have fiefs unpaid, and so might be turned. He would trade it for a fief for Marquard. He believes, falsely, that the Order would come with a fair hand.
