## At a glance
- A small, quick canon whose high voice carries across the cathedral yard.
- Keeps the fabric roll: lime, timber, lead for the roof, wages for masons.
- Oddity: a man who cannot hold his tongue, in a house where the walls have ears.

## Biography
Born in Wismar in 1295 to a ship-chandler, he came to Reval with a bishop's clerk in 1318 and has loved masonry ever since. The scaffold fall of 1331 left him with the nose and a lasting respect for ropes. He took charge of the fabric roll in 1335 and now argues with lime-burners about prices. He is generous, sociable and indiscreet: he repeats what he hears at supper because he thinks all men are his friends.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To finish the cathedral's north roof before he dies.
- **Fear:** A falling beam, or being thought a fool.
- **Contradiction:** He mistrusts the Danish officers and tells their servants so.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He has quietly lent chapter lime to a Lower Town builder against a private promise.

## Work and money
The fabric fund brings in about sixty marks a year from tithes and bequests; he spends it in lime, timber and iron. His prebend is thirty marks. A bad month is when a lime-burner's cart does not arrive.

## Faction and belief
Church, and the cathedral fabric above all. A small favour he does gladly; a large one he does if it keeps the roof on; informing he would never do deliberately, though he does so by accident. He keeps a rye cross over his bed.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Latin for office, Middle Low German with masons.
- **Delivery:** High and quick, rising when he is excited, overlapping other speakers.
- **Sample lines:** "Lime does not lie, brother." / "They say the levy is due after the feast, did you hear?" / "Wat schall ik seggen?"
- **Verbal tic:** "Between ourselves."

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows which walls are cracked and which masons drink. He would trade it for timber at fair price. He believes, wrongly, that the viceroy will tax chapter stone.
