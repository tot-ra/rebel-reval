- Thin, stooped, olive-fair, with protruding ears and a sword-cut on the jaw; a voice roughened by years of shouting into the quay wind.
- Rides the poorest fief on the hill and the largest house on the street.
- Depends on Lübeck credit for his daughters' bread and the roof.
- Heads a small circle of Hanseatic sympathisers without ever speaking in the same room as them.

## Biography
Born 1276 on a Wiek fief, he served the crown's levies, then spent twenty years as the knight at the Coastal Gate who checked the quay's toll-tallies, hence the hoarse voice. The scar came in 1318. He married late, to Alheid van Bocholt in 1321, and had two daughters when most men have grandchildren.

Fief rents failed in the bad years; in 1337 he took 30 marks on credit from a Lübeck merchant, who still holds a lien on the house. He has never missed an interest payment and his daughters still eat.

## Motivation
- **Want:** Two husbands for his daughters before the lien is called.
- **Fear:** The merchant's patience; dying in a rented house.
- **Contradiction:** A knight of the crown who leans on the town's money; proud and obedient.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** His "circle" is in practice a list of seven names he remembers and has never written.

## Work and money
His fief yields 12 marks, his debts cost him 4. He owes 30 marks to a Lübeck merchant, 6 to a baker and 3 to a saddler. His wife's spinning pays for bread. A bad month is a missed interest-day.

## Faction and belief
He depends on the Hanseatic side as a roof depends on a beam. A small favour is a word; a large one is a lodging; informing for the town he would do, if pressed, which he fears. He keeps St Nicholas and Lübeck's saints.

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows which Lübeck ships carry which letters. He would trade it for a mild extension of credit. He believes, falsely, that the council would pay his debts if he died in its service.
