## Biography
Born in 1278 in Lübeck, the son of a Minden cloth-seller, Ricbod was sent to a Lübeck notary at twelve and came to Reval in 1304 as a clerk. He took service with the Danish bailiff in 1312, kept it through the wars and has never left Rahukohtu.

Two things made him. In 1323 his first wife Alheid died in childbirth, leaving a son, Arend, born late, whom he raised on his own. In 1331 he married Gyse van Lemego, a widow he met at St Nicholas, and made peace with a household of two tongues. The Hanseatic men approached him in the 1330s with a small kindness, a repaid debt, and he has since passed them the tolls.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To retire to Lübeck with Arend married and the debt to the skipper cleared.
- **Fear:** A day when the Danish marshal reads his hand and the Rat reads his silence.
- **Contradiction:** He is warm to everyone on the hill and has been betraying them for a decade; he loves the Danes he deceives.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He believes Arend is the Crown's man, and has said nothing in case it is true.

## Work and money
He earns six marks a year as chief copyist, and the Hanse's small gifts bring another two. He owes the skipper four marks for a favour once done. He buys parchment and wine through a Lower Town merchant. A bad month is a toll reduced by a clerk who reads closely.

## Faction and belief
The Hanse is his city and he keeps it. For a small favour he would drop a figure; for a large one, copy a charter; to inform, he already does. He is a regular communicant at St Nicholas on feast days and holds the Hanse's sober trust.
