## Biography
Born in 1306 in a manor near Viborg, the second son of a knight, Peder was dubbed in 1328 in the Order's wars on the Narva road. A mace glanced off his helm in the same fight and bent his nose; he fought on and was praised.

Two things made him. In 1333 his elder brother died and the family manor passed to a nephew, leaving Peder with a small Estonian fief of eleven hides near Kose and a wish to hold on to it. In 1339 he discovered that the smith and the carters were cheating him by a few lispfund of iron a year; he began to weigh everything. He has since kept scales in his chamber and been a terror to merchants.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A fief he can pass to a son, or a wife with a dowry.
- **Fear:** Being cheated; being laughed at for caring.
- **Contradiction:** A knight who despises merchants but copies their tricks, and who gives alms with the left hand and measures the grain with the right.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He once bought a share of a Lübeck cargo of iron in another man's name, and fears it will be found out.

## Work and money
He draws knight's pay of ten marks a year and the fief's rents of about six marks in grain and fowl. He buys iron bar for horseshoes and mail repair from Lower Town smiths. He once split a bulk delivery of osmund iron with a younger knight to beat the price; the arrangement quietly lapsed after a quarrel over weights. He owes the saddler two marks. A bad month is a short grain delivery from Kose.

## Faction and belief
A Crown man, because the Crown holds his fief. For a small favour he would carry a letter; for a large one, ride out; to inform, he would write it, were he able. A devout hearer of mass who also carries a relic in his purse, a nail said to be from a Lübeck church.

## Knowledge and rumours
Knows what iron costs and who sells short. He would trade it for a safe road to Kose. He believes falsely that the Order will sell Harju back to the Danes.
