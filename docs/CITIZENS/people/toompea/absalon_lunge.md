## Biography
Born in 1292 at a manor in Harju held by a Danish knight, the son of the knight's falconer and an Estonian wet nurse's foster-child; he was nursed by the village woman Mari and learned Estonian before Danish. His father trained him in the mews.

Two things made him. In 1315, in the Order's war, the manor burned and he carried the peregrine out under his coat. In 1330 the viceroy's falconer died and he was promoted, bringing his birds up the hill. He has served the Crown since out of loyalty to the one man who valued the peregrine more than the manor.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To train a falcon fit for the king's own fist before his eyes fail.
- **Fear:** Mews fever; a hawk lost on the wing.
- **Contradiction:** Danish by blood and the Crown's man, but he would rather speak with a Harju beater than with a Danish knight.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He lets a hawk-trapper from the old Estonian village use the mews at night and says nothing.

## Work and money
He is paid five marks a year plus board, and a shilling for each bird trained for a buyer. He buys pigeons from the Lower Town and hood-leather from a saddler. A rival falconer on Rahukohtu takes buyers from him. He has eight marks put by. A bad month is a bird lost to the cold.

## Faction and belief
Loyal to the Crown because it keeps the mews. For a small favour he would lend a bird; for a large one, hide a messenger; to inform, he would, reluctantly. He keeps the old Estonian habit of tying a red thread on the first hawk of spring.

## Voice

- **Delivery:** Sing-song, rising at the ends of clauses, soft near the birds.

## Knowledge and rumours
Knows the routes of every hawking party and the habits of the hawk-trapper. He would trade it for a good bird. He believes falsely that the Estonian forest holds a goshawk that cannot be tamed.
