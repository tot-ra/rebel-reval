## At a glance
- A tall old canon with a husky voice and a high colour that looks like health and is partly cold wind.
- Sits in the second stall nearest the north door and knows who came late to mass.
- Oddity: the chapter's most pious man keeps a small private account of bread that does not appear in the chapter's books.

- **Body:** 175 cm, long-limbed, stooping a little at the neck from decades over a lectern; a long, slow stride that makes younger vicars trot to keep up.

## Biography
Born in 1279 in Lübeck, the third son of a cloth-cutter, he was sent to the cathedral school as the son to spare. He came to Reval in 1304, lived through the hungry Danish levies of the 1320s, and has held his stall since 1318. He buried two bishops' stewards and a chapter dean. The winter of 1341 emptied the alms chest, and he began carrying chapter bread down to the hovel by the wall where a widow lies with a rotting leg. He tells himself the chapter owes it. Now he lives on his prebend, his grain rents, and a small purse of other men's penances.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To die in his stall without the chapter finding the missing loaves.
- **Fear:** A visitation by the bishop's officers, who would count the bread.
- **Contradiction:** He preaches order and shuts his eyes to the viceroy's levies, yet steals alms to do good.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He leaves by the back lane after curfew to carry bread and a rag to the widow under the wall.

- **Spring 1343 disruption:** The viceroy's talk of a grain levy and the nervous watch on the gates make his night walks riskier; he now goes only on dark nights.

## Work and money
His prebend is roughly forty marks a year in grain and rents from three Harju villages, paid mostly in rye. The loaves cost him about three schillings a week, hidden in the kitchen's flour reckoning. A bad harvest cuts the prebend and the alms together.

## Faction and belief
Church to the bone, but as a body of duties, not a party. A small favour he does at once; a large one he weighs against the widow; informing on a neighbour he would refuse, though he would report heresy. He keeps the old habit of touching the lintel against fever.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Latin for office, Middle Low German for everything else.
- **Delivery:** Husky, low, with a long breath before each answer.
- **Sample lines:** "The chapter remembers." / "Gyse, what does the viceroy say of the grain?" / "Dat Brot is nich verlorn."
- **Verbal tic:** "Let us not hurry the matter."

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows the chapter's rent rolls and which villages are behind. He would trade that for a promise that the bishop's visitor will arrive late. He believes, wrongly, that the Order's brothers have already crossed into Harrien.
