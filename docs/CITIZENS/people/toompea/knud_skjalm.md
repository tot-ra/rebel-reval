## At a glance
- Tall for the garrison at 177 cm, lean as a rake, full brown beard going grey, and a thin reedy voice that surprises everyone who hears it.
- Keeps out of every whisper on the hill, and is believed to be dull for it.
- Sends his pay home by any skipper going west, wrapped in a rag knotted three times.
- Odd: he is related, he says, to a vicar choral he has hardly spoken to in ten years.

## Biography
Knud was born in 1306 at a Zealand village near Slagelse, second son of a tenant of a Hvide cousin, which is the only reason he carries the name Skjalm. His father died in the bad harvest of 1316 and his brother took the holding; Knud took the king's shilling at fifteen and was shipped to Reval with a Lübeck cog of recruits in 1321.

Two things made him. In 1327 a fellow soldier was flogged and sent home for carrying a note for a clerk; the note was nothing, but Knud was beside him and has never carried anything since. And in 1334 his younger sister Kirsten was widowed with three small children; he has sent her silver every year ever since. His mother's second cousin married the family of a vicar choral, so there is, he thinks, a Reval kinsman, though neither can name the link.

He now stands the wall on the east face, reads no letters, and keeps one rule: say nothing that could be repeated.

## Motivation
- **Want:** Enough silver to put Kirsten's eldest into a trade and her roof in thatch.
- **Fear:** A list: any list, of any side.
- **Contradiction:** A man of fixed rules who lies readily about his birthplace, giving three different villages depending on the listener.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He has twice been asked to join the Crown's circle and said he had a cough; he is afraid they remember.

## Work and money
About two marks a quarter plus rations; he keeps a mark in six and sends the rest. Shipping the silver costs him a few pfennigs and a cask of ale to the skipper. He owes nothing and is owed nothing. A bad month is a skipper who sails without calling.

## Faction and belief
None, by choice and fear. Not a rebel, not a spy: he would stand a wall but not carry a note. For a small favour he would hold a door; for a large one he would say he saw nothing; to inform, he would walk away. He prays to St Olaf for his sister and leaves a coin for the Danish saint on the cathedral step.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Danish with the garrison; slow Low German with townsfolk.
- **Delivery:** Thin and reedy, quiet, repeated twice.
- **Sample lines:** "I saw nothing; I stand here." / "Three nails and a ribbon, and do not tell my captain." / "Jeg ved intet."

## Knowledge and rumours
Knows the east wall's weak stones and the hour the Harju road empties. He would trade it for a safe berth for his nephew. He believes falsely that the viceroy will pay arrears at midsummer.
