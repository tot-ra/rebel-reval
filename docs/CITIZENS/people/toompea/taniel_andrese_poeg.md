## Biography
Born in 1307 in a Harju manor village east of Reval, the son of Andres, a bonded peasant of a Danish vassal. His father bought his freedom in 1320 with six marks and a cow, and Taniel went up the hill as a spit-boy.

Two things made him. In 1325 he ran away from the manor to avoid a levy and was caught; the vassal's steward flogged his father, and Taniel bought his father's release by labour. The second was the bad harvest of 1337 when the castle kitchens fed him and his mother with scraps for a winter. He now fears that a rising of the Harju kings will bind him again, and works for the Crown with all the energy of a man defending a kitchen.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To be cook-master and to bring his mother, sixty, into the kitchen loft.
- **Fear:** Being returned to a manor; a flogging.
- **Contradiction:** An Estonian, born peasant, yet he has no sympathy for those who ran away; he has too much to lose.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He passes food to a hidden runaway from his old village and does not tell the Crown.

## Work and money
He earns three marks a year plus board and a tunic; he buys spice from a Lower Town merchant and sells grease-drippings to a soap-boiler for a few pfennigs a week. He has two marks buried under the hearth. A bad month is a closed gate and no deliveries.

## Faction and belief
Active for the Crown out of fear of the manor. For a small favour he would send a loaf; for a large one, say who ate with whom; to inform, he would, but only about Estonians he does not know. He pours the first drop of every brew on the hearth-stone for the house spirit.

## Knowledge and rumours
Knows what each Dane and servant eats and who is ill. He would trade it for a good word to the steward. He believes falsely that the viceroy will take the kitchen's flour for the levy.
