## Biography
Born 1284 in Roskilde, apprenticed at twelve, and made master at twenty-four. He followed the crown's saddle orders to Reval in 1335, a year after his son married into the Rutu house. He had served in King Eric's levy during the Estonian troubles of 1315 and saw things he does not describe.

His wife died in 1338, three years after they both took a room in his son's house. He gave the workshop to Niels in 1340, but still sits in the corner and watches.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To see his granddaughter married and his son's debts cleared.
- **Fear:** Dying in a country that is not Denmark; the return of 1315.
- **Contradiction:** A silent man who is the circle's most reliable talker once a week.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He takes a folded scrap to a castle gardener each month and does not know its contents.

## Work and money
He has no income; his son keeps him. He owns a set of old awls, a bone-handled knife and a purse with 6 schillings. He gives his granddaughter a penny at Easter. A bad month is a month he becomes a burden.

## Faction and belief
He serves the crown from habit and fear: the 1315 rising taught him what the Estonians do when the crown is weak. A small favour is as easy as breathing; a large one he does without being asked; informing he does already. He keeps St Olaf's day and a Danish psalm.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Danish at home and with Niels Bjelke; Low German outside.
- **Delivery:** Monotone, slow, short sentences; no emphasis.
- **Sample lines:** "It is as it is." / "In Roskilde, the bread was better." / "Count them. Say nothing."
- **Verbal tic:** Pauses for a full breath before replying.

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows who comes and goes at the castle gate and when. He would trade it for his son's peace. He believes, falsely, that the Estonians would never rise in spring.
