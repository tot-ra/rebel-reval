## Biography
Born in 1322 at a small manor near Roskilde, the third son of a Zealand knight who traced a thin line to the Skjalm kin. Taken at eleven as page to a Danish bailiff and sent to Reval in 1337 to the viceroy's household, where the bailiff's patron promised a knighthood by twenty-one. He has been twenty-one for a month.

Two things made him. The first was the hunt in 1340 when he held the viceroy's horse through a boar's charge; he was praised and then forgotten. The second was the death of his father in 1341, leaving the manor to his eldest brother and Absalon a sword. He has attached himself to the Crown with the fervour of a man who needs it to win.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A knight's belt and a small fief on Harju, so he can stop being someone's squire.
- **Fear:** Being found out to be only a good rider.
- **Contradiction:** Insists on honour, but keeps a ledger of grudges and has reported a groom for a petty theft that nobody else cared about.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He believes a clerk in the town is a spy for the Order, and has told no one because the proof is a feeling.

## Work and money
No wage, only board and a yearly outfit; his father's silver buys the gilt buckle and the wax. He owes a mark to the saddler on Toom-Kooli for a repair. A bad month is a knight who forgets to dismiss him from the table.

## Faction and belief
Active for the Crown out of ambition, not love. For a small favour, he would take a message; for a large one, ride at night; to inform, he would, with a flourish. A devout hearer of mass who also wears a hare's-foot under his shirt.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Danish among knights; careful Low German with townsfolk.
- **Delivery:** High, quick, with a rising lilt at the end.
- **Sample lines:** "I held the horse through the boar, you know." / "A word, if I may, before the bell." / "Ved Sankt Olaf!"
- **Verbal tic:** "You know."

## Knowledge and rumours
Knows the viceroy's riding days and which knights quarrel. He would trade it for a good word to the marshal. He believes falsely that Harju peasants fear the sword of any Dane.
