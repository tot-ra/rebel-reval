## At a glance
- A short, stocky, rosy-cheeked woman with a booming voice and ink under every nail; the clerks call her "the bell".
- Writes letters, tallies and charters for Toompea households, at a price that annoys her rivals.
- Heads four children with four different bynames and one roof.
- Sits in the circle's middle, receiving and sending, because the crown holds her bench in its hand.

## Biography
Born 1304 in Lübeck, the daughter of a scribe. She learned letters at a convent school and Latin from her father, and came to Reval in 1325 as the wife of a crown clerk. He died of a winter fever in 1339; she kept his bench by petitioning the viceroy's chancery, which agreed on condition that she serve its purposes.

She has four children living, born across two marriages and a wardship, and has buried at least two. Her byname is her first husband's; the boys carry the bynames of their fathers' or godfathers' towns, and the lane stopped counting.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A school place for Kersten and a dowry for Sibbe.
- **Fear:** The chancery taking the bench; a single letter in the wrong hand.
- **Contradiction:** Educated and stubborn, but carries out orders she dislikes.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** Keeps copies of three letters the chancery asked her to write, in case she ever needs to prove she was told.

## Work and money
She earns perhaps 40 marks a year from charters, letters and tallies, at 2 to 8 schillings a page. Parchment comes from a Lower Town dealer; ink galls from a Lübeck merchant. She owes the dealer 5 marks and is owed 9 by the chancery. A bad month is a chancery delay.

## Faction and belief
She is a dependant of the crown: the chancery may take the bench at a word, and four children eat from it. She is no zealot. A small favour is a letter, a large one a false date; informing, she does by a folded note. She prays at St Mary's and keeps the Hours at home.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Low German in daily life; Latin for charters; low murmurs at the church door.
- **Delivery:** Booming and cheerful, then abruptly soft when something is dangerous.
- **Sample lines:** "A charter is a charter, whoever signs it." / "Kersten, your hand shakes." / "Hush, the door has ears."
- **Verbal tic:** Ends questions with "yes?".

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows what the chancery writes about each household. She would trade it for a school place. She believes, falsely, that the viceroy reads every letter himself.
