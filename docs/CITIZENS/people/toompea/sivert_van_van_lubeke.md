## Biography
Born in 1291 in Lübeck, the son of a notary's copyist, he was entered in a roll as "Sivert van Lubeke" and again as "van van Lubeke" when a clerk's pen slipped; both stuck. He came to Reval in 1322 to work for the Danish chancery and slipped across to the chapter when the viceroy cut wages. His eyes went strained at forty, and since then he copies with a hand-held ruling frame. He lives in a cell above the chapter room and takes private work at night.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To keep his eyes long enough to finish the chapter's cartulary.
- **Fear:** Going blind, and being asked to forge.
- **Contradiction:** A man who writes truth for a living and lets lies pass unchallenged.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He keeps a private copy of the chapter's rent roll, one village short.

## Work and money
His salary is about twelve marks a year plus board; private letters bring perhaps three schillings a week. Parchment and ink cost him two. He owes a Lower Town parchment-maker four schillings. A bad month is when the bishop's seal-keeper is away.

## Faction and belief
No faction; he writes for whoever pays, and thinks parties are a luxury for people with servants. A small favour he does for a penny; a large one he declines; informing he would do under pressure. He keeps a St Nicholas medal for travellers.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Latin for charters, Middle Low German for speech.
- **Delivery:** Low and unhurried, as if dictating; pauses before nouns.
- **Sample lines:** "Write it once, correctly." / "A half-penny is a half-penny." / "Dat steiht schreven."
- **Verbal tic:** "As it is written."

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows who owes whom, in Latin. He would trade a copy for parchment. He believes, wrongly, that the chapter will move to Dorpat.
