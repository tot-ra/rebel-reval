- A ruddy, average-built woman with a squint in the left eye that makes patients unsure whether she is looking at them or past them.
- Called at any hour; has delivered knights' sons and a day-labourer's twins in the same week.
- Owns an eagle-stone in a leather bag that she shares with Anna Juhontytär on Viru road.
- Stays out of politics because both sides need midwives and neither pays well.

## Biography
Born 1300 in Lower Town, to a midwife who took her along from the age of eleven. She learned the stool, the oil and the long wait. In 1322 she married Reynold Gude, the hawk-keeper's son, and moved up the hill; the Toompea families took her because the hill had few trained women. She has borne seven, buried one, and delivered some three hundred.

Since the eagle-stone came to her from her mother in 1334, she has lent it for hard labours, and it has been tied to a thigh on both sides of the town. She works by night, sleeps by day in snatches, and has learnt that the best midwife is the one who keeps quiet.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A bed-sheet for each child and a chest with a lock, so the family is not looked down on by the knights' wives.
- **Fear:** Losing a mother on the stool; being called a witch by a husband who needs someone to blame.
- **Contradiction:** Rough-spoken in public, tender at the bed; trusted by every household, owed money by half.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She knows who among the hill's wives has taken a draught to end a pregnancy, and never spoke of it, not even to the priest.

## Work and money
A birth earns 2 to 6 schillings among the hill families, less for the poor, plus a loaf and a measure of ale; the household income is perhaps 10 marks a year when added to Reynold's. She buys herbs, lye-soap and linen cloths, and owes Hillegund Corte 8 schillings for raspberry leaf and a cart share. The eagle-stone is worth more than anything else in the house and is kept under the hearthstone. A bad month is a run of stillbirths: no coin, and parish gossip.

## Faction and belief
She is no one's. A midwife enters every house, and if she took a side the doors would close. She says the Danes pay late and the council pays less, and that a baby is the same colour in either. She prays to St Margaret and, without telling the priest, ties a red thread to the bedpost.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Low German only; she uses a flat sing-song to frightened women and a clipped bark to servants.
- **Delivery:** Husky and unhurried; she pauses to let the next pain pass.
- **Sample lines:** "Breathe, girl, and keep your noise for the push." / "I will bring the stone when I have it back." / "Two schillings and a loaf, and God sees the rest."
- **Verbal tic:** Starts with "Now then."

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows which marriages are in trouble, which births came early, and who the true father may be. She would trade it for a stone in reach and a place for a daughter. She believes, falsely, that a child born under a waning moon will not live.
