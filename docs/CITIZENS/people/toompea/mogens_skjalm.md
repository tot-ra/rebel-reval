## Biography
Born in 1326 in the Pikk jalg house, the eldest surviving child, Mogens was taught his letters by his father at five and put to copying at twelve. He grew a full beard at fifteen and has been mistaken for a man since.

Two things made him. In 1340 he saw his father hide a creditor's letter in the oven and understood the family's danger. In 1342 he lent a friend a small kindness, a copy of a letter, and found it was a debt he could call; he has since called it twice. He now feels like a boy who has learned to move his father's pieces on a board.

## Motivation

## Work and money
He earns nothing but board and a pair of shoes; his father promises two marks a year at eighteen. He buys ink and quills from a Lower Town clerk with pocket-silver from carrying messages. He owes his sister a pair of scissors. A bad month is a letter he cannot read.

## Faction and belief
A sympathiser by inheritance; he sees what the Crown pays and what it risks. For a small favour he would copy a letter; for a large one, steal one; to inform, he does. He is devout and has a particular fondness for St Nicholas, patron of clerks.
