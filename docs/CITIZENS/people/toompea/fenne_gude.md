## Biography
Born 1283 in Lübeck, the daughter of a rope-maker, and brought to Reval at twelve when her father took work on the quay. She learned to spin from an Estonian neighbour and to bargain from the quay. In 1321 she married Johan Rotermund, a knight twelve years her senior, and moved up the hill; he brought a name and no money, she brought a loom and a cousin in the cloth trade.

She bore two sons, Marquard and Hartwig, and sells thread through the market. Her byname Gude is her father's, shared with a falconer on Rahukohtu and a knight on Toom-Rüütli, with no kinship she will admit.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A debt-free house and her sons settled.
- **Fear:** Her husband's old promises, which could cost them all.
- **Contradiction:** A knight's wife who keeps accounts like a merchant; she sells her thread to the council's side and sleeps beside the Order's friend.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** Carries word for a Lower Town circle that favours the guilds, and keeps her husband ignorant of it.

## Work and money
She earns about 20 marks a year, more than her husband's fief. Flax comes from Harju sellers via a Lower Town broker; her thread goes to three dealers at 4 örtug a skein. She holds a debt note of 3 marks from a knight's widow. A bad month is a month with no market.

## Faction and belief
Her sympathy for the Hanseatic side is bread and memory: the quay fed her. A small favour is a message; a large one, a night's lodging; informing she would not do. She prays to St Nicholas for the ships and ties a knot in her thread for luck.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Low German; she whispers.
- **Delivery:** Soft and almost inaudible, so buyers lean in.
- **Sample lines:** "Four örtug, and it is fair." / "A good dealer asks no questions." / "Husband is at mass, is he?"
- **Verbal tic:** Says "so" and tilts her head.

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows which merchants are good for credit, and which knights are not. She would trade it for a place for her sons. She believes, falsely, that the council would shelter Toompea if the hill fell.
