## Biography
Born in 1278 in Greifswald, a mason's son, he came to Reval in 1305 and laid courses on the castle's outer wall until a lime splash took the use of his left eye in 1318. He retired in 1338 when his knees went and moved in with his son Ludolf. A crown reeve, glad of an old man who hears things, pays him a few pfennigs for what he says at the well. He thinks it is neighbourly chat.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A quiet chair and a mug of ale by the stove.
- **Fear:** Being thrown out of his son's house.
- **Contradiction:** He loves his neighbours and sells their words.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He knows his son loses money and has said nothing.

## Faction and belief
Crown by habit and a few pfennigs. A small favour he does at once; a large one he declines; informing is his routine. He touches the lintel for luck.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Middle Low German only.
- **Delivery:** Gravelly, slow, repeats the last word.
- **Sample lines:** "Stone is stone, men are men." / "I only heard it at the well." / "Dat is een Kunst."
- **Verbal tic:** "As I heard."

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows which walls were built badly. He would trade it for a quiet home. He believes, wrongly, that the castle cistern is poisoned.
