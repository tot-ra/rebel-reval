## At a glance
- Sells his back by the day; when the gate-master's foreman lifts two fingers, Andres is already walking.
- Tells the whole beach where to buy tar and nails (from Gunnar Bengtsson) and would tell them where to buy honour if he knew.
- Oddity: a quick, high voice from a man of average frame; strangers think a boy is calling them.

## Biography
Born in 1301 in a Kalarand hut to a net-mender and a woman who salted herring for the Dominican friars. At nine he was carrying water, at fourteen he joined the labour gangs that rebuilt the harbour mole. He married Anna Aino tütar at twenty-three, and they have buried a son who lived two days, between Elsa and Mari. The hard winter of 1341-42 took the roof off the lean-to; he mended it with a barrel stave and borrowed tar, and has not stopped thinking about the next winter since. In the night, five or six winters ago, he and Olev Tanieli poeg carried Olev's feverish small boy to the herb-wife behind the church. Nobody has spoken of it since.

## Motivation
- **Want:** Enough dried fish and rye in the loft by Michaelmas that Anna does not have to ask a neighbour.
- **Fear:** Being too sore to stand at the gate; a man who cannot stand is replaced by noon.
- **Contradiction:** He praises Anna's stall to everyone, yet resents that her coin comes steadier than his.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He once took a half-pfund of ballast iron from a wreck and sold it unseen; he still hears the foreman's footsteps in his sleep.

- **Sundays and feast days:** Mass at St Olaf, a shave, a walk to the mole with the girls.
- **Spring 1343 disruption:** Rumours of a grain levy and of gate closures make hiring uneven; he stays near the gate and keeps his head down.

## Work and money
He earns about an örtug on a good day and nothing on three bad ones; across a year perhaps four marks, against which stands the plot rent and the barrow, borrowed from a carter he repays in labour. Anna's stall adds a mark or two. In winter they eat what the herring barrel gives. Spring is the thin season: Lent is over but the sea is only half open. A bad month means selling the girls' second blanket.

## Faction and belief
He has no faction. Rebels lose their houses; he has a roof to keep. Asked a small favour he would give it; a large one, never; to inform, he would first ask what it paid and then refuse. He believes in St Olaf and touches the doorframe when he leaves.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian only; at the gate he understands a few shouted Low German orders.
- **Delivery:** High, quick, a little breathless, with a habit of finishing other people's sentences.
- **Sample lines:** "Two barrels, then the stone, then I'm done, aye?" / "Buy from the Swede, he does not cheat." / "Aitäh, I'll mend your hatch on Sunday."
- **Verbal tic:** "Aye?" at the end of each sentence.

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows which foremen short the pay and which ships come in with unmarked cargo. He would trade this for a half-day's work. He believes the Danes will shut the Coastal Gate to Estonians once the grain levy begins; it is false, for now.
