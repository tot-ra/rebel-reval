## Biography
Born in 1312 in a Harju hamlet, came to town at sixteen after a bad harvest. He grew up with the old stump and the offering of salt and bread; the town priest called it superstition, and Priidik learned to keep quiet. A year ago Made Lauri tütar fed him broth when he lay sick, and later asked a small thing in return. He is not a fighter; he is a man with a debt and a habit.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A steady weekly wage and an unwatched Sunday in the woods.
- **Fear:** The priest finding the stump.
- **Contradiction:** He hates being a debtor yet takes credit as his living.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He leaves salt and bread at a stump past the Sand Gate every new moon.

## Faction and belief
A secret sympathiser of the old ways. His root is a childhood in the woods, now closed to him. A small favour he gives; a large one, only if it protects the stump; informing, never. He crosses himself and touches his hat to the stump both.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian only.
- **Delivery:** Booming, even in whispers; he cannot lower it.
- **Sample lines:** "Bales for Lai, move!" / "I owe, and I pay." / "Quiet, the forest listens."
- **Verbal tic:** "God and the trees."

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows which cargo is heavy and which light and where the old stump is. He would trade it for a debt cleared. He believes the priest has been told about the stump; false.
