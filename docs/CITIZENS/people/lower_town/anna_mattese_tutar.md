## At a glance
- A broad, short young woman at the second wheel in the loft, singing so low that new visitors think a man is in the room.
- Her fingers are so long that Wendla gives her the finest flax and the worst knots.
- Odd for a maid: she is the loft's peacemaker, and she is paid the same as the others.

## Biography
Born in 1317 to Matthias, a fisher at Kopli who drowned in 1329. Her mother went to a cousin and sent Anna to town at twelve. She spent six years with a clothier on Viru, learning fine work; when he died in 1335, Wendla hired her. She has sung in the loft since. A suitor, a Swedish boatman, left for Stockholm and did not write; she does not mention him.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A place of her own in the loft, with a window.

- **Secret or withheld fact:** She cannot swim, and dreams of the shore every Lent.

## Work and money
She earns a mark a year and keep. She saves three pfennig a month in a pouch inside her mattress. She owes nothing. Her work earns the house about two skeins a day of fine thread. A bad month is damp, when her hands swell and the thread snaps.

## Faction and belief
No faction. She would sing a lullaby for a rebel and a watchman alike. She believes in the thread-songs and the Holy Spirit equally, and puts her spindle in the corner on feasts.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian with the maids; careful German with the mistress.

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows which flax comes from which village by the smell. She would trade it for a window. She believes that a spinner who sings off-key sours the thread, which is folk lore.
