## At a glance
- A sturdy, full-bearded baker with flour in the creases of his neck and a voice like a gate hinge.
- Known for dark rye loaves and a bad habit of promising credit he cannot give.
- Odd: his oven burns before dawn and again after curfew, when no bread should be baking.

## Biography
Born in 1313 in the baker's house on Müürivahe, to a father who baked for the Danes' garrison and moved a boundary stake in the yard in the lean year 1326. He died in 1339 and Jaak took the oven at twenty-six, married to Gertrud since 1333. His voice went hoarse from calling loaves along the harbour wind. In 1341 a failed harvest ate his flour stock and he took grain on credit from the cap-maker Stepan Fedorovich at an unnamed rate. In 1342 a share in a boat voyage went badly and he lost 15 marks. Now he bakes, owes and listens at the oven door.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To clear his debts and teach Eerik the oven.
- **Fear:** The creditors comparing notes.
- **Contradiction:** A master baker whose loaves fill the street and whose own table is thin.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** At night he carries sacks of day-old loaves to a runaway serf hidden in a cellar, at his mother-in-law's request.

## Work and money
He bakes twenty-four rye loaves a day and a few wheat rolls, earning around 5 marks a month after flour and fuel. Rye comes from Siim Niklase poeg the miller; firewood from Harju carters. He owes 6 marks' worth of grain to Stepan Fedorovich, with unspoken heavy interest, and 15 marks to Lauri Villemi poeg for the boat share. A bad month is one when the miller delays and the dough has to be thinned.

## Faction and belief
No faction. He takes the sacks into the dark for Gertrud's mother, not for any king, and would stop if the cellar were found. For a small favour he would give bread; for a large one he would give a night; to inform he would lie to the watch. He makes a cross on each loaf with a knife.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian at home, rough German in the shop.
- **Delivery:** Hoarse from the harbour wind, pitched to carry over a crowd.
- **Sample lines:** "Rye, fresh, a pfennig the loaf!" "Credit? Ach, tomorrow, tomorrow." "Dat brot is nich vör di, Knecht."
- **Verbal tic:** Rubs flour from his beard before replying.

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows who buys white flour and who cannot pay, and the patrol's rounds. He would trade it for a quiet debt. He believes a loaf split at the door brings thieves, which is folk belief.
