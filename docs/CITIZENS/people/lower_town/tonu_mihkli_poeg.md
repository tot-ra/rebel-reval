## At a glance
- A pale, average-sized clerk with a squint, stooped over a wax tablet by the window with the best light.
- Known for a beautiful clean hand and for being the only Estonian on the street who writes Latin.
- Oddity: the brewer's malt accounts are in his ink, and his own wages are not in anyone's.

## Biography
Born 1319 in Reval to an Estonian carter, Mihkel, who drove salt from the harbour to the weighhouse and scraped together four marks to send his son to the cathedral school on Toompea. Tõnu learned his letters from a Danish canon, who beat him and taught him Latin. He apprenticed under a scrivener on Pikk, who died in 1338, and was hired by Bertold van Stade as a malt clerk in 1339 because he could count in two tongues.

He lodges in a closet off the counting room and writes the barley accounts, the tap-room tally and Bertold's private letters to Wismar. He has never been given a coat.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A clerkship in the Rathaus, with a stipend.
- **Fear:** That his eyes will fail before he has a place.
- **Contradiction:** He despises the Germans who call him "the Estonian" and copies their letters in a better hand than theirs.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He has copied Bertold's letters, once, for a friend who could not read, and has not forgotten what they say about the Rat.

## Work and money
His wage is board, a closet and twelve öre a quarter. His pen earns more for others at a pfennig per letter, which he does after dark. He owes a Pikk scrivener two öre for ink. A bad month is a candle short.

## Faction and belief
No faction. He reads enough to know what the Rat says and what the street says and keeps both to himself. For a small favour he writes a letter; for a large one he would hesitate; he would not inform, because writing is his only safety. He prays at St Olaf's and tucks a rowan twig in his tablet-case.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian with servants and at church; German with his master; Latin to himself.
- **Delivery:** Warm baritone, careful, a little slow when choosing a word.
- **Sample lines:** "Three lasts of malt, less one." / "I write what I am given." / "Ma ei näe hästi, aga ma loen." (I do not see well, but I read.)
- **Verbal tic:** Squints before answering.

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows the real barley prices, the household's private debts and who is writing to whom. He would trade for a Rathaus reference. He believes the false rumour that the Rat will soon require Estonians to carry written passes.
