## Biography
Paul was born in 1292 in a village on a Harju manor; his father bought freedom with beeswax and moved to town when Paul was ten. The family dealt in wax and furs for three generations, and a German sponsor, the late Hermann Vos, sponsored Paul's burgher oath in 1320. His wife Liis keeps the dairy. The roof leaked in the winter of 1342, and he borrowed five marks to mend it.

He keeps his cousins on the manors in his prayers, and he was ashamed in 1339 when two of them were bound to the land.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To marry his daughter Eeva well and keep the Vene house.
- **Fear:** Being named at the council as the Harju men's friend.
- **Contradiction:** He grumbles about the bound men yet buys their wax at the lowest price.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He carries word between a young spinner on the coast road and a clerk in the Lower Town.

## Work and money
He buys wax and pelts from Harju carters and sells to Hanseatic exporters; his turnover is some two hundred marks a year. He owes the tavern keeper Ghert Kleine five marks for the roof, half paid, due at Michaelmas, and is owed eight marks by Lutke van Rostok. A bad month is when a carter's cart is stopped at a gate and the wax turns up late.

## Faction and belief
He is a secret sympathiser because his kin are bound, but he does not want the town to burn. A small favour he does with a nod, a large one only through others, and informing he would refuse. He lights a candle to St Olaf and still pours a bit of beer into the ground at midsummer.

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows which manors have been bound and which have been burned. He would trade it for a safe road. He believes, wrongly, that the Danish viceroy will not hang a burgher.
