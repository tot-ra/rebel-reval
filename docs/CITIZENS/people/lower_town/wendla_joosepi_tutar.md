## Biography
Born in 1285 to Joosep, a wool-comber in Vene street, and put to the spindle at six. She spun for a clothier on Viru for thirty years and married Uku in 1332, after his wife Maret died, because he needed a woman for his two-year-old son and she needed a roof. She raised Ain as her own and set up a thread-house with three maids in the loft over the salt-cellar. She has buried a brother and no child of her own. She lives now between two clocks, the salt bell and the wheel.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To keep the spinning contract with the German cloth-seller through another year and see Ain steady.
- **Fear:** That her husband's debt to Tyde swallows the house and her loft with it.
- **Contradiction:** Warm with servants, ruthless about a short skein.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She has quietly saved four marks in an old crock under the loft floor, which her husband does not know about.

## Work and money
She buys flax from a Pühavaimu dealer and sells spun thread by the skein to a cloth-seller on Viru at about eight pfennig the skein for fine, less for coarse. The loft earns roughly one mark a week, of which the maids' keep eats half. She pays her maids a mark and their keep a year. She lends nothing and borrows nothing. A bad month is a wet one: damp flax rots and the maids' hands crack.

## Faction and belief
She wants no faction; she wants her thread paid. She would hide a girl in the loft for a night, and lie to a watchman if asked kindly, but she would not carry a message. She makes the sign of the cross at every spindle's first turn and puts out a bowl of milk at Midsummer.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian with the household; slow German with the cloth-seller.
- **Delivery:** Low and unhurried, a pause after each sentence as if checking the thread.
- **Sample lines:** "The thread does not care who is cross." "Kuule, tüdruk, no knots in my flax." "He talks of herring and I talk of wool, and we are friends."
- **Verbal tic:** Rolls the spindle whorl between thumb and finger before answering.

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows which clothier pays late and which girl in the lane is with child, since maids talk at the wheel. She would trade it for a good word to the cloth-seller. She believes the pestilence returns with the first swallows, which is false.
