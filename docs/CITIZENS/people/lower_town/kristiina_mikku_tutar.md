## Biography
Born 1300 to a fuller at the Kopli stream, she spun for her mother and sold thread at the market by ten. She married [Madis Jaani poeg](madis_jaani_poeg.md) in 1320 and moved into the narrow house on Laboratooriumi. Two sons died in infancy, 1324 and 1327; she mentions them only in prayer. She now spins for three households, keeps three daughters fed, and counts every candle.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A second spindle and a loom so Tilda can weave and earn more.
- **Fear:** Another bad winter, when the house had one fire for five.
- **Contradiction:** Terse in speech, generous in what she gives away.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She sometimes underweighs her own thread to keep a favoured client.

## Work and money
Spinning brings about 3 schillings a month; wool comes from the market by the pfund, flax from Pskov carters. Madis's wage is steady but small. A bad month is a missed delivery.

## Faction and belief
None. Madis's post is enough politics for one house. She would do a small favour, nothing larger, and tell nothing. She keeps old hearth customs: a spoonful of porridge for the hearth-keeper on feast days.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian at home; short, exact Low German for clients.
- **Delivery:** Clipped and precise, each word counted.
- **Sample lines:** "Three skeins, two schillings." / "Do not touch the spindle." / "Kuule, the fire."
- **Verbal tic:** "Exactly so."

## Knowledge and rumours
Knows who buys cloth for weddings or funerals. She would trade it for good wool. She falsely believes a skein spun on Friday brings luck.
