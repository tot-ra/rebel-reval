## At a glance
- A pear-shaped man with a full ash-blond beard and a voice that carries down Börsi käik before he does.
- Makes chests, benches, shutters and coffin boards; known for pegged joints that never loosen.
- Lends grain and silver on notched hazel tally-sticks, though he has never learned letters.
- His nose sits crooked to the right, and he touches it when he bargains.

## Biography
Jaan was born in 1308 to a joiner's journeyman of Harju stock who walked into Reval in the wars of 1313 and never walked out. His father taught him the plane and the peg; his mother taught him to count with notches, since neither could read. At nineteen a drunk Gotland carter broke his nose in a quarrel over a cart left across the lane, and Jaan set the bone himself with a thumb and a mouthful of beer. He took the workshop on Börsi käik at twenty-six when his master, an old German who had no son, sold it on easy terms. He paid the last of it in 1340. Now he keeps a household of six, a bench of two planes, three saws and a sack of beech pegs, and is slowly becoming the street's lender.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To see Ain take the bench over with his name on a guild-standing, and to own the walls he works in.
- **Fear:** A burned street. Everything he owns is wood and promises.
- **Contradiction:** He is loud and generous at table, yet counts every peg and forgives no debt, only postpones it.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** Nobody has ever read his tally-sticks back to him, so a trusted neighbour could have been cheating him for years; he suspects it and has never tested it.

## Work and money
Oak and beech from timber merchants at the harbour at a few örtug the board; sells chests at 2 to 3 marks, coffins by weight of wood. A good month brings 5 marks clear. He has 18 marks out with a merchant, 8 with a carpenter, and 45 marks of grain with a shoemaker. Shed rent eats nearly a mark a quarter. A bad month is a fire in the lane or a debtor's death.

## Faction and belief
No faction. His politics are timber and credit; a rising burns his stock and calls in his debts. Asked for a small favour he gives it, for a large one he asks what it costs the street, and he would not inform on a neighbour who owes him. He lights a candle at St Olaf's and leaves a heel of bread under the lintel for the house-spirit, as his mother did.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian at home and on the street; Low German with buyers, loud and over-slow.
- **Delivery:** Booming, relaxed, laughs at his own sums; drops to a mutter only when counting.
- **Sample lines:** "Oak, friend, not pine! Pine is for the poor and the dead." "Pay when you can, but count it in your head every night." "Mu kallis, ära puuduta seda!" (My dear, don't touch that!)
- **Verbal tic:** Taps the crooked nose before naming a price.

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows which houses on the street have rotten sills and which merchants quietly stock beams for barricades. He would trade it for honest payment. He believes the false rumour that the Danish viceroy will tax every plank sold in town.
