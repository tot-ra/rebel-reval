## At a glance
- Ships rye, wax and furs west in a share of a Lübeck cog, and brings back Flemish cloth and salt.
- Gravelly voice, always rubbing his beard as if counting under it.
- Lets his children play with a tanner's children, which Pikk finds odd.
- Watches the price of cloth like a man watching a dog he does not trust.

## Biography
Born 1303 in Reval, the second son of a Pikk merchant who had come from Hameln, and sent at fourteen to a Lübeck counting-house for four years. He returned in 1324, took a quarter share in a cog, and lost it in the storm of 1329, rebuilding from a single cask of wax. He married Yda in 1334 and built the house up to its present twelve souls. A friend's funeral in the winter of 1341 drew him into a quiet circle of men who feared the council's money would pass to the Order. He now trades the way he talks, with few words and long memory.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To see his two daughters married into Lübeck families and the cog paid off.
- **Fear:** A cargo lost, or the credit that rests on a handshake withdrawn.
- **Contradiction:** Careful with silver, yet lets a tanner's children run through his courtyard.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He has quietly paid the burial fee of an old craftsman and now counts him as a bound man.

## Work and money
Income from shares in Lübeck-bound cargoes, about 120 marks a year in good years, spent largely on stock and wages for twelve. He is owed 15 marks by a tavern keeper for a boat share that failed. A bad month is a late ship: the clerks idle and the maids are paid from the strongbox.

## Faction and belief
Active Hanseatic: he believes the council and the guilds keep the roads open, and fears that a rising or a Danish tax will close them. For a small favour he lends a cart; for a large one he stands surety; for informing he is a careful listener rather than a teller. Devout in the merchant manner, with a candle to St Nicholas before each voyage.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Middle Low German in trade; Latin for charters and the odd proverb.
- **Delivery:** Gravelly, unhurried, with a pause before each figure.
- **Sample lines:** "A handshake is credit, sir, not a gift." / "Wax is up and Lübeck is silent." / "Gode dag, Meinhard. Business is slow, I hear."
- **Verbal tic:** "Mark me."

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows which cargoes are late and which clerks are paid in kind. He would trade it for a safe berth for his cog. He believes the Order has already bought a councillor, which is false.
