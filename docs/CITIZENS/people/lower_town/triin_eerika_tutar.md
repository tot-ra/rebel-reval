## At a glance
- A slight, fair woman who flushes pink at the neck when she is pleased or cross, and says everything in the same level tone.
- Spins wool and flax at the doorstep with a spindle, even while she walks.
- The unofficial judge of which sellers in the lane are honest.
- Has borne five children and buried none, which she credits to a rowan twig over the cradle.

## Biography
Triin was born in 1312 in a Nõmme-side cottage outside the town walls, the daughter of Eerik, a woodcutter who sold fuel to the Lower Town. She spun for pay at ten, sitting in the sun with a drop-spindle. At seventeen she married Jaan, then a young journeyman, in a church wedding at St Olaf's, and she brought a distaff, two sheets and a pair of shears as her whole dower. Her first child, Ain, came in the second winter. She learned to hold a household in her head: the price of flax, who owed what, which child had a cough. She speaks in the same tone whether she announces a birth or a fire, and the street has long since stopped taking offence at it.

## Motivation
- **Want:** Four winters of bread and a roof for the children, and a second loom's worth of space so she can take on more work.
- **Fear:** A bad winter fever among the children; Ell has coughed since Candlemas.
- **Contradiction:** She seems cold and exact but gives away heels of bread to children not hers.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She keeps a few pfennigs in a hollow of the rafters, unknown to Jaan, as a fund for the day he lends one debt too many.

## Work and money
She buys raw fleece and flax from carters and sells spun thread to weavers at a few pfennig the skein; she earns perhaps 3 öre in a good month. Her thread pays the salt and the oil while Jaan's silver goes to the household debts. A bad month is a bad flax crop or a pedlar who sells short weight.

## Faction and belief
No faction. A rising would close the gates and starve the flax trade. She would give a neighbour bread, but never hide a fugitive under the children's roof. She keeps the old rites: milk at the threshold on Midsummer Eve and a rowan twig over the cradle, beside her prayers at St Olaf's.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian at home; plain Low German with sellers.
- **Delivery:** Monotone, low volume, quick and exact; never rises, even in rebuke.
- **Sample lines:** "The thread is short by a hand." "Ain, wash. Now." "Mis sa sealt teed?" (What are you doing there?)
- **Verbal tic:** Ends statements with "so."

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows who sells short weight and who is quietly in debt. She would trade it for a pedlar's honest price on flax. She believes the false rumour that a woman who spins on St George's Night will see a ghost.
