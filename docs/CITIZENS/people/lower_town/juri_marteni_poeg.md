## Biography
Jüri was born in the cold spring of 1326 in the Müürivahe house, the first child after two deaths, and his mother Eeva named him for St George to bring luck. He learned to knead clay at six and to sing the old counting rhymes from his grandfather Tõll, who told them the way other men tell the Pater Noster. At twelve he went with Tõll to a grove outside the Viru road for the spring offering, and the smell of smoke and ale on the roots stayed with him. At fifteen he was brought into a small circle by a woman who nodded at the right moments. Since then he has carried messages in empty jugs. He is a boy who has been given a task and a name for it, and he likes both.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To be counted among grown men and to see the old rites kept, not whispered.
- **Fear:** That his father finds out and beats the old rites out of him; worse, that his mother is ashamed.
- **Contradiction:** He loves the kiln and the pottery but wants to be somewhere else.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** Jugs he sells on market days sometimes carry a scratched sign on the base, a signal to the circle.

## Work and money
He earns his keep and a few pfennigs on the side from small errands. The clay is dug at Lasnamäe, wood is bought, and everything is carried by hand. He has no savings but owes the circle nothing, though they treat him as owing a little.

## Faction and belief
He is a secret sympathiser of the Cult of Metsik: the grove, the offerings, and the old counting. His reason is not politics; it is his grandfather's voice. He would pass a jug as a small favour, hide a person only if told, and never inform, because the cell is the only place he is trusted.

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows which jugs carry signs and which lanes watchmen skip. He would trade it for nothing. He believes the rumour that the Order will back the Estonians, which is false.
