## Biography
Born 1285 in Stockholm, daughter of a saddler, Birger. Apprenticed to a girdler at eleven, came to Reval in 1315 after the war closed the Baltic roads. She made belts for merchants for twenty-five years; Folke Eriksson, Erik's father, was her best customer and, in 1336, her lender. When her eyes weakened he let her the room above the kitchen for life, in return for her stock and her tools. Folke is dead; Erik keeps the bargain and she keeps the house's accounts of belts and buckles.

## Work and money
She no longer sells in public; her pension is her room and board, and about 4 marks a year from private belt orders. She buys scraps and thread, mostly from tanners. A bad month is a stolen buckle.

## Faction and belief
No faction: she was a child during the wars and thinks all banners cost the same. For a small favour she gives leather, for a large one a talking-to, to inform she says nothing. Devout at St Olaf; also leaves bread for the house-sprite.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Swedish in the house; Middle Low German on the street, at full volume.
- **Delivery:** Booming, slow, final.
- **Sample lines:** "I made belts when your father was a boy." / "Leather does not lie, and neither do I." / "Gode morgen, and shut that door."

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows which merchants have worn belts for years and which borrow rank. She believes the false rumour that the Dominicans keep a drowned man's girdle as a relic.
