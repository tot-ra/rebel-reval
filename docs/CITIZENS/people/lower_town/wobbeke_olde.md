## At a glance
- A tiny, lean, rosy-cheeked old woman whose soft whisper silences the hall.
- Widow of a ruined merchant, and now the quiet keeper of a Hanseatic circle's memory.
- Cannot read or write, and trusts only what she has heard.
- Takes the poor-box and the gossip equally seriously.

## Biography
Born in 1262 in Soest, she married a cloth merchant at fifteen and came to Reval in 1290. She bore six children and buried five; the sixth moved to Lübeck. Her husband's house failed in 1328 after a ship was lost, and he died a year later. The Holy Spirit house took her in at the death of her son-in-law. In forty years of Hanseatic dinners she learned who owed whom, and the circle that formed a year ago came to ask her.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To see the merchants honour their debts before she dies.
- **Fear:** A winter without wood and a grave without a name.
- **Contradiction:** She is an almswoman and gives orders to rich men.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She remembers a bankruptcy the Rat has buried.

## Faction and belief
Active on the Hanseatic side because the guild fed her marriage and shames her widowhood. For a small favour she would pass a word; for a large one she would remind a man of a debt; to inform she would name no one. She prays to St Anne and tells her beads like a ledger.

## Knowledge and rumours
Knows old debts and secret kin. She would trade it for firewood. Believes falsely that the Order will honour Hanseatic debts.
