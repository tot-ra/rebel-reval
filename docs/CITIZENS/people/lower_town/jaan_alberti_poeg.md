## At a glance
- A long-limbed, wind-reddened skipper with stubble and a pock-scarred forehead, who speaks in short exact sentences.
- Known for the best nose for weather in the roads and a clean copy of every bill of lading.
- Oddity: he has no German of his own, only the quay's few hundred words, and works through Latin and a clerk.

## Biography
Born in 1305 on a fishing shore near Viimsi, the son of Albert, a boatman. He was a ship's boy at ten, and learned his letters at fourteen from the friars of St Catherine in exchange for rowing them to Naissaar. He made mate on a cog and bought a half-share in a small ship in 1334 with the dowry of Marga Pärtli tütar. He now owns it outright.

He ships grain, salt and timber on the Reval-Gotland run, and sometimes more than the bills say.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A second ship and a name on the quay.
- **Fear:** Having his hold searched by a customs-man with a good nose.
- **Contradiction:** An honest hand with every bill, and a dishonest one with the cargo.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** Priidik Mikku poeg recruited him a year ago with a small kindness; Jaan hides a locker under the floor and has twice let raiders use his anchorage.

## Work and money
A good run brings 20 marks; ship costs eat 12. He splits a harbour cart and a day's labour each week with Meinhard Wise and quarrels about it every week. He trades favours in kind with Gottschalk van Deventer: Jaan supplies what Gottschalk lacks and Gottschalk returns work Jaan cannot do; the books are never even. He recommended Frederik van Brunswik to a third party and has since heard complaints that rebound on his own name. He regularly supplies Gottschalk Lippe, usually on credit settled at quarter-days; the arrangement is the backbone of both households' week. Tilman van Lubeke recommended Jaan to a third party and has since heard complaints. Mikk Simoni poeg regularly supplies Jaan and has twice held back stock to press an old point; Jaan has not forgotten. A bad month is a wrecked cargo.

## Faction and belief
A secret sympathiser of the harbour raiders; his reason is a half-share lost to a customs-fine in 1331. A small favour: an anchorage. A large one: a cargo. He would inform on nobody because he is one. He prays to St Nicholas and throws a coin into the sea at each departure.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian at home; Latin in bills; quay German by word.
- **Delivery:** Clipped and precise.
- **Sample lines:** "Item, so many barrels." / "Wind from the north-east. We wait." / "Ei, ei."
- **Verbal tic:** Looks at the sky mid-sentence.

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows every anchorage, which sailors will carry a letter and which pilots drink. He would trade it for a cheaper moorage. He believes the false rumour that the Vitalienbrüder have a fleet of thirty.
