## Biography
Born in 1303 in a Lübeck merchant's house in Reval, Hillegund was given to an Estonian wet-nurse, Mari, from the Lasnamägi hamlet, for her first four years, and learned the nurse's songs before German. She trained with a herb-wife on Vene, the same master who later trained Cecilia van Paderborne ([card](cecilia_van_paderborne.md)), and married Thomas Langhe ([card](thomas_langhe.md)) in 1325. She keeps a drying loft and a still-room, and is called to lying-ins and fevers across the quarter, paid in kind.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To teach her daughter Grete the herbs before the girl is married off.
- **Fear:** A fever in the house in the damp spring.
- **Contradiction:** A patrician's wife who walks to the poor's door at night.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She sells a poppy draught to a woman on Harju whom the priest would refuse.

## Work and money
She earns perhaps two marks a month in gifts and coin, which she keeps apart from her husband's. Herbs she grows in the yard or buys at the harbour; wine and honey for syrups are dear. A bad month is one without a dry day for the loft.

## Faction and belief
No faction. A herb-wife's trade depends on a quiet trust, and she keeps out of whatever her husband's table talks about. She would do a small favour, refuse a large one and tell no one who she has nursed. She prays to St Elizabeth and leaves a bowl of milk for the house-spirit.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Low German only, with a sing-song lilt; her nurse's Estonian songs she hums to herself at night.
- **Delivery:** Light, quick and lilting.
- **Sample lines:** "Drink it slowly." "The fever breaks at dawn or it does not." "Hush, mu laps." (my child)
- **Verbal tic:** "There, there."

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows which women are with child before their husbands. She would trade it for nothing. She believes the false rumour that comfrey cures the plague.
