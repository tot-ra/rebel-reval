## Biography
Born 1297 in Wismar, a baker's daughter, brought to Reval as a bride of twenty in 1317 with a cart of linen and an uncle's blessing. Her husband's first brewing season ran her ragged; by 1320 she had taken over the tapping, and by 1325 she was ordering the ale. She has borne five children and buried none, and counts it daily as a favour from St Gertrude.

The sore point of her life is a seat: at the Shrovetide feast of 1334 the wives of the councillors sat by rank and she was placed below the hearth, beside a skipper's widow. She has not forgotten and is polite to every woman who was there.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To put her two eldest well, and to see her tap-room the busiest on the street.
- **Fear:** A dry season or a bad cask that gives the street cause to talk.
- **Contradiction:** She prides herself on never gossiping and keeps a mental ledger of everyone else's debts.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She suspects Bertold meets people after mass he should not and has told no one; she watches the lane for who walks him home.

## Work and money
She sells house ale at one pfennig the quart and stronger at two, and a keg to inns by the barrel. Her profit is perhaps thirty marks a year, which is hers by custom to spend on the household. She buys cloth, spices and linen. A bad month is a rival who cuts the price or a customer who runs a tab.

## Faction and belief
She has no faction and wants none; she says politics is a quarrel over who pays for the beer. For a small favour she gives a quart; for a large one she asks the price; she would inform only on a thief. She says a prayer to St Gertrude for the cats in the cellar.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Middle Low German always, polite to guests and sharp to rivals.
- **Delivery:** Slow, with long pauses; she waits for the room to quiet.
- **Sample lines:** "...A pfennig the quart. ...And the cup is mine." / "...I did not hear that, and neither did you." / "...Dat is eene lange Rekenynge." (That is a long reckoning.)
- **Verbal tic:** Begins every answer with a breath and a pause.

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows who owes what at every tap-room on the street and who waters the ale. She would trade it for a better pitch at the harbour fair. She believes the false rumour that Irina Yakovlevna mixes ash into her ale.
