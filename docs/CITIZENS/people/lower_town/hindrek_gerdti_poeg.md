## Biography
Born in 1322 in a lane off the Viru road, son of a porter named Gerdt who was flogged at the pillory in 1340 for short weight; the weigh-master's own tally was wrong, and Hindrek has seen the same sums since. His mother sold her loom to pay the clerk who taught him Latin. At sixteen Niklas Uku poeg took him as a copyist because he could read a German hand. He is now journeyman in all but name, sleeps over the counting-room, and has begun to take the Black Cloaks' errands. His wage is four marks and board.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A merchant's share of his own, however small.
- **Fear:** Being named in a ledger as owing anyone anything.
- **Contradiction:** He hates debt and was bound to the cell by a loaf.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He copies the weigh-house tallies twice and keeps the second copy inside his tablet case.

## Work and money
He earns four marks a year and board. He copies, reckons, takes the books to the weigh-house and sorts incoming goods. With Meinhard van Campen he splits a cart and a day-labourer each week, eight pfennigs a share. He owes nothing; he has saved eleven pfennigs toward a goose-quill knife of his own.

## Faction and belief
Active Black Cloak. His root is his father's flogging; his branch is the sums, since he alone in the cell can see where the weights lie. A small favour is carrying a word; a large one he would do for the cell and not for the cause; he will not inform, but would weigh it if Gerdt's name were cleared. He keeps the Church's fasts and no folk rite.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian in the lanes, Low German in the counting-room, Latin only for set phrases.
- **Delivery:** Warm and measured, a lower voice than his size suggests.
- **Sample lines:** "Count it twice, then it is yours." "Ma ei tea, I do not know." "A loaf is not a bond."
- **Verbal tic:** Touches the wart before answering.

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows which weigh-house tallies are short and by how many pfund. He would trade it for his father's name cleared. He believes wrongly that the Rat's clerk keeps a second book for the Danish crown.
