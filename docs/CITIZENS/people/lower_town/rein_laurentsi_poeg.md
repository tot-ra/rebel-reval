## At a glance
- Keeps the one bath-house on Harju that serves Germans and Estonians on different days.
- Stirs the stove with a long iron rake whose handle he has worn smooth with his thumb.
- Slow-spoken and slight, he is never in a hurry and never misses a pfennig.

## Biography
Born in Reval in 1290 to Laurentsi, a fisher, and Lutsia, a wool-comber, Rein learned to keep a stove from an old bathkeeper on Harju whose house he later bought on instalments. He married Marga Simoni tütar in 1317; three children died, one lived and was lost in the winter of 1337, and a late daughter, Hele, now gives the house its noise. The Rat licenses his bath for a fee of 1 mark a year. He lives over the bath-house with his mother, wife, daughter and two maids, and never leaves it for more than an hour.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To hand Hele a bath-house with a clean licence and no debts.
- **Fear:** A fire, and the Rat withdrawing his licence for a careless word.
- **Contradiction:** A man who knows every secret and has none of his own to tell.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** A bath guest once left a sealed packet in the stove-room and never returned, and Rein has not opened it.

## Work and money
A bath costs 1 pfennig for a poor man, 3 for a merchant; weekly takings are about 2 marks in the good season. Birch whisks, firewood and water cost half. He buys wood from a Harju carter and pays the licence at Michaelmas. He owes 3 marks on the house. A bad month is a fire in a neighbour's roof.

## Faction and belief
He has no faction: a bath serves anyone with a pfennig, and a bathkeeper who takes sides ends with an empty bench. A small favour he gives; a large one he declines; informing he would do only to save his licence. He leaves a pinch of bread by the stove for the bath spirit and says an Ave at each opening.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian at home and with guests; slow German with the Rat.
- **Delivery:** Low and unhurried, never louder than the stove.
- **Sample lines:** "The stones are hot; sit." "I hear nothing in the steam." "Leil on hea." (The steam is good.)
- **Verbal tic:** "Easy now."

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows who comes alone and who comes with friends. He would trade it for his licence. He believes the false rumour that the Rat will ban Estonian bath days.
