## Biography
Henn was born in 1319 in a Harju village near Keila, the son of a tenant called Joosep. A Dominican friar who toured the villages noticed him counting sheep and took him to the friary school of St Catherine at ten, where he learned his letters in Latin and German. He left at seventeen and worked as an interpreter on the strand until [Wolter van Brunswik](../../people/lower_town/wolter_van_brunswik.md) took him on in 1340 as a journeyman clerk for the Estonian trade. His mother still lives in the village, and he sends her a mark at Christmas.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A burgher's right and a seat in the weigh-house as a sworn interpreter.
- **Fear:** Being asked by an Estonian carter to lie in German.
- **Contradiction:** A man who is Estonian in his heart and a German clerk in his coat, and uncomfortable in both.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He once translated a carter's complaint more softly than it was said, and kept the carter out of a flogging.

## Work and money
He earns his board, a gown at Christmas and two marks a year, with a fee for each translation at the strand. He sends a mark to his mother. He owes nothing and has six marks in a friary strongbox. A bad month is a mistranslation that costs a carter his load.

## Faction and belief
No faction. An interpreter who takes a side loses both. For a small favour he would translate a letter; for a large one he would translate it wrongly, and carefully; to inform he would translate silence. He is a faithful churchgoer and leaves a coin at a roadside cross near Keila.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian with carters; Low German in the house; Latin at the friary.
- **Delivery:** A warm baritone, even, with a smile in the vowels.
- **Sample lines:** "He says the load is dry. I say the load is dry." "Sit, friend, I will say it gently." "Tere, tere, what do you need?"
- **Verbal tic:** "In other words."

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows the Harju villages' grievances, the carters' names and the levy rolls. He would trade it for a burgher's right. He believes, wrongly, that the friars keep a list of every Estonian who can read.
