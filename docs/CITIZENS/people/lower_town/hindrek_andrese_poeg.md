## Biography
Born in 1272 in a fisher hut below the hill, the son of Eerik, a boatman. At twelve he walked the rope-walk for the old master Hinrik. In the war years after 1313 he laid cable for the Danish ships and saw what they were used for; he does not speak of the dead. He lost a front tooth on a snapped hawser in 1320. His wife died in the hard winter of 1332.

He gave the rope-walk to Peeter in 1335 and now supervises, sleeps late, and tells stories.

## Work and money
He draws no wage; his son keeps him in bread, beer and a place by the fire in return for judging every cable by touch and tooth. He holds one small savings, 4 marks sewn into his belt for his burial, and he refuses to lend it. A bad month for the house is a month in which he coughs and cannot test the cable.

## Faction and belief
None: a man who has seen a war does not choose a side again. He prays at St Olaf for the dead of 1313 and 1332, spits three times at a raven, and tells the children that the sea takes a tithe of every rope it makes. A small favour he gives freely; a large one only to Peeter; he would never inform.

## Knowledge and rumours
He remembers which captains paid in silver and which in promises, and what the Danish steward said in 1318. He would trade it for a warm seat and a listener. He believes the false rumour that the Order is already in the roads with forty ships.
