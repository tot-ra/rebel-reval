## At a glance
- A sturdy girl who speaks high and fast and goes red before she finishes a sentence.
- Born left-handed and beaten into the right, she is clumsy at both.
- Has the best memory in the house for who owes what, and no head for sums.
- Walks home from church with an alewife twice her age.

## Biography
Gertrud was born in 1320 in a hamlet on the Pirita, the daughter of a boatwright called Kaspar. As a child she reached for the spoon with her left hand and her mother rapped it with a ladle; by eight she was right-handed in front of company and left-handed in private. She came to Reval at sixteen and served a cook before Kaur took her in 1339 to manage the dairy. She is easily embarrassed and quickly forgiven, which suits the household, and she has no fixed ambition beyond a Sunday free of scolding.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To be asked to the harvest dance by the carter's son from Pirita.
- **Fear:** Dropping a full tub in front of the master.
- **Contradiction:** She blushes at praise and at scolding alike, and can tell the two apart only by the sound.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She has seen Gesa and Hele go out at night, and gave back a bundle she found, unopened.

## Work and money
She earns her keep, a gown at Christmas and one mark a year. She sells dairy waste to the pig-keepers for a penny. She owes a schilling to the alewife for a ribbon. A bad month is a sour churn.

## Faction and belief
No faction. A maid with no head for sums leaves politics to others. For a small favour she would lend a cloth; for a large one she would blush and refuse; to inform she would talk too much and be believed by nobody. She says the paternoster and leaves a crust for the dairy spirit.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian with friends; bright, fast Low German to the household.
- **Delivery:** High and quick, tumbling words, ending in a flush.
- **Sample lines:** "Oh, I did not mean, I only said, the cream is turning." "Hold the cloth, no, the other hand, oh." "Nobody told me, nobody ever tells me."
- **Verbal tic:** "Oh, bother."

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows who owes the dairy, who sneaks cream, and who goes out at night. She would trade it for a quiet Sunday. She believes, wrongly, that salt in the milk keeps witches off the churn.
