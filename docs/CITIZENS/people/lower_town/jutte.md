## At a glance
- A small, sallow woman at a spindle in the doorway, with a thin voice that carries across the lane.
- Known for thread that is even, fine and always delivered on the day promised.
- Odd for a German in an Estonian household: she understands her mother-in-law's Estonian perfectly and has never once admitted it.

## Biography
Jutte was born in 1305 in Reval to a Westphalian journeyman cooper who died of fever in 1318 and left a mother who took in washing and nothing else. A merchant's widow on Pikk employed her as a spinning-girl at twelve and taught her thread, grain and the habit of watching who came to the door. In 1334 she married Jaan Marteni poeg, an Estonian carrier twelve years her elder, against her mother's pride, because he laughed at the right moments and had a roof. The street never let her forget it, and the German women who once gave her work now give it with a tilt of the head. She lives on Müürivahe with Jaan, his mother, and two boys, spinning for the same Pikk house as ever.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To keep the German world within reach of her sons: a clean coif, a pew, a name that opens a clerk's door.
- **Fear:** Falling so far that the Pikk households stop sending wool.
- **Contradiction:** She resents the street for treating her as a lesser German and loves the Estonian man who made her one.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** Her yarn deliveries to a Pikk merchant's house carry more than yarn; she sews a knot into the third hank when there is word to pass.

## Work and money
She spins for a Pikk merchant's household and earns about a pfennig a pfund of wool, in all roughly a mark every quarter. Raw wool comes from them, flax she buys at the fair. The household's debt to Albert van Paderborne weighs on her more than on Jaan; she keeps the tally on a knotted cord. A bad month is when the Pikk house sends half the wool and the boys need new wooden shoes.

## Faction and belief
Core Hanseatic, from gratitude and fear: the guild households fed her mother and gave her work, and a rising would burn the doors she depends on. She would carry a knot-thread as a small favour, sew a lie into a large one, and hesitate at informing on her own street. She prays the rosary at St Olaf and does not know the Metsik charms Judit whispers over the hens.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Middle Low German with everyone; she understands Estonian but replies in German.
- **Delivery:** Thin and reedy, carrying; she speaks in short complete sentences.
- **Sample lines:** "The third hank is for the clerk, no other." "Dat is nich recht, Jaan." "Wool is dear, and thread is cheap, and I am cheapest."
- **Verbal tic:** Counts under her breath: "eins, twe, dre."

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows which Pikk houses have stopped paying and which clerks are anxious. She would trade it for a place for her sons. She believes the Danes will not hold Toompea past summer, which is hope.
