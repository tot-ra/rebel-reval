## At a glance
- A master carpenter with a house on Pikk, which is more than most Estonians in Reval can say; he keeps it by being useful to Germans.
- Walks the Estonian night-watch squad's round two nights in five, and is the one who never seems to mind the cold.
- Speaks without rise or fall, so that threats, jokes and prices all sound alike.
- Chimney quarrel with his furrier neighbour, a debt owed by a baker, and a standing grudge with a stonemason.

## Biography
Born 1306 in a Harju village, one of five sons of a wheelwright called Villem, and sent to Reval at twelve to a carpenter on Hobusepea who needed hands more than fees. He survived the apprenticeship, the plague-free but hungry winter of 1325, and the master's death, after which he married the widow's debt rather than the widow and paid it off in nine years. A German timber factor stood surety when he bought his house; the favour has never been named and never been repaid in full. That is how a Zimmermann with a missing finger came to be asked, quietly, what the Estonian watch hears on the harbour road. He said what he heard. He has been saying it for six years, and has told himself that it is only like selling a plank.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To leave his sons a house nobody can take back, and a name on the carpenters' roll of the Ämter.
- **Fear:** That the surety is called in, or that the watch squad learns where his talk goes.
- **Contradiction:** Estonian by every word and meal, yet he reports on Estonians to Germans in order to remain among Estonians who have a roof.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He once left out a name, a carter's boy with a stolen axe, and has never decided whether it was mercy or forgetfulness.

## Work and money
Income is house-framing, roof-trusses and joinery for Pikk households, about 6 marks in a fair month, plus timber sold on to smaller builders. The watch pays a few pfennig a night. He is owed 15 marks by a baker for a share in a boat voyage, and bills himself patient. Stock is pine and oak by the lispfund; a bad month is a wet one, with no roofs to frame and Veronika's spinning carrying the household.

## Faction and belief
He leans to the guilds and council money because they build and pay, and he is the kind who honours the hand that holds the surety. For a small favour he will describe a street; for a large one he will name a man, and has done. He cannot refuse an informer's role he already holds. Christian, with a pine twig over the door at midsummer.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian at home; Middle Low German to merchants, careful and correct.
- **Delivery:** Monotone, slow, no emphasis; he repeats the last word of what was said to him before answering.
- **Sample lines:** "Beam's rotten. Rotten at the foot." / "Fifteen marks. I said I would wait. I am waiting." / "Ei midagi. Nothing to tell."
- **Verbal tic:** "As it stands."

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows who walks the harbour road after curfew and which watchmen drink. He would trade it for his debts forgiven. He believes that the Vogt keeps a book of every informer's name, which is false.
