## Biography
Born in Reval in 1320, the son of a German scrivener who died in the 1322 fever, Kersten was nursed by an Estonian wet-nurse from the Harju side and took her cadence into his German, which is why German is never quite his own. He was taught letters by a Dominican brother at St Catherine's and placed in Heyno's counting room at sixteen. He has been a journeyman since 1341. He is unmarried, with no house and no savings, which is why a Blackhead fellow called on him last year. He lodges in the counting room's loft.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A house of his own and a place among the unmarried merchants at table.
- **Fear:** Being found out as a man of two languages, and thus of two loyalties.
- **Contradiction:** He is proud of his Latin and ashamed of his accent.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He copies a few of the master's letters twice, and keeps the second copy in a hollow of the loft beam.

## Work and money

## Faction and belief
He sympathises with the Blackheads because they seat the unmarried, and he is one. A small favour, a letter copied; a large one, he would think about; informing, no. He prays to St Nicholas, and keeps his wet-nurse's blessing, a knot in a thread.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Middle Low German with a sing-song Estonian cadence under it, Latin in the ledger.
- **Delivery:** Sing-song, soft, with rising ends.
- **Sample lines:** "I will copy it fair." "No, I did not say that." "Ma ei tea." (I do not know.)
- **Verbal tic:** "Fair copy."

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows whom the master writes to. He would trade for a place among the Blackheads. He believes the false rumour that the Rat will bar clerks from the weigh-house.
