## At a glance
- A small, shrunken man with a full beard gone mostly grey, standing on a salt-barrel lid to be seen over the herring stalls.
- Every April the skin peels off his nose and forehead in flakes, and the street knows spring has come.
- Odd for a master with a plot of 238 m2: he sells by hollow measure and thumb-taste because his brass beam and weights are pawned.

## Biography
Born in 1280 in the lane behind the Holy Spirit hospital, son of Laurentsi, a salt-porter who bought the plot with a lifetime of sacks. Uku cried herring at the market from the age of twelve; the voice made him. He took over the stall in 1308, married Maret in 1327, and lost her to a winter fever in 1331, leaving a son, Ain, aged two. He married Wendla in 1332, who raised the boy as her own. In 1340 a salt ship was crushed in the ice off Kolga with his share aboard; he borrowed 24 marks from the tanner Tyde and pawned his scales. He is now careful, quiet, and always counting.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To buy back the brass beam and weights before Michaelmas, and see Ain stand in the stall in his place.
- **Fear:** That Tyde sells the pledge to a German who will undercut him with true weights.
- **Contradiction:** Talks loudest in the street and tells his wife least about the debt.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** His late father moved the boundary stake toward the Pikk yard by an arm's length after the wars; Uku has known since he was twenty and has never measured it.

## Work and money
He buys Lüneburg salt by the barrel off the Lübeck ships and Baltic herring from Estonian fishers, and sells in bowls and half-bowls. A good week brings two marks; Lent weeks bring five. Tyde holds his scales and weights for 24 marks; Uku pays in labour, two afternoons a week, which Tyde values at an öre each. He owes the sailmaker half a mark for sacks. A bad month is one without a ship: the barrels run low and the household eats its own stock.

## Faction and belief
Uku keeps out of politics because a debtor who speaks loudly has the debt called. He would carry a barrel for anyone for pay, and say nothing after. He fears inquiry more than any lord. He salts the threshold on Midsummer Eve and kisses the Holy Spirit door-post.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian at home and stall; plain German with buyers; a loud sing-song herring cry for the market.
- **Delivery:** Clear and carrying, with a rising end that makes every sentence a price.
- **Sample lines:** "Salt, good salt, grey as a gull's back, two bowls for a pfennig!" "Ei, I do not weigh it, I know it." "Come back after the bell, master, the barrel is not yet open."
- **Verbal tic:** Licks his thumb and holds it up before naming any price.

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows when salt ships are late before the Lübeck factors do, because the porters talk. He would trade it for a loan against Michaelmas. He believes salt thrown on a drowned man's doorstep wards off the sea, which is folk practice, and that the Pikk merchant is about to buy the whole street, which is false.
