## At a glance
- A sturdy man who barely clears a tall woman's shoulder and fills a doorway anyway.
- Speaks in short, exact sentences and never says "about" when he can say "four and a half".
- Keeps accounts on tally sticks and in his head; has not been out by a pfennig in years.
- Owes the knife-smith half of a roof.

## Biography
Nicolaus was born in 1305 above the cooperage his father Dietrich ([Dietrich van Wismar](../../people/lower_town/dietrich_van_wismar.md)) kept on Rataskaevu, the first of the family to be born in Reval rather than Wismar. At fifteen he went to an old alehouse master on Lühike jalg, a man who taught him the cellar, the small-beer boil and the rule that the measure is the whole trade. The scar is from that house. When the master died the cooperage became a tavern: Dietrich retired to a stool by the fire and Nicolaus put his own sign over the door in 1336. He never learned letters, so he learned numbers by notch, and trusts nothing he cannot hold. A new roof in autumn 1342 left him in debt for the first time.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To clear the roof bond before Michaelmas without selling a cask on credit.
- **Fear:** A bad batch that sours a whole week's trade and the street's talk with it.
- **Contradiction:** He prides himself on fair measure yet has twice held back stock to win an argument.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He waters the small beer one part in ten on fast days and tells himself the Church would approve.

- **Sundays and feast days:** Mass at St Nicholas, then the house is shut until noon; he sits with his father and says very little.
- **Spring 1343 disruption:** Grain-levy talk fills the tap-room and empties purses; he listens, serves, and repeats nothing.

## Work and money
He brews small beer and buys strong ale in, selling over the counter and by the cask to stalls and kitchens. A good week clears about a mark and a half after malt, wood and the maid's keep. He owes Magnus Birgersson 6 marks on the roof, borrowed in autumn 1342, due at Michaelmas, with about 3 paid. A bad month is a wet malt delivery or a drunken fight that costs a table.

## Faction and belief
No faction. A tapster who takes a side loses half his customers, so he takes none, and says so. He lights a candle at St Nicholas for his dead wife Gertrud, and pours the first dregs of each brew on the cellar floor, as his father did.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Low German only; blunt with customers, careful with the Vogt's men.
- **Delivery:** Clipped and precise, quiet, with pauses where numbers go.
- **Sample lines:** "Four and a half, not five." "Pay on the day or drink at another door." "Lat dat sin." (Let it be.)
- **Verbal tic:** Ends statements with "exactly."

## Knowledge and rumours
He hears every complaint on the street and who drinks beyond his means. He would trade it for time on the roof bond. He believes the false rumour that the council will tax small beer by the cask.
