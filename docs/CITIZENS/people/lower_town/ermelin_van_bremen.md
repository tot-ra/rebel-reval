- Spins by the window every free hour, even on feast-day evenings, and never lets the yarn lie idle.
- Speaks German with a sing-song lilt that older Estonian servants answer in kind.
- Known on Lai for her tidy house and her quick, quiet hand with a coin.
- Oddity: a Hanseatic merchant's wife who is the Danish side's most reliable ear.

## Biography
Born in Reval about 1295, the daughter of a Bremen wine-factor and an Estonian dairymaid from the Harju edge. She was raised by her mother's kin as much as her father's, which left the lilt in her voice. At twenty-two she married [Meinhard Wise](./meinhard_wise.md), who needed a wife who could manage servants of both tongues. Her father's wine reached the viceroy's table on Toompea, and that was how she first carried a note up the hill. Now she runs the house and spins; the yarn pays for the household's linen and the small silver she gives away.

## Motivation
- **Want:** Safety for her sons and a Reval that stays under the Danish crown's tolerable peace.
- **Fear:** Rebels in the lanes, burning, and her husband discovering who she writes to.
- **Contradiction:** She loves the town yet thinks the council's rule is greedy; she fears Toompea's soldiers and still feeds them news.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She passes small items (who is short of money, which councillor is angry) to a Toompea contact after mass, and she knows the Hanseatic circle meets in her own house.

## Work and money
The household income is her husband's; hers is her yarn, sold to Lai weavers for about 6 schillings a lispfund-weight batch and spun for the household's linen. She keeps a small purse of about 3 marks of her own. A bad month is a late shipment and no money for the maids' wages at quarter-day.

## Faction and belief
Her faith in the Danish crown is born of her father's trade and of a quiet fear of the council's grain men. She would carry a small message, pass a name for a larger one, but never inform on her husband. She attends St Nicholas and keeps a sprig of rowan above the hearth.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Low German in the hall, Estonian with the maids and her mother's kin.
- **Delivery:** Soft, quick, with a singing rise at sentence-ends; slows when worried.
- **Sample lines:** "Leave it, I will do it." "Heaven keep you, child, and keep your tongue." "Mu arm, mu arm" when her hand shakes.
- **Verbal tic:** "There now."

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows who owes the house and who visits Toompea. She would trade for her sons' safe passage. She believes that the rebels plan to burn the Lai warehouses, which is false.
