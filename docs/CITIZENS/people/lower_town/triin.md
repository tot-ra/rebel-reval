## At a glance
- A stocky woman with wind-reddened cheeks who goes to market with a basket on her hip and says almost nothing.
- She takes long pauses before answering, so buyers wait, and often drop the price.
- Odd for a market-woman: her silence is her bargaining.

## Biography
Born in 1312 in a Harju village, one of five. She came to town at sixteen and has served the Rode kitchen for fifteen years, since before the children were born. She taught the eldest to eat porridge. She sends her wages home at Michaelmas.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To keep her place and be left alone.
- **Fear:** Being put out in a bad winter.
- **Contradiction:** Silent in the house and loud in her thoughts.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She keeps a half-pfennig of every purchase in a jug behind the hearth.

- **Sundays and feast days:** Mass at St Olaf's, then a rest by the kitchen hearth.
- **Spring 1343 disruption:** Prices climb and she brings back less; she hears the levy talk at the stalls.

## Work and money
She earns a mark a year and keep. She spends about two marks a month at the market for the house and counts the change twice. She owes nothing. A bad month is one when the mistress doubts her change.

## Faction and belief
No faction. She hears everything at the market and repeats nothing. She would shelter a girl in the kitchen for a night. She leaves milk by the hearth at Midsummer.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian at home; slow German at market.
- **Delivery:** Slow, with long pauses, as if each word were weighed.
- **Sample lines:** "...Three pfennig." "Ei. No." "...I will think."
- **Verbal tic:** Holds her basket with both hands before answering.

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows how much every house on Raekoja ate. She would trade it for a day's rest. She believes the new moon spoils fish, which is folk belief.
