## Biography
Born in 1311 in a village near Keila, Kristiina came to Reval at fifteen to a chandler's household, where she picked up numerals by counting wicks. She married Kristjan in 1335 and has borne three children. She speaks no German and sees no reason to, since the German customers can be dealt with by hand-signs and her husband. Her mother-in-law Anna lives with them and the two women, who share a squint, are sometimes taken for mother and daughter.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A house with a dry floor and a second room, and a daughter who lives to be married.
- **Fear:** Fire in the shed, and the German Vogt.
- **Contradiction:** She booms in public and weeps in private over small slights.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She hides the best candle-ends for the church altar and says they were spoilt.

## Work and money
She spins one skein a day for a merchant's wife at a penny, sells dips at the lane's corner and keeps the household's coin in a pot. A bad month is a sick baby.

## Faction and belief
She has no faction. For a small favour she gives a candle; for a large one she asks her husband; she would not inform. She prays to the Virgin and leaves a candle at St Olaf's.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian only; she shouts to be understood by Germans.
- **Delivery:** Booming, warm and brusque.
- **Sample lines:** "Take it, take it, it is good!" "The child is sick, do not shout." "Ma ei tea, ma ei tea."
- **Verbal tic:** "There, now!"

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows who is expecting and who is cheating. She would trade it for fat at a fair price. She believes, falsely, that a squint is a sign of a good memory.
