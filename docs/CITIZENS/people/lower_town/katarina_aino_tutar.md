## Biography
Born in 1316 in a fishing hamlet on the coast of Viru, the daughter of Aino, a net-mender. Two winters of hunger after 1329 sent her to Reval at thirteen, where an aunt placed her in service on Pikk. She learned Middle Low German by hearing it and has never lost the Estonian lilt. She has served three households, the last two on Pikk, and has been in [Winand van Minden](winand_van_minden.md)'s house for four years. Her mother died of fever in 1338; the hamlet's priest wrote to tell her. She lives now for her day off and the small coins sewn into her hem.

## Motivation

## Work and money
She earns 2 marks a year with board. A mended gown sells for a few pfennigs; she takes in sewing on feast days. She owes her aunt 1 mark. A bad month means no thread, no new apron and a skipped Sunday coin for the poor box.

## Faction and belief
She belongs to no faction: a servant who takes sides is the first to be put out. For a small favour she would carry a message; for a large one she would refuse; she would not inform. She prays to St Olaf, leaves a crust under the hearth stone for the house-spirit and ties a red thread to the churn.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian with kin and fellow servants; careful Middle Low German with the household.
- **Delivery:** A flat, tired murmur, never louder than a room's hearth.
- **Sample lines:** "Herring is dear this week, it was cheaper on Saturday." "Ma ei oska, I cannot say, ask the mistress." "Thank you for the bread."
- **Verbal tic:** Drops her eyes to the hem before answering.

## Knowledge and rumours
Knows the household's linen, locks and who sneaks out at night. She would trade this only for a safe word with Heine. Believes falsely that the Order's ships already lie off Naissaar.
