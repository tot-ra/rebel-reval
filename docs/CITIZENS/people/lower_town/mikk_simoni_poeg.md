## Biography
Mikk was born in 1281 to an Estonian grain-dealer in the Lower Town and married Mett in 1303. He took the oath in 1305, was trained in German houses, and built a house of stone by 1320. He has fathered five children, of whom two live: Ain and Ruth. Last year he was fined nine marks for forestalling, buying grain at the gate before the market bell, and Lutke van Brunswik stood surety for him.

He cannot sleep past three, and at the window he has seen the baker Jaak twice slip out of the back lane after curfew.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A merchant bench seat for Ain, even if only by marriage.
- **Fear:** A lawsuit over the forestalling fine.
- **Contradiction:** He bribes the market clerk and lectures his apprentices on honesty.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He still owes Lutke van Brunswik the nine marks.

## Work and money
He trades grain, wax and salt, with some 700 marks in stock at the warehouse. Dmitri Fedorovich owes him forty-five marks from a dowry loan. He sells to the skipper Jaan Alberti poeg and has twice held back stock to press an old point. A bad month is a seized cargo.

## Faction and belief
He has no faction: the council will not have him and the rebels would take his warehouse. A small favour he does for interest; a large one never; informing he might, if it kept his silver. He keeps a St Nicholas candle and a Harju charm in the same chest.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian at home; Middle Low German in trade; Latin only for oaths.
- **Delivery:** Booming, interrupting, lowers his voice to a whisper when afraid.
- **Sample lines:** "Weigh it again, and weigh it honestly this time." / "I sleep little and I see much." / "Mina ei räägi."

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows who leaves the baker's back lane after curfew and what lies on the quays. He would trade it for the nine marks. He believes, wrongly, that Lambrecht is a smuggler for the Vitalienbrüder.
