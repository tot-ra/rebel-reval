## Biography
Born 1320 in Soest in Westphalia, one of seven children of a tanner. His father died in 1334, and an uncle sent Lutke at fourteen to Reval with a letter. He lodged with an acquaintance in a Pikk lane in the winter of 1337-38, when the harbour froze early and bread was short; neighbours shared what they had. He joined Borchard's counting-room in 1339 and has risen through accuracy, not charm. He writes with his left hand, which a Lübeck clerk once tried to beat out of him.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A clerk's post with a share in a cargo.
- **Fear:** Hunger returning, and losing the post through a slip of the pen.
- **Contradiction:** Fast-talking, yet never says anything of consequence.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He once copied an entry wrongly and quietly paid the difference from his wages.

## Faction and belief
None; he cannot afford a side. A small favour he gives, a large one he refuses, and informing he would do only under threat. He prays the psalms of St Nicholas before a voyage and leaves a coin for the poor.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Middle Low German; Latin for the clerks' proverbs.
- **Delivery:** High and quick, swallowing the ends of words.
- **Sample lines:** "Not today, sir, the ink is wet!" / "Seven bales, not eight, I counted." / "Tante, you are too kind."
- **Verbal tic:** "By my count."

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows every cargo that passed the quay in a month. He would trade it for a senior post. He believes Westphalian beer cures a fever, which is false.
