## Biography
Born 1304 in Reval to a merchant family from Herford in Westphalia. Apprenticed in Lübeck at twelve, he returned in 1325 to take over his father's cloth and wax trade. In 1328 he borrowed 30 marks from [Berend van Wismar](berend_van_wismar.md) to pay the marriage settlement to the van Brunswik family; the marriage with [Gyse van Brunswik](gyse_van_brunswik.md) went ahead and the debt remains. Council in 1334, burgomaster in 1341. He has since learned that every decision costs a friend.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To hand his son a house with no debt and a council seat.
- **Fear:** A grain levy so heavy it brings the harbour to a halt.
- **Contradiction:** A burgomaster sworn to the order who privately thinks its rules unjust.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He has twice seen a servant leave his neighbour's house after curfew and has told no one.

## Work and money
His trade is cloth, wax and herring between Reval and Lübeck; a good cargo nets perhaps 40 marks. Coopers and porters supply casks and handling; delays from either cost him. He owes Berend 30 marks and pays interest in wax each Candlemas. A bad month is a late ship.

## Faction and belief
Core member of the council order, as his office demands. His own reason is the harbour: if the hill's levy empties it, no household on Lai is safe. His quiet sympathies lie with those squeezed by the same hand. A small favour he grants; a large one only if the council is not named; informing he would do if his children were threatened. He tells his paternoster beads and hears Mass at St Nicholas.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Low German for business and council; Latin for charters and prayers.
- **Delivery:** Monotone, even when furious; his neck reddens instead.
- **Sample lines:** "The council has noted your concern." / "Wax is wax, sir." / "Ik slape nicht, but I listen."

## Knowledge and rumours
Knows who is behind on council taxes and who has quietly sold ships. He would trade it for debt relief. He falsely believes the Livonian master already has a spy within the council.
