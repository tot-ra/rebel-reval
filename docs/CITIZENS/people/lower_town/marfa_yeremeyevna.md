## At a glance

- She bakes rye and rusks for half of Raekoja, and keeps her debts in her head and her loaves on a long peel.

## Biography
Born in 1310 at Pskov, daughter of Yeremey, a miller. She married Grigory, a baker who traded to Reval, in 1331 and came with him. He died on the Narva road in 1340, leaving her the oven, two daughters, and his mother Fevronia. She took in the orphaned children of Grigory's brothers. She hired a Swedish journeyman, Gunnar, and kept the oven lit.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To keep the oven and see all five girls fed.
- **Fear:** A guild ruling that closes a Russian's shop.
- **Contradiction:** Suspicious of Germans and owes her fine to one.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She sends a tenth of her takings to a Pskov church box through a carter.

## Work and money
She buys rye from a Harju miller on credit, and sells loaves at a pfennig each. A good week is two marks. Albert holds her peel and trough as pledge for nine marks; she bakes for his house to pay it off. She holds a weaver's tools as pledge for five marks. She is owed five by a brewer. She owes Berend six. A bad month is a wet one: the dough will not rise.

## Faction and belief
She leans toward Pskov and Novgorod because her roads and kin are there, and the Germans' staple rules cost her half her market. She would pass a letter for a small favour, shelter a carter for a large one, and would not betray a neighbour. She crosses the dough and kisses the first loaf.

## Voice

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows who in Raekoja has not paid for bread. She would trade it for a note on her fine. She believes the Germans will close the Russian chapel, which is false.
