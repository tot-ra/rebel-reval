## Biography
Born 1298 in a Harju village to a German bailiff's household and nursed by an Estonian woman, Anu, who sang her to sleep and taught her to count in Estonian before German. Sent to Reval at twelve as a kinswoman's help, she married Hildebrand at sixteen with a modest dowry and a good head for figures. Of nine births she has raised six; the three buried at St Nicholas lie under one small stone. In 1336 she took a corner of the front room for a Krämer's stall, after the Rat allowed wives to trade in the husband's name. She laughs loudly because her mother-in-law once told her not to.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To see Lysbeth married into a house with a courtyard and Arend into the church school without charity.
- **Fear:** That her children inherit a street where grain cannot be had at any price.
- **Contradiction:** Sells to Estonians gladly and would not let one sit at her table.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She is part of a circle she was drawn into a year ago by a kindness, and she tells Hildebrand nothing of it.

## Work and money
She buys thread and pins from Lübeck packmen, oil from Karja carters, spices from a Hanseatic factor, and sells at a slight margin by weight. Takings perhaps 60 marks a year. Lamp-oil is a vice: she burns too much late in the shop. A bad month is a stolen weight and a lost customer.

## Faction and belief
She is Hanseatic by blood and trade, and sympathetic by temper. A small favour she gives gladly, a large one she gives only if a child is at risk. Hearth-bread offered to the house spirit when the baker is not looking.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Low German with the shop; a few Estonian words with Anu's songs and the maids.
- **Delivery:** Sing-song, lilt rising at the end; loud for a woman, soft at prayer.
- **Sample lines:** "Two pfennig the dozen, and I count honest." "Tuesdays the oil comes; don't be late." "Mind the lamp, girl."
- **Verbal tic:** "Ja, ja, leve," said while she counts.

## Knowledge and rumours
She hears prices and marriages; she would trade a rumour of a grain levy for a safe corner. False belief: that the Dominicans are hoarding salt.
