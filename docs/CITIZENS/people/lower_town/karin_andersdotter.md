## At a glance
- A stooped, round-bellied girl with ears that stick out under her coif, always carrying a bucket or a basket.
- Speaks so softly that people lean in, and so she hears more than she says.
- Oddity: she keeps a small trade in pins, ribbon and needles that no one has ever called overpriced.

## Biography
Born on the Kopli shore to a Swedish boatman's widow, Karin was sent to Reval at twelve and placed with Jöns Gunnarsson through a parish contact. She has scrubbed his floors for seven years for bed, board and a mark and a half a year. Her mother died two Lents ago; a brother fishes out of Kopli. She has no dowry, so she is making one in pennies. A scrap of red ribbon she bought at Candlemas, never worn, sits in her pin-paper as a reminder of what she is saving for.

## Motivation
- **Want:** Twenty marks, a husband with a boat, a window of her own.
- **Fear:** Getting ill and being turned out.
- **Contradiction:** She is meek in the house yet a stubborn haggler at the gate.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She has counted nineteen schillings under the loose hearthstone and told nobody, not even Sigrid.

## Work and money
Wages: a mark and a half a year plus shoes at Michaelmas. Side trade: pins, needle-papers and thread-ends bought from a Krämer at cost plus a pfennig; about four schillings a month profit. She pays no rent, owes nobody, and keeps the proceeds in a knotted cloth.

## Faction and belief
No faction; a maid on a mark and a half cannot afford a side. A small favour she gives; a large one she cannot. She would not inform, being too frightened of being identified. Prays to St Olaf for calm seas for her brother.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Swedish in the house, Middle Low German at the gate, both barely above a whisper.
- **Delivery:** Soft, breathy, almost whispering; sentences trail.
- **Sample lines:** "Pins, two for a pfennig, and I'll not charge you for the paper." / "I only heard it, I did not say it." / "Ja, ja, I'll fetch it."
- **Verbal tic:** "I only heard."

## Knowledge and rumours
She hears the lane's small news first. She believes, wrongly, that wearing a knot of red thread keeps fever from the house.
