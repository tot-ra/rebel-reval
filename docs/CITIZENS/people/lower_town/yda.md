## At a glance
- Spins at the hall window so that passers-by see a quiet house.
- Her soft, nearly whispered voice forces servants to lean in.
- Keeps the household books in numerals and chalk.
- Has no byname, only the old Hanseatic habit of her given name.

## Biography
Born 1308 in Reval, daughter of a Lai grocer who was ruined by the winter of 1317 and left his daughter to a convent school for three years. She married Borchard in 1334 with a dowry of 60 marks and has run the household since, burying one infant son in 1338. She learned numerals from the nuns of St Michael and keeps accounts in chalk before the clerks copy them. She came to the house as a stranger to its quarrels, and has spent nine years making herself indispensable and invisible.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A quiet house, and her daughters schooled with the same nuns who taught her.
- **Fear:** Her husband's credit failing, and the hall emptied by creditors.
- **Contradiction:** Speaks in a whisper yet governs twelve people with no raised voice.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She reads Borchard's chalk marks on the beam and knows how many men owe him quietly.

## Work and money
She spins yarn for house use and sells surplus, about 2 marks a month; mostly she manages the budget of a household that eats about 5 marks a week. A bad month is a late ship and a bare larder.

## Faction and belief
None. Politics in her house is the husband's affair, and she prefers not to know details. A small favour she grants quietly; a large one she refers to Borchard; informing she would do only to save her daughters. She keeps the feast of St Gertrude with a spun-flax offering at the altar.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Middle Low German only.
- **Delivery:** Soft, almost whispering; servants learn to lean in.

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows each servant's habits and each family debt on the street. She would trade it for her daughters' safety. She believes a bad omen follows a dropped spindle, which is false.
