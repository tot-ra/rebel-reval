## Biography
Born in 1274 in a Harju village on a monastery grange, Judit learned tablet-weaving from her mother and carried it to Reval at nineteen. She wove belts for the journeymen's festivals, garters for the Swedish captains and braids for dowries. She married Marten, a mason's carrier, who died of a fall from the wall scaffold in 1320, and she raised Jaan in a lane that burned twice. Her eyes tired in 1336 and she stopped weaving at the loom's width for the market, keeping only narrow bands. At the burial of an old friend in the autumn of 1342, she heard the whisper that men called the old way and has repeated what she hears ever since.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To see Kornel past his sixth winter and Laurents past his third, and to have her bones laid with the right words.
- **Fear:** The Rat's bailiff finding the grove-offerings in her bag.
- **Contradiction:** She attends every Mass and trusts the old ways more than the priest.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** Her bands carry patterns that mean something to the cell.

## Work and money
She sells belts, garters and narrow bands for two to six pfennigs each; in a good quarter that is a mark, which she gives to Jutte. Her thread comes from Jutte's leftover flax and the dyer's rejects. She owes nothing, and prefers it. A bad month is one when her fingers ache and the thread knots.

## Faction and belief
Cult of Metsik, informer, by old loyalty: she grew up with the grove offerings and sees no quarrel between them and the Mass. She tells the cell what is said at the market and the church door, never names. For a small favour she would leave a band on a gatepost; for a large one she would hide an offering bag; to inform on the cell she would say nothing and pretend deafness. She leaves a bowl of porridge for the house-spirit at the hearth.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian at home; short, plain Low German at market.
- **Delivery:** Low and unhurried, a pause before every answer.
- **Sample lines:** "Seven and seven, and the seventh is blue." "Leave it, boy. The hen knows." "Ei, I did not hear. I am old."
- **Verbal tic:** Counts the tablets under her breath before replying.

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows which church-door whispers are repeated twice and by whom. She would trade it for a safe grave. She believes a speckled hen warns of fire, which is folk belief.
