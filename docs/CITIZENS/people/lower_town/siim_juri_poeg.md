## At a glance
- The loudest voice on the lane; you hear him sawing, singing and arguing before you see him.
- Supplies scaffold poles, shuttering and joists to the stonemason Paul Antsu poeg on quarter-day credit.
- Is paid by the piece, and is proud of it.
- Wants nothing from the faction men; he wants a bench of his own.

## Biography
Born 1320 in a fishing hamlet near the Kopli peninsula, a fisherman Jüri's second son who followed the boats until a squall drowned his father in 1333. His mother took him to Reval at thirteen, and a Hobusepea carpenter took him on. Four years later Lauri bought his indenture and finished the training. Siim became a journeyman in 1341 and has worked beam and plank since. He sings at the bench, and Veronika has twice told him to hush. He is paid by the piece, and has the habit of counting wages on his fingers and losing track.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A bench of his own and a wife with a dry house.
- **Fear:** A fall from a roof, the ruin of a workman's body.
- **Contradiction:** Loud and merry, yet shy of any woman who speaks to him first.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He once cut a joint wrong and hid it under plaster to avoid blame.

## Work and money
Paid by the piece, about 3 marks a month with board; Paul Antsu poeg takes timber on credit settled each quarter-day. A late payment at Easter would cost Siim a month's wage. He owns his tools and a good adze.

## Faction and belief
None. He sees no gain in sides; a wage is a wage, and the price of oak is the only politics he follows. A small favour, carrying a beam, he gives; a large one he would not; informing he thinks is shameful. He carves a cross into the first beam of every roof and a small sun beside it.

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows which roofs on Pikk leak and which beams are rotten. He would trade it for a good tavern evening. He believes the mason's yard is haunted by a drowned apprentice, which is false.
