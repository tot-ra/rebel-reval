## Biography
Helga was born in 1327 in Reval, the eldest living of Anna and Bengt Magnusson's children and the one who has carried the others since she was six. She learned to spin from her mother, to count by the tally-string, and to read the market from the stall-keepers. She is old enough to be spoken for and has noticed it. Her grandfather Magnus tells her the Visby tales; her father tells her nothing, which she has noticed too.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To keep the household's accounts straight and to marry where she is not a drudge.
- **Fear:** A bad marriage, and her father's debts falling on her dowry.
- **Contradiction:** She prides herself on being practical and has been seen smiling at a day-labourer's jokes.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She has counted the night-time visits to her father's shed and kept the number to herself.

## Work and money
She spins two skeins a day and is given two pfennigs a quarter for ribbon. She keeps the household's rye tally and notices when the price rises a farthing. A bad week is a sick sister.

## Faction and belief
She has no faction. For a small favour she carries a message; for a large one she tells her mother; she would not inform, for she would not know what to tell. She prays to St Catherine and avoids the cellar where her father keeps the cloth.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Swedish at home; neat Middle Low German in the market.
- **Delivery:** Clipped and precise, each sum said once and not repeated.
- **Sample lines:** "Two pfennigs short." "I can count, thank you." "Det stämmer inte."

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows the rye price on every stall. She would trade it for a pair of better shoes. She believes, falsely, that anyone who marries in May will die poor.
