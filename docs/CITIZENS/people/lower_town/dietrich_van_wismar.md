- Speaks German with the sing-song of the Estonian lanes, which he learned before he learned guild manners.

- Quietly does small kindnesses, and remembers who took them.

## Biography
Dietrich was born in 1277 to a Wismar cooper who came north for the herring trade and an Estonian washerwoman he married after the fact. He grew up in the lanes beside the harbour, with Estonian boys for playmates and his mother's songs in his ear. He took the cooper's trade at twelve, became a master in 1303 and trained half the cellarmen on the street. In the wars of 1313-1325 he made the water-casks for the walls and never forgot that the Hansa paid for oak when the Crown did not. His wife died in 1330; he handed the cooperage to his son Nicolaus, who made a tavern of it ([Nicolaus van Wismar](../../people/lower_town/nicolaus_van_wismar.md)).

## Motivation
- **Want:** A quiet street where oak, salt and hoops still arrive on the first ship of the season.
- **Fear:** A siege, and barrels with nothing to carry.
- **Contradiction:** He loves the Estonian lanes that made him, yet backs the Germans who rule them.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He carries a message from time to time because someone once paid his doctor.

## Work and money
Retired; no income of his own beyond a modest rent from a hoop-stock shed his son lets him keep, about 8 schillings a quarter. He lives on his son's bread and gives back advice. He owns his cooper's tools, a chest and a bed, and is owed a favour or two by older men. A bad month is a shipment of oak that is sold before he can inspect it.

## Faction and belief
Hanseatic sympathiser, mild. His reason is oak: the Hansa's ships bring staves, hoops and salt, and a town cut off starves. He would carry a message across the street, give a night's lodging to a trusted man, but not take a knife to anyone. He keeps St Nicholas's candle, and a hearth spoon of milk for the house-spirit.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Low German with a lilt; Estonian only when alone with servants.
- **Delivery:** Soft, sing-song, slow; stops mid-sentence to listen.
- **Sample lines:** "Oak does not lie, boy." "I was young when the walls were new." "Ole poeg, tule." (Old son, come.)
- **Verbal tic:** Says "ja, ja" between sentences.

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows which ships bring oak, which cellars leak and who is behind with the cooper's bill. He would trade it for a quiet word about his son's debt. He believes the false rumour that the Order is already in the harbour.
