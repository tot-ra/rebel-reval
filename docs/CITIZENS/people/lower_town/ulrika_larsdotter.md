## At a glance
- Sells needles, thread, ribbon, wax and dyed wool from a stall and from the front room; counts in her head and never writes a sum.
- Gravelly voice that carries over the market without being raised.
- Walks wide-legged and rolling, and has begun to carry her belly in front of her.
- Speaks only Swedish; buys German words as she needs them, by the pennyworth.

## Biography
Born 1317 on Gotland, daughter of a fisherman and salter, Lars Fisk, who died at sea in 1334. Her mother sent her to Reval at seventeen to a cousin's stall, and she learned numbers from the market itself. She married Erik Folkesson in 1340 with a dowry of 20 marks she had earned and kept. Kristina was born in 1342. She keeps her own stall, a strip of the front room, and her own small purse. She thinks of herself as the household's truer merchant, and is probably right.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To make the stall large enough to buy a house of her own, in her own name.
- **Fear:** Dying in childbirth with the purse unrevealed.
- **Contradiction:** Hard bargainer with strangers; cries over a dead sparrow.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** Seven marks in a salt crock under the loft stair, and a second child she has not yet told Erik about.

## Work and money
She buys thread, needles and wax from pedlars and the Lübeck ships, wool from the spinners, and clears roughly 8 marks a month in the spring. Her stall pays for the household's ale and for her purse. A bad month is a bad winter: sales drop, but the thread keeps.

## Faction and belief
No faction; she keeps out of politics because politics cannot be weighed. Asked for a small favour she gives credit, for a large one she asks collateral, to inform she says nothing and means it. She keeps a rowan twig and a Gotland tale of a sea-woman who counts the fishermen.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Swedish at home and in the stall; scraps of Middle Low German, always in the third person.
- **Delivery:** Gravelly, steady, low pace; counts on her fingers while she speaks.
- **Sample lines:** "Two örtug, and you may count them." / "I weigh it, you pay it." / "Jo, jo, come tomorrow."
- **Verbal tic:** "By weight."

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows which stalls dilute the dye and which pedlar has stolen stock. She would trade it for a better pitch. She believes the false rumour that the nunnery sells short-weight wax.
