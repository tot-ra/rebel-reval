## Biography
Wendla was born in 1304 in a fisher family at the harbour, the daughter of Siim, and went to the washing-places at nine when her mother's lungs failed. She slipped on the ice in the winter of 1330 and carries the scar, and says nothing of how she was carried home. She married Hinrick, then a journeyman, in 1331 and brought her brother Simon into the yard as ostler when he was dismissed from a Pikk stable. She earns by taking in the laundry of four households and by dealing in lye. Her mother taught her the Metsik words for fire and water, and she has said them over the tub every day since.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To keep her name and price at the washing-stage and to see Triin safely married.
- **Fear:** The Rat's inspectors finding out whose linen she has carried messages in.
- **Contradiction:** A woman of the fraternity at the feast masses who holds the old words in her mouth.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She knows her husband carries messages and has never told him that she knows.

## Work and money
Four pfennigs per bundle of linen and a mark a month for lye soap sold on; the household takes about three marks a month from her tub. Wood for the copper she buys by the cart, ash from the bakers. She is owed 12 pfennigs by a Pikk house. A bad month is one when the shore ice breaks late.

## Faction and belief
Cult of Metsik, secret, from her mother's words: the old rites seem to her as ordinary as soap. She would repeat a word in the tub as a small favour, hide a small bundle as a large one, and go silent if pushed to inform. She says the Paternoster and leaves ash at the hearth for the house-spirit.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian only; she answers German in short nods and glances.
- **Delivery:** A sing-song Estonian cadence, low, with the end of each sentence rising and falling.
- **Sample lines:** "A clean sheet tells no tales." "Water remembers, so we say." "Ei, that is not my price."

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows which house has a sick child and which has a new mistress, from the sheets. She would trade it for her daughter's safe dowry. She believes lye-water kept on a Thursday heals chilblains, which is folk belief.
