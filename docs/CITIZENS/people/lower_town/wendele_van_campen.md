- Sturdy and short, with a voice that carries across the yard and a wart she covers with her thumb when she is shy.

## Biography
Wendele was born in 1328 in the big house on Sauna street, the first child after two that did not live. She learned the spindle at five, numerals at nine from her mother's tally-strings, and prayers from the nurse. She expects to be betrothed within two years and has opinions about that. She has her mother's flush and her father's nerve, and sometimes the nerve gets her slapped. She is proud of the thread-work and a little afraid of the cellar, where the casks are and the rats.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To be trusted with her own wheel and a share of the outworkers' accounts before she is married off.
- **Fear:** Being sent to a husband in Lübeck, and her mother's disappointment.
- **Contradiction:** She is the loudest in the house and the one most afraid of speaking at the church door.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She keeps a pfennig-hoard of ribbon money under a loose board and has lent some to a younger servant.

## Work and money
She spins for the house, not for pay, and has her mother's leave to sell her own spare thread for a penny or two. She buys needles, ribbon and pins from small sellers and gets a fair price. Her purse holds seven pfennigs at most.

## Faction and belief
She takes no side. At fourteen her politics are her father's moods and the price of thread. If asked for a small favour she will carry a message; a large one she would tell her mother; she would never inform, since she does not know what to tell. She keeps St Catherine as her saint and ties a red thread on the cradle for luck.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Middle Low German, loud and rapid; hushed in church.
- **Delivery:** Clear, carrying, with a trailing laugh; she shouts across the yard without meaning to.
- **Sample lines:** "Mother says three skeins, not two!" "Buy from Judit, she gives honest weight." "Sach dat nich wieder!"
- **Verbal tic:** "Truly, truly."

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows which seller gives short measure and who is going to marry whom. She would trade this for a day off from the wheel. She believes, falsely, that the cellar rats are an ill omen for the family cargo.
