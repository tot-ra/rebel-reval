## Biography
Gyse was born in 1305 in Goslar, a mining-town merchant's daughter, and was sent to Reval at twenty-one to marry [Wolter van Hamelen](../../people/lower_town/wolter_van_hamelen.md), whom she had met twice. She has borne nine children, and buried two. She learned to keep accounts from her mother and to bargain from the Goslar market. After the first bad year she began buying raw flax and wool thread from Estonian outworkers, a business she now directs from the upper rooms with eleven women on the books. She is the only one in the household who has noticed that the 18-mark fine has not been paid.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To marry Egbert and Drude well and see all seven living children grown.
- **Fear:** A seizure of the house, with the nursery above.
- **Contradiction:** She scolds the children for waste yet hoards candle-ends in a box.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She has sewn a mark's worth of thread-money into the hem of a winter cloak in case the house must be left in a night.

## Work and money
She buys raw thread by the pfund from outworkers and sells spun thread and plain linen to cap-makers and weavers. She keeps her own purse, about twenty marks a year clear, and quietly funds the household's small bills. The 18-mark fine on her husband she knows of, and has not named. A bad month is a late flax cart or a spoiled batch.

## Faction and belief
No faction. She is a patrician's wife and does not have a vote, but her purse has more weight in the house than the council does. For a small favour she would give thread; for a large one she would give her name, if her husband agreed; to inform she would not, since she would be asked about the fine. She says the rosary at night and leaves a bit of wool at the hearth on Candlemas.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Middle Low German always, loud and plain.
- **Delivery:** Booming, quick, carrying across a market and quieting only for the baby.
- **Sample lines:** "Three pfund, not two and a half, and no cheating!" "Wolter, speak up, the clerk cannot hear you." "Hush, the child is asleep and I am not."
- **Verbal tic:** "Mark me."

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows what each woman in the lanes spins and who is behind on rent, and what the household really owes. She would trade it for safe passage for the children. She believes, wrongly, that wool washed in rain-water spins finer.
