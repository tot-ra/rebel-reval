## At a glance
- Lean, wiry, one shoulder higher than the other, with a pale scar across a cleft chin.
- Talks in short exact sentences, the way one speaks to a nervous horse.
- Hires out the van Soest cart-horses on the side and sells travellers' fodder, which other ostlers resent.
- Has a bad habit of knowing the price of oats to the pfennig.

## Biography
Born in 1326 in Lübeck's Hundestrasse, the son of a carter named Overdyk who left for the Flemish roads and did not return. Taken to Reval at ten by a carrier's wife and apprenticed to an old ostler at the Toompea lower yard, who also trained the Toompea ostler Tõnis Alberti poeg, a grown man taken late. Hired by the van Soests at fifteen, after a hard winter. The chin scar is a roan mare's kick, earned the day he stayed in the stall to settle her.

He sleeps over the stable on straw and his own cloak, and speaks of his master's house as "ours".

## Motivation
- **Want:** To own a pair of horses and a license to hire them out, before he is twenty-five.
- **Fear:** Being named a thief, and being flogged for it.
- **Contradiction:** Painfully exact about money in public, careless about whose oats he sells when the master is away.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He buys cheap bags of oats from a carter at the Sand Gate who asks no questions, and sells them on at the going price.

## Work and money
One mark a year and his keep, plus a few pfennigs per horse hired out, which he keeps. Tools: curry-comb, hoof-pick, pitchfork, a leather bucket. He holds six pfennigs and owes the cook two. A bad month is a lame horse.

## Faction and belief
No faction. A servant who is German by birth and poor by trade stays out of politics because both sides would use him; he would take a message for a coin and no more. He keeps St Eligius (patron of horses) and spits for luck.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Middle Low German, clipped and exact, with horse-talk.
- **Delivery:** Clipped, fast, almost no pauses.
- **Sample lines:** "Four pfennigs, not five." "Easy there, easy." "That mare, she bites."
- **Verbal tic:** Ends sentences with "so."

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows which riders left the gates at night and which horses were taken in short order. He would trade it for an honest price on oats. He believes, wrongly, that the Vogt is buying every horse in town for the Danes.
