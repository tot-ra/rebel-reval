## Biography
Beke was born in 1317 in Deventer, a Zuiderzee town, to a fishwife and a ship's cook, and came to Reval at nine on a cog her uncle served. She grew up on the harbour front, shouting over wind, which is why her voice is hoarse. She learned Estonian from the other fishwives, and for three years carried baskets for a Danish quartermaster's wife on Toompea, who taught her to sew and to listen. When the wife went home in 1338 Beke took service with Nicolaus van Wismar. She keeps one tie to Toompea, and it is not a sentimental one.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A house of her own with a hearth and a latch, bought with pins and patience.
- **Fear:** Being asked to name a name under the Vogt's roof.
- **Contradiction:** She is the tap-room's best listener and its most trusted hand, and neither trust nor listening is quite what the van Wismars think.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** The Danish viceroy's steward knows her face, and her name in his book is a good one.

## Work and money
She earns about 3 marks a year, plus keep and a Christmas shirt, and adds a few schillings a season from small goods sold on credit. She buys cheap ribbon from a pedlar at the Sand gate and sells for a pfennig more. A bad month is a customer who vanishes owing a pfennig, or a lost basket.

## Faction and belief
Danish Crown, active. Her reason is the quartermaster's wife who gave her a trade and a sense that Toompea keeps its word. She would carry a message, give an hour's lodging, but not name a neighbour. She says her Paternoster in Low German and ties a red thread to the cellar door for the hearth-spirit.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Low German in the tap-room; Estonian with the market women.
- **Delivery:** Hoarse, fast, low; she leans in to be heard.
- **Sample lines:** "Mind your elbows." "Pay, or I remember." "Kuule, kallis." (Listen, dear.)
- **Verbal tic:** Calls everyone "sweet" and means it differently each time.

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows who meets whom in the back corner and who pays in foreign coin. She would trade it for a safe lodging. She believes the false rumour that the viceroy will open Toompea's granary to the town.
