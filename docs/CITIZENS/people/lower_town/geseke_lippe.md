## At a glance
- Keeps a small counter in the front room: needles, thread, pepper, saffron by the pinch, ribbon.
- Counts coins by listening to them on the board; never glances at the silver.
- Tells her husband the street's news at dinner as if it were weather.

## Biography
Born in Lübeck in 1292, the daughter of a spice-chandler on the Breite Strasse, she was married at twenty-six to Borchard and arrived in Reval with two chests of linen and a ledger she had kept since she was nine. She buried a daughter in 1327 in St Nicholas's yard. She took over the front room's counter in 1332, the year the town stopped minting, and has dealt in silver by weight ever since. She joined the Krämer fraternity in 1335. She likes the work because no one watches a woman at a counter. Now she lives with three sons, a full house of servants, and a husband who does not sleep.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A counter that pays its own way, so her sons inherit something not owed to her husband's creditors.
- **Fear:** Her husband's name in a ledger she cannot read.
- **Contradiction:** She scorns gossip and sells it, in the form of "Did you hear?", to the right customer.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She has sold small credit to three people who are under her sympathies, and has not told Borchard who they are.

## Work and money
She buys pepper, ginger and saffron from Lübeck factors, thread and needles from a Dunkri hosier, ribbon from Flemish bales. A good week brings about two marks of profit. Her hoard sits in a small chest under the stockroom floor: 11 marks in weighed silver. A bad month is a late cog and a customer who "forgets" a debt. She trusts numerals and no one's promise.

## Faction and belief
She is a Hanseatic sympathiser because her trade lives on open roads and Lübeck weights. For a small favour she will carry a message in a thread-packet; for a large one she would ask for something in writing she could not read. She prays to St Nicholas and puts a lump of salt on the windowsill on Walpurgis eve.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Middle Low German always; counter-talk is brisk, house-talk is lower.
- **Delivery:** Warm baritone, unhurried, deepening when she disapproves.
- **Sample lines:** "A pinch is a pinch, not a handful." "I sell thread; I do not sell what I hear." "Wat kost dat? Dat is to vel." (What does that cost? That is too much.)
- **Verbal tic:** "Mark you."

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows who buys saffron, who has stopped, and which wives are quietly selling linen. She would trade this for early word of the next Lübeck cog. She believes the rumour that the viceroy is raising the pepper duty.
