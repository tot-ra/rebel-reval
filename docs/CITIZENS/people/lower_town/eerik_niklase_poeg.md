## Biography
Eerik was born in 1297 in a fishing hamlet near Rägavere, the son of a Niklas who carried salt inland, and went to sea at twelve on a Danish trader. The rope-burn on his neck is from a snapped halyard off Öland in 1325 that nearly hanged him; the pocks are from a smallpox winter at ten. He has crossed to Lübeck, Visby and Novgorod and speaks only Estonian aboard, with sailors' German in a few words. He has no wife; she died in childbirth with his child in 1327. Since then he has found it easier to be nobody's husband. He berths at the harbour inns each season and picks up whatever small trade the ships allow.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A boat of his own and a cottage on the shore at Kopli.
- **Fear:** Being caught with the wrong rope on his shoulder.
- **Contradiction:** Honest with every friend, dishonest with every cargo.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** The rope and tar he sells came off two Lübeck ships; he never took a whole coil, always a fathom at a time.

## Work and money
He earns 4 öre a month on voyage, plus a few pfennig from small sales of offcuts at 2 pfennig a fathom. He owes the innkeeper a mark. A bad month is a search of the berth.

## Faction and belief
No faction. Politics is for those with land; he would carry a package for a coin, and would not hide a fugitive from the watch. He would never inform on a sailor, but he would sell news. He prays to St Nicholas, and spits in the sea for luck.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian at all times; a few German words of trade.
- **Delivery:** Sing-song cadence, fast, laughing; drops to a whisper when selling.
- **Sample lines:** "Best tarred hemp this side of Visby, friend, and half the Hansa price." "It fell off a ship, did it? Then it was loose." "Ära räägi liiga palju." (Don't talk too much.)
- **Verbal tic:** Winks with the left eye after a price.

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows which ships have light holds and which watchmen look the other way. He would trade it for silver or a berth. He believes the false rumour that a tarred pigtail turns a rope-burn into luck.
