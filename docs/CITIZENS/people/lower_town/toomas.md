## At a glance
- A slight, freckled man whose knuckles are white with old chilblain scars; he hums low through his nose while he works.
- Master of the harbour-side building trade, employed by German masters who sign the contracts he does the work for.
- Walks the night round with the Estonian squad: the town's way of having its own people police the lanes.
- Oddity: owns a deep, dry cellar full of squared stone and not one coin of spare silver.

## Biography
Born 1293 in a fisher hamlet under Kopli, taken at twelve as a hod-boy by a German mason who was raising the walls of the new harbour cellars. He learned the trade by carrying, then by watching, then by being allowed to cut the quoins nobody would see. Master by 1322, after the guild's old rule that an Estonian may not hold a bench was quietly bent for a man the council needed. His son was stillborn in 1325; his three living children are daughters. He married [Elin Nilsdotter](elin_nilsdotter.md), a Swedish widow's daughter from the fish quay, and the house on Laboratooriumi has been loud with her accent and his hum since. He now lives on cellar work, harbour wall repairs and a few commissions for burgher families, and he counts the days to Michaelmas.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To see a daughter married into a craft household that owns its own bench.
- **Fear:** The roof bond going bad, and the town's Germans learning how little he really has.
- **Contradiction:** Walks the watch to keep order, yet shares whispers he never repeats.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He once heard a name at a funeral and has said it to no one, not even Elin.

## Work and money
Income is piece-rate: a cellar vault costs a German master about 6 marks of silver, of which Toomas keeps a third after paying two journeymen and the lime-burner. Stone comes by barge from the Estonian coast, lime from the kiln at Kopli. He lent 9 marks in autumn 1342 to a merchant for a roof; it is due at Michaelmas and about half has been paid. He rents out a corner of his yard to a carter. A bad month is a frozen quarry road and a stopped site: then the loan is all he owns.

## Faction and belief
He counts himself with the guild-and-council order that gives him contracts, and he fears its anger more than he loves it. No role: he attends, pays dues, says nothing. Asked for a small favour he will carry a message; a large one he will weigh against the Michaelmas bond; as informer he would refuse and then be ashamed. He leaves a crust on the lintel for the house spirit and crosses himself at every church door.

## Knowledge and rumours
Knows which cellars on the street are damp, which walls sag, and which Germans' walls hide a bricked-up cupboard. Would trade it for a guarantee on the bond. He believes, falsely, that the Order's knights are already camped at Wesenberg.
