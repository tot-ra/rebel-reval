## Biography
Born 1327 in Zwolle on the Ijssel, son of a boatman who was lost on the river when the boy was nine. A cousin of his mother, a ship's mate, brought him to Reval in 1341 and bound him to Hartwig van Minden for four years. Hartwig feeds him, sleeps him above the counting-room, and promises a clerk's stool at the end if he learns the Latin he does not yet know. He lugs bales, weighs wax and carries the tally bundle, and has gradually taken the quayside by heart.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To be promoted from carrying to counting.
- **Fear:** Being sent back to Zwolle where nobody remains.
- **Contradiction:** Looks slow but is quicker than most of the clerks he serves.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** Keeps a private list in his head of every barrel Gerhard van Hamelen has set aside.

## Work and money
No wage, only board and a pair of shoes a year, plus a pfennig now and then from a satisfied sea captain. He buys ribbon for his mother's grave-memory from Magdalena. Savings: six pfennigs in a sock.

## Faction and belief
None; he is an apprentice, and the law owns his time. He would run an errand, never inform. He says the paternoster slowly, in the wrong order sometimes, and touches the quay's mooring post for luck.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Low German only.
- **Delivery:** Slow, with long pauses; each sentence arrives complete.
- **Sample lines:** "...Four lispfund and a half." "I had it, I gave it, he has it." "Dat dürt."
- **Verbal tic:** A pause before "yes".

## Knowledge and rumours
Knows the quay's weights. Believes falsely that ships whose mate sings badly will sink.
