## Biography
Reimar was born in 1305 at Groningen, the son of a cloth-fuller who died of a fever. At twenty he came east with a Lübeck factor and worked as a porter in Reval, learning Estonian from the other porters. In 1341 a Reval merchant swore on his deathbed to make a pilgrimage and died before he could; his widow hired Reimar for the vow, to walk to the shrine at Santiago and back and bring a scallop shell. He has been waiting in Reval since February for a ship to Lübeck from which the road runs south. The widow pays him in instalments, and he lives off the first.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To finish the vow honestly and return with the shell.
- **Fear:** A ship that never sails and a vow left half-kept.
- **Contradiction:** He is walking a stranger's penance but feels the weight of it as his own.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He has already spent part of the widow's fee on a debt back in Groningen.

## Work and money
He earns nothing but the widow's fee of 12 marks, paid in thirds. He lives on pfennig a day and gives one in three to the poor. A bad month is a missed payment.

## Faction and belief
No faction. A pilgrim has no cause but the road. He would carry a letter to Lübeck, and would not inform. He keeps the vow strictly, fasts on Fridays and carries a splinter of the True Cross, or so he is told.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Low German in church and with skippers; Estonian with porters.
- **Delivery:** A flat, tired murmur; no emphasis.
- **Sample lines:** "I walk for another man, and it is a heavy thing." "A ship to Lübeck, friend, any ship?" "Jumal halasta." (God have mercy.)
- **Verbal tic:** Sighs before each reply.

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows which ships' masters are honest and which will take a pilgrim for free. He would trade it for a passage. He believes the false rumour that a scallop badge keeps off storms.
