## Biography
Yda was born in 1332, the fourth of the family, and talked early. She was reading numerals at five, and at nine corrected the parish clerk's sum before the whole class. [Gyse van Goslar](../../people/lower_town/gyse_van_goslar.md) has begun letting her check the thread-weights. She dislikes dirt, noise and bad arithmetic in that order.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To keep the household accounts like her mother.
- **Fear:** Being told she is wrong when she is right.
- **Contradiction:** She hates mess but loves the cellar.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She found the 18-mark bond among her father's papers and has not said a word; she has counted it twice.

## Work and money
She has a few pins and a pfennig in a purse. She checks the thread-weights twice a week. A bad week is a wrong sum.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Low German, clipped.
- **Delivery:** Clipped and precise, each word set down like a coin.
- **Sample lines:** "That is not four. That is three and one." "Mother, the clerk said 'lispfund' wrong." "Please do not touch my pins."
- **Verbal tic:** "Precisely."

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows the weights, the number of stairs and the hour each servant comes down. She would trade it for a book of numbers. She believes, wrongly, that every number has a colour.
