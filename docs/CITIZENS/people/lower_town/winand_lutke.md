## Biography
Born in 1316 in Soest, he came to Reval at nineteen with a letter to a cousin's house and found the cousin dead. He has kept books for four different merchants. The fourth sent him to lodge with Gyse Hardekop to be near the cloth trade, and he has stayed.

He is unmarried, which is the root of his circle: the young merchants' men who want a place at the table when the council changes. He does not call himself their leader.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A merchant's seat and a wife with property.
- **Fear:** Dying a clerk in a rented bed.
- **Contradiction:** He is a man of great patience who cannot wait.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He holds the circle's small purse in a hollowed book and knows exactly who has paid.

## Work and money

## Faction and belief
A sympathiser of the unmarried brotherhood; his reason is a seat at the table, not ceremony. A small favour: pass a note. A large one: sign a name. He would not inform. He prays at St Nicholas.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Low German at table; Latin in letters; Estonian in the market.
- **Delivery:** A flat, tired murmur.
- **Sample lines:** "It will be done when it is done." / "Do not write that down." / "Ja, ja, later."
- **Verbal tic:** Sighs before a figure.

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows who has paid the circle and who owes. He would trade it for a seat. He believes the false rumour that the council will bar unmarried men from the guild.
