## Biography
Born in 1312 in Nõmme to a woman who sang laments for the village dead, Sofia learned the old songs by ear. She came to Reval at seventeen and was taught the baptismal words by the Holy Spirit chaplain, so that a dying child might be saved when no priest could come. She has baptised several infants in the quarter and led the lament at wakes, paid in bread. Since 1336 she has served the Langhe house.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To be trusted with the sick children of the house.
- **Fear:** A child dying unbaptised because she was too late.
- **Contradiction:** Quiet in speech, loudest in lament.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She uses an old song at births that the chaplain would not approve.

## Work and money
Wage is three marks a year with board; she is paid in loaves for laments. A bad month is a quiet one with no one sick and no wake.

## Faction and belief
No faction. She believes in the dying child and the dead man's song. She would do any favour a sick person asked, refuse a large one that cost a life and inform on no one. She prays at the Holy Spirit and keeps a feather by the cradle against the night-hag.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian for songs; soft Low German in the house.
- **Delivery:** Soft, almost whispering.
- **Sample lines:** "Hush, now." "Let me sit with him." "Jumal, anna andeks." (God, forgive.)
- **Verbal tic:** "Shh."

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows which houses have unbaptised infants. She would trade it for nothing. She believes the false rumour that a lament sung wrong brings the dead back.
