## Biography
Born in Reval to a German widow who sewed for the Dominicans, he went to the St Nicholas parish school and learned his Latin from a lay brother. At fifteen he joined a Krämer's counting room, then Beke's two years ago. He survived the hard winter of 1341 on turnips and borrowed candles in a Lai lane room. Now he reads for the house, and writes the quarterly accounts in a clear hand.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A stall of his own by thirty, without a quarrel.
- **Fear:** A bad name; clerks live on trust.
- **Contradiction:** Prides himself on neutrality, yet cannot help reading letters left open.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He keeps a private tally of who owes what to whom on Pikk.

## Work and money
He earns his board and 8 marks a year. He lends 1 mark to a Lai friend and has 3 marks saved. He is owed a favour or two. A bad quarter means no raise.

## Faction and belief
None, by choice: his trade lives on being everyone's clerk. Asked for a small favour he would copy a letter; for a large one he would decline; to inform he would say that nothing was written. He prays quietly and carries a St Nicholas medal.
