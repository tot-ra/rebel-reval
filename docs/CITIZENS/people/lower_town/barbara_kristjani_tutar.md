## Biography
Born in 1304 at Kopli, the daughter of a fisherman, Kristjan, Barbara carried fish from the boats to the stalls from the age of twelve. A winter cough settled in her voice after she carried a catch through a blizzard in 1331. In 1334 the butcher Thomas Langhe ([card](thomas_langhe.md)) took her on to run the wash-house and pantry, as she could count and could not be cheated. She is trusted with the keys.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To bring her sister's orphan to the house as a scullion.
- **Fear:** Losing her voice altogether.
- **Contradiction:** Stern with the maids and secretly lends them coin.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She keeps a second, unwritten tally of what the cook steals, and shares it with no one.

## Work and money
Wage is five marks a year with board; she sends three to Kopli. A bad month is one when salt runs short.

## Faction and belief
No faction. Her world is pantry and quay. She would do a small favour, refuse a large one and report nothing unasked. She prays to St Olaf and leaves a fish-head on the sill at the first herring.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian at home; hoarse Low German in the house.
- **Delivery:** Hoarse, low, like wind through a rope.
- **Sample lines:** "Salt is short." "Count it again, girl." "Ära ole loll." (Do not be a fool.)
- **Verbal tic:** "Hm."

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows what every sack in the pantry weighs. She would trade it for a ribbon. She believes the false rumour that a bell cracked at St Olaf's means a storm.
