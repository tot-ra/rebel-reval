## Biography
Cord was born in 1324 in Cologne, the son of a cloth-trader who failed in 1336 and left him to the charity of an uncle who sent him to Reval. He was taken into [Wolter van Brunswik](../../people/lower_town/wolter_van_brunswik.md)'s house at fifteen as a pen-scraper, and has risen to journeyman. He speaks slowly because he once stammered, and lets silence do what the tongue cannot. He is thrifty, shy and watchful, and is saving to buy a half-share in a cog.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A half-share in a cog by thirty, and a place at the Hanse table.
- **Fear:** Poverty like his father's last winter.
- **Contradiction:** A careful clerk who walks home with a servant, and does not mind who sees.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He has copied three letters for a circle of merchants and told no one the contents.

## Work and money
He earns his board, a gown at Christmas and a mark at Michaelmas. He saves four schillings a quarter in a sock. He owes nothing and is owed two schillings by Dietrich. A bad month is a copied letter with a blot.

## Faction and belief
Hanseatic sympathiser: his hope of advancement lies with the Hansa's trade and its circle. His own reason is fear of poverty, not duty. For a small favour he would copy a letter; for a large one he would hesitate and go pale; to inform he would stammer, then do it. He says the Latin psalms by rote and carries a stub of candle from his father's last shop.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Latin in the counting-room; slow Low German elsewhere.
- **Delivery:** Slow, long pauses, then a short firm sentence.
- **Sample lines:** "I will... consider it." "The weight was... forty-two. I wrote forty-two." "Mind the ink."
- **Verbal tic:** A pause before "yes".

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows what the master dictates and who comes at night. He would trade it for a share. He believes, wrongly, that a clerk who learns Russian will be made a factor in Novgorod.
