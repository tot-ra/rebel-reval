## At a glance
- A sturdy man of middle height with half-closed, heavy-lidded eyes, left hand on the beam of the great scale.
- He weighs every sack of rye, bale of wax and cask of herring that enters the market, and stamps the lead seal.
- Odd for a weigher: he cannot read, and knows every weight by the mark stamped on it.

## Biography
Born in 1300 in Reval, the son of the previous weigher's assistant. He was taught to feel a pound in his hand before he could count to twelve. In 1326 he took over the beam when the old man died. A skipper once tried to bribe him with a half-mark; Ropert took the half-mark to the council and was paid for it. He lives in the town hall near the scale-house.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To keep the town's weights honest, because his name depends on it.
- **Fear:** A fire in the scale-house and a lost standard.
- **Contradiction:** Incorruptible at the beam and quietly bound to a circle of merchants.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He has weighed a few bales more lightly for one merchant, once, when asked kindly.

## Work and money
He earns ten marks a year and keep, and a quarter-pfennig for each lead seal. His fees run to a mark a month in a busy spring. He owes nothing. A bad month is a closed harbour.

## Faction and belief
He is bound to the guilds by trust and fees. He would weigh fairly for anyone, keep a secret for a small favour, and would not inform. He puts a lead token in the church box at each feast.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Middle Low German, the weigher's trade terms.
- **Delivery:** Clipped and precise, each word set down like a weight.
- **Sample lines:** "Two lispfund and a half." "The beam does not lie." "Dat is recht."
- **Verbal tic:** Taps the beam twice before reading it.

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows each merchant's real volume. He would trade it for a safe scale-house. He believes a lead seal can be forged by the Danes, which is false.
