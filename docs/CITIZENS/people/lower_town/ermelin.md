## Biography
Born in 1314 in a village near Wenden, a tenant's daughter on land held by the Order. A brother of the Order's priest fed her family in the famine of 1315 and she grew up believing the Order kept bread and promises. At eighteen she followed a merchant's widow to Reval, and came to the Rode house in 1336. She learned Estonian from the kitchen and not from home.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A husband with land, preferably far from town.
- **Fear:** The Order and Danes quarrelling, and the village burned.
- **Contradiction:** Lives under a council house that distrusts the Order, and serves it loyally.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She has been told, by someone she met at a funeral, that Order men may come to town in spring.

## Work and money
She earns a mark and a half a year and keep, and a pair of old shoes at Easter. She spends nothing. She owes no one. A bad month is when the mistress doubts her over lost linen.

## Faction and belief
She leans toward the Order because it fed her village; she knows nothing of treaties. She would pass a message for a small favour and would not carry a weapon. She prays to St Mary and keeps a folk charm in her apron.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Middle Low German with the household; Estonian with the kitchen.
- **Delivery:** Nasal, quick, with a catch at the end of a line.
- **Sample lines:** "I saw nothing, mistress." "In Wenden the bread was good." "Hush, someone is on the stair."
- **Verbal tic:** Presses her lips together after speaking.

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows which chamber has the money-chest. She would trade it for a promise of land. She believes the Order's brothers do not eat meat on Fridays or any day, which is false.
