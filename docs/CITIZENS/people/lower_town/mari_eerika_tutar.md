## Biography
Born about 1314 in a hamlet near a sacred grove of lime trees on the Harju road. When the manor cut the grove for timber in 1329, her mother wept at the stumps and taught her the rites that remained. She went into service at nineteen, first with a skinner, then with Jaak Lauri poeg, where the work is lighter and the bread better. She sleeps on a pallet in the kitchen.

Her mother is dead; her brother is in the village, bound to the manor.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A garden plot of her own where her mother's herbs can grow.
- **Fear:** The parish priest reading her out by name, and a grove being cut again.
- **Contradiction:** She prays at mass with her whole voice and trusts what she buries more.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** On the eve of St George she will leave a bowl of porridge and ale at the strand wall where the old women once did; she does not know who else will.

## Work and money
Wages are 1 mark a year with shoes and board. Her side trade, a bundle of mint or a cake of wax, brings 6 to 10 pfennigs a week, which she hides in a hollow of the garden wall. She sells at a fair price. A bad month is a wet May.

## Faction and belief
A sympathiser of the old ways: her reason is a stump in a field. She would pass a charm or a word; she would not take up a blade, and she would inform on none. She keeps Christmas and the old winter fires alike.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian; a few Low German words in the market.
- **Delivery:** Husky, quiet, rhythmic.
- **Sample lines:** "Take it, it's fair." / "The first cup is not yours." / "Ära karda, do not fear."
- **Verbal tic:** Touches her apron pocket before she answers.

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows where each herb grows along the wall and which women trust her. She would trade the knowledge for a garden plot. She believes the false rumour that the priest will burn every herb bundle after Easter.
