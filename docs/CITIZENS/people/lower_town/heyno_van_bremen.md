## Biography
Born in 1316 in Bremen, the second son of a notary's clerk, Heyno was apprenticed at twelve to a Lübeck factor and sent to Reval at nineteen as a junior reckoner. Borchard van Groninghe took him on in 1337, after a fire in the counting room taught him he needed a clerk who could write fast. Heyno has never had a quarrel with anyone, and thinks it the proudest fact of his life. A year ago he was drawn into a quiet circle of Hanseatic men who pass word to Lübeck and weigh what the Rat will tolerate. He lodges in the clerks' room off the counting house, one floor above the maids' loft.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A small house of his own in Bremen, a wife who reads numerals, and a ledger of his own.
- **Fear:** A mistake in a ledger that could be traced to his hand.
- **Contradiction:** He prides himself on being invisible, and he gathers the most information in the house.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He keeps a second small tally of every visitor to the councillor's door; none of it is in his master's books.

## Work and money
He earns 8 marks a year with board and bed. He lends a few schillings to harbour clerks at a modest rate. His pen, tablet and cap are his own. A bad month is a late cog and a master whose temper shortens at the table. He keeps 14 schillings in a hollow in his bed-post.

## Faction and belief
He is an active Hanseatic man because he has seen what trade becomes when a prince can close a harbour. A small favour is a note; a large one a night's watching; informing on a councillor he would refuse. He crosses himself at St Nicholas and prays to St Nicholas for ships.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Middle Low German in speech, Latin in the ledger.
- **Delivery:** Monotone, even, no emphasis; he reads every number in the same voice.
- **Sample lines:** "Forty marks, two schillings, six pfennigs." "It is written, so it is." "Dat is nich mien Sak." (That is not my affair.)
- **Verbal tic:** "As written."

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows every cargo and every name that visits the councillor. He would trade this for a letter of introduction to a Lübeck house. He believes the false rumour that the Rat will close the weigh-house during Whitsun.
