## At a glance
- Ink on the right fingers to the second joint, always, whatever he scrubs with.
- He writes three hands: a fast tally script, a clean letter hand, and Latin for contracts.
- Known to the harbour porters as "the German's Estonian" and to the clerks as the one who never forgets a figure.
- Oddity: an Estonian, bearded after the old fashion, in a trade nearly closed to his people.

## Biography
Born at the harbour in 1323 to a fisher named Mihkel who died at sea when Uku was ten. The St Olaf chaplain taught him letters in exchange for hauling water; a Lübeck factor spotted his tally-hand and trained him until the factor left Reval in 1340. [Meinhard Wise](./meinhard_wise.md) hired him that winter because he came cheap and he never made a sum wrong. He now sleeps in the counting-room loft and sends half his silver to his mother.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A clerk's post in his own name, and a roof for his mother.
- **Fear:** Being let go the moment a German nephew needs the desk.
- **Contradiction:** He resents the German masters yet takes pride in their trust.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He has noticed that cargo noted in the ledger and cargo at the toll differ by a barrel or two, and he has told no one.

## Work and money
Wage is about 2 marks a quarter plus board; he adds a few pfennigs copying letters for neighbours. He owes a chaplain a debt of gratitude, not silver. A bad month is Meinhard's ship delayed and wages late.

## Faction and belief
No faction; a clerk who takes a side loses the next post. He does small favours for neighbours, refuses larger ones, and would never inform on his master. Baptised at St Olaf, he still leaves milk for the house-spirit his mother taught him.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian at the harbour, Low German at work, Latin in the ledger.
- **Delivery:** Quick, singing Estonian cadence under the German.
- **Sample lines:** "It is written, so it is." "Three barrels, not four; look." "Anna andeks, master."
- **Verbal tic:** Taps the table twice before a number.

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows every bale in the house. He would trade it for a recommendation. He believes the false rumour that Estonians will soon be barred from the quay.
