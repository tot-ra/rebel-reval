## Biography
Born in 1295 in Maardu, the daughter of a cooper called Simon, Marga came to Reval at twelve as a bath-house maid and married her master's apprentice, Rein, in 1317. She bore four children; three died before their fifth birthday and one, Piret, in the winter of 1337. Hele came late, in 1337, and is the household's sun. She spins for a Harju weaver and sells birch whisks at the bath door. She lives over the bath-house with her husband, his mother, her daughter and two maids.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A quiet house, a fed child and a winter without fever.
- **Fear:** Losing Hele; and Rein's silence when a guest says something dangerous.
- **Contradiction:** She is the household's softest voice and its hardest bargainer.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She keeps Piret's wooden spoon in her apron and has never told Rein.

## Work and money
She earns 3 schillings a week from yarn and whisks combined, about 8 marks a year; twigs come from a Harju carter, thread from a weaver. A bad month is a damp stack of twigs.

## Faction and belief
She has no faction; she buried too many children to wish for a fight. A small favour, yes; a large one, only for Hele. She says the Our Father over the spindle and ties a red thread at Hele's wrist.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian at home; plain German at the door.
- **Delivery:** A flat, tired murmur, never raised.
- **Sample lines:** "Take one, it is a pfennig." "Do not wake Hele." "Küll ta tuleb." (He will come.)
- **Verbal tic:** A sigh before answering.

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows who comes to the bath and who does not. She would trade for medicine for Hele. She believes the false rumour that a fever comes with the first cog.
