## At a glance
- A bearded man bent slightly forward at the shoulders, a padded sealskin yoke-cushion folded under one arm.
- Everyone who needs a cart-hire split or a bale moved at the Coastal Gate asks him first; nobody thanks him.
- Odd for a man who lives off the merchants: he says almost nothing in German, and then in a sing-song that makes clerks smile and him watch them smile.

## Biography
Born on the Aida shore in 1302 to a fisher family; his father drowned when Ain was eleven, and the boy carried salt herring for a harbour-master to eat. He married Liis in 1325 and has buried none of his own children. He took a place in the Estonian night squad in 1336 for the pay in rye and the right to sleep through the Mass bell. He is part of the shore, not of the guilds, though every mark he earns comes from them.

## Motivation
- **Want:** Four living children fed through another summer and a second room built onto the back.
- **Fear:** A fall on the quay that ends the yoke and leaves Liis alone with the shop.
- **Contradiction:** Keeps the merchants' peace on the watch and quietly wants them humbled.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** Counts, on the stick, every bale he sees go through unmarked.

## Work and money
He earns about one örtug a day in good weeks, less in ice. Watch pay adds a lispfund of rye a month. He and Borchard van Groninghe split the cart-hire and day's labour; Ain pays half of two marks a month. Credit at the miller runs 3 marks. A bad month is a week of fog without ships.

## Faction and belief
He lives under the guilds' money and says so to no one. Tyde knows more than he ought. Ain would carry a package for the cause, never sign or speak for it. He keeps a hearth-offering of milk on the threshold and a St Olaf's medal.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian at home; short plain German at the quay.
- **Delivery:** Slow, sing-song, ending lines upward.
- **Sample lines:** "Half the cart is mine, half the horse is yours, and the whole of the argument is ours." "Kolm kotti, mitte neli." "I did not see it, so I cannot have lost it."
- **Verbal tic:** Taps the stick against his thigh before answering.

## Knowledge and rumours
Knows which quay crates are never opened and who pays to keep it so. Would trade it for a placed word for Lauri. Believes the sea-fog on St George's Eve means a ship of the Order is hidden behind it, which is false.
