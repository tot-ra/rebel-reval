## Biography
Born in a spinner's lane near the Karja gate, Ell learned the spindle at five and the numerals at fourteen from a tally-keeping aunt. She married Jüri in 1327, brought a dowry of three bolts of linen, and had three sons in nine years; two other babies are buried at St Olaf's. She taught herself to run the house and hold her tongue. Merchants of the street send her fleece to spin because the thread is even, and her work pays the maid's wages.

## Motivation
- **Want:** Three sons grown and a cooperage that does not need her thread to stay open.
- **Fear:** The Danish levy on thread and a second winter of dear grain.
- **Contradiction:** She prides herself on never owing, and she is the one who owes.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She meets Gottschalk Lippe after mass and passes him what the market women say about the viceroy's tolls.

## Work and money
She spins about a pfund of wool a week for eight pfennigs, more in flax season. Merchants send her fleece; she sells fine thread to the tailor and cap-maker nearby. Her money buys the household's cloth and the maid's wage of six marks a year.

## Faction and belief
Ell wants the guilds' peace because war empties the market. She would give a small favour freely, a large one grudgingly, and an informant's word never. She lights a candle to St Olaf at the harbour altar and ties red thread round the cradle post.

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows what every wife on Harju paid for rye and who is quietly selling a ring. She would trade it for a place for Henn. She believes the viceroy means to take every third sack.
