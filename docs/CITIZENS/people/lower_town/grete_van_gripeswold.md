## At a glance
- Pear-shaped, 165 cm, sun-browned, with a warm baritone that startles novices.
- Keeps the convent kitchen, the brewhouse and the almsgate bread.
- Counts the poor's loaves wrongly in the poor's favour.
- A fast-breaker's guilt: Lent has just ended and she is hungrier than she admits.

## Biography
Born in 1284 at Greifswald on the Baltic coast, the youngest of eight in a baker's house; she came to Reval with an aunt in 1298 and entered the convent in 1302. She has kept the kitchen since the famine year of 1315, when she learned to stretch rye with pea flour. She has never been thin and has never regretted it.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A full almsgate every day, even when the Rule says otherwise.
- **Fear:** A hungry spring and a short wheat harvest.
- **Contradiction:** A fasting sister who tastes everything.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She hides loaves in the ash-pit for the poor, entered in the tally as burnt.

## Work and money
She owns nothing. The convent buys rye, barley and malt by the last; she knows each price. About forty loaves a week go to the almsgate. A bad month is when the brewhouse mash sours.

## Faction and belief
Church, core. Her root is hunger remembered; her branch is the almsgate. A small favour she does; a large one she weighs; she will not inform on a hungry man. She keeps the Rule's fasts, and drops a crust in the brewhouse corner for the old spirit of the mash.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Low German with the town; Latin in choir.
- **Delivery:** Warm baritone, slow, rolling; laughs in the chest.
- **Sample lines:** "Eat, eat, God sees the plate." "Salt first, then prayer." "Burnt, I tell you, quite burnt."
- **Verbal tic:** Tastes a pinch of salt before answering.

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows what each household buys and what it leaves. She would trade it for a better malt. She falsely believes bread baked on Fridays will not mould.
