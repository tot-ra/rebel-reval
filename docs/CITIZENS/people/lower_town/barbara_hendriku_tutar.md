## Biography
Born in 1314 at Kose on the Harju road to a cart-wright, Hendrik, Barbara had a younger brother, Toomas, who carried sacks for a tithe-collector. In 1341 he struck a Danish clerk's man who had slapped an old woman, and was flogged at the Smiths' Gate until he could not stand; he died at Michaelmas. She has been in the van Paderborne house since the spring after, and was taken into a small circle of Black Cloaks at his wake. She sings still, but never his songs.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To see the clerk's man and the post that flogged her brother brought low.
- **Fear:** Hearing her brother's laugh in her own and weeping before the mistress.
- **Contradiction:** Merry in company, hard in private.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She has heard Albert and Mikula, the dyer, trade the same whispers each Sunday.

## Faction and belief
Black Cloaks, secret: she shares the root grievance of the flogging post but acts from love of her brother, not hatred of the Danes. She would pass a small word, carry a large one only for Gertrud and refuse to inform. She keeps the old way: a ribbon on the doorpost at midsummer and a prayer to St Olaf.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian in the kitchen; careful Low German with the family.
- **Delivery:** A warm baritone, slow-rolling, quick to laughter.
- **Sample lines:** "A good table, a good word." "Tule siia, laps." (Come here, child.) "I did not hear, master."
- **Verbal tic:** A low "Hm-hm" before she answers.

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows who among the guests drinks too much and talks. She would trade it only to Gertrud. She believes the false rumour that Danish clerks mark every household's grain by night.
