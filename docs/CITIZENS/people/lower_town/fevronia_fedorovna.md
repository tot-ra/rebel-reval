## Biography
Born in 1280 in Pskov, wife of Fedor the baker, with whom she came to Reval in 1305. Fedor died in 1325. She raised three sons, Fedor, Grigory and Gavril, at the oven; Grigory was Marfa's husband. All three died within five years. She keeps the shop's memory and the old recipes.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To see the granddaughters grown and the shop kept.
- **Fear:** Being a burden.
- **Contradiction:** Humble in the house and fierce about the recipe.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** The recipe for the Easter loaf is hers alone, and she has told no one.

## Work and money
She makes rusks from stale bread and sells them for a pfennig a bag. She gets keep from Marfa. She owes nothing. A bad month is no rye.

## Faction and belief
None; she remembers a time when every faction wanted bread. She would feed anyone, hide a child, and refuse to carry a letter. She wears an icon on a cord and keeps the Pskov fasts.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Russian at home; slow German at the stalls.
- **Delivery:** Slow, with long pauses, humming between sentences.
- **Sample lines:** "Sit, child, eat." "In Pskov we had better rye." "Ne speshi."

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows the oldest recipes and the oldest debts. She would trade them for a granddaughter's safety. She believes a loaf dropped face-down brings a death, which is folk belief.
