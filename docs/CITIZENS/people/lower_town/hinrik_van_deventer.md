## Biography
Born in 1267 in Deventer on the Ijssel, Hinrik came to Reval at twenty as a journeyman cutter and took the master's mark in 1297. He taught his son Sander van Deventer ([card](sander_van_deventer.md)) to cut, and to cut right-handed, which he has regretted. In the famine winter of 1316 he and a lane of neighbours on Harju, among them Taniel, shared a barrel of rye meal and a stolen sheep's head. He retired from the bench in 1336 after a fall on the Cattle Gate ice. He now lives by the hearth of his son's house, paying with advice nobody asks for.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To see the shop pay its way and Frederik handed a proper last.
- **Fear:** Dying in the debt his son is hiding from him.
- **Contradiction:** Insists on precision, and cannot see that his own hands now spoil it.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He knows about the boat money and has said nothing, sleeping on a purse he hid in the hearth-stone.

## Work and money
He earns nothing now but the pension of the shoemakers' fraternity, a few pfennigs every quarter day, which he gives to Margarete. His tools hang on the wall: a cutting knife, a boxwood last, awls. His savings, about two marks, he has told no one of. A bad month is one where Sander asks for a loan.

## Faction and belief
No faction. He is too old, too deaf to politics and loyal only to a clean seam. He would do a small favour, refuse a large one, and inform on nobody. He prays to St Nicholas for the dead and leaves a heel of bread on the sill at Michaelmas.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Low German only, spoken slowly to Estonians.
- **Delivery:** Clipped and precise, a short breath between words.
- **Sample lines:** "Cut once. Cut true." "That stitch is wrong. Again." "Dat is nich recht."
- **Verbal tic:** "Precisely so."

## Knowledge and rumours
He remembers who owed whom in 1316 and which houses took bribes. He would trade a story for a warm seat. He believes the false rumour that the old Danish governor secretly hoarded rye in that famine.
