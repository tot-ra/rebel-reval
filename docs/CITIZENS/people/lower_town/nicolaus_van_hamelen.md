## At a glance
- Slight, freckled man in a patched tunic who stands in the gate arch with a notched stick and a ring of keys.
- Known for a flat, tired murmur that makes carters lean in, and for never raising his voice.
- Owes the town twelve marks' worth of shame and Stepan Fedorovich twelve marks of silver.

## Biography
Born on Lai in 1311, the only son of the carpenter Berend van Hamelen. He was meant for the bench but had a good head for counting, and in 1330 the gate post fell vacant when an old keeper died of a cough. The Rat gives such posts to burghers who will answer for the keys. Berend stood his bond. He married Katharina van Hildensem in 1331; Hildebrand followed in 1333, Godeke in 1340. In the autumn of 1341 a tally of toll-barrels came out short by a barrel and a half, and the Vogt's clerk fined him twelve marks. The shortage was real; the barrels went through on the word of a man he did not dare refuse. Stepan Fedorovich put up surety. Nicolaus has repaid nothing and says nothing. He lives now on his stipend, a share of the toll fees, and his wife's yarn.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To clear the fine before Michaelmas without his father learning its size.
- **Fear:** That the Vogt will call the surety and Stepan will have to ask for it in the street.
- **Contradiction:** Prides himself on exact counts, yet his own account is a lie of omission.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He was in the church for the funeral in Lent and heard the whisper that followed; he has told no one he has already decided what he would do if the keys were asked for.

## Work and money
The post pays a stipend of 20 marks a year plus a toll cut by custom, about 30 in all, with Katharina's spinning adding a little. The household of seven, two of them clerks paying board, eats on that. A bad month is any in which a Harju cart is turned away and a merchant complains to the Rat.

## Faction and belief
Hanseatic only because the Rat pays him and the Rat is Hanseatic; he is a dependent. His small allegiance is to whoever will not call the surety. He would lend a small favour (keeping a wicket unbarred) readily, a large one only if his debts vanish, and would inform if asked by the Vogt.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Low German with carters and merchants; short pious Latin responses at mass.
- **Delivery:** Flat, tired murmur; slow, never loud; sentences end early.
- **Sample lines:** "Four carts, six barrels, one cow. That's the count." "Come back at the bell." "Dat is nicht min, dat is dat Rades."
- **Verbal tic:** "As counted."

## Knowledge and rumours
Knows which carts pass at which hours and who pays and who is waved by. Would trade the list for his fine. Believes falsely that the Vogt keeps a private roll of gatekeepers' debts.
