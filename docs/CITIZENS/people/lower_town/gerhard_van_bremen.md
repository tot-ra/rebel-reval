## At a glance
- A clean-shaven man of middle height with a rosy face and a voice that fills the council chamber without effort.
- He writes the minutes, the plot-rolls and the oaths, and nothing in Reval's law happens without his pen.
- Odd for a man so loud: he says nothing at all about the one thing he has measured.

## Biography
Born in 1289 in Bremen, trained in the chancery of Lübeck, and called to Reval in 1322 as scribe. He rose to clerk in 1331 on his predecessor's death. His wife died in 1338, leaving no child. He lives in the town hall with his scribes and the council's servants. He is accurate, proud of it, and tired.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To retire with a pension and a house in Bremen.
- **Fear:** A fire or a viceroy's order that burns the plot-rolls.
- **Contradiction:** Insists on exact record and keeps one fact unrecorded.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He measured the boundary between the town hall yard and the Rode house in 1339; the stake is a hand's breadth off, and he has never entered it.

## Work and money
He earns thirty marks a year, plus fees for each charter (a quarter-mark for a sealed deed). He owes small sums to three friends. He keeps the council's chest key. A bad month is a council quarrel, with no fees.

## Faction and belief
He believes in the council's law and the order of the books. His circle is people who help the council quietly; he does not like owing them. He would give a copy for a small favour, delay a charter for a large one, and would not inform. He gives a penny to the poor at each Mass.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Middle Low German in council; Latin in charters.
- **Delivery:** Booming, even in a whisper, with a trained pause before each clause.
- **Sample lines:** "It is written and it is so." "Let the record show it." "Dat is nicht mine Sake."
- **Verbal tic:** Presses his seal ring to his lip.

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows every plot and every debt in Lower Town. He would trade it for a pension. He believes the viceroy keeps a second set of rolls on Toompea, which is false.
