## Biography
Dietrich was born in 1322, the son of a Zwolle skipper drowned off Naissaar in 1327 and an Estonian laundress who kept him in a loft off Vene. He picked up his mother's tongue first and the German of the lane after. A priest of the Holy Spirit taught him letters in exchange for carrying water, and in 1338 [Wolter van Brunswik](../../people/lower_town/wolter_van_brunswik.md) took him on as a pen-boy. In the winter of 1342 his mother died of cold in the loft; he buried her at the Holy Spirit's expense. He lives now in the clerks' loft.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A clerkship at the town hall, with a salary and a seal.
- **Fear:** The loft and the cold, and a master who dies owing wages.
- **Contradiction:** He despises beggary and gives half his pennies to beggars.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He sends a schilling a quarter to the Holy Spirit for the priest who taught him; nobody knows.

## Work and money
He earns his board, a gown at Christmas and one mark a year. He saves two schillings a quarter and gives one to the church. He owes Cord van Kolne two schillings. A bad month is a master's temper.

## Faction and belief
No faction. A clerk who writes both sides' letters belongs to neither. For a small favour he would copy a note; for a large one he would refuse politely; to inform he would not, because he has no one to trade. He says the psalms in Latin and a charm for warmth in Estonian.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Latin in the counting-room; Low German with the sing-song of Estonian.
- **Delivery:** Even and lilting, soft at the ends of sentences.
- **Sample lines:** "Three lispfund, in a clean hand." "I wrote what was said, not what was meant." "It was a long winter."
- **Verbal tic:** "As written."

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows what the master writes and burns, and which lane lofts leak. He would trade it for a town-hall post. He believes, wrongly, that the town clerk is dying.
