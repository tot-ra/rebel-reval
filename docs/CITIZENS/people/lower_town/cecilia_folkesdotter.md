## At a glance
- Lean, sallow, nasal-voiced, and always counting under her breath.
- Runs the household's cupboard and the maids' mornings with a short stick of authority she never uses.
- Oddity: her stepson Halvard is the only person who can make her laugh.

## Biography
Born about 1283 to a Swedish boatwright named Folke in the Harbour quarter, she spun for a Lübeck cloth-factor from the age of nine. A first marriage ended with her husband drowned off Nargen in 1319; she kept the wheel and her dowry. In 1334 she wed Jöns Gunnarsson, taking on a five-year-old Halvard and a house of smoke. She is a stepmother who feeds everyone first and herself last. A soapstone spindle whorl, cracked across, was her mother's and is kept in her apron pocket; she will not use it, only turn it. Now she spins for three weavers and keeps the household accounts in her head, since neither she nor Jöns can write.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A quiet house, with the Michaelmas rent laid by in good time.
- **Fear:** Another bad winter like the last, when the hearth ran short and the maids sickened.
- **Contradiction:** Hard on the maids over waste, yet she sneaks extra porridge into Jaak's bowl.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She knows Jöns walks home with a woman from the church door and says nothing; he sings in the yard afterwards, and she would rather have the singing.

## Work and money
She earns about two schillings for a pound of yarn spun and delivered, roughly five marks a quarter; wool comes in tied fleece lots from carters, flax from Estonian growers at market. She sells to weavers on the lane and takes thread-ends in barter. Savings: six marks sewn in a bolster. A bad month is one when a weaver fails to pay and she must carry the skeins another quarter.

## Faction and belief
No faction; a spinner depends on whoever buys cloth, and cloth is Hanseatic money. A small favour she would do; a large one she refuses unless Halvard is hurt; she would never inform, and would say she did not see. Folk charms: wool-ash at the threshold on St Walburga's eve.

## Voice

- **Delivery:** Nasal, brisk, short sentences, counting aloud.

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows which weavers pay late and which maids steal. She believes, wrongly, that a spring comet is a warning against marrying in April.
