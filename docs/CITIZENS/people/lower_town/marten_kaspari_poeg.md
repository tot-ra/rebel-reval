## Biography
Born in 1325 to a St Olaf's parish carter who wanted his son off the road. A canon at St Olaf's taught him letters for a year's carting of firewood; he learned Latin from the Dominicans at fourteen and was hired by Jaak Lauri poeg because the cook was tired of being cheated by clerks who could read what he could not. He has now been in the house two years and knows every debt.

He lives in the garret over the counting corner. He sends coins to his mother and polishes his shoes at dawn.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A desk in a German merchant's counting-house, with his own sealing wax.
- **Fear:** Being known as a cook's clerk for ever.
- **Contradiction:** He despises tally sticks and admires the man who uses them.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He quietly translates the true price of salt into Latin and copies it into a private book.

## Work and money
Wages are 2 marks a year plus board. Dietrich Zierenberg supplies the kitchen on credit settled at the quarter-days; this arrangement is the backbone of both houses' week, and Marten keeps the book. He saves a few pfennigs for a good penknife. A bad month is a missed quarter-day.

## Faction and belief
None. His ambition needs the council's good opinion, so he avoids all talk. He is a pious boy who says the Ave on the stairs and fears hell for lying in ledgers.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Latin for books, Low German with guests, Estonian at home.
- **Delivery:** Gravelly, careful, a pause before every figure.
- **Sample lines:** "Item, four pfund of pepper, not three." / "It is written, so it is so." / "Jah, jah, I'll note it."
- **Verbal tic:** Begins answers with "Item".

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows who owes whom on Nunne and Lai. He trades that for a letter of recommendation. He believes the false rumour that the council will limit Estonian clerks.
