## At a glance
- A short, pink-faced councillor in a good grey kirtle whose stubble is scraped off only once a week, on Saturday.
- Sits on the council, owns a deep house on Vene, and is quietly short of silver after a bad voyage.
- Oddity: left-handed, yet his ledgers are in a stiff upright hand; he keeps the left for everything else.

## Biography
Godeke was born in 1284 in Stralsund, the third son of a cloth-dealer, and was sent to Reval at fifteen as a Lübeck merchant's boy. He learned to write with the right hand because his master rapped the left; to this day he signs and keeps books with the right and does everything else with the left. He married Cecilia in 1305, bought the Vene house in 1320, and sat on the council from 1333.

The voyage of last autumn is his wound. He took a share from the joiner Jaan Jaani poeg in a boat for Narva with salt and Flemish cloth; it struck the Vaindloo shoal and the salt melted in the bilge. He still owes eighteen marks. A year ago he was also beholden to Birger Magnusson, who lent him a kindness at a bad moment, and he has hated the tie since.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To die with a clean ledger and a seat for Bernd on the merchant bench.
- **Fear:** A creditor naming the debt in public, in church or at the council table.
- **Contradiction:** A councillor sworn to the law, he carries messages after mass that no law would allow.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He once gave Abele Fromme a sack of rye and a favour in return, and now uses her as a go-between.

## Work and money
He trades salt, wax and cloth, buying herring and wax from Estonian carters and shipping to Lübeck. Quarter-day settlements cover the carter's week; the Narva loss left him eighteen marks in debt to Jaan Jaani poeg, about a third of his ready silver. A bad month is when the carters ask for coin.

## Faction and belief
He is Hanseatic because the council is his only honour; the Danish viceroy's tolls on the hill and any rising from the manors would both cost him more than they cost a poor man. His reason is the ledger, not loyalty. A small favour he does without question; a large one he weighs for a week; informing he has already done, in his own mind.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Middle Low German at home and council; Latin only for charters and psalms.
- **Delivery:** Nasal, quick, voice rising at the end of questions; he clears his throat before naming a sum.
- **Sample lines:** "What is owed is owed, and what is owed me is owed twice." / "I said nothing and you heard nothing, so we are agreed." / "Dat sall wi seen."
- **Verbal tic:** "As it stands."

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows who among the council is pledged beyond his goods and who is not, and keeps those lists in his head. He would trade that knowledge for a cancelled debt. He believes, wrongly, that Jaan Jaani poeg is waiting to call in the eighteen marks at Michaelmas.
