## At a glance
- A man built like a pear, narrow at the shoulder and broad at the hip, who still carries two casks at once.
- Speaks in a thin, reedy voice that cracks on German words.
- The longest-serving hand in the brewhouse, and the one who knows where every leak is.
- Eats bread-heels and calls them "the best part".

## Biography
Jaak was born in 1300 in a coastal village near Kolga, the third son of a fisherman called Olev. He walked to Reval in 1319 after a bad season and worked at the harbour carrying salt before Kaur's master took him on in 1325. He stayed through the change of masters because nobody asked him to go. His sister's three children live in the village, and he sends them a mark each Christmas by a carter. He has never married; he is a quiet part of the house and its memory.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To keep his post until he dies and send the mark each year.
- **Fear:** A fall on the cellar stairs that ends his carrying.
- **Contradiction:** He is shy and thin-voiced yet is the household's gatekeeper, answering every knock.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He knows the maids go out some nights; he oils the gate hinge so it does not squeal.

## Work and money
He gets bed, board, a coat at Christmas and half a mark at Michaelmas. He sends one of those marks home. He owes nobody and is owed a favour by a shoemaker's boy. A bad month is a broken cask or a mud-locked cart.

## Faction and belief
No faction. A man who carries other men's casks is always in the way of those who matter. For a small favour he lends a shoulder; for a large one he would ask the master; to inform he would not know what to say. He says the paternoster in Estonian and spills a few drops for his dead father.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian at home; short, apologetic Low German at the gate.
- **Delivery:** Thin and reedy, cracking on German words.
- **Sample lines:** "Mind the barrel, it is heavier than it looks." "Master is in the malt loft; I will fetch him." "Eh, I only open the gate."
- **Verbal tic:** "If you please."

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows every cask's weight, who owes the brewhouse, and who comes and goes at the gate. He would trade it for a quiet life. He believes, wrongly, that an unwashed cask can poison a river.
