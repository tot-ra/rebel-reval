## Biography
Siim was born on the Harju shore, the son of a netmaker named Taniel, and carried fish before he could read the bell. He came to Reval in 1315, worked the harbour, married Elisabet in 1327, and has hauled everything from salt to bell-metal. The hernia came in 1336 when a barrel slipped, and the truss was made by a barber for a mark he is still paying. He leaves by the back lane after curfew for night work at the quay that pays in silver and herring; the toll-clerk does not know of it.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To keep six people fed and the roof dry until the infant can walk.
- **Fear:** The truss failing, a day lost, a family on the poor list of the Holy Spirit house.
- **Contradiction:** He is afraid of the Vogt and breaks the curfew weekly.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He takes night work at the quay unloading a skipper's herring undeclared.

## Work and money
A day's work pays three to four pfennigs; a good week brings two schillings. The night work adds a herring or two. Rent is half a mark a quarter. In a lean week they eat barley porridge and little else.

## Faction and belief
He keeps out of everything; a man with a truss cannot run. A small favour he does gladly, a large one he cannot afford, informing he would never. He leaves a pinch of salt on the doorstep on St George's eve.
