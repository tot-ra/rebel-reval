## Biography
Born 1303 on Kooli to a net-mender. He worked the harbour as a boy and lost his voice to wind, rain and shouting at gulls. In 1330 the council's clerks gave him the Cattle Gate key because he could count heads of cattle without a tally and because no German wanted the post. He married Helena in 1329. In autumn 1342 he borrowed 18 marks from the baker Ulf Eriksson for a new roof after a gale took the old one, and has paid about half since.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To pay off the bond by Michaelmas and keep the roof dry.
- **Fear:** Losing the gate post, and with it the town's grey cloak.
- **Contradiction:** Sworn to the council's gate, but quietly sympathetic to those who grumble against it.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He has twice let a man through after the bell and has not told his wife.

## Work and money
A small yearly wage from the council paid in silver by weight, plus a pfennig now and then from drovers. Debt of 18 marks to Ulf Eriksson, half paid, due at Michaelmas. A bad month, such as a plague of cattle fever, closes the gate to herds and the tips with it.

## Faction and belief
Hanseatic only as a dependant: they pay him. His sympathy runs toward those who grumble at the grain levy, because he sees the carts leave. A small favour: yes; a large: only if it cannot be traced to him. Informing: if a clerk asked directly. He keeps the feasts and makes the sign over the gate.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian at home; Low German at the gate.
- **Delivery:** Hoarse, short, wind-roughened.
- **Sample lines:** "One pfennig for the herd, friend." "The bell is the bell." "Kuule, I saw nothing."
- **Verbal tic:** "So it goes."

## Knowledge and rumours
Knows who comes and goes and when. Trades for a loan extension. Believes falsely that a Danish captain will arrive with the grain levy.
