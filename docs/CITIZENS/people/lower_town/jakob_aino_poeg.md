## At a glance
- Head hangs forward, shoulders high: a permanent sack-bow.
- Speaks slowly, with long pauses that merchants mistake for stupidity.
- Left-handed, which makes him awkward on the sack-line and ambidextrous with a hook.
- Everyone owes him a day's carrying and nobody has paid it back.

## Biography
Born 1281 in a village near Kalarand, son of a Harju peasant. His father was bound to a manor after 1295 and Jakob left at fifteen to carry in Reval. He has carried grain, salt and herring for forty-seven years; his neck stopped straightening in his forties. He married Sigrid Ragnvaldsdotter in 1327 and Judit came late, in 1330. In 1338 the Rat flogged his nephew at the pillory for carrying a sack past the weighing-house; Jakob was made to watch, and has not spoken of it.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A year in which Judit is married and he can rest.
- **Fear:** The pillory, and the loss of his back.
- **Contradiction:** He says nothing, and carries the quarter's secrets.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He passes sealed scraps hidden in sack-hems to a boy named Jaan; he does not know what they say.

## Work and money
He earns about 1 örtug per heavy load and clears 2 marks a month in good weather. He buys credit at Berend Rode's warehouse. A bad month is a sprained back.

## Faction and belief
A secret sympathiser of the Black Cloaks: not from hatred of Germans but from the sight of his nephew's back. Small favour: a sack-hem; large: he would hesitate; informing: he would say nothing for a day. He keeps the old Estonian grove-days and goes to Mass.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian at home; slow Middle Low German with merchants.
- **Delivery:** Slow, with long pauses.
- **Sample lines:** "Sack... heavy. Always heavy." / "Aga... nii see on." / "Dank, Herr."
- **Verbal tic:** A long "Well."

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows what is in every warehouse and which merchants are short of grain. He would trade it for a day's rest. He believes the false rumour that the Rat will flog porters who rest.
