## At a glance
- A rosy, unremarkable woman with a distaff always under her arm, who seems to be everywhere and never in a hurry.
- Known for even thread and for sitting still through a sermon.

## Biography
Born 1309 in a village near Rannamõisa, to Jakob, a fisherman, and his wife. Her brother Jaak was flogged at the pillory in 1339 over a disputed measure of grain, and died of the fever that followed. She came into town at sixteen to spin for a merchant's household and married Paul in 1334. She learned the knot-code from an older woman who sat beside her in St Olaf's one Lent. Now she sits at the wheel on Katariina käik while her mother-in-law minds the children.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To keep the household fed through spring and to see that no child of hers is flogged for a measure.
- **Fear:** Being found out; leaving Kaspar and Kadri without her.
- **Contradiction:** A woman who values quiet hands and a still tongue, who works for a cause that needs both.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She has twice seen [Lutke van Rostok](../../people/lower_town/lutke_van_rostok.md) leave by the back lane after curfew and has told no one, not even Paul.

## Work and money
Spinners are paid by weight of thread, a few pfennig a pound; she makes perhaps two schillings a week when work is steady. Wool comes from a weaver on advance; spun thread returns to him. The family also lives on Paul's wage and the watch pay. A bad month means eating into seed meal.

## Faction and belief
Black Cloaks courier, strong but reluctant: her root is Jaak's flogging, her branch is practicality, not rage. A small favour: yes, if it fits in a skein. A large one: only if the children are safe. Informing: no. She prays to St Olaf and keeps the old rites at the hearth.

## Voice

## Knowledge and rumours
Knows who delivers what and who walks after curfew. Would trade it for a safe route home. Believes, falsely, that the watch has a list of her cell.
