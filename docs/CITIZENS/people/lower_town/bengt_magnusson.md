## Biography
Born in 1297 in Visby on Gotland to the old Magnus Gunnarsson, Bengt went to sea at twelve and hauled lines for Gotland cogs until a cargo of cloth he was guarding was taken by raiders, who gave him a scar and a share. He learned weaving afterward from a Flemish journeyman in Reval and took a master's bench in 1330. He married Anna Birgersdotter in 1325 and borrowed twelve marks from the belt-maker Hildebrand van Campen for his younger sister's dowry in Visby. The loom keeps eight under his roof. He is respectable and heavily in debt to respectable men.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To clear the twelve marks and put Helga with a decent journeyman.
- **Fear:** Fire in the weaving-shed, and being named by a raider who is caught.
- **Contradiction:** A man of the Amt who stands at the feast masses, and yet hides cargo that belongs to nobody who will say so.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He finishes plundered cloth and passes it on, and takes silver for his silence.

## Work and money
He weaves two bolts a month of coarse wool, sold to tailors on credit settled at quarter-days; the arrangement with the tailor Ants Mihkli poeg is the backbone of his week. He owes twelve marks to Hildebrand and pays four schillings a quarter. The loom, one fulling-trough and a stock of dye are his property. A bad month is a late quarter-day and a spoiled bolt.

## Faction and belief
His sympathy for the harbour raiders is a debt and a grudge: Hanseatic buyers pay weavers less each year. A small favour he grants; hiding cloth he does for silver; he would inform on no one, for his own name stands behind the cloth. He believes in St Olaf and in the fulling-saint, and spits when ravens fly over the shed.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Swedish at home and with raiders; halting Middle Low German with the Amt.
- **Delivery:** Hoarse and low, with a harbour rasp that carries only a few steps.
- **Sample lines:** "A bolt is a bolt." "I know nothing of the dye." "Det går bra, det går."
- **Verbal tic:** "Ja, ja."

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows which ships are overdue and whose cloth is unmarked. He would trade it for debt relief. He believes, falsely, that the Danish viceroy will take every loom for a royal cloth levy.
