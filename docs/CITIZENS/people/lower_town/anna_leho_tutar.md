## Biography
Born 1317 in Reval to a tallow-boiler, Leho, on Müürivahe; at nine she tipped a pot of boiling fat over her right hand and still has the glossy scar. She was sent to sell candle ends at fourteen. In 1335 she married Jüri, then a young barber, and has run the door-front shop since 1337. She has two children and a third who did not live.

She is the brisker of the two; Jüri brings home the money and she makes it go further.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A proper shop-window and a stall at the Easter fair.
- **Fear:** Fire, and the neighbour's chimney dropping bricks.
- **Contradiction:** She gives credit to the poor and scolds them loudly for owing.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She has been quietly lending a few pfennig to a runaway's wife, and does not tell Jüri.

## Work and money
She buys small goods in bulk at the harbour and sells at a pfennig or two profit. Her profit is about six marks a year. She keeps credit on notched sticks, one per customer, and holds about two marks owed. A bad month is a customer who vanishes.

## Faction and belief
No faction; her politics end at the price of salt. For a small favour she gives credit; for a large one she gives a day's help; she would not inform, but would shout if a thief came. She keeps a rowan twig over the door and crosses herself at the friary.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian only; she nods through German.
- **Delivery:** Clear, carrying and quick; she laughs mid-sentence.
- **Sample lines:** "Salt, needles, onions: what will you have?" / "No, I do not write it down; I remember." / "Kuule, mis sa seal vahid?" (Hey, what are you staring at?)
- **Verbal tic:** Slaps the half-door when she makes a point.

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows who owes what in four lanes and who has a new coat. She would trade it for a window. She believes the false rumour that salt will be taxed by the Rat at Midsummer.
