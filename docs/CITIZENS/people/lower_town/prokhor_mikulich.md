## Biography
Prokhor was born in 1305 in Novgorod to a wax-dealer's family on the Trade Side. He first came to Reval at nineteen with a winter caravan and has returned every year since, now staying at the court through the summer. He married in Novgorod and has a daughter there.

He knows the court yard to the span. When he noticed, three winters ago, that the boundary stake behind the wax-store had moved a hand's breadth toward his side, he measured it with a cord and said nothing. The neighbour, the baker Jaak, inherited the quarrel from his late father, who moved the stake.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To bring his wax home at a fair price and see his daughter wed.
- **Fear:** The Order's riders on the Narva road.
- **Contradiction:** He is a model guest and a stubborn measurer of land.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He passes the Hansa's price lists to a friend in Pskov.

## Work and money
He sells Novgorod wax and furs wholesale to German merchants, as guest-law demands, and buys salt, cloth and herring. Some four hundred marks pass through his hands in a year. A bad month is a delayed caravan.

## Faction and belief
He sympathises with Pskov and Novgorod because the Order and the Hansa both squeeze the trade routes. A small favour he does, a large one with the court's blessing, informing never. He prays before an icon and burns a candle for the dead of the road.

## Voice

- **Delivery:** Warm baritone, slow, polite, ends a point with a small smile.
- **Sample lines:** "A stake is a small thing until it is not." / "Weigh, friend, and we will both be pleased." / "Da, da, slava Bogu."

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows who cheats on weights and where the Order's scouts rest. He would trade it for a safe road. He believes, wrongly, that Jaak intends to build on the strip.
