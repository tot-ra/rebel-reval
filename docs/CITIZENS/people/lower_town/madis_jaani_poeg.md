## Biography
Born 1294 in Kopli to a fisher, he came to town at fifteen and apprenticed with a smith who cast hinges for the Short Hill Gate. When the smith died in 1324 the council kept Madis on to tend the gate itself, for a labourer's wage. He married [Kristiina Mikku tütar](kristiina_mikku_tutar.md) in 1320 and has raised three daughters. He has seen the gate shut on the grain-bound peasants and open at midnight for a viceroy's courier, and says the door is his only job.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To keep the post and see Ruth and Lutsia married to craftsmen.
- **Fear:** A day when he must choose which side to shut the gate against.
- **Contradiction:** A council dependant who whispers with people who dislike the council.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** Once opened the postern for a night rider who did not sign the book.

## Work and money
Pay is 6 schillings a month, a loaf and two ells of cloth at Martinmas. Kristiina's spinning adds 2 schillings. Iron for hinges comes from a smith's scrap; grease from a candle-maker. A bad month is a closed gate: no toll tips and no overtime.

## Faction and belief
He is council-dependent and says so. His loyalty is to the post, not the council. A small favour he would do, a large one only by night, informing never. He leaves a pinch of salt on the gate-sill at the new moon.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian at home; Low German at the gate.
- **Delivery:** Low and unhurried, lets silences stand.
- **Sample lines:** "The gate opens at prime." / "Names, please, for the book." / "Kuule, nobody sleeps tonight."

## Knowledge and rumours
Knows who passes the gate and when. He would trade for his post's security. He falsely believes the viceroy keeps a secret tunnel from the hill.
