## Biography
Born in 1305 in a Wiek (Läänemaa) shore village to a smith named Kristjan; apprenticed at twelve to a Lower Town nail-smith, and made journeyman at twenty. He married Kadri Tanieli tütar in 1327 and has buried two infants. He took over the Voorimehe shop in 1334. A bad winter in 1342 forced him to eat his seed-iron and sell the family's one cow; he has not forgotten. He survived on loans, and then Niklas Uku poeg asked him to stand surety for a fine, and he could not say no to the man who once advanced him iron on credit.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To apprentice Priidik with a proper smith, under St Canute, and not to the nail-hammer.
- **Fear:** The Rat's bailiff arriving to take the anvil for the fifteen marks.
- **Contradiction:** A loud man who cannot say no to a quiet one.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He has pledged his wife's silver betrothal ring to the brewer for six marks of grain and has told her it is at the goldsmith's for cleaning.

## Work and money
He forges nails: shipwright's spikes, roof-nails, horse-shoe nails. A thousand sells for about a mark's worth of silver, with iron and charcoal taking more than half. He pays Berend van Wismar for a shed at the back of Pikk, eight pfennigs a month, for tools and firewood. He owes the brewer six marks. A bad month is when the shipwrights go to Swedish nails.

## Faction and belief
No faction. A man with six children cannot afford a rising, and forges for whoever pays. A small favour he does; a large one he does for family; he would not inform, but would be tempted if the surety were cancelled. He leaves a nail-head at the doorstep for luck and prays at St Olaf's.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian only; a few trade words in Low German picked up from buyers.
- **Delivery:** Booming, quick, ending phrases with a shrug.
- **Sample lines:** "One nail more, one nail less; same hammer." "Ei, no, I cannot say no!" "The iron remembers."
- **Verbal tic:** Strikes the anvil edge once with the hammer before answering.

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows which shipwrights are short of nails and which merchant is late with a debt. He would trade it for a quiet settlement. He falsely believes Swedish nails are made of poorer iron.
