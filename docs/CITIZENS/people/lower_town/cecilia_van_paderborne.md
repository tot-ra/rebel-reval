## Biography
Cecilia was born in 1286 in Paderborn, the daughter of a spice-dealer who moved to Lübeck. She learned the herb trade from the apothecary's widow who also trained Hillegund Corte years later; the master taught her to know a plant by touch before name. She married Godeke van Stralesund in 1305 and bore him four children, of whom only Bernd lived past childhood. The tremor began in the hard winter of 1337, after she nursed her sister's household through the flux.

She keeps a still-room behind the kitchen and sells to neighbours on the quiet; her husband tolerates it because it brings in a few marks and good will.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To teach the craft to someone before her hand fails altogether.
- **Fear:** Dropping a measure of nightshade into the wrong jar.
- **Contradiction:** She keeps herself out of politics yet hears every household's secrets at the sickbed.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She has seen her husband's hand shake over a tally and knows he is nearer ruin than he admits.

## Work and money
She makes about ten marks a year from simples, mostly feverfew, rue, willow bark and honey salves, bought in bulk from carters and from the Dominican garden. She no longer buys with Eeva Henni tütar after a quarrel over weights. A bad month is one where the first herbs are late and the winter stock is thin.

## Faction and belief
She belongs to no party, and says the sick have no faction. She sees how both the rich and the poor die the same way. A small favour she does at once, a large one only if a child is in danger, and informing she would never do; her patients' words are closed. She keeps rue over the door and a St Anne medal inside her bodice.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Middle Low German only; she hums the old herb names under her breath.
- **Delivery:** Thin and reedy, patient, slowing down at dosages.
- **Sample lines:** "Not a spoonful more, or it is poison." / "Hold still, child, the root does not bite." / "Dat is gode Krut."
- **Verbal tic:** "By weight."

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows who has the pox, who is with child before marriage and who drinks. She would trade none of it, except to save a life. She believes, wrongly, that willow bark cures the plague.
