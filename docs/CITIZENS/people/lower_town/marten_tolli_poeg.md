## At a glance
- Brown-grey potter in a clay-grey apron; always smells of wet earth and birch smoke.
- Ten people sleep in a 49 m2 plot, so he builds shelves up the walls and rents nothing.
- Speaks slowly; customers mistake it for stupidity and pay the first price.
- Keeps one cracked pipkin on the sill, his first successful pot, never sold.

## Biography
Marten was born in Müürivahe in 1298, the son of Tõll Uku poeg, who worked wood and then took up the lathe. The boy did not like the sawdust and apprenticed at fourteen to a clay-digger outside the Viru gate. He learned to read a clay by chewing a pinch of it. He married Eeva Henni tütar in 1320, in St Olaf's, and they have had nine children, two of them buried in the churchyard. The kiln behind the house was built in the winter of 1329, when Tõll's sight was still good enough to level the bricks. The winter of 1341 nearly took the whole house: the clay froze in the pit, a pot-order from the Dominicans was cancelled, and Marten sold his second cart to buy rye. Now he is a middling, careful man whose pride is that no household of his has begged.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To put Jüri to a proper master's bench, not just his own, and to keep Lembit out of the clay pit.
- **Fear:** Another winter like 1341, when the youngest cried from hunger and no one in the lane could lend.
- **Contradiction:** He distrusts talk and gossip, yet the church door is where he learns every price.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He has suspected for a year that Jüri goes to the grove at Lasnamäe, and has said nothing, partly because he went himself as a boy.

- **Spring 1343 disruption:** Whispers of the grain levy keep customers from buying, and he fires smaller batches to save wood.

## Work and money
He sells coarse pipkins, bowls and jugs for a few pfennigs each. A good month is about a mark in sales; fuel and clay take a third. Clay he digs himself; firewood comes from a carter by the cord. He owes the dealer in birch two örtug. The plot is his, but small; the family's grain depends on Eeva's herb money in the lean months.

## Faction and belief
He has no faction; a man with nine children cannot afford to be seen. He listens, does a small favour for a neighbour, and refuses large ones. He would not inform. He is a plain churchgoer who pours a little ale on the kiln stones on St George's morning, as his father did.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian at home and at the wheel; slow Middle Low German with customers.
- **Delivery:** Low, unhurried, with long gaps; he finishes the pot before he answers.
- **Sample lines:** "A pot is honest or it is not. Come back in a week and see." "Ega ma kiirusta; savi ei kiirusta." "If the hens are in again, bring the jug, not the apology."
- **Verbal tic:** Taps the rim of whatever he is holding with a knuckle before he speaks.

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows which houses on Müürivahe cannot pay and which are only pretending. He would trade the knowledge for clay-pit access or a cart. He believes the rumour that the Dominicans will refuse Estonian pots for the altar, which is untrue.
