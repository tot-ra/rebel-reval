## Biography
Born in 1296 in the forest country behind Porvoo, the son of a tar-burner named Lauri, Paavo made charcoal and tar until a fire took the kiln in 1324. He walked the shore to Reval and began to sell what his father had taught him: birchbark pouches, tar-water for coughs and little rowan charms against the evil eye. The townsfolk, even the German wives, buy them and say they do not. He lives at the harbour berths and works the lanes of the Lower Town. He has no kin in Reval and has come to like it that way.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To buy a plot outside the walls and burn tar again.
- **Fear:** A fire at his berth.
- **Contradiction:** A pedlar of charms who prays to the saints.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** His rowan charms are blessed by no one, but he has twice been paid by priests for them.

## Work and money
He earns about 3 öre a month and pays a pfennig a night for the berth. Bark and tar cost him a third. A bad month is a damp pack.

## Faction and belief
No faction. He keeps the old forest rites quietly: a bowl of milk for the tree-spirits, a twig of rowan for the door. He would give small help, would not hide a fugitive, and would never inform. He prays at St Olaf's and does not see a contradiction.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Finnish to himself; Low German to sell.
- **Delivery:** Husky; drops to a whisper for charms.
- **Sample lines:** "Rowan for the door, tar for the cough, and God for the rest." "Three pfennig, no more." "Voi voi." (Oh dear.)
- **Verbal tic:** Touches his cap before naming a price.

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows which wives are expecting and who fears an evil eye. He would trade it for a good pitch. He believes the false rumour that tar-water keeps off the plague.
