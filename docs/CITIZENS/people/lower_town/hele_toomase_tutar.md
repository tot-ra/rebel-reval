## Biography
Hele was born in 1311 on a farm near Rapla, the daughter of Toomas, a tenant who sang at weddings. At fifteen she was bound to her lord's kitchen and sang through the work; at nineteen she left, no one quite knows how, and was taken on at a Reval inn. She came to Kaur in 1337. A year ago a boy of sixteen, [Jüri Marteni poeg](../../people/lower_town/juri_marteni_poeg.md), mended her cracked bread-crock at his Lai kiln for nothing and mentioned, lightly, the ones who keep the old ways. She joined, and discovered she was senior to people who would never say so.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To keep the circle small and the singing loud.
- **Fear:** A silence that makes someone look at her.
- **Contradiction:** She loathes owing anyone and has made a circle of people who owe her.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** The songs she sings through the day are signals; she changes a verse to tell the circle when the grove will meet.

## Work and money
She earns her keep, a gown at Christmas and one mark a year, which she gives to the circle's common bowl. She owes nothing in coin. A bad month is a burnt loaf in front of the master.

## Faction and belief
Cult of Metsik, sympathiser; her root grievance is being bound to a lord's kitchen, her branch is love of the songs. For a small favour she would sing a verse; for a large one she would hide a bundle in the dairy; to inform she would sing louder. She says the paternoster at mass and pours milk on the hearth-stone at dusk for the house-spirit.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian for songs and jokes; bright, plain Low German for the market.
- **Delivery:** Clear and carrying, with laughter rolling through.
- **Sample lines:** "Eggs, fresh eggs, who wants the best eggs?" "Sing it with me, little one, you know the words." "Ha! The master has burned his supper."
- **Verbal tic:** "Ah, ah."

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows the circle's names and the places of the grove. She would trade it for the circle's silence. She believes, wrongly, that the new priest at St Olaf is deaf.
