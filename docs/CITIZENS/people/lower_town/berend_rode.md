## At a glance
- A gaunt man, short for a councillor, whose flush climbs from his collar to his hairline whenever he is contradicted, and who never raises his voice.
- Cloth and wax pass through his cellar to Novgorod, and a good half of Pühavaimu porters have eaten his credit.
- Odd for a councillor: he stands surety for a Russian baker's fine and no one can say why.

## Biography
Born in 1298 in the Rode house on Raekoja to Hinrik Rode, a cloth-dealer out of Westphalia, and sent at fourteen to a Lübeck factor for four years. He came home in 1316, married Ellen Overdyk in 1327 and took his seat on the council in 1338 after his father died in 1336. His uncle Evert lives in the same house and still gives orders about the stable. Berend has made himself the council's accountant, the man who finds what other men missed.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To keep the council's seat in German hands and to see Heyno, his three-year-old son, grow into the Rode name.
- **Fear:** A viceroy or a master of the Order with a new tax, and a debt he cannot call.
- **Contradiction:** Believes in order and law and quietly stood surety for a foreigner who cannot pay.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** The boundary stake his father moved in 1321 shifted the Rode line a hand's breadth onto the town hall yard; Berend has checked it twice at night and has told no one.

## Work and money
He sells Flemish cloth east to Novgorod and buys wax and furs back, turning a hundred marks a season. Credit runs to porters and carters; the ledger holds about forty marks owed to him. He stood surety for six marks last year. A bad month is a closed road: wax sits in the cellar and the interest runs.

## Faction and belief
He wants the council to answer to no viceroy and no master. His circle is a few merchants who agree on little but the council's seat. He would give a loan for a small favour, a seat for a large one, and would not inform. He pays his tithe on the dot and lights two candles at St Nicholas.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Middle Low German in council and counting-room; Latin for charters and the priest.
- **Delivery:** Monotone, never loud, with pauses that lead others to fill the silence.
- **Sample lines:** "Let us reckon it again." "It is not the sum, it is the order of the sums." "Ik hebbe dat nicht vergeten."
- **Verbal tic:** Clicks one counter against another before speaking.

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows the council's grain stocks to the last lispfund. He would trade it for a safe road to Novgorod. He believes the viceroy will levy the ship-tax in May, which is false.
