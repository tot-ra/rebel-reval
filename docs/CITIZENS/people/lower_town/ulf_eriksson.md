## At a glance
- Master of one of the two big ovens on Pikk, up before the matins bell and asleep by compline.
- Husky from flour in the throat, and always a little stubbled because the razor waits for Saturday.
- Lends small sums at fixed terms, and writes every one in notches on a hazel stick.
- Slips out by the back lane after curfew with a sack, and has never been asked where.

## Biography
Born 1280 in Kalmar, a miller's son who came to Reval in 1297 on a herring ship and found work with a Swedish baker on Pikk. He married the master's daughter, buried her in 1318, and later married Kristina. He survived the hungry years of 1316-1318, when he baked rye with chaff and was cursed in the street for the price. He became a master of the St Canute fraternity in 1322. He learned Low German from customers, and Swedish still sits in his throat. He bought his plot in 1326 and has not left the bakehouse for a week since.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To hand the oven to a son-in-law who respects it.
- **Fear:** A sudden bad harvest, which means a mob at the door and a ruined shop.
- **Contradiction:** A stickler for the weight of a loaf, yet quietly gives away the broken ones.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** After curfew he carries the day's unsold bread, against the fraternity's rule, to the Holy Spirit gate.

## Work and money
Flour from the Harju mills, wood from Pikk carters, sales to cooks and shops, about 12 marks a month in a good one. He is owed 18 marks by a gatekeeper (half paid) and holds a miller's grudge. A bad month is a late mill delivery.

## Faction and belief
None, because the oven belongs to no party and bread must be sold to all. A small favour, a loaf, he gives freely; a large one he asks time to weigh; informing he refuses outright. He keeps Swedish St Erik's day and puts a pinch of flour in the fire on Midsummer.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Swedish with kin and servants; Middle Low German with customers, slowly.
- **Delivery:** Husky, patient, with a cough between phrases.
- **Sample lines:** "Weigh it. Weigh it again." / "Rye is rye, but rye in April is gold." / "Tack, tack. Next."
- **Verbal tic:** "By the loaf."

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows who buys on credit and which households are short of bread. He would trade it for peace on the lane. He believes the council has hidden a full granary, which is false.
