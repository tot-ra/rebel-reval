## At a glance
- A pale woman of middle height, left-handed, who walks fast and talks faster, keys swinging at her belt.
- She runs the spinning room in the upper floor and the kitchen with equal exactness.
- Odd for a patrician wife: she reads numbers and not letters, and her husband trusts her sums over his clerk's.

## Biography
Born in 1297 to Dietrich Overdyk, a Lübeck clothier trading in Reval; he taught her to count at the table before she could write. She married Berend in 1327 at a feast of forty guests and bore him six children, four living, and a pair lost in infancy. She ran the household through her father-in-law's death and through Berend's seat. She has become the house's quiet treasurer.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To marry Ermelin well, into a house with a good wool contract.
- **Fear:** A fever in the house in a bad spring; she has buried two.
- **Contradiction:** Exacting with servants and generous in secret with beggars at the door.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She skims a few pfennig a week from housekeeping into a purse for her daughters' dowries, and knows exactly how much.

## Work and money
She manages a household of twelve on about four marks a month. Flax comes from a Harju supplier; thread goes to the cloth-seller. She pays servants at quarter-days. She holds a small pot of silver aside. A bad month is Lent's end, when the kitchen costs more than the cloth.

## Faction and belief
No faction; her loyalties are the table and the family. She would give bread for a small favour and would not hide a fugitive. She keeps St Anne's day and spins nothing on it.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Middle Low German only; she leaves Latin to the priest.
- **Delivery:** High and quick, finishing sentences before others begin.
- **Sample lines:** "Count it again, Triin." "That is two pfennig more than yesterday." "Ik wil dat sehen."
- **Verbal tic:** Taps the key ring against her thigh.

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows what every house on Raekoja paid for its rye. She would trade it for a good husband for Ermelin. She believes the new moon spoils the milk, which is folk belief.
