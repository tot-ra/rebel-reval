## Biography
Born 1316 in a fishing hamlet near Kopli. At twelve she gutted herring for a quay-woman; at twenty she was taken by the Zierenberg house as the household's fish-buyer and scullery maid. In the winter of 1339, caught on the ice, she lost two toes; they still ache. She has kept her shouting voice, her sea-bred cheerfulness and her place, and she shares a bench with the other maids.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A warm room of her own near the quay.
- **Fear:** Ice and the winter's return.
- **Contradiction:** Loud and merry, and hides how much her feet hurt.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She has a bag of salt herring she sells on the side to a poor widow.

## Work and money
Pay is 5 schillings a year and board. A barrel of herring costs her master about 2 marks; she bargains it down by a few pfennig. A bad month is a cold snap that stops the boats.

## Knowledge and rumours
Knows what boats come in and who sells under the price. She would trade it for a warm shawl. She falsely believes herring will not come to a harbour where a priest has drowned.
