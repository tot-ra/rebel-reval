- A short, rosy man at a bench by the street window, stitching with a hog-bristle needle while customers talk at his back.
- His voice never rises or falls, so nobody can tell when he is angry; his wife reads his ears instead.
- Owes money in three directions and avoids the Cattle Gate at dawn, when the herd goes out.

## Biography
Born in 1290, son of the shoemaker Hinrik ([card](hinrik_van_deventer.md)), Sander learned to cut on the left-handed pattern and was beaten into cutting right-handed. He took the master's bench in 1321 and has stitched for the harbour merchants since. His rash year was 1342: a quarrel over a pair of unpaid boots ended with a customer's man on the floor, and the Rat fined him three marks; the same autumn he put eighteen marks into a boat voyage with Evert the gatekeeper's money behind him, and a storm off Gotland took the cargo of hides. He now lives by arithmetic done in the dark: which creditor is nearest, which door to walk past.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To clear his name before Michaelmas and put Frederik on a bench of his own.
- **Fear:** A creditor naming the sum aloud in front of customers.
- **Contradiction:** Proud of honest work, yet pays no debt on its day.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He has sold two pairs of shoes under the table to pay the shed rent, and told Margarete the leather was spoiled.

## Work and money
He makes about a dozen pairs of shoes a month and mends twice that, earning roughly four marks in a good month. Leather comes from the tanners at the stream; thread, pitch and bristle from the harbour stalls. The house is his own, but the shed for tools and firewood is rented from the brewer Kaur at a price both think unjust. Debts: three marks (the fine, standing with Mikk), eighteen marks (the boat), and the shed rent in arrears. A bad month is one without a wedding.

## Faction and belief
No faction. He lacks the credit to gamble on any side, and a rising would call in his debts at once. He asks no questions of anyone who pays. Faith is plain: a candle at the shoemakers' altar in St Nicholas, and a sprig of rowan over the door. A small favour he will do; a large one he refuses; he would inform on nobody, because it would mean speaking to an official.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Low German in the shop; careful Estonian with carters and the herdsmen.
- **Delivery:** Flat, level and quiet, with no change of pitch for good news or bad.
- **Sample lines:** "The shoe will be ready Thursday. The money is also due Thursday." "Kaur has raised the shed again. I noticed." "Wat kost dat? Nix, dat is all."
- **Verbal tic:** "So it is."

## Knowledge and rumours
He reads trades from worn heels. He would trade the knowledge for a week's grace on any debt. He believes the false rumour that the Rat will forgive petty fines at midsummer.
