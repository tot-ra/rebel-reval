## At a glance
- Stocky, bearded, heavy-eyelidded, speaks so softly that buyers lean in across the counter.
- Runs the largest house on the lane: eleven souls, a warehouse and a cellar.
- Holds pledges on half the street's tools, always in writing, never with a raised voice.
- Flushes crimson when lying, so he rarely does it aloud.

## Biography
Albert was born in 1311 on a Harju estate whose steward dealt in wax and honey, and was sent to the Dominicans at St Catherine's to learn letters and Latin so he could keep the steward's accounts. A merchant from Lübeck bought him out of the steward's service at sixteen and trained him in the cellar. After his master died in 1337 Albert took the house on Börsi käik, married Magdalena and brought Made Peetri tütar to keep it. The Germans write only the one name in the ledgers because nobody asked for another. Estonian trade partners from the manors still bring him furs. At the funeral of an old forest-keeper in 1340 he first heard the whisper of the Cult of Metsik, and has said yes to it every season since.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To win a seat in the Kindergilde before his son is old enough to be mocked for his name.
- **Fear:** A warehouse fire, and being named.
- **Contradiction:** He grows rich on German trade while handing its news to people who want it gone.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** Wax he sells to St Olaf's is blessed at a grove first.

## Work and money
He ships wax, furs and tallow to Lübeck and buys cloth, salt and iron back; he holds about 400 marks in stock. He lends against tools: 9 marks on a baker's, 6 on a merchant's. A bad month is a lost ship.

## Faction and belief
Cult of Metsik, informer; moderate loyalty. His reason is wax: he believes the forest keeps the town alive. He would give news freely, a small sum for the grove, and nothing that sends the Germans to burn it. He lights St Olaf's candles, and pours a thimble of mead at the threshold.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian in the cellar, Low German with buyers, Latin only in letters.
- **Delivery:** Soft, almost whispering, never hurried.
- **Sample lines:** "Weight is not an opinion." "I never said that. I said it was possible." "Kuule, kuule." (Listen, listen.)
- **Verbal tic:** Pauses before "friend."

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows which skippers carry extra cargo and which clerks gossip. He would trade it for silence on his pledges. He believes the false rumour that the viceroy plans to seize all wax stores.
