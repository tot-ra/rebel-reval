## Biography
Born in 1297 at Münster as a baker's son, Ludolf married young and lost wife and child in a famine winter. He took the pilgrim's badge at Münster in 1336 in memory of them and set out south. At Lübeck he spent his money; at Reval, in 1337, he found that the quay would feed a pilgrim who told a good story. Each spring since he has announced the road to Santiago and found a reason to delay: a sick knee, a lack of ships, a dream. The quay knows, and he knows that it knows. It is a bargain of silence, and he tells stories with a good heart.

## Faction and belief
No faction. A pilgrim in name, he fears the road more than the town. He would carry a message and would not inform; he tells stories and is believed. He prays at the shrines of St Olaf and St Nicholas and calls himself the friend of both.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Low German.
- **Delivery:** High and quick, rising with excitement when telling of the road.
- **Sample lines:** "On the road to Compostela I met a man with three hands." "Next spring, surely, with God's grace." "Ja, ja, so is dat." (Yes, yes, so it is.)
- **Verbal tic:** Begins every story with "Now listen."

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows every story told on the quay and which cooks give the best soup. He would trade it for a coin. He believes the false rumour that the scallop badge protects against drowning.
