## Biography
Born in 1301 near the harbour, the daughter of Marten, a sail-maker who sewed canvas for the Danish ships and died in the winter of 1327. She learned Low German as a girl selling smoked fish on the quay, and her ear nicked itself on a fish-hook at eight. She married Peeter in 1329 and has borne him three living children and buried a fourth.

She runs the household while he runs the rope-walk. When a German merchant comes to the door she speaks first, and Peeter nods at the right moments. She is the reason the rope-walk is trusted.

## Motivation
- **Want:** Her children fed and her father-in-law warm.

- **Contradiction:** She speaks German better than her husband and prefers not to be heard doing it.

## Work and money
She spins about 3 lispfund of hemp yarn a month for the rope-walk; it is the first cost of every cable and is never charged. She sells the leftover tow to caulkers for 8 pfennigs. She keeps the household purse, knows the sums owed to Erik Folkesson and by Gunnar Knutsson to the örtug, and does not tell Peeter how often she walks to the market to avoid the lender's lane. A bad month is wet hemp that rots on the beam.

## Faction and belief
None. She prays to the Virgin at St Olaf and hangs a bunch of rowan over the infant's cradle. A small favour she will do for anyone who is polite; a large one only for kin; she would say nothing to the Vogt, since she says nothing to him now.

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows the price of every ell of canvas and every fathom of cable in the quarter. She would trade it for a place for her daughter in a German house. She believes the false rumour that the Order will close the harbour.
