## Biography
Born in 1325 in Reval, the second son of a cloth-seller, Wilke was sent to the St Nicholas school at eight and learned Latin from a priest with a heavy hand. At fifteen he was placed with Thomas Langhe ([card](thomas_langhe.md)) to learn accounts, since his father could not afford the Pikk counting-houses. He now keeps the tallow books, hide counts and salt tallies, and reads the council's notices aloud to the apprentices. He believes he deserves a clerk's bench on the Rat.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A post under the town clerk before he is twenty.
- **Fear:** Being caught in his small errors and sent back to his father's shop.
- **Contradiction:** Writes in Latin for order, bends it for gain.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He has copied one of Thomas's letters to a merchant on Pikk to practise his hand.

## Work and money
He earns two marks a year and board, plus New Year gifts. Parchment and ink he buys from the scriveners by the Dominicans. A bad month is one without cattle to count.

## Faction and belief
No faction; he thinks of his own ledger and no cause. He would do a small favour for a coin, refuse a large one and inform if a clerk's bench were offered. He prays to St Nicholas, patron of scholars.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Low German with the household; Latin when showing off; broken Estonian with the servants.
- **Delivery:** Booming, quick, with a flourish at the end of lines.
- **Sample lines:** "It is written, so it is so." "Pecunia non olet, they say." "Now, hear me out."
- **Verbal tic:** "Item."

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows what Thomas pays and what he owes. He would trade it for a clerk's post. He believes the false rumour that the Rat will hire ten new clerks at midsummer.
