## At a glance
- Plays shawm at the Rathaus on council days and fiddle at weddings, so he has eaten at both ends of the town.
- Taps the doorframe three times before entering any house; clicks his tongue to count the beat.
- Stoops like a man still bending over a caulker's seam.
- His hens are a standing treaty violation against the neighbour.

## Biography
Born about 1297 in the lane behind St Olaf's, the son of a harbour caulker, Villem. Albert spent his boyhood bent over pitch and oakum until a wedding piper who lodged with his mother noticed that the boy could blow a reed without wheezing. He was bound to that piper for six years, learned shawm and fiddle by ear, and never learned letters. The stoop stayed, and so did the habit of counting.

He bought his first good fiddle, "the Dutchman", from a Zeeland skipper in 1336 for two marks and a barrel of herring, and still talks about the bargain. He married Berta, a dairy-woman, in 1333. They have lost one child in infancy; three live. He took the journeyman Mikk Kristjani poeg in at Michaelmas. He now teaches Mikk because his own breath is shortening, and he knows it before anyone else does.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To hand a working stand to Mikk or Kaur before his lungs fail, and to see the hens stop trespassing.
- **Fear:** The day he cannot hold a long note and the Rat engages a German piper from Lübeck.
- **Contradiction:** Precise on the beat, careless on the latch of the hen-yard.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He has been clipping fees: an extra örtug here and there from Estonian weddings so he can quietly underprice German ones, and so keep both trades.

- **Sundays and feast days:** Mass at St Olaf's, then the busiest day of his week, playing for dancing in someone's yard.
- **Spring 1343 disruption:** The Easter-week weddings paid well. Talk of closed gates and grain levies makes hosts cancel feasts, and a cancelled feast is a cancelled wage.

## Work and money
The Rat pays him 4 marks a year to pipe on council days; weddings add 6 örtug each plus a meal, a guild feast a mark. In a good month he clears about 2 marks. Lent is his bad month: no dancing, no pay, and the household lives on barley and Berta's dairy. He owes the reed-seller a mark and a half. He buys boxwood and gut from the harbour and sells nothing but his breath.

## Faction and belief
None. He plays for both German and Estonian hosts, and a faction would cost him half his audience. If asked a small favour he would pipe a signal; a large favour he would refuse; informing he would refuse flatly, since the next wedding depends on being trusted. He is devout at mass and also lays a crust under the hearth on Thursdays.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian at home; Middle Low German with hosts and the Rat.
- **Delivery:** Clipped and precise, quick, counting off syllables like beats.

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows who dances with whom at every feast in Lai and Pikk, and who left early. He would trade it for a good reed or a month's firewood. He believes, falsely, that the Rat plans to take away the harbour piper's yearly fee.
