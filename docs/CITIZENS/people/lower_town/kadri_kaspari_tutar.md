- Stocky, 160 cm, with a wind-reddened face and protruding ears that her veil cannot hide.
- Speaks in short clipped sentences, each word placed exactly, like eggs in a basket.
- Rises before Matins to milk the convent's two cows.
- Hears more at the gate than the portress does.

## Biography
Born in 1279 on a Harju manor, daughter of a herdsman named Kaspar; the manor sent her to the convent at sixteen as a gift in the year of the great snow, and she took simple vows in 1296. She learned the Hours by ear and has never read a letter. She has milked cows for forty-seven years and mourns each by name.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To be buried within hearing of the cow-byre.
- **Fear:** The murrain, which took two cows in 1330.
- **Contradiction:** Silent by vow, yet the best informed woman on the street.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She sends a little cheese to her brother's family at the manor each Michaelmas, hidden in a basket of eggs.

## Work and money
She owns nothing. The convent sells about eighty eggs a week and a pound of butter at the Aida market, at a few pfennigs, and the money goes to the purse. She knows the weight of every egg by hand.

## Faction and belief
Church, core. Her root is simple loyalty to the house that kept her; her branch is the animals. A small favour she gives; a large one she cannot; she would inform only to protect a cow. She keeps the Rule and tucks a bundle of straw at the byre door each St John's Eve.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian in the byre; slow Low German at the market.
- **Delivery:** Clipped and precise; no wasted words.
- **Sample lines:** "Twelve. Take them." "Ei. No." "Cow first."
- **Verbal tic:** Taps each egg with a knuckle before naming a price.

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows who is buying and who is selling in Lower Town, and who has gone hungry. She would trade it for hay. She believes, wrongly, that a black hen laid the egg that cracked at Easter.
