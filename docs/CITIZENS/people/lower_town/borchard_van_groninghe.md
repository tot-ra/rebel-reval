## Biography
Born in Groningen in 1288, third son of a cloth factor, Borchard was shipped to Lübeck at twelve to count herring barrels and came to Reval at twenty-four as a junior partner in a grain-and-wax house. He married Geseke Lippe in 1318 and bought the Dunkri house with her dowry and two bad winters of Novgorod wax. The failed harvest of 1341 taught him what he now believes: that the city survives on who owes whom. He took a council seat in 1336. He lives well and sleeps in snatches;

## Motivation
- **Want:** To hand his sons a house that still owes nothing to Toompea, and a Reval whose harbour stays open whoever holds the hill.
- **Fear:** A levy that empties his lofts, and the day his two ledgers meet in public.
- **Contradiction:** He lectures the Rat on honest weights, yet lets a Danish clerk's grain debt sit in a cover book no one audits.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He has twice seen Ludolf van Munster slip out through the back lane after curfew and has told no one, not even the circle.

## Work and money
He ships rye, wax and furs east, cloth and salt back, in shares with two Lübeck partners. This spring he is cash-short. He owes Niels Skjalm six marks' worth of grain from the failed harvest, and is owed three by Ghert Kleine. A bad month means a late cog and Geseke asking why the marten collar was not sold.

## Faction and belief
He is core Hanseatic because the Hanse built the only Reval he has known. In the circle he passes Lübeck-bound word. A small favour he gives easily; a large one he weighs for a week; informing on a councillor only if his lofts were at stake. He keeps a saucer of milk at the hearth, as his mother did.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Middle Low German at council and counting-room, Latin for charters, a few Estonian words to porters.
- **Delivery:** Warm baritone, slow, each sentence ending a half-beat early as if checking the sum.
- **Sample lines:** "Seven marks, and the weights are mine, so we need not argue." "I sleep when the ships do, which is seldom." "Dat is nich wat ik seggen wull." (That is not what I meant to say.)
- **Verbal tic:** "By weight."

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows the Rat's grain stock to the lispfund and Ludolf's back-lane habit, and would trade the latter for a seat on the grain committee. He believes the false rumour that Toompea will levy rye at Whitsun.
