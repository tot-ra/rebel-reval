## At a glance
- Average height, sun-browned, with a close-trimmed brown beard and one shoulder visibly lower than the other.
- Hired by the voyage on Lübeck cogs and berthed at the harbour inns between sailings.
- Carries a cord with knots for every day of the season, since he cannot write.
- Thin, reedy voice that carries oddly well across water.

## Biography
Heine was born in 1297 in a salt-boiling village by Lüneburg and came to the Baltic at fourteen as a cook's boy on a Lübeck cog. The right shoulder was pulled out of its socket in a gale off Gotland in 1319, set by the carpenter, and never sat level again. He married a Lübeck rope-maker's daughter in 1330; she died of a fever in 1338. Their small daughter now lives in Lübeck with his sister, and a good part of every wage goes there by a skipper's hand. In the winter of 1341-42, when the ice kept him in Reval, she boarded with a widow on Tolli and fell sick one January night, which is how he came to owe something to the Russian trader there. He berths each season at the harbour inns and sails by the voyage.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To save enough silver by Michaelmas to buy a half-share in a small boat and stop sleeping on other men's decks.
- **Fear:** A fall from the yard; his shoulder is failing and he knows it.
- **Contradiction:** Gentle with children and strangers, short with the men who share his berth.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He has begun to hide his shoulder's weakness from the mate, and has paid a pedlar for a salve that does nothing.

- **Sundays and feast days:** Mass at St Olaf's among the harbour men, then an hour on the quay looking at the ships that carry letters to Lübeck.
- **Spring 1343 disruption:** Gate-closing rumours keep ships in harbour, which means no hire; the spring grain levy talk makes the innkeepers raise the berth price by a pfennig a night.

## Work and money
He earns about 6 öre a month on a good voyage and a few pfennig for day labour in port. The berth costs him a pfennig a night; the rest goes to his daughter's keep and a pot of tar for his clothes. A bad month is a fortnight of ice or a fall that costs him the arm.

## Faction and belief
No faction. A hired hand has no politics; a rising would shut the harbour and his income with it. He would carry a message for a coin and would not hide a fugitive; he would not inform for fear of the quay's revenge. He crosses himself at St Olaf's and touches the mast-foot for luck.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Low German at work and prayer; he understands a little Estonian from the quay.
- **Delivery:** Thin, reedy, slow; he pauses to find the word.
- **Sample lines:** "Three pfennig and a heel of bread, and I am yours for the day." "My girl is in Lübeck; she writes me nothing, for neither of us can." "Dat is so, dat is so." (That is so, that is so.)
- **Verbal tic:** Strokes the knotted cord at his hip when he thinks.

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows which skippers are short of crews and which ships have been in Reval too long. He would trade it for a day's hire. He believes the false rumour that a ship that lies in port at St George's Night will never come home.
