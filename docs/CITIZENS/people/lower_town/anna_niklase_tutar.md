## At a glance
- A short, broad woman who moves at one pace and arrives everywhere on time.
- Runs the laundry and the kitchen fire, and knows the weight of every wet sheet in the house.
- Remembers a small favour at the Coastal gate differently from the girl who did it.
- Has a laugh that surprises the household because it is so rarely used.

## Biography
Anna was born in 1310 in a fishing hamlet at Kopli, the daughter of a net-mender called Niklas, and came to town at fourteen to wash linen for a Dominican guest-house. She served three households before the van Luneborchs took her in 1336, and she stayed because the kitchen was warm and the mistress counted wages on the day. She married nobody, though a carter from the Viru road asked her once, and she said she would think about it, and he took a different girl. She has outlasted two older maids and is now, quietly, the fixed point of the kitchen.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A tidy corner of her own with a bed and a chest, bought at the mark a year she is saving.
- **Fear:** A fever in the kitchen that takes the whole household and the post with it.
- **Contradiction:** She hates being hurried yet keeps the whole kitchen on time.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She cannot remember the Coastal gate favour as the other girl tells it; she remembers owing a pfennig and has never paid it.

## Work and money
She earns her keep, a gown at Christmas and one mark a year, which she keeps in a twist of cloth in the chest. She buys lye and soap in small lots from the Raekoja bakers' wives and sells the ash back to a dyer. She owes a pfennig to a girl at the Coastal gate and has been "about to pay" for four years. A bad month is a late thaw when the sheets will not dry.

## Faction and belief
No faction. A maid has no vote and little sleep; she would rather the matter went away. For a small favour she would lend soap; for a large one she would think about it for a month; to inform she would not understand the question. She says the paternoster in Estonian and lays a clean sheet on the bed of any maid who falls ill, as her mother taught her.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian with Helena and the Kopli women; a plain, slow Low German to the family.
- **Delivery:** Low and unhurried, with long gaps, as if each word were weighed.
- **Sample lines:** "The sheets will dry when they are ready." "I owe her a pfennig and I shall pay it when I remember." "Sit, the porridge is on."
- **Verbal tic:** "In time."

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows which linens are whose, who has sick sheets, and who is carrying a child before the household does. She would trade it for a quiet corner. She believes, wrongly, that lye of beech ash cures a cough.
