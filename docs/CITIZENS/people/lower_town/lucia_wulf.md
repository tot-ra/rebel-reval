## Biography
Born in 1286 in Reval, daughter of a cooper named Wulf; she grew up speaking the Estonian of the cooperage and the German of the church. She entered St Michael's at nineteen after her sister married into a house on Suur-Kloostri. She learned writing from the old chaplain and has kept the sacristy since 1318. She lost her back teeth to a fever in 1331 and has eaten soft bread since.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A clean altar for the feast and an unbroken supply of good gall-nuts.
- **Fear:** A fire among the linen and candles.
- **Contradiction:** A strict keeper of silence who talks freely at the gate.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She has been giving ink and a few quills to Estonian lay sisters who want to learn their letters.

## Work and money
She owns nothing. The convent's ink sells for about four pfennigs a horn, to town clerks and the chaplains, and the money goes to the purse. She counts wax by the pfund and notes every stub.

## Faction and belief
Church, core. Her root is the order's discipline; her branch is the linen. A small favour is lending a quill; a large one she would refuse; she would not inform, but she hears confession-talk at the gate. She trusts the reliquary's saints and keeps a sprig of rue under the altar.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Low German with the town, Estonian with lay sisters, Latin in choir.
- **Delivery:** Clear, carrying, precise; a rasp on sibilants where the teeth are gone.
- **Sample lines:** "Black as oak-gall, black as the Rule." "Tule, come, the wax is warm." "Do not touch the linen."
- **Verbal tic:** Counts candles aloud in Latin.

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows whose names are in the death-masses. She would trade it for a pound of good gall-nuts. She believes, wrongly, that ink made at full moon is blacker.
