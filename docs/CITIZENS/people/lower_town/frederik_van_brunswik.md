## At a glance
- A councillor who still counts his own bales, left-handed, on a board of pegs.
- Clipped, exact speech; he finishes other men's sentences when they are wrong.
- Rents out sheds and lends on roofs, always at a rate that is just defensible.
- Sallow and freckled, with a cleft chin that gives his silence a stubborn look.

## Biography
Born 1283 in Reval, the third son of a merchant from Braunschweig. He was a boy of thirty when the old wars ended, and remembers the grain shortage of 1316-18 with horror. He joined the Kindergilde at twenty-five, took over the family trade at thirty, married Sophia in 1319 and was elected to the council in 1334. He is a careful man whose caution is partly fear: the family fortune was lost once and rebuilt. He sits on the council's grain committee and speaks little there.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To pass the house and the trade intact to Dietrich, and a council seat for the family.
- **Fear:** A rising that closes the gates and a council that cannot pay its debts.
- **Contradiction:** Careful and exact, yet lends at rates no priest would bless.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He keeps a second list of guild members he regards as unsound, and shows it to no one.

## Work and money
Trade in rye, wax and furs, about 200 marks a year; plus rents on sheds and loans. He lent 6 marks to a tailor in autumn 1342 for a roof; the bond falls due at Michaelmas and about half is paid. A bad month is a closed port.

## Faction and belief
Core Hanseatic: he is the guilds' voice in council on grain and tolls, and believes the Lübeck law is the only wall that keeps the Danes and the Order at bay. For a small favour he writes a letter; for a large one he calls in a debt; for informing he is the one informed. Devout, he endows a candle at St Nicholas every Easter.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Middle Low German on the council; Latin for charters.
- **Delivery:** Clipped, precise, faintly irritated.
- **Sample lines:** "State the figure, please." / "The bond falls due at Michaelmas, as written." / "Na, Godeke, nothing to say, then."
- **Verbal tic:** "Precisely."

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows the council's grain stocks and who in the guilds is behind with dues. He would trade it for a council seat for his son. He believes the Order will arrive by sea before midsummer, which is false.
