## Biography
Born in 1273 on Gotland, Magnus was fifteen when the townsmen of Visby and the countryfolk fought over the walls in 1288; he carried water behind a barricade and learned to hate the sound of horns. He made sails for thirty years, followed his son to Reval in 1330 when the Visby yard failed, and gave up the needle only when his eyes went. He buried his wife in 1335. He is proud of his son and anxious for him, and says it by criticising the loom.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To see the new baby christened and to be buried beside his wife.
- **Fear:** Fire, and being a burden.
- **Contradiction:** A man who shouts at everyone, yet gives his bread to a stray dog.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He has noticed the weaving-shed bolts that arrive in the night and has told his son to be careful, in Swedish, so the servants cannot follow.

## Work and money
He earns nothing and costs a half-mark a year in food, which he repays in patched canvas sold at the quay for a few pfennigs. His own chest holds a palm-thimble and his wife's ring. A bad month is a cold one.

## Faction and belief
He has no faction beyond the Gotland of his childhood. For a small favour he would mend a sail; for a large one he would tell a story that warns; he would not inform. He prays to St Olaf and puts a sail-needle by the door for luck.

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows how a sail is cut and how a cog sinks. He would trade it for a warm seat. He believes, falsely, that the Gotland men will return to take Reval.
