## Biography
Born 1274 in Bremen, brought to Reval at eleven by an uncle who put her to a braider on Pikk. She worked her own frame from the age of twenty and never married; she sold braids to guilds and merchants for forty years. During the famine of 1316 she ate bark and a neighbour's cat and watched a child die on the step, which she has never told anyone.

In 1334, with her hands failing, she sold her workshop to the Stenhus family in return for lodging and a modest keep for life, a Leibrente she negotiated herself. She has a warm room off the kitchen and a high stool by the fire.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To die in her own bed in the warm room, and to see the street not go hungry again.
- **Fear:** Hunger, and the day the annuity is renegotiated.
- **Contradiction:** She is grateful to the Stenhus family and listens at their kitchen wall for what the poor will pay.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She carries scraps of news between two women in the Sauna lane and a clerk on Pikk, and has done so since Lent.

## Work and money
Her annuity is six marks a year in coin and keep; she braids a few ells a month for friends. She lends small sums to the maids. A bad month is a cold snap that stiffens her hands.

## Faction and belief
She is a dependent of the Hanseatic house and loyal to the family that keeps her. Her sympathy with the rebels is from hunger and memory, not slogans: she wants no child to be left on a step. For a small favour she carries a message; for a large one she would offer her room; she would not inform. She says her rosary and leaves a bowl of milk for the house spirit on St Martin's Eve.

## Voice

- **Delivery:** A warm baritone, slow and rounded; speaks in short, comfortable sentences.

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows who in the house is courting whom and who has a new debt. She would trade it for a seat by the Rathaus door on feast days. She believes the false rumour that a Danish ship is bringing grain at last.
