## Biography
Born in 1320 in a small town on the Swedish coast of the Baltic, the son of an inn cook who died of a fever when Lars was twelve. He shipped as a cook's boy on a herring cog and landed in Reval at seventeen. Jaak Lauri poeg took him on for his knife work and has kept him since, paying better than any other house on Nunne. The burn scar was his first lesson in respect for hot iron.

He now lives under the kitchen stair and sends nothing home because there is no home to send it to. He wants a copper of his own.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To be named master cook and rent a hearth in his own name.
- **Fear:** Losing the use of the scarred hand, and the day somebody asks him to sign for his wages.
- **Contradiction:** A man of great precision with measures who forgets which feast day it is.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He once ruined a guest's dinner with an unknown fish and Jaak covered for him; the debt sits on him like a stone.

## Work and money
Wages are 3 marks a quarter plus board; he keeps 1 mark in a boot. His best tool is a marble mortar shared with Wobbeke Rotermund of Lühike jalg, passed back and forth for pounding spice; each keeps a mental ledger of how long the other has held it. Stock comes from the market and Jaak's strand deliveries, which Lars does not ask about. A bad month is a spoiled eel.

## Faction and belief
None. A Swede in a town of Germans and Estonians is first to be blamed in a riot, so he keeps his head low and his knife in its sheath. He says the Swedish grace before eating, and sprinkles salt in the fire on Saint Lucy's day.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Swedish with other Swedes, Low German with the household.
- **Delivery:** Clipped and precise, each word given an edge.
- **Sample lines:** "Not that spoon. The other." / "Salt is a thought, not a handful." / "Det är bra, that is good."
- **Verbal tic:** Counts under his breath in Swedish while he stirs.

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows every kitchen's secret recipes by taste and which house sends away good meat. He would trade it for a copper pot. He believes the false rumour that German masters poison their rivals' wine.
