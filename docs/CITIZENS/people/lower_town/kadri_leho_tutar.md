## Biography
Born in 1308 in a Harju village, the daughter of Leho, a quarryman. She came to town at fifteen, married the mason Hinrick in 1327, bore Peeter in 1331, and learned the trade at his side. Hinrick fell from the Viru Gate scaffold in 1339. She took a second husband, Eerik, who died of a winter fever in 1342, a few weeks before their son Andres was born. The widow kept the tools, the lime pit and the contacts.

She is now head of a poor household and a skilled worker whom the masons' fraternity does not recognise.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To buy a share in a lime-kiln and a proper roof.
- **Fear:** Losing the boys to the Harju manor or the fever.
- **Contradiction:** A seller who bargains like a merchant, yet is shy of being called one.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She holds back lime and sand when a carpenter owes her, and twice lied about the shortage.

## Work and money
She earns around three marks a month in a good season, from foundations and walls. Lime comes from a Harju kiln, sand from the strand. Debts: one mark to the grocer. A bad month is rain on an open trench.

## Faction and belief
No faction. A widow with children cannot risk a side. She would give a small favour for a fair price, a large one for none, and would not inform. She keeps the old stone-offering by the lime pit and attends St Olaf's.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian at home; plain German on the site.
- **Delivery:** Sing-song, loud, direct.
- **Sample lines:** "Stone is stone, price is price." "Ära tule, do not come with your promises." "I carry my own."

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows which walls are weak. She would trade it for a good price. She believes, wrongly, that the Order will pay masons double.
