## Biography
Born in 1299 in Reval, the son of a gate-warden's cousin, Gerdt took his father Paul's keys at Viru Gate in 1330. His wife Kadri ([card](kadri_hendriku_tutar.md)) spins; their daughter Ann ([card](ann_gerdti_tutar.md)) spins too. The woad vat began as a way to pay the winter fuel bill; he now dyes a few bolts for the weavers in Nunne in the cellar, and the blue never leaves his hands. He lends a few marks, patiently, to men he knows.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To see Ann married to a master and the cellar paid off.
- **Fear:** A carter bribed through at night, and his name on the Rat's list.
- **Contradiction:** A council servant who suspects his fellow keepers of cheating the same council.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He knows which carter paid whom at the Viru Gate, and has said nothing.

## Work and money
He earns about two marks a month from the council plus tips, and a mark from the dye. Woad comes from the harbour merchants; dye goes to Nunne. Credit: six marks owed him by a tanner. A bad month is one when the gate is shut for a levy.

## Faction and belief
Hanseatic, dependent: the council pays him and a Dane or an Order man would not. He acts from the plain wish to keep his post. He would do a small favour, refuse a large one and inform if the council asked directly. He prays at St Olaf and leaves a bowl of ale for the house-spirit on St Martin's Eve.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian at home; careful Low German at the gate.
- **Delivery:** A sing-song cadence under the German, slow and cheerful.

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows what comes in and out at night. He would trade it for his post. He believes the false rumour that the keepers will be replaced by Danish soldiers.
