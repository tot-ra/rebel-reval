## Biography
Born in 1320 in Wismar, a baker's second son. The parish priest of Wismar taught him letters. Tideman came to Reval at nineteen with a letter to the Hanseatic men. In Reval Father Wessel van Brunswik of Niguliste became his confessor; he baptised the child of Tideman's married sister and buried an uncle last winter. A sailor, Detlev Starke, paid for his berth in 1342; a merchant, Borchard van Groninghe, lent him a winter cloak the same year. Each claims him.

He copies freight lists and letters, and walks the town with his eyes open.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To be a factor in a Hanseatic counting house.
- **Fear:** Being found out to serve two masters.
- **Contradiction:** He is loyal to the League and to the two men who helped him; he hopes they never need him at once.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He has copied one letter twice, with a changed line.

## Faction and belief
An active Hanseatic hand, serving for gratitude, not profit. A small favour: copy a letter. A large one: carry a message. He would inform on a rebel who threatened his masters, and would regret it.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Low German; Latin in letters.
- **Delivery:** A flat, tired murmur.
- **Sample lines:** "I will copy it by evening." / "I was not asked." / "Ja, ja, Father."
- **Verbal tic:** Blows on his fingers.

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows what the Hanseatic men write about the town. He would trade for a post. He believes the false rumour that the Order has sent a spy.
