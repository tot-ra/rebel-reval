## Biography
Ludolf was born in 1310 in Münster, the younger son of a cloth-dealer, and came to Reval at nineteen as factor for a Lübeck house. He saved, bought a share in a cog and by 1338 owned the Rataskaevu house with its yard and stable. He has not married: a Blackhead's seat in Reval's circle is for the unwed, and he guards it. He writes his own letters in Latin and Low German, keeps two sets of accounts and trusts few people with either. In the last year he has been carrying words to men who prefer not to be seen with him, and leaving by the back lane to do it.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A seat among the merchant elders before he is forty and a marriage worth the wait.
- **Fear:** A letter in the wrong hand, and a ship that does not return.
- **Contradiction:** A man of ledgers and exact weights who runs on rumour and favour.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He uses his Harju cartage as cover for carrying private notes.

## Work and money
He ships grain, wax and furs to Lübeck and imports cloth, salt and iron, holding about 350 marks in stock and shares. He buys cartage from the carter Taniel on credit settled at quarter-days. He is owed 18 marks by a crown clerk, patiently. A bad month is a lost cog or a cart stuck in the Harju mud.

## Faction and belief
Blackheads, sympathiser. His reason is the unmarried merchants' standing: a bachelor with money and no seat is a man the council overlooks. He would carry a letter, lend a cog's hold, but not arm anyone. He keeps the saints' days properly and puts a pinch of salt at the stable door.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Low German in trade; Latin in letters; clipped German with servants.
- **Delivery:** Low, unhurried, pauses before every number.
- **Sample lines:** "A promise is a cheaper thing than a cog." "Say it again, slower." "Gode morgen, Magnus." (Good morning, Magnus.)
- **Verbal tic:** Says "to be precise" before a lie.

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows which cogs are late and which councillors are in debt. He would trade it for a quiet seat. He believes the false rumour that the Danish crown will ban Harju grain exports.
