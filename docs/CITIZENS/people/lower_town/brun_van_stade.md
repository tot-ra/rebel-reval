## At a glance
- A tall thin youth with downy whiskers and a nose that bends right, usually up to the elbows in the mash.
- Known in the lane for answering a question about two breaths late.
- Oddity: the son of a councillor who speaks better Estonian than most Germans because he grew up among the maltsters.

## Biography
Born 1325 in the great house on Katariina käik, the eldest child. At six he was set to turn malt; at eleven he could tell a good mash by smell. At fourteen he was kicked in the face by a dray horse in the yard, and Brother Rotcher from the friary set his nose, which healed crooked. His mother says it gives him character.

He is the only one of the children old enough to be trusted with the brewhouse and young enough to want to escape it. He has fought with Ghert Kleine, the tavern keeper on Dunkri, since the winter of 1341 when Ghert refused to sell him barley on credit during a shortage.

## Motivation
- **Want:** His own mash tun and his father's silence replaced by praise.
- **Fear:** Being a worse brewer than his father; also horses.
- **Contradiction:** He sulks at being made to work and cannot stay away from the brewhouse.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He has brewed a small batch of his own recipe, with juniper, hidden in a barrel behind the malt loft, and has never given it to his father to taste.

- **Spring 1343 disruption:** His father will ride out for barley the day after St George's; Brun will run the brewhouse for a week and is delighted and terrified.

## Work and money
He receives board and a few pfennigs for his own use, spent on a knife and a second-hand leather belt. Barley comes from Harju manors and, in a pinch, from Ghert Kleine's stock, which Ghert holds when he wishes to press a point. A bad week is a sour mash.

## Faction and belief
He has no faction and little curiosity about the Rat. His loyalty is to his own brewhouse; his anger is for Ghert Kleine and the price of barley. For a small favour he would carry a keg; for a large one he would ask his father; he would inform on no one, being unable to decide fast enough. He puts a few grains in the first mash for luck, as the maltsters do.

## Voice
- **Registers:** German at home, Estonian with the maltsters and carters.
- **Delivery:** Slow, with long pauses; answers a breath late and then plainly.
- **Sample lines:** "...The mash is not ready." / "...I will ask my father. ...He will say no." / "Dat is nich recht." (That is not right.)
- **Verbal tic:** Sniffs his sleeve before he answers.

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows the real malt stocks, which inns take sour beer and which lanes the maltsters are muttering in. He would trade it for credit at Dunkri. He believes the false rumour that horses can smell fear in a brewer.
