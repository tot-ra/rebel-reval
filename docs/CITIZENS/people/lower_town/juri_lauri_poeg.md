## Biography
Born on the Karja road, the son of a stave-splitter, Jüri was apprenticed at twelve to a German cooper by the harbour. He lost three back teeth to one abscess, and in 1324 bought the Harju house with eleven years of savings and a loan from his old master's widow. He married Ell Mikku tütar in 1327. He serves the Estonian night watch because a householder's turn comes round every sixth night, and he tells nobody he enjoys the silence.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To hand Henn a cooperage with no debts, and to pay Kaur on the quarter-day without borrowing against the next.
- **Fear:** A bad stave season, or being named to the Vogt as someone who whispers.
- **Contradiction:** He despises being owed favours and yet runs his whole week on credit.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** A year ago the gatekeeper waved his hoop cart through after the bell. Since then he has attended one funeral and one whisper he does not describe to his wife.

- **Spring 1343 disruption:** The gate closes earlier, the watch is doubled, and hoop-iron is dearer by a penny a pfund.

## Work and money
A herring cask sells for about two schillings, a good ale-barrel for five. He buys oak staves from Prussian shipments and iron hoop-bands from the smiths. Kaur takes barrels and supplies ale on credit, settled at quarter-days. A ruined batch costs him four marks and a night's sleep.

## Faction and belief
He sides with the guilds because the guilds buy barrels. His circle watches Danish levies and gate tolls; it is not a rising. A small favour he repays; a large one he weighs against his household; informing he refuses, though he would pass a warning. He still pours a little first beer at the cresset.

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows who pays late and which gate-guard takes a hoop as a bribe. He believes Reimar's oak is timber landed at night from a Livonian cog; it is not.
