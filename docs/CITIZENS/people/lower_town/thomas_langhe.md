## At a glance
- A broad, gravel-voiced man in a good brown kirtel who still wears a slaughterer's apron on market morning.
- Lends small sums that sit like stones on the borrower's back, never forgotten and never called.
- Grows stubble all week and shaves on Saturday, so he looks two men.
- Splits a cart with a Viru butcher and quarrels about it weekly.

## Biography
Born in 1304 in Reval, son of a Lübeck-trained butcher, Thomas took his father's stall at twenty-one and married Hillegund Corte ([card](hillegund_corte.md)) in 1325. He drives cattle from the Harju manors through the Cattle Gate and sells hides to the tanners. He rose to the Rat in 1338 on the strength of bread and meat sold cheap in the dearth. A year ago he did the town clerk a small kindness, and has been quietly gathering allies on the council since.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To keep the cattle roads open and his seat on the Rat for his sons.
- **Fear:** A levy on cattle, and the Order marching in to set prices.
- **Contradiction:** Generous with his debtors, then keeps the account for years.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He has promised Gerhard van Bremen a seat on the clerk's bench in return for lists of the Rat's votes.

## Work and money
He slaughters about thirty head a month and sells to the Rat's table and the harbour. Cattle come from the Harju manors by the Cattle Gate; hides go to the tanners. Credits: three marks to Tõnis for a dowry, and many small sums. A bad month is one when the herd is stopped at a gate.

## Faction and belief
Hanseatic, core: the council's money feeds his trade and a free road feeds his herd. He acts from the practical wish to keep prices stable and the Rat in charge. He would give a small favour, refuse a large one that cost his seat, and inform only for a clear price. He pays for a mass for his father at the Dominicans.

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows who on the Rat is in debt to whom. He would trade it only for a seat or a price. He believes the false rumour that the Order intends to take the Harju herds.
