## Biography
Born in 1302 in a Harju hamlet, son of a peasant named Jaak; he came to the convent at fifteen as a hired boy when the manor was short of grain. He rose to head gardener in 1331 when the old man died. He lives in a lean-to by the garden wall and is paid in kind plus a few marks. His wife died in 1338; his one daughter married into a Wiek village.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To plant a pear tree that will fruit in his lifetime.
- **Fear:** A late frost in May, which would kill the whole spring crop.
- **Contradiction:** Proud of his craft, yet sells his best cabbages cheap to buy goodwill.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He keeps seed from a Flemish cabbage he swapped for a barrel of dung, which the nuns do not know is not a native variety.

## Work and money
He has board, a corner of the garden and a mark and a half a year. Market sales go to the convent purse, but he keeps the cracked pots and the windfalls. He owns a spade, a hoe, a donkey cart and a dog.

## Faction and belief
No faction. A servant who wants to see his seeds grow stays out of the rising. A small favour he does; a large one he refuses; he would inform only if his garden were threatened. He crosses himself over each seed bed and leaves a first radish at the garden wall for the old earth-spirit.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian in the garden; basic Low German at market.
- **Delivery:** Gravelly, slow, rising into a growl when contradicted.
- **Sample lines:** "Moon first, spade after." "Ära puutu mu kapsaid, do not touch my cabbages." "Frost comes, frost goes."
- **Verbal tic:** Counts the beds on his fingers before speaking.

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows which beds sprout early, and who steals from the orchard. He would trade it for a graft of a good pear. He believes, wrongly, that a night of rain after sowing curses the seed.
