## Biography
Born in 1271 in Reval to a ship's carpenter, he became a master shipwright in 1305 and built cogs and small keels at the harbour yard for thirty years. In 1317 the ice stayed until May and his yard was sold for the king's levy at half its price; he never forgave the Danish steward. He retired in 1332 and moved into his nephew's house after his brother Hinrik's death in 1336. He keeps the stable and the harbour opinion.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To live to see a firm hand on the town, a hand that pays what it owes.
- **Fear:** Dying before the debt to his yard is settled, and being buried in a Danish ditch.
- **Contradiction:** Genial and generous with strangers, hard on Berend.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He has met a travelling Order agent twice at the Dominican cloister and has said nothing to his nephew.

## Work and money
He lives on the sale of the yard (twelve marks a year in rent from the new owner) and a small share in a Gotland cog. He owes nothing, and lends a few marks to men like Hildebrand. A bad month is a wreck among his shares.

## Faction and belief
He sympathises with the Order because it pays and keeps its word. He would pass on gossip for a small favour, shelter a messenger for a large one, and would not betray a friend. He keeps the old harbour custom of pouring ale into the water at the first ship.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Middle Low German, the harbour dialect, with shipwrights' words.
- **Delivery:** Hoarse from the harbour wind, rumbling, with chuckles.
- **Sample lines:** "In the ice-year I saw a cog stand on its keel." "A good plank does not argue." "De Orden betalt, de Koning nicht."
- **Verbal tic:** Taps the adze against his boot.

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows every keel in the harbour by its sound. He would trade it for a fair word about the yard's old owner. He believes the Order has a ship waiting at Dagö, which is false.
