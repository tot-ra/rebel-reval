## Biography
Born in Soest, Westphalia, to a salt merchant, she came to Reval at nineteen to marry Eler Lippe. She learned the shop from his mother. At twenty-six she hid a stolen dowry chest and told the chaplain Ropert van Kolne in confession; he refused absolution until she returned it. She did, at a cost of six marks and a quarter's shame. She has respected and resented him ever since.

## Motivation
- **Want:** Every child fed, schooled and married into a safe house.
- **Fear:** The baby, Katharina, falling ill with the spring fever.
- **Contradiction:** She wants a godly house yet counts the poor-box twice.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She keeps one silver mark in a hollow bedpost, unknown to Eler.

## Work and money
The shop makes 20 marks a quarter in notions and spices. She buys from the same Lübeck houses as Eler and pays at quarter-days. She owes 6 marks to a carrier and is owed little. A bad month is a sick child and an empty shelf.

## Faction and belief
No faction: a woman in her place is expected to have none, and she has learned the use of that. A small favour, she would give; a large one, she would ask her husband; to inform, she would pray first. She keeps the Marian feasts and a hearth-pot of salt on Midsummer.

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows what every Pikk wife has bought in Lent. She trades it for the best cloth. She believes the false rumour that bad water at the Smiths' Gate caused the winter fever.
