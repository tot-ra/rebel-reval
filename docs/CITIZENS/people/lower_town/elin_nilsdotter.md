## Biography
Born 1291 in a fishing parish on the Swedish coast of the Gulf of Finland, and brought to Reval at nine by her widowed mother, who found work on the Swedish quay. She was apprenticed to a washer-mistress at fourteen, learning lye, ash and the way to beat out a stain without tearing linen. She worked in other women's houses, then took a tub of her own. She married Toomas in 1323 after he had mended the hearth wall of her rented room. They have had three living daughters. Now she takes in wash from three German houses, sells small goods from a basket, and keeps the household accounts in her head.

## Motivation
- **Want:** Her daughters settled and herself never again in a rented room.
- **Fear:** The lye finally taking her hands; being dependent on a German mistress's good opinion.
- **Contradiction:** Careful with every pfennig, yet gives credit to women she knows will never pay.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She has hidden a small purse of silver pennies behind a loose stone in the cellar wall, and Toomas does not know.

## Work and money
She earns about 4 pfennig per household load and washes for three houses; weekly taking is perhaps 2 schillings. Ash comes from bakers, lye from the soap-boiler on Sauna street, linen cord from the ropewalk. Toomas's earnings are lumpy, so her coin keeps the pot filled in a bad month. She owes nothing, but is owed small sums by neighbours, and writes none of it down.

## Faction and belief
No faction: she is a foreigner twice over, Swedish among Estonians and Germans, and trusts none of the three. A small favour she would do; a large one only if it protected her children. She would not inform. She keeps Swedish church customs, sprinkles ash at the threshold on St Walpurgis Eve, and prays to St Erik and St Olaf, the northern saints.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Swedish with family and neighbours; a few Low German market words.
- **Delivery:** Slow, with long pauses; people lean in to hear the end of a sentence.
- **Sample lines:** "It will come out. Give it time." / "I do not sell dear. I sell once." / "Tyst, child, listen."
- **Verbal tic:** Presses her tongue into the tooth gap before answering.

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows who in the German quarter is poor, because she sees the shifts mended three times. She would trade it only for safety. She believes, falsely, that wetting linen with Easter water keeps off the plague.
