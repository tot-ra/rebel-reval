## Biography
Born 1295 in Reval to a labourer, Laurents, who dug clay for the potters outside the Viru gate. At twelve Andres began as a clay-treader, and by twenty he threw his own pots. He bought a workshop with a kiln on Katariina käik in 1325 with his wife's dowry, and has fired two kilns a week since. The winter of 1341 was a bad one: the kiln wall cracked and he lost a month's work, but he and his neighbours shared fuel and peas.

His chimney, which should have been raised two courses of brick, still smokes into the barber's loft. He has meant to fix it for two winters.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A new, taller kiln chimney, and a place for his daughters.
- **Fear:** A cracked kiln, and the cost of brick.
- **Contradiction:** A proud craftsman who cannot spare two marks for the one repair that would make peace with his neighbour.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He has already bought the bricks and hidden them behind the shed, because he cannot yet face asking the barber for forgiveness.

## Work and money
He sells jugs at two pfennig, pots at one and glazed bowls at three, to the lane, the market and the inns. Income about 25 marks a year. Clay comes from the pits outside the Viru road, lead for glaze from Lübeck via the market, wood from the carters. He owes a wood-seller 3 marks. A bad month is a cracked firing.

## Faction and belief
No faction; he thinks the Rat and the crown are weather and the kiln is the only thing to mind. For a small favour he gives a pot; for a large one a day at the wheel; he would not inform. He leaves a pot-shard at the kiln mouth for the fire spirit before the first firing of spring.

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows who buys lead glaze and who waters the clay. He would trade it for a taller chimney. He believes the false rumour that a kiln fired on a new moon never cracks.
