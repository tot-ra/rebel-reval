## Biography
Born 1307 in a Kopli fishing hamlet, the daughter of a net-mender. At twelve she was kicked by a cart-horse in the harbour and the shin was set by a bone-wife in a way that stayed crooked. She learned to count with pebbles from a Swedish stall-keeper and married Ain in 1325. She has carried four children to term and two to the churchyard. Since 1335 she has kept the window shop, with credit from Hinrick Henni poeg, and now holds the family's real money.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A proper counter and a brass balance of her own.
- **Fear:** A fine for a short weight, or a winter when the children cough.
- **Contradiction:** Counts every örtug yet gives candle-ends to anyone who cries.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** Keeps eleven marks in a salt crock, unknown to Ain.

## Work and money
Takes in about 5 örtug a day. Buys salt by the lispfund, thread by the hank. A fine of three schillings for a short weight would take a month. Owes the supplier 2 marks.

## Faction and belief
No faction; politics is a thing that makes the price of salt go up. She would hide a neighbour's goods, not a man. She keeps a saint's wax stub and a charm of rowan over the door.

## Knowledge and rumours
Knows who is behind on their rent on Aida, by their candle use. Would trade it for a good scale. Believes that the new Danish tax will fall on shops measured by window width, which is false.
