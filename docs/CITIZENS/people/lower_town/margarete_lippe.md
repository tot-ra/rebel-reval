## At a glance
- Ten centimetres taller than her husband, with a drop spindle always turning in the left hand.
- Runs the household purse; Sander does not know how little is in it.
- Thin, reedy voice that carries across a courtyard when she wants it to.
- Listens after mass more than she prays, and hates that someone taught her to.

## Biography
Born in 1293 in Lübeck's Beckergrube lane to a spinner mother, Margarete came east at twenty as the wife of a Reval widower who died in a year; Sander van Deventer ([card](sander_van_deventer.md)) married her in 1320 for her dowry linen and her head for figures. She buried an infant daughter in 1326 and bore Frederik at forty-one, which she calls God's joke. She spins for the merchants on Pikk because wool and flax outwork is the one income the Rat cannot fine. A year ago, after Sander's fine, the flax stopped and Tibbeke sent a sack of it unasked.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To keep the three maids fed and fed on time, and Frederik in school.
- **Fear:** Seeing the house and shop sold to meet a bond.
- **Contradiction:** Prizes independence and has accepted a gift she cannot repay.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She has lent Sophia her own ribbon, and does not know what Sophia carries in her head.

## Work and money
She spins about three pounds of flax a week at a few pfennigs per pound; Ann and Sophia carry and card. Raw flax comes from the Pikk merchants; yarn goes back by weight to the weavers in Nunne street. Her earnings cover bread and candles and are most of what Sander has not spent. A bad month is one when the merchant is late or the yarn short in weight.

## Faction and belief
Hanseatic sympathy, weak and dependent: the cloth merchants feed her, and the Rat keeps the roads open. She has no hatred for anyone, only a debt she does not like. A small favour she will do; a large one she would refuse unless Frederik were threatened; she would inform only about merchants she thinks cheat the spinners. Church is St Nicholas; she leaves flax on the hearth at Candlemas.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Low German at home; Estonian with maids and traders.
- **Delivery:** Thin, reedy, carrying; fast when counting, slow when guarded.
- **Sample lines:** "The yarn is short a quarter pfund and I say so now." "Say nothing, Sander." "Gode Gott, wat een dag."
- **Verbal tic:** "As I counted."

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows who owes money to whom among the weavers' wives. She would trade it for flax at a fair price. She believes the false rumour that the Rat plans a levy on spun yarn.
