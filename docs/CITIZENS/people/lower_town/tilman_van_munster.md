## At a glance
- The oldest preacher in the house, sturdy and white-haired, who gives the Sunday sermon to Estonians in Estonian.
- Known for walking slowly home from church and for stopping at every doorway to ask after a child.
- Oddity: a German friar who dreams in Estonian and curses in Westphalian.

## Biography
Born 1277 in Münster to a cloth-fuller, Tilman took the habit at seventeen and was sent to Reval in 1300 with a letter from his provincial. He learned Estonian from the lay brothers and from the women at the herring tubs, and by 1310 he was preaching it from a cart in the market square. During the siege years of 1313-1325 he buried both soldiers and peasants, and refused to preach against either. He saw the Danish bailiff flog a man outside the friary gate in 1319 and thinks of it whenever he preaches obedience.

His knee has failed since a fall on ice in 1338. He still preaches every second Sunday and hears confession on Saturdays; he lives in a cell with a view of the lane, where he watches for children.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To leave the friary a short Estonian catechism, written in Latin letters, that a lay brother can read aloud.
- **Fear:** That the parish priest of St Olaf's will have the Estonian sermons stopped once he is gone.
- **Contradiction:** He loves the Estonians and cannot bring himself to ask the Rat to treat them better, for fear of losing his sermons.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He keeps a bundle of Estonian proverbs on scraps in his cell, some with a pagan edge, and has never shown them to the prior.

## Work and money
Tilman earns nothing; the friary feeds him. He spends small coin on ink and a little honey for his throat, perhaps two örtug a month. His sermons bring gifts in kind: a net of herring, a bundle of firewood, a loaf, which he hands to the cook. A bad month is a winter of ice, when the knee keeps him in.

## Faction and belief
He belongs to the Church by vow; his sympathies lie with those he preaches to, but he would never lead a crowd. For a small favour he gives a blessing, for a large one he prays, and he would not inform. Privately he leaves a crumb for the house spirit in the kitchen on Christmas Eve, a habit from his mother.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Latin in choir; Estonian in sermons; Middle Low German at table.
- **Delivery:** Husky and slow, voice cracking on long sentences; pauses to cough, then goes on.
- **Sample lines:** "A sermon is a cart: if it is too heavy, no one pulls." / "The Lord counted the sparrows; I count the hungry." / "Tule, laps, ma saadan sind koju." (Come, child, I will see you home.)
- **Verbal tic:** Clears his throat before every third sentence.

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows what Estonians say in confession about the grain levy and who is hungry on which lane. He would trade nothing for it, but would speak for a man in trouble if asked. He believes the false rumour that the Order has already bought land at Rakvere for a new house.
