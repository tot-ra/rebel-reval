## At a glance
- Walks Pikk with a hitch in the left stride and a cocked head, because the left eye does not follow the right.
- One of the youngest councillors, sat in the Rat on the strength of his late father's stall and his own careful ledgers.
- Keeps a household of ten, among them two clerks, a half-grown apprentice and five maids.
- Rarely raises his voice; people lean in to hear him, which he has learned to use.

## Biography
Born on Pikk in 1306 in the house his father built with money from the Novgorod fur and wax trade. At nine he broke his left shin leaping from a warehouse hoist on a dare; the bone-setter was drunk and the leg healed short. His father's fury taught him that carelessness costs silver, and he has been careful ever since. He spent four years in Lübeck in a counting house, returned in 1330, and took over the Reval business when his father died in 1337. In 1341 the Rat co-opted him after a vacancy; he suspects he was chosen because he promised to be quiet. He is a widower of no recorded marriage in the census; the household is simply his clerks, maids and apprentice. Now he lives on small margins and steady habit.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To keep the Pikk house and its name intact, and to be known as the councillor who never signed a bad bond.
- **Fear:** A rising that burns the warehouses, and the loss of Lübeck's trust.
- **Contradiction:** He hates waste yet keeps the unused upper floor heated for guests who never come.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He has twice seen the moneychanger Gavril leave by the back lane after curfew, and has told no one; he does not know whether to treat it as leverage or danger.

## Work and money
Imports Westphalian cloth and salt, exports wax and furs eastward on shares with Lübeck partners. Turnover is perhaps 400 marks in a good spring; bad ice or a lost cog means one of his two bonds goes unpaid. He holds 15 marks of petty debts owed to him and owes the Rat nothing. Wages: a clerk 8 marks a year with board, a maid 2 marks.

## Faction and belief
He is Hanseatic because the council and the trade are his home, not out of zeal. For a small favour he would delay a report; for a large one he would risk a bond; to inform he would hesitate until it protected the house. He prays at St Nicholas, keeps the Lenten fast exactly and lights a candle for his father on the saint's day.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Middle Low German in council and at table; Latin for charters.
- **Delivery:** Low and unhurried, with long pauses that make hearers fill the silence.
- **Sample lines:** "We shall see what the tally says." "A bond is a promise with a date on it." "Dat is nicht min sake, but I note it."
- **Verbal tic:** Rubs the wart with a forefinger before naming a figure.

## Knowledge and rumours
Knows which skippers are late with freight, who is behind on Lübeck bills and Gavril's night movements. He would trade silence about the last for a seat on the next ship-share. Believes falsely that Reimar Blyde's stock comes from a wreck on the Estonian coast.
