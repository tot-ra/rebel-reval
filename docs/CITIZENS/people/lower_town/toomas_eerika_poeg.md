## At a glance
- A short, thick-set man with a thin early-receding hairline and a high, quick voice that goes up when a horse does.
- Speaks only Estonian; gets by in German with grunts and the names of tools.
- Knows every hoof in the lane by its print.
- Never sleeps in the house if the mare is sick.

## Biography
Toomas was born in 1314 in a Harju village on the Tallinn road, the son of a manor groom named Eerik, and grew up under the stable roof of the manor. At sixteen he walked to Reval to escape being bound to the land and found work at the Rataskaevu livery of a German master who also, later, trained [Kersten Rode](../../people/lower_town/kersten_rode.md). When the master died in 1335 he took the post at the van Luneborch house, where a single cart-horse, a mare and a mule need him daily. He sleeps in the stable loft and has not seen his father's farm since the winter of 1336.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A stable of his own, even a lean-to, and a horse that is his.
- **Fear:** Being returned to the manor as an escaped bondsman if an old lord's reeve ever looks hard.
- **Contradiction:** He is gentle with animals and sharp with people, especially children.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He was never freed; his father's lord has not claimed him, and he has told nobody that he fled.

## Work and money
He gets bed, board, a coat at Christmas and half a mark at Michaelmas. He buys oats and straw from the market women at the household's cost and takes a penny commission when he can bargain it down. Harbour carting for the house earns the master about a mark a week. He has no debts and a mark and a half in a sock behind a stable board. A bad month is a sick horse.

## Faction and belief
No faction. A runaway keeps his head down and his mouth shut, and he has done both for seven years. For a small favour he would hitch a cart; for a large one he would hide a runaway in the straw, for he knows what they are; to inform he would go cold. He says the paternoster in Estonian and ties a red thread on the mare's halter at St George, against the evil eye.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian only; German in nouns and nods.
- **Delivery:** High and quick, sentences stumbling over each other when excited, almost soothing to a horse.
- **Sample lines:** "Easy, girl, easy, he is only the master." "Hay is dear, hay is always dear." "I saw nothing; I was with the mule."
- **Verbal tic:** "Eh, eh."

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows who has horses, who owes for fodder, and which carters take runaways to the Pärnu road. He would trade it for a horse of his own. He believes, wrongly, that a white mare brings the plague.
