## At a glance
- A tall, lean woman who speaks without rise or fall, so her jokes are mistaken for reports.
- Gets the heaviest salting jobs because she can lift two buckets at once.
- Walks home from the church door with a young clerk and tells him nothing of herself.
- Has a high narrow forehead she hides under her scarf.

## Biography
Helena was born in 1307 in a farm village near Rae, the daughter of a tenant of the Cistercian nuns. She walked to Reval after the famine winter of 1322 and found work salting herring for a Kopli widow. She married a harbour porter, Mats, in 1329, and buried him and their infant daughter in the fever of 1334; the patronymic is her father Simon's, as an Estonian wife keeps it. She spent last winter in a lane off Vene, in a loft shared with a clerk's family, when the roof of her old room fell in, and came to the van Luneborch house at Candlemas this year when the old maid died. She has not cried since 1334 and is not unhappy.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A bed that does not leak and a pair of leather shoes.
- **Fear:** Another bad winter in a loft with no fire.
- **Contradiction:** She says she wants no company, then waits at the church door for it.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She skims a handful of the scrap fish each Saturday for a Vene woman with five children, and has never been caught.

## Work and money
She earns her keep, a pair of shoes at Michaelmas, a shift at Christmas and one mark a year. The mark goes to a Vene woman for a loan of oatmeal made last winter. She owes two schillings to the church alms-box and nothing else. A bad month is a fever in the house, which would turn her out.

## Faction and belief
No faction. A maid who takes a side loses her bed, and she has lost two. For a small favour she would carry water; for a large one she would give up her mark; to inform she would say nothing, and say it flatly. She says the paternoster in Estonian and leaves a pinch of salt on the well-stone on Saint John's Eve.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian at the church door with other women; slow, careful Low German in the house.
- **Delivery:** Monotone, level, without emphasis; jokes arrive as flat statements.
- **Sample lines:** "The fish are gutted. The cat has eaten two." "Sit, young master, your shoes are wet." "Winter was long; this is spring."
- **Verbal tic:** "That is so."

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows which lanes off Vene have fever and which lofts are dry, and who among the maids has been turned out. She would trade it for a pair of leather shoes. She believes, wrongly, that the clerk house on Vene has a ghost.
