## At a glance
- Short, sturdy, rosy, with a cleft chin and a warm baritone that carries across a courtyard.
- Cooks the Easter and St Nicholas dinners herself though two hired cooks stand idle; she says no one else salts a goose properly.
- Speaks kitchen Estonian to the maids, to the astonishment of visiting wives.
- Holds a small, practical network of kitchen doors that tells her what the town is about to do.

## Biography
Born in Lübeck about 1285, the daughter of a Rotermund who kept a cookshop in the Breite Strasse. She came to Reval at twenty-six as the bride of Tideman van Soest, bringing a dowry of forty marks, a copper kettle, and a stubbornness about gravy. Her mother's kitchen taught her to listen: a cook hears the price of herring before the brokers do. She learned Estonian from the girls who carried water, and has never lost it.

She buried two sons, bore one daughter, Jutte, late, at forty-four, and runs a house of nine people.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To see Jutte married well and her own recipes outlast her, and to keep the house fed through whatever the levy brings.
- **Fear:** Hunger in her own larder; a house that cannot feed its maids is a house that is dying.
- **Contradiction:** A woman of the council's household who talks to maids and fishwives as equals, and who would still call the Vogt if they burned her stores.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She passes small word of the lanes' temper to the guild side, and believes no one has noticed that she does.

## Work and money
The household's food costs about three marks a week in a lean spring; she keeps the purse for it. She trades her own surplus dried peas and smoked fish to neighbours for malt. Her wafer irons and a copper kettle are worth more than most gowns on the lane.

## Faction and belief
Active on the Hanseatic side, out of loyalty to a house and a trade, not slogans. A small favour is a dish of food; a larger one is a message carried; she would inform only to save the stores. She keeps St Martha's day and sets out a bowl of milk by the hearth.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Middle Low German for the house, Estonian for the yard and maids.
- **Delivery:** Warm baritone, quick, laughs mid-sentence.
- **Sample lines:** "Salt first, then argue." "Wat is dat, child? Eat." "The levy is a stone in the soup."
- **Verbal tic:** "Mind your pot."

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows which households are low on rye and which merchants have stopped sending to the lanes. She would trade it for a favour that never has to be named. She believes, wrongly, that the new Vogt is poisoning the town well at the Sauna.
