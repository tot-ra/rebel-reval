- Average height, fair and quick to flush, with a pale cold-sore scar on the upper lip that she touches when thinking.
- Speaks slowly, with long pauses; strangers think her dull, the household knows she is weighing every word.
- Keeps the linen-chests in order and can tell from a sheet which guest slept in it.
- Hums a Swedish psalm tune when sewing and stops when someone enters.

## Biography
Born in 1314 at Nyland on the Finnish coast to a Swedish pilot named Ragnvald, who drowned off Porkkala in 1330. A cousin brought her to Reval at seventeen and found her a place with the van Soests. She learned German slowly and with great care, because a wrong word cost a maid her place. Her silences became a habit and then a defence.

She lives in the loft above the linen-room, has no kin in town, and keeps a small wooden Saint Birgitta on her chest.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A place in a house where she is not the only Swede, or a husband who does not hurry her speech.
- **Fear:** Saying the wrong thing in the wrong tongue to a master.
- **Contradiction:** Reserved to a fault, but the one in the house everyone confides in.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She has read the household's tally of linen against what the mistress sells, and knows two bolts are missing.

## Work and money
Three marks a year and her keep, plus cast-off linen she sews into her own shifts. Tools are needles, thread, a lye-tub, shears. She keeps eleven pfennigs under a loose board. A bad month is a stained sheet, charged to her wage.

## Faction and belief
No faction. A new arrival in a foreign town keeps her head down; she would run a small errand for a neighbour, no more. She keeps Saint Birgitta's day and says her prayers in Swedish.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Swedish in prayer; careful Middle Low German.
- **Delivery:** Slow, long pauses, soft.
- **Sample lines:** "I will say it... when I have it right." "Ja, so it is." "A sheet remembers."
- **Verbal tic:** Pauses mid-sentence and touches her lip.

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows the household's linen and who sleeps badly. She would trade it for a letter read aloud. She believes, wrongly, that the new Vogt forbids Swedes the harbour.
