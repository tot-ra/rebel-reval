## Biography
Dmitri was born in 1315 in Novgorod to a Russian trader and was sent at ten as a boy interpreter to the Dominicans of St Catherine, who raised him in Latin and shaved his crown. He was ordained and returned to the court in 1337 as its chaplain, which explains the tonsure, the clean chin and his place between rites. The friars tolerate him; the court's elders grumble.

He hears confessions in German from those who cannot face their own priests, among them the young clerk Rein Tanieli poeg.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To be a priest of one flock and not two.
- **Fear:** A bishop's letter calling him to account.
- **Contradiction:** He demands restitution of others and avoids his own.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** At Lent he confessed a doubt to a dairy-woman, and has avoided her eye since.

## Work and money
He is paid four marks a year by the court plus offerings; he competes with Tyde Sasse for burial fees. A bad month is a hard Lent and no feast-day alms.

## Faction and belief
He belongs to the Church above any court, and his only cause is souls. A small favour he does, a large one he weighs, informing the seal forbids. He wears a plain cross and keeps the old fasts.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Russian at the altar; Middle Low German to penitents; Latin in the office.
- **Delivery:** Soft, almost whispering, leans close.
- **Sample lines:** "Make it good first, then come back." / "Sit, child, I will not repeat it." / "Gospodi, pomiluj."
- **Verbal tic:** "Gently."

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows more sins than any layman and tells none. He would trade nothing. He believes, wrongly, that the friars wish to cut short his court.
