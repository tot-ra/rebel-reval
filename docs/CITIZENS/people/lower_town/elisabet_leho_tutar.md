## At a glance
- A small, lean woman who steps to the loft shutter at each bell and sniffs the wind.
- She is the oldest maid and the smallest, and is usually the one who carries the heaviest sacks.
- Odd for a town servant: her skin is weathered tan from the boats, and she still speaks of tides as her family does.

- **Body:** 150 cm, lean and wiry, narrow hips, sinewy forearms; walks with a sailor's wide-footed step, leaning into wind that is not there.

## Biography
Born in 1314 on a Kopli fishing beach to Leho, who owned a share in two boats. Her father and elder brother were lost to a storm in 1332; her mother sold the share and sent Elisabet to town. She served a ropemaker's wife on Aida until 1338, then came to Wendla's loft. She has spun for five years and still does not like the smell of flax.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To save enough for a boat share and go home.
- **Fear:** A sudden storm, and the news that arrives with it.
- **Contradiction:** Wants the sea and feels sick on a boat.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She sends half her pay to her mother on the shore by a carter, and lies to Wendla about where it goes.

## Work and money
She earns a mark a year and keep, and sends half. She buys nothing. She is owed two pfennig by Mari from a lost bet. A bad month is the loss of her carter, leaving her money stranded.

## Faction and belief
No faction. She would bring a message to the shore for anyone who paid. She believes in St Olaf and in a seal's grin as an omen, and leaves a fish head on the threshold.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian with the maids; mostly silent in German.
- **Delivery:** Thin and reedy, with a high end on each sentence like a gull.
- **Sample lines:** "The wind is in the east, no boats today." "Tuul keerab, ära mine." "That sack is not heavy, I have carried worse."
- **Verbal tic:** Wets a finger and holds it up.

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows which boats are overdue and which skippers are lying. She would trade it for a ride to the shore. She believes the sea will give back the dead at Midsummer, which is folk belief.
