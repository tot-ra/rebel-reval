## At a glance
- Walks with a rolling, wide-legged gait, as if the cobbles were a deck; he went to sea as a ship's boy before he kept a ledger.
- Ledger first, conversation second: his sentences are short and numbered.
- Lends grain and silver to men who cannot refuse, and never names the interest.
- Keeps a dozen hens behind the warehouse; they are his only extravagance.

## Biography
Born 1313 in Åbo, son of Folke Eriksson, a grain-and-tar shipper who moved to Reval in 1322 when Åbo's harbour tolls rose. At seventeen Erik served a season on a Visby cog and took the burn from it, and the lesson that goods are cheap where a ship lands and dear where a man needs them. Folke died in the winter of 1338; warehouse, debts and favours came to Erik at twenty-five. He married Ulrika Larsdotter in 1340; Kristina was born in spring 1342. He is not loved but he is dependable: a cart ordered of Erik arrives on the day he said, or a boy comes to say why. He sleeps badly after quarter-days.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A place in the Kindergilde, and a council bench for his daughter's husband to inherit.
- **Fear:** A bad shipping year that calls in his own credit before his debtors have paid.
- **Contradiction:** Gentle with hens and infant, merciless with a late repayment; he thinks both are good housekeeping.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** Part of his cheap stock comes off Finnish coasters that skip the Coastal Gate toll because a cousin's skipper takes the wrong channel. He calls it custom. It is smuggling.

- **Spring 1343 disruption:** Levy talk and closed gates make him buy rye early; he fits a second lock to the warehouse before St George's Night.

## Work and money
He buys rye, salt, tar and Finnish hides at the quay and sells to bakers, brewers and Aida's shipwrights, at roughly a sixth margin when ships come on time. Perhaps 140 marks stand out on credit, including 12 marks of grain and 15 marks of silver, none in the Rat's books. A bad month is a late ship: he calls in the small loans first.

## Faction and belief
No faction: every banner in Reval means a levy on warehouses. For a small favour he obliges, for a large one he names a price, to inform he would first ask who benefits. He keeps St Olaf's feasts and nails a rowan twig over the hen-house door.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Swedish at home and with skippers; Middle Low German on the quay; Latin in charters, badly.
- **Delivery:** Clipped and precise, low volume; numbers before names.
- **Sample lines:** "Twelve marks, grain, Michaelmas. That was the word." / "Wat kost dat, un wanner?" / "Your hens, Henneke. My beer. Same as last time."
- **Verbal tic:** Ends statements with "so."

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows which skippers short-weight and which councillors are in debt; he would trade it for a safer berth. He believes the false rumour that the viceroy will tax every warehouse door.
