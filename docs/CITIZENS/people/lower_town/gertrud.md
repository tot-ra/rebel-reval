- A broad-shouldered woman behind the shop board, forearms white to the elbow, speaking so softly that customers lean in.

## Biography
Born in 1311 on Müürivahe, daughter of Elisabet, a Harju-born woman who baked braided fast-day loaves for the Dominican guest-house. She learned dough at her mother's elbow and married Jaak in 1333. Three sons followed: Eerik, Jaak and, this February, Henn. In 1338 a bad winter killed her first daughter at two weeks; she speaks of it never. She now manages the till, the dough and the books, which are tally sticks. Her whisper is not shyness; it was learned in a house where walls are thin.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To bake enough to keep the shop open and the boys fed.
- **Fear:** Jaak's creditors and the cellar where her mother's sacks go.
- **Contradiction:** A quiet woman whose shop is the loudest in the lane.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She knows where Jaak goes at night and prays he is careful.

## Work and money
The shop nets about 3 marks a month from her side alone, honey-cakes and wheat rolls being dearer than rye. Honey comes from a Harju beekeeper, spice from the Novgorod clerks at a good price. She keeps a leather purse with 2 marks hidden behind the flour bin. A bad month is when the sponge fails or a neighbour asks for credit.

## Faction and belief
No faction; her mother's politics she keeps at arm's length, because a rising would close the Novgorod trade. For a small favour she gives bread; for a large one, a night's shelter; to inform she would go silent. She places a cross in each loaf and speaks the old words at the oven door only when her mother is not listening.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian at home, low German at the board.
- **Delivery:** Soft, almost whispering, with a slight pause before every price.
- **Sample lines:** "Two pfennig, and a half for the rye." "Hush, the boys are sleeping." "Dat sal wi seen, na Paschen."
- **Verbal tic:** Wipes her hands on her apron before answering.

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows which household is in arrears and which Russian clerk is homesick. She would trade it for her mother's safety. She believes a loaf that falls from the peel foretells a death.
