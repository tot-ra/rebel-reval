## Biography
Born in 1320 in a fishing village near Åbo (Turku) on the Finnish coast, Margareta came to Reval at sixteen with a herring-skipper uncle and was placed in service by the Swedish priest at St Olaf's. She has served at the van Campen house six years. She sends two pfennigs a quarter home to a widowed mother. She cannot read and has not wished to. She is friendly with the stable hand and shy of the master.

## Motivation
- **Want:** Enough savings to buy a bed and a chest, and to marry a fisher or a boatman.
- **Fear:** Dismissal, and a fever like the one that took her father.
- **Contradiction:** She sings in a voice the whole yard hears but never speaks her mind in the master's hall.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She keeps the leftover broth in a cracked pot for a begging widow at the back gate, which the mistress has not noticed.

## Work and money
She earns three marks a year with board and a gown at Easter. Of that she sends one home. She buys the household's eggs and herring with the mistress's coin and is checked against the tally. A bad month is a missed measure of malt.

## Faction and belief
She belongs to no party; a servant's politics is a day's work. For a small favour she would carry a basket; for a large one she would think of her post and refuse; she would not inform. She prays at St Olaf's and leaves a bowl of milk on the cellar step.

## Voice

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows which servants of which house are paid late and which gossip is true. She would trade it for a bit of ribbon. She believes, falsely, that a cat born on Good Friday cannot be drowned.
