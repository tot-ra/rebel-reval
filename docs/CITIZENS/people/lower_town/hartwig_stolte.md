## At a glance
- Sturdy, ruddy, 154 cm, with a high early-receding hairline that the lane's boys call "the cathedral".
- Counts everything: candles, wicks, steps, chimney bricks.
- Warm baritone, breaking-voice rumbles at the wrong times.
- Is the only German in a Swedish house, and resents being corrected.

## Biography
Born in 1329 in Reval to a German day-labourer, Stolte, who died of fever in 1338. His mother apprenticed him at ten to an old chandler who then died; Sven Svensson took the indenture over. A year ago the quarter-day wage was shorted and Hartwig calculated it on a wax tablet. The old master's methods, a steady hand with a wick and a dislike of waste, marked him.

He sleeps in the loft on a sack of wicks and means to be a master himself.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To finish his years and set up his own vat.
- **Fear:** Being sent back to his mother in disgrace.
- **Contradiction:** A boy who wants to be trusted with the till but cannot stop correcting the master's sums.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He has kept a private tally of the wax used against the wax sold, and the two do not agree.

## Work and money
No wage; board, bed and a pair of shoes yearly. He earns a few pfennigs from errands. Tools: wick-scissors, ladle, mould. A bad month is a spilled vat.

## Faction and belief
No faction. A boy of fourteen with an indenture has no choices. He would run a small errand for a penny, never anything large. He prays aloud to Saint Nicholas, patron of boys.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Middle Low German only.
- **Delivery:** Warm baritone, blunt, cracks at the wrong word.

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows how much wax is truly used. He would trade it for a bigger share of supper. He believes, wrongly, that tallow will be taxed at Michaelmas.
