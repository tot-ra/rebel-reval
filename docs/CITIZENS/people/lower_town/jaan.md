## At a glance
- A lean, freckled boy with a wart on one cheek and a cleft chin who negotiates the price of everything, including his dinner.
- Speaks low and slowly, so older men think he is wise.
- Sells spent grain to the swine-keepers and keeps a pfennig of every shilling.
- Carries a message he does not understand in the lining of his cap.

## Biography
Jaan was born in 1329 on the Harju road, the son of a carter who was flogged in 1337 for failing to deliver a levy of oats, and who died of the fever after. His mother put him with Kaur at ten. He learned to judge malt, boil and sell the spent grain, and to keep quiet. A man called Gerdt gave him his first pfennig for carrying a word to a stable, and the chain grew from there. He has no politics, only a debt to the men who gave him money for being quiet.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To buy his own copper and pay his mother's rent.
- **Fear:** Being beaten as his father was.
- **Contradiction:** A businessman at fourteen who gives away half his earnings.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** Two pfennigs a week for carrying a word to a maid, [Kadri Andrese tütar](../../people/lower_town/kadri_andrese_tutar.md), and a word back; he has never asked what the words mean.

## Work and money
He earns bed, board and a shilling at quarter-days, and a pfennig a week on the spent grain. Two pfennigs a week come from his errands, which he hides in a hollow beam. He owes nobody and is owed six pfennigs by [Peter van Bremen](../../people/lower_town/peter_van_bremen.md). A bad month is a spoiled brew he is blamed for.

## Faction and belief
Black Cloaks, sympathiser. His root grievance is his father's flogging; his branch is the pfennig, not the cause. For a small favour he would carry a word; for a large one he would ask the price; to inform he would calculate. He says the paternoster quickly and touches a cloth scrap sewn into his cap.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian with the carters; slow, calm Low German with merchants.
- **Delivery:** Low and unhurried, a pause before each number.
- **Sample lines:** "A pfennig for the word, two for the answer." "The grain is dry, and dry grain is dear." "I carry; I do not read."
- **Verbal tic:** "Let us see."

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows the malt price and who buys it, and the three stable doors where the circle knocks. He would trade it for his own copper. He believes, wrongly, that the Black Cloaks pay a mark for each message.
