- Short, pear-shaped German maid who corrects everyone's grammar with two precise words.

- Never shouts, never hurries, never forgets.

## Biography
Born in 1315 in Reval to a carter from Münster, Sophia learned every street by the cart route. Her father Wessel took a Danish toll-bill on the Toompea road in 1329 and spent the next six years paying it; he died in 1336 owing silver to a Pikk merchant who let the debt lapse for a promise. She was placed with the van Deventers at twenty-one and has stayed. She cannot read, so she keeps the household accounts by knot and rhyme, and after the winter funeral of a spinner at St Nicholas she was quietly told what to carry and to whom.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To have enough saved to marry a journeyman and keep a room of her own.
- **Fear:** Being found out and her name read aloud in the Rat's court.
- **Contradiction:** Obsessed with order, and works for something that could set the town on fire.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She carries a short chain of names and numbers in her head, and knows the third member of her cell by voice only.

## Work and money
Wage is three marks a year plus board; the faction adds a few pfennigs for errands now and then, which she sews into her hem. She buys flax and wool for Margarete. A bad month is one when a message is changed and she cannot confirm it.

## Faction and belief
Hanseatic, active: her father was ruined by a Danish toll and she believes a town of merchants who keep accounts is safer than a hill of knights. Her reason is order and a dead man's debt. She would carry a small message, refuse to hide arms, and inform only on a merchant who cheated spinners. She prays to St Nicholas and ties a knot in her counting thread for each Mass she hears.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Low German; Estonian with market women.
- **Delivery:** Clipped and precise, as if reading from a list.
- **Sample lines:** "Two pfund wool, one pfund flax, three pfennigs. Check it." "I heard what I heard. No more." "Dat is richtig."
- **Verbal tic:** "Exactly."

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows which merchants pay on time and who takes bribes in the weigh-house. She would trade it only to someone she trusts through the chain. She believes the false rumour that Danish soldiers will seize every merchant's cellar at Easter-tide.
