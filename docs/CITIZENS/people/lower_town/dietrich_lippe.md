## Biography
Born in 1323 to Gyse and Hinrik Lippe, he was four when his father coughed himself to death. He learned the loom at his mother's knee and graduated to the big broadloom at fourteen when the old journeyman left. He has never travelled farther than the Tallinn clay pits. His half-silence began after a fever at nine, when he lost his voice for three months and found it small.

He lives in the loom room, sleeps beside the warp beam, and is thought dull by everybody except those who buy his cloth.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To weave a bolt so fine the Kindergilde buyers ask for him by name.
- **Fear:** A broken heddle and the cost of replacing it.
- **Contradiction:** He is shy with people and bold with the thread; the cheapest yarn he refuses on principle.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He hides a scrap of coloured wool, the only red thread in the house, in a crack in the beam.

## Work and money
He is paid in board and a few pfennigs; his mother holds the money. A bolt of grey broadcloth takes six days and sells at 2 marks. The cart on Wednesdays is shared with Mikula Stepanovich, the dyer, and the argument over the fee is a weekly ritual. A bad month is a snapped warp.

## Faction and belief
None: the loom keeps him from politics, and his mother's politics are enough for the house. He prays quickly and avoids the evil eye by not looking at strangers.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Low German in a near whisper.
- **Delivery:** Soft, almost whispering; listeners lean in.
- **Sample lines:** "It is a good cloth." / "The wool is damp, I think." / "Ja, ja, mother."
- **Verbal tic:** Stops mid-sentence to count a thread.

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows the weight and grade of every wool shipment by touch. He would trade it for a day alone. He believes the false rumour that dyers poison the vats with lead.
