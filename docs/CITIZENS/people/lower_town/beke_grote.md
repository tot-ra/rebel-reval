## Biography
Born 1312 in Reval, a cartwright's daughter in the lane below Pikk. Her father died when she was five and her mother took in sewing. In the winter of 1337-38, when the harbour froze and the bakers closed, she and a baker's wife in the same lane shared a rye loaf between four houses. She has served Borchard since 1339. She never learned letters, and counts on her fingers and a string of knots.

None; she has no time for it. A small favour she gives, a large one she cannot, and informing she would do only for bread. She keeps St Martin's day and leaves a crust on the sill.

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows the servants' gossip of the whole street. She would trade it for a warm blanket. She believes a rye loaf set on a window keeps the ghost of famine away, which is false.
