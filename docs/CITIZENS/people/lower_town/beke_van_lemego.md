## At a glance
- Hardly taller than her own counter; customers lean over it to see her.
- Sells thread, pins, pepper, saffron by the pinch and second-hand gossip free with every purchase.
- Laughs rarely now, though her face still says she once did.
- Sits two benches from the councillor Eler Lippe at church and trades news with him at the door.

## Biography
Born in Lemgo, brought to Reval at nineteen to marry Tideke, a Krämer on Pikk twenty years her elder. They kept the shop together; she kept its books in her head, since she reads only numerals. Tideke died of the lung fever in 1339, and the Pikk neighbours expected her to sell. She kept the stall, paid his debt to a Lübeck wholesaler in three Michaelmas instalments, and took on a clerk, a maid and an apprentice. She talks little about Lemgo and less about the two children who died in the cradle.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To leave the shop debt-free to a sound heir, Segebode if he proves honest, so that no one can say Tideke's name went under.
- **Fear:** A rising that closes the gates and the Lübeck carriers with them; she lived through the empty shelves of 1332.
- **Contradiction:** She preaches thrift yet buys a half-pfund of saffron every Easter that she cannot sell.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She has heard her maid Ilsabe speak Danish in her sleep and has not decided what to do with it.

- **Sundays and feast days:** Mass at St Nicholas, a visit to Tideke's grave, a pot of small beer with Ermelin on the walk home.
- **Spring 1343 disruption:** Levy talk empties the market of carters; she stacks salt and thread in the cellar and sends Segebode to ask Lübeck prices twice a week.

## Work and money
The shop turns about 14 marks a quarter on thread, needles, spice and ribbon. She buys from Lübeck wholesalers on credit settled at quarter-days, owes about 9 marks, and is owed 3 marks by neighbours she will not name. A bad month is one in which the carriers do not come: the saffron sits and the rent falls due.

## Faction and belief
She wants the council's order and Lübeck's roads, because a shop needs both. Asked for a small favour she would pass a word; for a large one she would hide goods or papers in her cellar; to inform on a neighbour she would refuse. She keeps the Marian hours, and a sprig of rowan above the shop door.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Middle Low German; a shop murmur for customers, a sharp whisper for the circle.
- **Delivery:** Flat, tired and low, so that people stop talking to hear her; she never raises it.
- **Sample lines:** "Pepper is a quarter up since Lent, and I did not make the price." / "Say it twice if it is true, and not at all if it is not." / "Wat de Rat nich süht, dat süht de Krämersche." (What the council does not see, the shopkeeper sees.)
- **Verbal tic:** "As I counted it."

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows who owes what on Pikk, who has stopped buying thread and who has begun buying lamp-oil by the quart. She trades what she knows only for news of the gates. She believes the false rumour that the Toompea garrison is being doubled by sea.
