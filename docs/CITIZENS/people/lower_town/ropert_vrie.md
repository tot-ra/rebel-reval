## At a glance
- A big, ruddy man with a round belly and a crooked nose, standing at the council door with a staff.
- He announces every councillor by name, and his voice carries through the hall.
- Odd for a doorman: he gets his bread from the merchants and treats them all equally.

## Biography
Born in 1303 in Reval, son of a quay porter. He joined the council's service at eighteen as a stable boy and rose to doorman by 1330. He married in 1325; his wife died in 1336 and their only son in infancy. He sleeps in a closet by the council door and has never missed a day, except when his nose was broken.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A room of his own with a window, instead of a closet by the door.
- **Fear:** The council dissolving and his post with it.
- **Contradiction:** Looks stolid and listens to everything.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He has been asked to keep a list of who arrives early to council and who leaves late, and he does.

## Work and money
He earns eight marks a year and keep. His keep is paid by the council. He owes a small sum to the baker. A bad month is a winter fever in the closet.

## Faction and belief
He depends on the guilds for his post and so leans their way. He would open a door for a small favour, keep a secret for a large one, and would inform if his post were threatened. He keeps St Nicholas and a folk charm over the door.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Middle Low German, the council's formal phrases.
- **Delivery:** Gravelly and slow, with a boom at the door.
- **Sample lines:** "Herr Rode, the council waits." "I only open the door." "De Dore is tho."
- **Verbal tic:** Strikes the staff on the floor once.

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows who attends and who sleeps in council. He would trade it for a room with a window. He believes the clerk sells copies on the side, which is false.
