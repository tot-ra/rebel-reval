## Biography
Born about 1303 in Reval, the son of old Reynold van Wismar, a small merchant who left debts and a Pikk house. Berend learned letters from the Dominicans, and has kept the Smiths' Gate for eleven years by favour of the Rat. His father quietly moved a boundary stake against a neighbour on Nunne; Berend inherited the stake and the shame. In the autumn of 1342 he borrowed 30 marks from Nicolaus van Luneborch to reroof, and has paid half. Lutgard keeps the house; the children keep the books of his fear.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To clear every debt before the gate becomes a place of danger.
- **Fear:** Losing the gate, with its fee and its house.
- **Contradiction:** Careful with figures, careless with friends.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He has quietly noted for Wobbeke Rotermund who passes the gate after curfew.

## Work and money
He draws 8 marks a year from the Rat, plus a pfennig toll share on carts. He is owed 30 marks by Meinhard van Hervorde (a dowry loan), 5 marks by Jaak Lauri poeg (a surety fine), and owes 15 marks to Nicolaus van Luneborch. He rents a shed to a nail-smith.

## Faction and belief
A dependent of the council: his post depends on it. Small favour: a note of who passes. Large favour: a quiet night closing. Informing: he already does, and dislikes it. He prays the rosary at the gate.
