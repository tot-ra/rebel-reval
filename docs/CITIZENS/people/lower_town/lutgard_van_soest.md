## Biography
Born 1298 in Soest in Westphalia, the daughter of a linen-weaver who traded to Lübeck; sent to Reval at nineteen as a bride. Her husband's house was a warehouse with a bedroom over it. She learned Estonian from the cook, a woman called Marju, and is the only member of the household who can scold a carter in his own tongue. She bore four children and buried one in 1337, a girl of two, whom she does not mention.

She has spun thread for thirty years. The household uses perhaps a third; the rest she sells in small skeins and twine, and the money is hers by custom.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A loom of her own, in the warm back room, for fine linen.
- **Fear:** That the youngest boy will be sickly like her lost girl.
- **Contradiction:** A councillor's wife who thinks the Rat a lot of fools and says so only to her thread.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She sells twine to Estonian craftsmen at the price she paid for flax, so they pay no more than the German ones.

## Work and money
Her thread brings about fifteen marks a year. Flax comes from Estonian growers via the market, for about a mark the lispfund. She sells skeins by the weight and twine by the ell. A bad month is a wet flax crop.

## Faction and belief
She has no faction; she thinks of politics as the men's weather. For a small favour she gives twine; for a large one she would ask her husband; she would not inform. She keeps a St Gertrude medal in the thread basket against mice and a pinch of salt under the loom.

## Voice

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows which women in the lane need flax and which men are in debt. She would trade it for a good loom. She believes the false rumour that Estonians' thread is unlucky on St John's Eve.
