## At a glance
- A sturdy girl with sandy plaits and a wart on her right cheek who counts everything aloud.
- Known for knowing which cups are missing before the maid does.

## Biography
Born 1332 in the great house, the third child. She learned numerals from the tap-room tally and Estonian from the maltsters, and she was set to wash cups at seven. At nine she caught a thief who was taking pewter spoons and reported him in the same flat voice she uses for everything, which frightened the thief more than a shout.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To be put in charge of the cups.
- **Fear:** Geese, the cellar in the dark, and being sent to the nuns.
- **Contradiction:** She is stern with her brothers and hides in the loft when a ship's captain is loud.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She hides a lump of honeycomb in a clay pot behind the cup-shelf.

## Work and money
She has no wages. She is promised a silver ring at fourteen if the cups are all there each Saturday. A bad week is a missing cup.

## Faction and belief
She has no faction. She believes in St Nicholas, who watches the family pew, in the cat who guards the malt loft, and in counting. She would tell if asked, but only what she counted.

## Voice
- **Registers:** German with the family, Estonian with the maltsters.
- **Delivery:** Flat, even and quick; no pitch.
- **Sample lines:** "Eleven cups. One is missing." / "I counted. I counted twice." / "Dat sind veer, nich dree." (That is four, not three.)
- **Verbal tic:** "Exactly."

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows which cups are chipped and who drank from them. She believes the false rumour that a cup with a crack will bring a fever.
