## Biography
Born in 1293 at Lübeck, the son of a ship-caulker named Ludolf, he signed on the Reval run at fifteen. When the harbour clerk wrote his name in the wage list in 1318 he wrote "Ludolf van van Lubeke", copying the van twice; the crew laughed, Ludolf kept the name. He has been twice shipwrecked and never married. He is the oldest hand among the berths and, since his last shipmate died, the loneliest. He has taken to calling at St Olaf's for advice, a habit that surprises him.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To find someone to whom he can say out loud that he is afraid of dying at sea.
- **Fear:** Drowning, and being buried nameless.
- **Contradiction:** A hard hand with a clear, carrying voice who hates to speak of himself.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He has nightmares about the 1331 wreck and a drowned boy he could not reach.

## Work and money
He earns about 6 öre a voyage and a pfennig or two of port work; the berth costs a pfennig a night; he gives the church two pfennig a month. A bad month is a wreck.

## Faction and belief
No faction. He is too old and too tired for causes; he would carry a message for the chaplain and would give a night's shelter. He prays the Aves on a knotted cord and promises a candle to St Nicholas every voyage.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Low German; a few words of Latin prayer.
- **Delivery:** Clear and carrying, slow when saying anything about himself.
- **Sample lines:** "Van-van, they call me. A clerk's mistake, and mine to keep." "Father, I sleep badly. I do not know why I tell you." "God help us, God help us."
- **Verbal tic:** Says "so" and clears his throat.

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows the chaplain's schedule and who visits the church by night. He would trade it for a kind word. He believes the false rumour that a ship named for a saint never sinks.
