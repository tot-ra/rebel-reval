## Biography
Born in 1298 into the Snelle family of Reval, cloth-sellers, Metteke learned the spindle at six and the Estonian tongue from her nurse. At twenty-seven her father matched her to the Kampen-born merchant Meinhard, and her dowry bought his second cog. She has borne five children and buried none. Her mother's death in 1337 left her the keys, the linen chests and a ledger of outworkers she can read only as numerals. She keeps the house in order, the girls at their wheels and her thoughts to herself.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A good marriage for Wendele and a clean ledger for Hartwig, with no council levy touching the house's thread-work.
- **Fear:** A bad winter among the outworkers, and Estonian women seen at her door at curfew.
- **Contradiction:** She is the sternest mistress in the street and cannot bear to turn a spinner away who has a sick child.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She passes small news from her spinners, who hear everything at the wells, to a few men who share her view that trade must stay open.

## Work and money
She gives flax and wool to about thirty outworkers on credit and pays by the pfund of finished thread, collected weekly; a good week returns two marks to the house. She sells yarn to weavers such as her neighbour and to the cloth-sellers in the Snelle family. A bad month is a short flax delivery. Meinhard keeps the large sums; she keeps the keys and her own purse of ten schillings.

## Faction and belief
Her reason is the thread: if the council or the Order closes the roads, the outworkers starve first. A small favour she grants with a word; a large one with a debt in return; she would inform only to save her spinners. She holds to the Church, burns a candle for her mother, and still ties rowan to the stable door.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Middle Low German to family and merchants; plain, slow Estonian to outworkers.
- **Delivery:** Clipped and precise, not loud; each sentence ends like a shut lid.
- **Sample lines:** "Three skeins, two short. Explain." "Come in, you are letting the cold in." "Wat du seggst, dat schrieve ik nich up."
- **Verbal tic:** "Exactly so."

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows who is in debt and who has bought bread on credit across three lanes. She will trade it for a safe road. She believes, falsely, that the Danes will levy every household's spindles for the royal wool.
