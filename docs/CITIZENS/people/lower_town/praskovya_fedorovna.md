## Biography
Praskovya was born in 1308 in a village near Novgorod, the daughter of a cattle-keeper. She married Mikula in 1331, travelled west with a Novgorod trading party, and set up in Reval with a pair of goats and a borrowed yoke. Two children followed, Grigori in 1333 and Praskovya in 1335. The cold-sore scar is from a winter on the road. She walks Reval's lanes six days a week with the yoke, knows every kitchen door and has found that people tell milk-women what they would not tell a priest.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A cow of her own and a roof that does not leak.
- **Fear:** A milk-sickness that kills a customer, and her name with it.
- **Contradiction:** A woman who says little and carries the most.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She keeps a list in her head of every door she is trusted at.

## Work and money
She earns about 6 marks a year from milk, curds and butter, bought at dawn and sold by noon. She splits bulk deliveries with other dairy-women. A bad month is a sour churn, or a gate shut at dawn.

## Faction and belief
Pskov and Novgorod, core. Her reason is the village she left and the fear that her children will grow up with no home at all. She would carry a message, pass on a price, and name no one. She crosses herself in the Orthodox way and sprinkles a drop of milk on the doorstep for the house-spirit.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Russian at home; Low German in the lanes.
- **Delivery:** Husky, level, steady; never raised.
- **Sample lines:** "Milk, fresh, warm." "I did not hear that." "Spasibo." (Thank you.)
- **Verbal tic:** Says "so" before every price.

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows every kitchen's gossip, who has a sick child and who has a lodger. She would trade it for a cow. She believes the false rumour that the Hansa will forbid milk sales on Sunday.
