## Biography
Born in 1295 at Stralsund, the son of a ship's chandler, Cord took the Reval run at twenty. In 1327 a mooring line parted and a cask crushed his shin; the barber set it badly. He took command in 1331 of a cog financed by a council merchant in Reval, and mortgaged half to the same backer. He is not of the merchant Godeke's line, though people assume so. He owes the Hansa's backers more than the ship will ever earn, and has learned that a man in debt may be asked to do small things which become large. He is, without choosing to be, the centre of a circle of whispers.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To own his ship free before the limp ends his sailing.
- **Fear:** Foreclosure.
- **Contradiction:** A man too prudent for causes who is bound to six of them by one debt.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He keeps a list in his head of every person he knows to be in the circle, and fears that he is the only one who knows them all.

## Work and money
He earns about 18 marks a voyage and owes his backers 70 marks on the ship, paid at 10 per voyage. A bad month is a missed payment.

## Faction and belief
Hanseatic guilds, dependent. His reason is his debt and his shin: only the circle keeps him afloat. He would pass news for a small favour, delay a cargo for a larger one, and name no one. He prays at St Nicholas and carries a charm of ship's oak.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Low German in the counting room; Latin in bills.
- **Delivery:** Low and unhurried; dry.
- **Sample lines:** "A debt is a rope with two ends, friend." "I saw no one. I never do." "So, so. Wi seht." (So, so. We shall see.)
- **Verbal tic:** Sets his staff before speaking.

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows whose names are in the circle, and none of their reasons. He would trade it never. He believes the false rumour that the Order will lend ships to any skipper who joins.
