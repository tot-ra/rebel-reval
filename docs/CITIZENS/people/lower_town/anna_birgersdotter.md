## Biography
Born in 1301 in a Gotland farm-village, Anna married Bengt Magnusson at twenty-four in Visby and followed him to Reval when he took his master's bench. She has borne eight children, buried three, and carries the youngest, Folke, born only this week. Her father-in-law Magnus lives under her roof and she cares for him as for a child. She cannot read, but her tally-strings of thread outrun many clerks' books.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To see Helga and Ingeborg settled with decent journeymen and Folke christened without incident.
- **Fear:** Another bad winter, and a debt called in.
- **Contradiction:** A cautious woman who has never refused a hungry neighbour at the door.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She suspects her husband's silent night errands and has never asked.

## Work and money
She spins about two pfund of yarn a week for Bengt's loom and sells the surplus in the lane. The house runs on her thrift; she keeps one schilling and a half in a jar. A bad month is a sick child and a missed quarter-day.

## Faction and belief
She has no faction; she keeps the house. For a small favour she would give bread; for a large one she would weigh the risk to her children; she would never inform. She prays to the Virgin and to St Olaf, and sprinkles salt on the threshold.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Swedish at home; plain Middle Low German in the lane.
- **Delivery:** Thin and reedy, often raised against noise.
- **Sample lines:** "Sit, eat, you are thin." "That wool is not yours." "Gud hjälpe oss."
- **Verbal tic:** "Well, well."

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows who is short of bread and which spinners are sick. She would trade it for a seat at the Amt feast. She believes, falsely, that a baby born in Easter week is lucky for the house.
