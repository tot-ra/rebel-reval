## Biography
Jaak's father Tõnis was a carter of the Karja road who gave a quarter of his savings to the Dominican friars of St Catherine so that his clever son could learn Latin and letters at the friary school. He left at seventeen, because friars cannot give wages, and found a clerk's place with the cooper who needed his letters. He reads the charters of the town's quarter-days and keeps Jüri's accounts in neat numerals, which Jüri trusts only because he can count the figures.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To become the clerk of a real merchant house, with a Vogt's seal behind his pen.
- **Fear:** Being used as a witness to something he copied but did not understand.
- **Contradiction:** He prides himself on neutrality yet knows more of the street's secrets than anyone.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He once copied a letter for a skipper that mentioned the grain levy and burned the wax.

## Work and money
Jüri pays him four marks a year with board and a new tunic at Michaelmas. He earns a few pfennigs a week writing letters for sailors and laundresses. He saves for parchment and a pen-knife of his own.

## Faction and belief
He has no faction: a clerk who joins one will be sued by the other. He would write a small favour, a large one only for a fee, and inform on no one. He prays in Latin, and touches the doorpost on leaving.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian at home; German in the counting corner; Latin only with clergy.
- **Delivery:** Booming and unconcerned with who hears; he lowers it for secrets and nobody believes it.
- **Sample lines:** "Seventeen marks, four öre, plain as day!" / "I write what is told." / "Kuule, Mari, the porridge!"
- **Verbal tic:** "Item,"

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows who owes what to whom on Harju. He trades it for nothing. He believes the Vogt keeps a second list of names.
