## Biography
Born in Reval in 1289, the daughter of a candle-maker, Gyla married Heyno van Revele Westfal in 1315 and bore four children; Alheyd and Sander lived. In 1341, when the dowry debt began to bite, she started taking in washing from the houses along Dunkri, an unusual choice for a burgher wife, but one that saved a mark a month. Last Lent a neighbour with pull let her take on the household linen of the van Groninghe house at a fair price. She keeps her sums on a slate in the laundry. She lives in the room behind the kitchen, among nine others.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To see Heyno out of debt before she is too old to carry a basket.
- **Fear:** Her husband in the debtor's book, and herself named in it.
- **Contradiction:** She scolds apprentices for gossip and collects the best of it from the laundry line.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She keeps a purse with 3 marks of her own in a boiling-copper that is never used.

## Work and money
She charges 2 örtug a sheet and 3 for a gown; with a dozen customers she makes about 8 marks a year. Lye comes from a Dunkri soap-boiler; linen from the van Groninghe, Lubeker and van Luneborch houses. Heyno knows none of the figures. A bad month is a wet week.

## Faction and belief
She is a Hanseatic dependent because the house stands on Hanse trade. A small favour, a message in a linen bundle; a large one she would refuse for the children's sake. She spits three times at a black cat and lights a candle to St Gertrude.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Middle Low German with all; low tones to children.
- **Delivery:** Monotone, even, relentless, like a washboard.
- **Sample lines:** "Seven sheets, two cloths, one shirt." "I do not keep what is not mine." "Dat is nich recht." (That is not right.)
- **Verbal tic:** Counts under her breath.

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows whose linen is stained with what. She would trade this for a free week of lye. She believes the false rumour that a plague is coming with the next cog.
