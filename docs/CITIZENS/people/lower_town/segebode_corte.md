- Sits up at the shutter at night and watches the lane.

## Biography
Segebode was born in Reval to a family that came from Westphalia two generations back, and was sent as a boy to Lübeck to learn the counting house. He returned in 1305, married Gyse van Hildensem in 1308, and sat in the council from 1329. He lost his front tooth on a ship's ladder in 1331. He survived the raids of the 1320s by keeping three ships at sea and none in the harbour at once. Now his house on Harju holds a dozen people, two grown children are in Lübeck, and he is lonelier than anyone suspects.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To hold the council steady until the Danes' toll is lifted, and to leave his house to a clerk who can read a ledger.
- **Fear:** A rising that burns the Lower Town before the Order can be bought off.
- **Contradiction:** He leads a quiet circle against Toompea's tolls and chairs a council that obeys the viceroy.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He pawned his tools of the counting room, the brass scales, to a tailor for eight marks after a cargo went astray, and works off the interest by writing the tailor's letters.

## Work and money
He ships grain, wax and furs east and buys Flemish cloth, salt and iron west. A cargo's profit is perhaps two marks in ten, and a lost cargo costs him a year. He supplies the moneychanger Gavril Gavrilovich with bar silver and cloth on credit and keeps three clerks, a maid, an ostler and a servant. His weekly fight with Jüri Lauri poeg is over a cart that costs eight pfennigs a day.

## Faction and belief
Segebode believes in the Hanse because it feeds Reval. He gives small favours to those who write him letters, large ones only to those who owe him, and informs on nobody who has bread to lose. He confesses weekly and lights a candle for his dead sons.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Middle Low German in council and at table; Latin for the priest and the ledger.
- **Delivery:** Slow, with long pauses that make younger men fidget.
- **Sample lines:** "Mm... three marks. Not four." / "We shall see how the wind lies." / "Nu denn, Kersten."
- **Verbal tic:** "In due time."

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows who is behind on dues, which clerk copies for two houses and who leaves the lane at night. He would trade silence for loyalty. He believes the Danes will seize the harbour toll on St George's Day.
