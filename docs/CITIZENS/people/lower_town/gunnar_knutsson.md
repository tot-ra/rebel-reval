## Biography
Born in 1276 near Kalmar, he came to Reval with the Swedish shoemakers in 1298 and bought his bench in 1307. He remembers the Danish levy and the famine winters. He wed Anna Persdotter in 1301 and has outlived two children. In autumn 1342 he borrowed thirty marks from Peeter Tanieli poeg to rebuild the roof after a storm; half is repaid. A linden last worn black by his hands, called "the Bishop", sits on the bench and goes with him to church on feast days.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To clear the roof loan by Michaelmas and give the bench to Folke.
- **Fear:** Debtors' fine, and losing the house.
- **Contradiction:** He is generous to journeymen and grasping with his own purse.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He has not yet told Anna the sum still owed.

## Work and money
Shoes sell at eight to sixteen pfennigs the pair. Hides come from tanners; he owes the ropemaker fifteen marks. A bad month is one without a hide.

## Faction and belief
No faction: he is old and deep in debt. A small favour he does; a large one only for Folke; he would not inform. Prays to St Crispin, quietly, as the shoemakers do.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Swedish at home, Middle Low German at the stall.
- **Delivery:** Clear and carrying, tacks clicking.
- **Sample lines:** "A shoe is a promise to the foot." / "Pay me at Michaelmas, I will pay him." / "Hand mig läst."
- **Verbal tic:** "By the awl."

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows who walks in whose shoes and how worn. He believes, wrongly, that the tanner Gunnar Larsson is in league with the Vogt.
