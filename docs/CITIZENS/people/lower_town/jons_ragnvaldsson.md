## Biography
Born 1314 in Stockholm, son of Ragnvald, a customs scribe. Learned letters from the Franciscans, Latin from a parish priest, and double-entry-style tallies from a Lübeck factor. Came to Reval in 1335 as clerk to Folke Eriksson and has kept Erik's books since 1338. His voice went on the quay: three winters of shouting weights over wind. His hope is his own small trade; his habit is private lots.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To buy a share in a ship and be called merchant.
- **Fear:** Going blind before he has saved enough.
- **Contradiction:** Meticulous with his master's accounts, loose with his own.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He buys damaged salvage cheaply at the quay and resells it sorted; to the other clerks it looks like smuggling.

## Work and money
Wage 8 marks a year with board and a share of commission; private lots clear perhaps 4 marks. He buys from skippers and sells to petty traders. A bad month is a wet cargo or a lost slip.

## Faction and belief
No faction: a clerk who takes sides loses his master and his letters. For a small favour he writes a letter, for a large one he refuses, to inform he asks for a fee. St Olaf, and a saint's medal he keeps in his purse.

He knows the names on every manifest. He would trade it for a safe berth. He believes the false rumour that the Rat plans a new weighing-house tax.
