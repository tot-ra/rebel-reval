## Biography
Born about 1302 in Reval to a weaver on Nunne, she married Hinrik Lippe at nineteen and bore Dietrich in 1323. Hinrik died of a lung fever in 1329. She married Johan Hardekop, a cloth-cutter, in 1331 and buried him in the winter of 1340. Between the two husbands she took in children whose fathers or kin had left them: Adelheid, Evert, Metteke, Katharina and now an infant, Bela. She kept the house because she kept the looms.

She now lodges two merchants' clerks to pay the roof-tax and teaches girls to spin on the stair.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To leave Dietrich a loom and a name the guild will honour.
- **Fear:** A failed wool shipment, and a winter like 1340.
- **Contradiction:** She lends grain at heavy unspoken interest and prays for her debtors by name.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** Her best reed is held as a pledge by a Russian baker; she has told the household it is being mended.

## Work and money
Income is 10 to 12 marks a quarter from cloth sold to the Kindergilde merchants, plus clerks' board at 3 marks a quarter each. She advanced Heyno van Bocholt, a cooper on Nunne, 15 marks' worth of grain after his failed harvest; the interest is unspoken and heavy. She herself owes Marfa Yeremeyevna, a baker on Raekoja, 5 marks against her pledged tools, repaid in weekly labour. A bad month is a wool shipment that does not come.

## Faction and belief
A Hanseatic sympathiser because her cloth sells only through guild money; her reason is the loom, not the League. She would pass a message, lend grain to a friend, and decline to stand a watch. She prays at St Nicholas and ties a red thread to the cradle against the evil eye.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Low German only; plain and warm.
- **Delivery:** A warm baritone, unhurried.
- **Sample lines:** "The cloth is the cloth. Sit, we will talk after." / "A pledge is a pledge, and I can wait." / "Ach, Kind, mind the shuttle."
- **Verbal tic:** Says "so, so" before bad news.

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows which merchants are late with wool and which clerk copies what. She would trade for a good reed. She believes the false rumour that the Vogt will tax every loom.
