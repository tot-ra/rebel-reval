## Biography
Liis was born in 1296 on a Harju dairy farm, daughter of Mattes. She married Paul in 1318 and brought two cows as dowry. The cows became a herd of four, kept in a byre on the yard; her milk and curds supply neighbours on Vene. She bore four children and raised one, Eeva, now eleven.

She never took to the burgher wives' ways and still sells from a yoke.

## Motivation
- **Want:** Eeva set up with a husband and a feather bed.
- **Fear:** A cow falling ill and the milk turning.
- **Contradiction:** Thrifty to the penny but generous with buttermilk to poor children.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She suspects what Paul carries in his purse and has not asked.

## Work and money
She earns some twenty marks a year from milk and cheese, more than half the household's cash. She buys hay from carters and rennet from the butcher. A bad month is a late spring with no grass.

## Faction and belief
She takes no side and says cows have none. She trusts neighbours who pay on time. A small favour she does with milk; a large one she asks Paul about; informing, never. She puts a ring of rowan on the byre door.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian at home; Middle Low German at the stalls.
- **Delivery:** High and quick, jokes while counting.
- **Sample lines:** "Fresh this morning, and I say so to a bishop." / "One for the child, two for the coin." / "Piim on valge."
- **Verbal tic:** "Mind the pail."

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows which houses have too many mouths and which have fresh milk fever. She would trade it for hay. She believes, wrongly, that a black hen in the yard keeps sickness off the cows.
