## At a glance
- Tall for a woman, hung with bundles of drying herbs; people hear the rustle before they see her.
- Sells simples at the door and the market; cures colic and coughs, charges little, and dreads the word "witch".
- Her mother's recipes live only in her head; her counting is done in knots on a cord.
- Has not forgiven a bad batch of poppy syrup in 1342.

## Biography
Eeva was born in 1303 to a herb-woman, Henn's daughter, in a hamlet beyond the Viru gate, where her mother gathered for the Dominicans' infirmary. She learned to find yarrow on a hillside before she could read a prayer, which she never did. She came to Müürivahe at seventeen to marry Marten, and her first job was to bury a stillborn child. Her mother died in the hard winter of 1334, leaving a worn mortar and the recipes. In 1342 a mislabelled batch of poppy syrup, made with too much seed, put a carter's child to sleep for two days; the child woke, but the carter's wife has cursed her name ever since. Now she makes her rounds with a basket and a dour face, and works harder to repay trust.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To have her name cleared and to pass the craft to Ursula, who has the nose for it.
- **Fear:** A real death on her tab, and the council deciding she is a poisoner.
- **Contradiction:** She teaches caution yet cannot say no to a crying neighbour.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She knows Jüri leaves at night and suspects where; she has hidden a bundle of grove-charms in her herb chest.

## Work and money
She earns perhaps a quarter-mark a month, more in the fever season. Raw material comes from the garden strips, the meadows, and a few bought items such as honey and pepper. A pound of honey costs her six pfennigs. She has a small credit with the apothecary of Pikk and owes nothing; her coins go into rye.

## Faction and belief
No faction. She keeps both a cross and a knotted charm. She would give a small favour, a salve; she would refuse to hide fugitives, since a raid would end her children's livelihood. She would never inform.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian at home; careful German at market.
- **Delivery:** Thin and reedy, rising at the end of a thought; she lowers it when naming a remedy.
- **Sample lines:** "Boil it, do not burn it." "Tulge homme tagasi; see aitab." "I counted twice; the knots do not lie."
- **Verbal tic:** Says "mind you" in German before every warning.

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows who has a cough, who is with child, and who buys what. She would trade it for honey or a good stove. She believes that eating raw onion cures plague, which is false.
