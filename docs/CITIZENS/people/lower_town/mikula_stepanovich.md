## Biography
Mikula was born in 1304 in Pskov, a fuller's son, and came to Novgorod's trade-house in Reval as a vat-hand at nineteen. He learned woad and madder from a German master and has worked for the clothiers of Nunne ever since. He married Praskovya in 1331 in Novgorod, brought her west, and has two children. He is a guest, not a burgher, and the guild fraternity will not let him in. In the winters since 1339 he has carried quiet messages between the Novgorod merchants on Vene, and he calls it only "talking to my countrymen".

## Motivation
- **Want:** To keep his children fed and the vats paid for, and to see a Russian name recognised in town.
- **Fear:** A dye-house fire, and being sent home with nothing.
- **Contradiction:** He is cheerful at the vat and dreads the same road every Sunday.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He knows which Novgorod merchant is paying the Vogt's clerk for passes.

## Work and money
He earns about 8 marks a year as a hired dyer, paid by the piece. He buys woad and alum from harbour dealers and sells nothing. He lent Kaur the brewer 5 marks in autumn 1342; the bond falls due at Michaelmas and about half is paid. He splits cart-hire with two weavers. A bad month is a bad batch of blue.

## Faction and belief
Pskov and Novgorod, active. His reason is the fraternity door that stays shut: a Russian craftsman is good enough for the vat but not for the bench. He would carry a message, pass on a price, but refuse to name a countryman. He crosses himself in the Orthodox way and puts a blue thread over the door.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Russian at home and among countrymen; Low German with the clothiers.
- **Delivery:** High and quick, tumbling; he repeats the last word.
- **Sample lines:** "Blue is blue, friend." "I say this once, twice." "Slava Bogu." (Glory to God.)
- **Verbal tic:** Rubs his beard with the back of a blue hand.

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows which clothiers are late in paying and which merchants are buying Russian cloth. He would trade it for a safe passage. He believes the false rumour that the Hansa will expel Russian guests at midsummer.
