## At a glance
- Runs the kitchen and its hearth on Müürivahe before the household stirs.
- Known for slow, low speech, and a flush that climbs her neck at the word of a master.
- Oddity: she is the only one in the house who never gossips, and everyone tells her things anyway.

## Biography
Born 1315 in a fishing village east of the city; her father Jüri drowned in a spring storm in 1327, and her mother sent her to Reval at twelve to live with an aunt who took in washing. At sixteen she came to the van Campens as a scullery girl, and over twelve years learned the hearth, the pickle crock, and the rule that the mistress's temper follows the price of salt. She saved eight marks and lost four to a sick brother. She lives in the kitchen loft with Tuulikki.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A small room of her own, and a husband who does not hit.
- **Fear:** Dismissal in a lean spring and the road back to the shore.
- **Contradiction:** Proud of the house's silver while it is not hers.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She keeps a pinch of salt and a bread heel at the hearth-stone every Thursday for her father.

## Work and money
She earns 3 marks a year with board and a pair of shoes at Michaelmas. Fish and peas come from the market; bread from the baker on the lane; salt is the mistress's own measure. A bad month is a burnt pot and a docked wage.

## Faction and belief
No faction. She has nothing to give and a wage to lose. Asked for a small favour, she will give it; a large one, she will think of her brother; informing she would refuse out of sheer slowness. Folk practice at the hearth; church on Sundays.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian with servants and market; Low German to the family.
- **Delivery:** Low and unhurried, never raised.
- **Sample lines:** "Porridge is ready, it will keep." "Herring is dear, mistress." "Aeg on küps, wait."
- **Verbal tic:** "Well, then."

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows what the household eats, who is ill, and which quarrels are real. She would trade it for a better room. False belief: that the baby's fever came from the cat.
