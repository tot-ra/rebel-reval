## At a glance
- A heavy, bearded man whose face is permanently boiled red from the mash-tun and whose complexion is otherwise the colour of tallow.
- Left-handed, which German neighbours consider unlucky and he considers handy for stirring.
- Speaks only Estonian, in a tired murmur, and lets his accounts speak German for him.
- Buys everything on credit and pays on the quarter-day to the hour.

## Biography
Kaur was born in 1301 in a Harju village, and came to town at twelve to carry water for a Dominican brewer. He learned the mash, the hops and the barley market, and in 1331 bought burgher right for ten marks, the first Estonian on Vana-Posti to do so. The next year he took over the brewhouse when his master died childless, and he has run it with a household of ten. He is not liked by the German brewers and not trusted by the Estonian carters, and he knows both. In the winter of 1342 the roof of the malt-loft fell in, and he borrowed against it.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To keep the brewhouse and leave it to a trained hand, not to creditors.
- **Fear:** A sour batch before a feast, or a German brewer's complaint to the council.
- **Contradiction:** He is a burgher who cannot read and a master who still carries water.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He knows two of his maids go out on certain nights, leaves the yard gate unbarred, and chooses not to ask where.

## Work and money
He brews small beer and strong ale for the taverns, selling by the barrel and on credit. A good week clears three marks. The roof loan of 5 marks from [Mikula Stepanovich](../../people/lower_town/mikula_stepanovich.md) is due at Michaelmas, about half paid, and a dowry loan of 5 marks to [Marfa Yeremeyevna](../../people/lower_town/marfa_yeremeyevna.md) remains. A bad month is a late barley cart or a sour batch.

## Faction and belief
No faction. A burgher among burghers who does not belong cannot afford a side. For a small favour he would give ale; for a large one he would give time; to inform he would say he was in the cellar. He pours the first dipper of each batch on the yard soil and says the paternoster in Estonian.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian always, with German nouns for goods and prices.
- **Delivery:** A flat, tired murmur, close to inaudible over the copper.
- **Sample lines:** "Quarter-day is quarter-day." "The mash does not wait for the council." "Tell your master the ale will be there; the barley is late."
- **Verbal tic:** "Well, well."

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows which taverns water their ale and who is behind with the malt-tax. He would trade it for an extension. He believes, wrongly, that a cat in the malt loft sours the mash.
