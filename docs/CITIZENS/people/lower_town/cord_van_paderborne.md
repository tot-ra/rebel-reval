## Biography
Cord was born in 1325 in the lanes behind the Coastal gate, the son of a Paderborn cooper's journeyman who came to Reval for work and an Estonian fish-wife from Kopli who sold herring from a basket. The father died of a lung fever in 1334, and the boy was raised in his mother's tongue and sent to the van Luneborch house at twelve for his keep and a trade. He has kept his father's name and his mother's cadence. Old Nicolaus calls him "the sing-song" and has made him journeyman a year early because he cannot cheat on a weight he can remember. He lodges in the loft above the stall and pays nothing but work.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To buy his own board at the Aida end before he is twenty-five, and to bring his mother off Kopli.
- **Fear:** A fever, like his father's; being turned out for a spoiled catch.
- **Contradiction:** He remembers every debt for a decade yet loses his own knife twice a month.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He has seen where the master goes after curfew and has followed once as far as the strand; he is more frightened than curious.

## Work and money
He earns his keep, a shirt twice a year and half a mark at Michaelmas, and sells at the board on a half-penny commission. He cannot write, so keeps his tally on a knotted cord and in his head. He owes nobody, and is owed two schillings by a Dunkri carter. A bad month is a quiet board or a spoiled catch he is blamed for.

## Faction and belief
No faction. He is too young, too poor and too German-named to be wanted by the lanes and too Estonian-spoken to be trusted by the burghers. For a small favour he would carry a basket; for a large one he would run; to inform he would stammer. He crosses himself and also spits three times at a crow, as his mother does.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Low German at the stall, with the sing-song of Estonian under it; Estonian with the Kopli women.
- **Delivery:** Quick and rising, each price ending on an upward note.
- **Sample lines:** "Two pfund of flounder, a half-penny cheaper if you take the head." "He had one salt cod and two herring, and owes a penny, Master." "Don't ask me, I only count."
- **Verbal tic:** "Eh?" at the end of each price.

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows every regular's purchases and debts for three years. He would trade it for a safe place to put his mother. He believes, wrongly, that Wolter Snelle waters his brine.
