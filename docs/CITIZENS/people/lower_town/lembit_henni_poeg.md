## Biography
Lembit was born in 1307 in a village near Jüri, son of Henni, a horse-dealer's boy. He came to town in 1325 and worked in an inn stable before Paul hired him in 1336. His wife died in childbirth in 1339; he does not speak of it.

He took the hoof-searcher from a farrier who left Reval, and Toomas Eerika poeg borrowed it first.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A stall of his own at the horse fair.
- **Fear:** A horse going lame under his care.
- **Contradiction:** Bellows at men, whispers to horses.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He has not returned the hoof-searcher for eleven days.

## Work and money
He earns three marks a year and board. Oats and hay come from Harju carters and a few pfennigs go to the smith for shoes. A bad month is a lame horse.

## Faction and belief
He takes no side because a horse has none. A small favour he does with a laugh, a large one only if no horse is at stake, informing never. He ties a red thread on the mule's bridle.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Estonian to horses; Middle Low German to others, loudly.
- **Delivery:** Booming, laughing, drops to a murmur near animals.
- **Sample lines:** "Easy, easy, who is a fine fellow?" / "Eleven days, and he says he is busy!" / "Hoo, hoo."

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows which horses in town are sick. He would trade it for a good bit of leather. He believes, wrongly, that a white mare brings luck.
