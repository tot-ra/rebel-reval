## At a glance
- A thin, stooped friar with ears that stick out from the edges of his hood, always carrying a basket of linen or dried herbs.
- Speaks German in a sing-song rhythm that makes strangers think he is Estonian until he answers in Latin.
- Oddity: the brothers' healer who is himself always cold; he sleeps with a hot brick in the infirmary.

## Biography
Rotcher was born in Reval in 1282 to a Saxon skipper who was away eight months in the year. He was raised in the kitchen by an Estonian nurse named Mari, who sang him to sleep and taught him his first words; his father sent a priest to beat the vowels out of him at six, and the lilt stayed. At sixteen he entered the friary as a novice, was taught to read Latin by an English brother, and was trained in the infirmary by a Brunswick friar who died of the fever of 1316. He nursed the sick through the famine of 1316-17 and buried nine brothers himself. He has run the infirmary since 1325, a long low room with eight beds, half-empty in spring. This spring he has a small cough he tells nobody about.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To keep the infirmary supplied well enough that nobody dies on a bad shelf of linen.
- **Fear:** The cough that does not clear, and being moved from the infirmary to the choir benches.
- **Contradiction:** He heals by herb and bleeding from habit while privately distrusting both; he tends the sick with his hands more than his books.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He never learned to say the Estonian Our Father properly and has been quietly learning it from the sick, one phrase at a time, so he can pray at their bedsides.

## Work and money
The infirmary runs on bequests and the friary purse: perhaps four marks a year on lint, razors, honey, salves and linen by the ell. He buys small items from the Estonian barber-surgeon down the lane, whose razors hold an edge. A bad month is a fever in the Lower Town.

## Faction and belief
He belongs to the Church by vocation and has no stake in politics; he would shelter the wounded from either side, since a sick man is not a faction. He would not inform. He keeps Mari's practice of leaving a spoon of milk on the sill on St John's Eve and tells himself it is for the cat.

## Voice
- **Registers:** German to the brothers and townsmen; Latin at prayer; a handful of Estonian words at the bedside.
- **Delivery:** Sing-song, with pitch lifting on accented syllables as in Estonian. Soft, unhurried, never raised.
- **Sample lines:** "Lie still; the bone will not mend by being brave." / "A cough is a debt, my son, and it is always paid in spring." / "Kumm, Kind, ik hebbe Water." (Come, child, I have water.)
- **Verbal tic:** Ends advice with "so" on a rising note.

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows who is coughing in which house. He would trade it for linen for the infirmary. He believes the false rumour that the fever of 1316 began in the Smiths' Gate quarter.
