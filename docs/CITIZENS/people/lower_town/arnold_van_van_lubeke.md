## At a glance
- A white-habited, black-cloaked friar with a tanned face, the only one in the house who is always walking toward the market.
- Sells the friary's surplus (candle ends, garden onions, mended rope) at honest weight, and is known for it.
- Oddity: a man of prayer who weighs everything twice. He carries a small balance in a leather case under the scapular.

## Biography
Born 1282 in Lübeck to a barrel-hoop maker in the Mengstrasse, Arnold was given to the Dominicans at fourteen and sent east in 1303 with six brothers to reinforce the house at Reval, which the Order of Preachers had held since the 1240s. In the famine winter of 1316 he learned what he still believes: that the poor go hungry because someone miscounted. He took over the cellarer's market errands in 1325 and has not stopped since.

His quarrel is with the nuns of St Michael's, who sold the house a short honey measure in 1338; he has never said so aloud and never bought from them again.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To hand over the accounts to the prior with every pfennig matched, once, before he dies.
- **Fear:** Being thought a merchant in a habit, which the older brothers sometimes mutter.
- **Contradiction:** He preaches poverty and cannot sleep with a column unbalanced.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** The friary's silver for the year is eight marks short, and he has been covering it with his own sales of candle ends. The prior has not been told.

## Work and money
The friary lives on gifts, bequests, a garden and Lower Town alms. Arnold turns the spare into silver by weight: candle ends, garden onions, mended rope, a little cheese, at a margin of perhaps three örtug in the mark. He buys herring and peas for the brothers by the lispfund. A bad month is a short measure noticed only at the gate.

## Faction and belief
He belongs to the Church because he entered it as a boy; he has no politics beyond honest weight. A small favour he would do; a large one he would weigh first. He would not inform on a penitent. He never starts a count on a Friday, a Lübeck superstition.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Middle Low German at market and in the cellar, Latin in choir.
- **Delivery:** High and quick, words tumbling, voice rising at the end of a price.
- **Sample lines:** "Three örtug the pfund, and that is after I have counted the dirt." / "Brother, a thing is not cheap because it is small." / "Ik segge nich, dat he lüget; ik segge, he wegt nich." (I do not say he lies; I say he does not weigh.)
- **Verbal tic:** Repeats the last figure aloud: "Two marks, two marks."

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows who is buying grain ahead and who sells short. He would trade it for a quiet word with the prior about the missing eight marks. He believes the false rumour that the nuns of St Michael's keep two sets of weights.
