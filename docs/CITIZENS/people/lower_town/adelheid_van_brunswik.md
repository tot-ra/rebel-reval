## Biography
Born in 1279 to a burgher family of Reval, with Brunswick roots; placed at the convent at fourteen with a dowry of eight marks. She took the veil in 1294 and served in the infirmary through the epidemics of 1316 and 1322. She is the oldest nun who still climbs the dormitory stairs unaided. She talks to plants, and the lay sisters say they answer.

## Motivation
- **Want:** To see the herb-bed come up in April and to pass her remedy-book to a younger sister.
- **Fear:** A damp winter that takes her knees and leaves her in a chair.
- **Contradiction:** A silent professed sister whose voice is the loudest in the house.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She has been writing her remedies in Middle Low German, not Latin, in a small book hidden in the linen press, so the lay sisters could read them.

## Work and money
She owns nothing; the house keeps all. The herb-bed's surplus sells at the gate for a few pfennigs a bunch to the town's wives, and the money goes to the convent's purse. Her only currency is sage, honey and time.

## Faction and belief
Church, core. Her root is a loyalty to the house that fed her; her branch is her patients. A small favour she grants; a large one she weighs against the Rule; she will never inform against anyone who comes to the infirmary gate. She prays the Hours and touches the lintel of the herb-gate for luck.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Middle Low German with townsfolk; Latin in choir and for sick-room prayers.
- **Delivery:** Clear, carrying, brisk, even in whispers.
- **Sample lines:** "Sage for the throat, mint for the stomach." "Deo gratias, and wash your hands." "Hold still, child."
- **Verbal tic:** Names the plant before the person.

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows who is ill in Lower Town and what each has been eating. She would trade it for a good cutting of rue. She believes, wrongly, that cold water in April causes fevers.
