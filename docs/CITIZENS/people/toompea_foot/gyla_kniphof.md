## At a glance
- Slight, 165 cm, freckled, with unusually long fingers and a gravelly voice far older than she is.
- Closes mail-rings with the left hand, the best in the lane, piecework by the hundred.
- Bites her nails to the quick and has horny thumbs from the ring-pincers.
- Says nothing about what she hears.

## Biography
Born in 1320 in Lübeck, daughter of a drover named Kniphof; her mother died of a fever and her father put her aboard a cog to a kinswoman in Reval, who died the next winter. She was taken into Winand's house at fifteen for her board, and discovered that her long fingers closed rings better than the men's. She cooks, scrubs and closes rings in the evenings for a pfennig a hundred.

## Motivation
- **Want:** Her own pair of ring-pincers and a stall of piecework.
- **Fear:** Being named in the wrong room.
- **Contradiction:** Hears everything and cultivates deafness.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She keeps a second wage, from piecework done at night, in a hollow bedpost.

## Work and money
She earns board and a mark a year, plus about forty pfennigs a quarter in piecework. She owns a spare apron, a pair of pincers and a wooden comb.

## Faction and belief
No faction. A servant in a house where Crown, council and rebels all send messages cannot afford a side. A small favour is closing a few rings; a large one she would refuse; she would inform only to protect her wage. She prays by rote and crosses herself when the clerk passes.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Middle Low German only.
- **Delivery:** Gravelly, low, short sentences.
- **Sample lines:** "Ten, twenty, thirty." "Ich hörte nichts, I heard nothing." "The fire is lit."
- **Verbal tic:** Counts on her fingers when nervous.

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows who comes at night and what they carry. She would trade it for a place of her own. She believes, wrongly, that the castle clerk keeps a book of the maids' names.
