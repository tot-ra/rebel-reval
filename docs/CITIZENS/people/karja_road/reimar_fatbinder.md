## Biography
Born in 1290 at Lübeck, apprenticed at twelve to a cooper on the Trave, Reimar followed a herring ship to Reval in 1316 and stayed because wood and work were cheap. He married Telseke Blyde in 1330, a widow with her own stall, and moved into a plot outside the Cattle Gate. He never learned letters; he learned weights, hoops and the faces of men who lie about contents. Now he makes herring barrels, salt tubs and ale kegs for the quay, and sits quietly in a hall he is not invited to.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A master's seat in the fraternity before his sons grow.
- **Fear:** A rival proving that his oak is stolen.
- **Contradiction:** He guards the Hanse's interests while undercutting its coopers.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** Some of his staves come cheap off a broken-up cog at the beach, no questions asked.

## Work and money
A herring barrel earns about three pfennigs, a keg for a brewer five; a good week is two schillings. Oak comes from Vitae-quarry carts and the beach scrap-heap; hoops are birch and willow. He owes a smith eight örtug for a drawknife and is owed four öre by a quay dealer. A bad month is a wet season that warps staves.

## Faction and belief
A core Hanseatic man by habit and by trade: the Hanse buys his casks. His reason is bread, not brotherhood. A small favour, watching a cargo, is easy; a large one he does for coin; informing on the quay he does without being asked. He prays at St Olaf and carves a cross on the first stave of every cask.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Middle Low German with a Lübeck lilt; a few Estonian trade words.
- **Delivery:** Low and unhurried, thumping wood in the pauses.
- **Sample lines:** "Herring wants oak, not pine. Tell your master." / "I do not say what is in it. I say what it weighs." / "Dat holt is good."
- **Verbal tic:** Thumps the nearest barrel.

## Knowledge and rumours
He knows which casks are false-bottomed. He would trade it for a place in the fraternity. Rumour he believes: that the Order will take the Danish towns by autumn.
