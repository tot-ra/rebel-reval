## At a glance
- She sells needles, thread, salt, tallow dips and pins from a trestle by the Cattle Gate and knows the price of each in three kinds of silver.
- Known for a nose set slightly crooked to the right, a gift of a badly shod mule.
- Oddity: she keeps her entire stock and ledger in her head, with beads on a cord for sums.

## Biography
Born in 1296 in Reval to a salt-weigher's family, Telseke was kicked by a mule at nine and kept the nose. Widowed young of a first husband (a cartwright, drowned in the Narva river), she kept his stall and married the cooper [Reimar Fatbinder](../../people/karja_road/reimar_fatbinder.md) in 1330. She bore [Rembert](../../people/karja_road/rembert_fatbinder.md), [Gerlach](../../people/karja_road/gerlach_fatbinder.md) and Cord. Her stall pays the rent; Reimar's casks pay the food.

## Motivation
- **Want:** A permanent booth in the Lower Town market.
- **Fear:** Reimar's cheap oak being traced to her.
- **Contradiction:** She prides herself on honest weight and has an unspoken pact with a husband who cheats.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** She has begun to say aloud things she has never said to Reimar.

## Work and money
She makes about a schilling a week in a good one; stock is bought on credit from Hanse wholesalers. Owes a mark and two öre. A bad month is a sunny Sunday with the market empty.

## Faction and belief
None: politics is a pot she does not stir; a rising would empty the market. She will give a small favour to anyone, refuse a large one and never inform. She prays to St Olaf for dry weather and puts salt on the threshold.

## Voice
- **Registers:** Middle Low German at the stall, a few Estonian words for customers.
- **Delivery:** Low and unhurried; she lets silence sell.
- **Sample lines:** "Two pfennigs, and I weigh it twice." / "Bring the empty. I'll not take full." / "Nu, wat schall ik seggen?"
- **Verbal tic:** Touches the crooked nose.

## Knowledge and rumours
She knows who buys on credit and who has stopped. She would trade it for a better stand. Rumour she believes: that a ship of salt has been sunk by pirates.
