# Canon: Reval Rebel

This document establishes the historical and narrative baseline for *Reval Rebel*. It organizes facts, characters, and terminology, clarifying what is historically attested, what is plausible within the historical context, what is drawn from folklore, and what is invented for the game's narrative.

## Confidence Labels

Every entry in this document uses one of the following confidence labels to guide implementation:
- **`attested`**: A verifiable historical fact, person, or term present in primary sources or established historiography.
- **`plausible composite`**: A fictional element built strictly from historically accurate archetypes, structures, or plausible events.
- **`folklore`**: An element drawn from period-appropriate or regionally appropriate myth, belief, or oral tradition, presented subjectively within the game.
- **`invented`**: Entirely fictional content created to serve the game's specific narrative, dramatic, or gameplay needs, restricted by the boundaries of the established history.

## Timeline (1342–1346)

The playable narrative covers spring 1343 through the 1346 sale. Act 1-2 design lives in shipped packages and [`docs/reports/p5_001_act2_design.md`](./reports/p5_001_act2_design.md). Act 3 design is approved for implementation in [`docs/reports/p6_001_act3_design.md`](./reports/p6_001_act3_design.md), with runtime and activation still gated by its named P6 tasks. Sources folded here under P7-008: [`history/HISTORY.md`](../history/HISTORY.md), [`history/TIMELINE.md`](../history/TIMELINE.md), [`story/STORY.md`](../story/STORY.md).

### Backstory and Act 1 (to 23 April 1343)

* **Spring 1342 (rising taxes and hollow Danish authority)** - **`attested`** (context) / **`plausible composite`** (street colour)
  * Valdemar IV's effort to redeem pawned Danish lands drives extraordinary taxes on Danish Estonia, including the church. German vassals, the Livonian Order, and Hanseatic merchants hold the real local power while the crown looks distant.
  * Playable Act 1 is **spring 1343**, not 1342. Use 1342 only as rumour, ledger debt, and bark about last year's levies. The legacy STORY title card that labels Act 1 "1342" is corrected here.
* **Pre-April 23, 1343 (The Simmering City)** - **`invented`** (Narrative framing)
  * The game's five day/night cycles take place in the immediate build-up to the historical uprising. Tensions are high; factions are maneuvering.
  * Living City progress uses Hope / Fear per [`docs/SYSTEMS/LIVING_CITY.md`](./SYSTEMS/LIVING_CITY.md). Legacy STORY "Chaos" as a third city meter is **not** canon (see Promoted and rejected story seeds).
* **April 13, 1343 (Easter Sunday)** - **`attested`** (Source: Julian computus tables [5ko.free.fr, UU Gent]; liturgical calendar dossier R-017)
  * Lent ends; meat and dairy return for observant burghers. Easter Monday (14 April) closes trade per Lübeck-law custom. Post-Easter market feasting resumes; convoy preparation begins. Easter is **ten days before** St George's Night, not two.
  * **Canon verdict P0-166:** 13 April is the single approved Easter date for the playable calendar. The Julian computus tables and R-017's dated feast table agree; the former 21 April value is rejected as a computus error. R-015 must retain the correction note and remove any remaining conflicting date before Research can return it for Canon re-review.
* **April 23, 1343 (St. George's Night)** - **`attested`** (Source: Chronicles of Balthasar Russow / Livonian Chronicle of Hermann de Wartberge)
  * A signal fire on a hilltop in Harju County triggers the uprising. Coordinated attacks begin on German nobles, merchants, and clergy.
  * The "Four Kings" (Neli kuningat) are elected by the rebels.
  * Viru Gate Open / Seal / Break families that close Act 1 are **`invented`** act-boundary framing on this attested night; they become fixed input to Act 2.

### Act 2 (siege calendar)

* **Late April 1343 (Siege of Reval begins)** - **`attested`** (Source: chronicle accounts of the siege; project synthesis in `history/HISTORY.md`)
  * Rebel forces from Harju County lay siege to Reval (Tallinn), demanding surrender from the Danish-held city. Rebel diplomacy offers Reval to Swedish bailiffs in exchange for aid - **`attested`** appeal; Swedish capacity and landing remain limited.
  * Playable Act 2 uses three siege phases - investment, sortie/supply, assault - that keep this attested investment on the calendar while steering local cost inside missions.
* **April 1343 ([Padise Monastery Massacre](../wiki/events/padise_monastery_massacre.md))** - **`attested`** (Source: chronicle tradition; `history/HISTORY.md`)
  * Rebels sack the Cistercian house at Padise and kill monks who had not fled. Act 2 may surface this as rural news, evidence, or world-travel aftermath; full Padise playable phases remain Act 3 (**P6-002** / **P6-009**).
* **Early to mid-May 1343 ([Paide negotiations / Four Kings killing](../wiki/events/paide_negotiations.md))** - **`attested`** (Source: Livonian chronicle tradition, including Hermann de Wartberge)
  * Master Burchard von Dreileben lures the Four Kings under truce to Paide Castle (Wittenstein); the talks break into accusation and the envoys are killed.
  * Exact day-order versus the 11-14 May field battles is contested across chronicle reconstructions. The killings themselves are **`attested`**. Act 2 places the Paide beat as the dramatic finale after Kanavere and Sõjamäe so survivor and warning states can matter; that playable sequencing is **`invented`** campaign framing and must not be sold as a settled primary-source chronology.
* **May 11, 1343 ([Battle of Kanavere Bog](../wiki/events/battle_of_kanavere_bog.md))** - **`attested`** (Source: Livonian Order records / chronicle tradition)
  * A rebel force defeats an Order detachment near Kanavere bog - a real but limited field success before the main Order army arrives.
  * In play, mission branches may change named survivors and local casualties; they must not overturn the attested rebel tactical success.
* **May 14, 1343 ([Battle of Sõjamäe](../wiki/events/battle_of_sõjamäe.md))** - **`attested`** (Source: Livonian Chronicle of Hermann de Wartberge; later chronicle tradition)
  * The decisive defeat of the main rebel army by Master Burchard von Dreileben’s Livonian Order forces near Lake Ülemiste. The mainland rebellion is largely crushed.
  * In play, steerable local cost may change who lives or what evidence survives; the attested Order victory and collapse of the main field army remain fixed.
* **May 16, 1343 (Order leverage on Toompea)** - **`attested`** (Source: Danish-Order transition context; see Toompea dossiers)
  * Order military and political leverage over the Danish castle rises after the field victories; used as the Act 2 → Act 3 authority bridge, not as an April baseline.
* **May 18-19, 1343 (Swedish fleet offshore)** - **`attested`** (Source: chronicle accounts of Swedish bailiffs' response)
  * A Swedish fleet arrives too late to save the mainland rising; it becomes aftermath colour and an Act 3 seed, not a playable relief victory.
* **May 26, 1343 (Pskov raid toward Otepää)** - **`attested`** (Source: chronicle accounts summarised in `history/HISTORY.md`)
  * A Pskovian force under Prince Ivan enters the Bishopric of Dorpat and reaches Otepää, then turns back after learning Harju is suppressed; Order contact near Vastseliina follows. Not a playable alternate victory over Sõjamäe. Act 2/3 may use it as emissary news, forged-dispatch evidence, or world-travel colour without opening Dorpat as a campaign city.

### Act 3 seeds (occupation through sale)

* **July 24, 1343 (Saaremaa / Ösel rising; Pöide)** - **`attested`** (Source: `history/TIMELINE.md`, `history/HISTORY.md`; Act 3 design mapping: [`docs/reports/p6_001_act3_design.md`](./reports/p6_001_act3_design.md))
  * Islanders kill German lords, drown priests, and take Pöide Castle after a broken safe-conduct. Chronologically after the mainland collapse; playable placement belongs to Act 3 (**P6-001** / **P6-004**), not as an Act 2 rewrite of Sõjamäe. Mission outcomes may steer local cost, survivors, and evidence only.
* **February 1344 (Karja Fortress; Vesse)** - **`attested`** (Source: `history/TIMELINE.md`, `history/HISTORY.md`; Act 3 design mapping: [`docs/reports/p6_001_act3_design.md`](./reports/p6_001_act3_design.md))
  * Order forces reinforced from Prussia cross the frozen sea, storm the rebel Karja stronghold, and execute leader Vesse. Act 3 branches may steer local cost and evidence only.
* **Winter 1345 (Saaremaa pacification; Maasilinna / Soneburg)** - **`attested`** (Source: `history/TIMELINE.md`, `history/HISTORY.md`; Act 3 design mapping: [`docs/reports/p6_001_act3_design.md`](./reports/p6_001_act3_design.md))
  * Final island submission; Order begins the "castle of atonement." Maasilinna remains concept metadata and aftermath colour in P6-001; it must not be presented as a completed later castle in the 1343 Pöide layout without a later activation decision.
* **1346 (Sale of the Duchy of Estonia)** - **`attested`** (Source: `history/TIMELINE.md`, `history/HISTORY.md`; Act 3 design mapping: [`docs/reports/p6_001_act3_design.md`](./reports/p6_001_act3_design.md))
  * Valdemar IV sells Danish Estonia to the Teutonic Order (then to Livonian administration). This is the active campaign close (ADR 0008 Act 3 / **P6-001** / **P6-005**). There is no 1351 plague epilogue.

## Political landscape (1342–1343)

Folded from `history/HISTORY.md`. These frame faction pressure; they do not authorize army/fleet sims or other-city campaigns.

* **Hollow Danish crown over the Duchy of Estonia** - **`attested`**
  * Northern Estonia is Danish on paper; viceroy power on Toompea is thin compared with German vassals, the Livonian Order, and Hanseatic autonomy in Lower Town.
* **Livonian Order as regional military power** - **`attested`**
  * Semi-autonomous Teutonic branch; Master Burchard von Dreileben is the decisive mainland suppressor in May 1343.
* **Hanseatic Reval merchants** - **`attested`**
  * Member since 1285; trade autonomy and profit protection make them natural order-restoration allies when the uprising threatens routes.
* **Sweden (Magnus IV) as limited intervenor** - **`attested`**
  * Bailiffs in Turku/Vyborg receive rebel offers; fleet arrives mid-May too late. Internal Scandinavian pressures limit deeper intervention.
* **Pskov / Novgorod eastern pressure** - **`attested`** (events) / **`plausible composite`** (emissary NPCs)
  * Pskov is de facto independent of Novgorod by 1343; both are Order adversaries. Playable emissaries may appear in Reval/Harju; Novgorod and Pskov cities stay non-playable.
* **Lithuania succession distraction** - **`attested`** (context)
  * Post-Gediminas contest (Jaunutis vs Algirdas/Kęstutis) keeps Lithuania from meaningful rebel aid. Background only unless a later ADR opens a Lithuania line.
* **Grievance stack (tax, culture, land)** - **`attested`** (tax/extortion theme) / **`plausible composite`** (personal cases)
  * Extraordinary Danish taxes, cultural/religious pressure, Hanseatic trade capture, and erosion of free-peasant status. Wigand-of-Marburg-style violence against rural households may colour quests when labelled chronicle tradition, not player-authored atrocity tourism.
* **Viljandi rye-sack infiltration** - **`attested`** (chronicle tale; Renner)
  * Failed rebel attempt to enter Fellin hidden in grain sacks, betrayed by a mother bargaining for her son. Usable as Act 2/3 rumour, bark, or a content-only echo mission; not a second playable field-battle climax.
* **Haapsalu / Ösel-Wiek western front** - **`attested`** (siege tradition)
  * Läänemaa rebels besiege the bishop's seat at Haapsalu. Optional world-travel colour after activation gates; not required for Act 1 slice.

### Historical figures promoted for mention

* **Magnus IV Eriksson** - **`attested`** - King of Sweden/Norway/Scania; distant authorizer of the late Swedish fleet response.
* **Prince Ivan of Pskov** - **`attested`** (chronicle naming) - Leads the May 1343 Pskov force toward Otepää.
* **Vesse** - **`attested`** (chronicle tradition) - Saaremaa rebel "king" captured at Karja, 1344.
* **Jani Beg, Simeon of Moscow, Lithuanian dukes** - **`attested`** as wider-world context only; no direct Baltic campaign agency in the playable acts.

## Terminology

* **Reval** - **`attested`** 
  * The historical name for Tallinn, used by the Danish and German ruling classes and the Hanseatic League.
* **Hareapea swim pools / mill-pond-like reach** - **`plausible composite`**
  * The city plan's deeper bend and downstream bridge reaches are reversible gameplay bathymetry. Exact 1343 depths, a dam, and the location of a mill pond are **`unknown`** here; this does not attest a named mill or pond. No dam or mill is added. Runtime scope: [city stream](SYSTEMS/CITY_SEA.md#stream-moat-and-wake).
* **Toompea (Domberg)** - **`attested`**
  * The upper town / castle hill of Reval, seat of the Danish viceroy.
* **Harju County (Harjumaa)** - **`attested`**
  * The region surrounding Reval where the uprising ignited.
* **Livonian Order** - **`attested`**
  * An autonomous branch of the Teutonic Knights, the de facto military power in the region, operating from strongholds outside Reval.
* **St. George's Night Uprising (Jüriöö ülestõus)** - **`attested`**
  * The modern Estonian name for the 1343 rebellion.
* **The "Four Kings"** - **`attested`**
  * Four Estonian leaders chosen by the rebels to lead the siege of Reval; later treacherously killed by the Livonian Order at Paide.
* **Hingepuu** - **`invented`** / **`folklore`**
  * The reflection entity in the game, inspired by Baltic animist concepts of the "soul tree" or sacred groves, but formalized as a game mechanic to confront Kalev with his choices.
* **Spirit sight, auras and soul lights (*hingetuled*)** - **`invented`** / **`folklore`**-inspired ([ADR 0041](./adr/0041-spirit-sight-auras-and-soul-lights.md), accepted)
  * The hero's clairvoyant view of the same world, in which people and animals carry seven soul lights: the seven NATURAL aspects (the Hingepuu tiers) seen on the body. Not drawn from a recorded Baltic tradition; the Sanskrit term "chakra" is anachronistic for 1343 Livonia and is never used in player-facing text. The aura shows strength, weak points and inner conflict, never a moral verdict.
* **Church services, soul-light soot and collective channeling** - mixed labels ([ADR 0046](./adr/0046-church-services-and-collective-channeling.md), proposed; no runtime yet)
  * **Canonical hours and vespers** - **`attested`** as the Latin Church's daily office, vespers at evening. Lay attendance of weekday vespers in a Reval parish church is **`plausible composite`**.
  * **Sunday and feast-day mass obligation** - **`attested`** in Western canon law well before 1343. Feast dates follow the Julian calendar; Easter 1343 fell on 13 April (**`attested`** by computus).
  * **Annual confession and Easter communion** - **`attested`**: canon 21 *Omnis utriusque sexus* of the Fourth Lateran Council (1215) binds every adult to confess to their own parish priest at least once a year and to receive communion at least at Easter. Lay communion more often than that is not assumed.
  * **Elevation of the Host and the elevation bell** - **`attested`** as Western practice from about 1200 (Paris synodal statutes; a bell at the elevation ordered by the papal legate Guy of Paré at Cologne, 1201-1203). Its use in Reval's parish churches in 1343 is **`plausible composite`**. Monstrances (c. 1350+) and Corpus Christi processions with them are not shown.
  * **Parish of each household by nearest parish church; service times (mass about 09:00, vespers about 17:00)** - **`plausible composite`**; the parish bounds of St Olaf's, St Nicholas' and the Holy Spirit church in 1343 are **`unknown`**.
  * **Sauna cleansing, hiis groves and offering stones** - **`folklore`** as cleansing and offering rites; sacred groves and cup-marked offering stones as places are archaeologically and ethnographically recorded, their 1343 rite content is not.
  * **Soot (*tahm*) on the soul lights, the priest as conductor and the column of light** - **`invented`**, a game reading of the spirit layer. It never states that a sacrament works or fails; the picture shows burden and strength, not a verdict on the Church or on folk belief.
* **Capital vices and soul attachments** - mixed labels ([ADR 0055](./adr/0055-soul-attachments-and-sin-inclinations.md), proposed; no runtime yet)
  * **Period sin lists** - **`attested`**: Cassian's eight principal vices (c. 420, after Evagrius); Gregory the Great's *Moralia in Iob* XXXI (c. 590) with pride as the root above seven (vainglory, envy, wrath, sadness, avarice, gluttony, lust); the scholastic seven capital vices with *acedia* for sadness and pride as root (Peter Lombard, Aquinas *ST* I-II q. 84 a. 4); the 13th-century SALIGIA mnemonic (pride, avarice, lust, wrath, gluttony, envy, acedia). Confession examined by the vices and restitution of ill-gotten goods and usury before absolution are **`attested`** canon practice. Their use by Reval's Dominicans in 1343 is **`plausible composite`**. The SALIGIA attribution to Hostiensis is common but unchecked.
  * **The game uses eight names on seven lights** (gluttony and lust share the root; pride and vainglory both kept), the Gregorian-scholastic set with *acedia*. Ids are the Latin names (`sin.gula`, `sin.luxuria`, `sin.avaritia`, `sin.ira`, `sin.invidia`, `sin.superbia`, `sin.acedia`, `sin.inanis_gloria`).
  * **Attachments (`attachment.*`), inclinations and the sin-to-light mapping** - **`invented`**. They describe what binds a person and where a weak light leans, never a verdict or a score.
* **Harbour vessels (Spring 1343)** - mixed labels; full cards in [`reports/baltic_vessels_1343.md`](./reports/baltic_vessels_1343.md) (R-952)
  * **Cog / cog-like cargo in the roadstead** - **`attested`** as a type from the Peeter wreck (18.08 x 6.64 m, second-quarter 14th-c. pottery, dendro TPQ 1296) and the Lootsi 8 hull (24.5 x 9 m, timber felled around 1360). Lootsi is a large comparandum, not an April 1343 launch date. Cogs stand off and are lightered; they are not boarded and not beached at Kalamaja.
  * **Stern rudder on the large local cargo hull** - **`attested`** on Lootsi (four iron gudgeons on the false stern). Peeter hang is **`unknown`**: AVE 2015, the Fat Margaret page, and Rebane 2023 do not name gudgeons or a side rudder, DIGAR `nlib-digar:432237` (Lätti and Roio 2020) is unavailable on both the public and National Library intranet, and the 2025 Kadriorg Cog monograph stays shop-only (R-993). Side-to-stern transition is underway in this century; do not default a 1343 cog to a steering wheel, and do not copy Lootsi's stern hang onto Peeter as fact. Clearing read: **R-1075**.
  * **Lootsi mast station** - **`attested`** as a keelson band, not as an LOA fraction. The maststep block starts 4.40 m from the keelson forward end and is 2.75 m long on the 14.95 m keelson (Reinvars 2023). The stem is missing; 24.5 m is wreck length. CO-06 must place a Lootsi-scale mast inside that band and must not invent an LOA%.
  * **Kalamaja working boats** - **`plausible composite`**. No published Reval fishing wreck. Open clinker boats in the Nordic 5-10 m tradition; a second, heavier inshore size may use the Gedesby hull (12.5 x 5.2 m, c. 1300) as a ceiling, not as a Reval measurement.
  * **Open rowing / ferry boat** - **`plausible composite`**. Smaller than Gedesby; oars primary. Exact Reval length is unknown.
  * **Lighter / Estonian *lodi* (lodja-type)** - **`plausible composite`** as a shoal-draft transfer barge; 14th-c. origin is handbook tradition. Do not use 20th-c. *OAT* / Jõmmu numbers, a steering wheel, or a diesel as 1343 fact.
  * **Saaremaa strait craft** - **`plausible composite`** as an open beachable clinker boat. Salme I/II are 8th-9th c. and must not be copied as 1343 lengths.
  * **Rejected 1343 features** - gaff and other later fore-and-aft mainsails, lateen on a cog, multi-masted *kraweel*, ratlines as a shroud ladder, full-carvel cog sides, four stone piers of cogs, *haabjas* as the strait ferry, and 19th-20th-c. *juha* / *lootsik* types. See the report reject list.
* **Building families (Spring 1343)** - mixed labels; full cards in [`reports/reval_architecture_typology_1343.md`](./reports/reval_architecture_typology_1343.md) (R-959)
  * **Diele-dornse merchant house** - **`attested`** as a Reval/Hanseatic type (gable to street, cellar). The 7-11 m strip is an H04 analogical band (**`plausible composite`**), not a measured 1343 frontage. Hoist practice is a later-survival type (H27); April 1343 prevalence is **`plausible composite`**. Per-house wood/stone mix and 1343 hypocaust share are **`plausible composite`**. Door/window metres and roof pitch by cover are **`unknown`**.
  * **Timber front and craft *boda*** - **`attested`** coexistence of timber and stone (H04); *boda* as a named two-room rented workshop (H04; AWB 508 *bodarum*, 1341). Exact 1343 *boda* dimensions **`unknown`**. Decorative diagonal Fachwerk is not a Reval default.
  * **Log smoke dwelling and two-part barn-dwelling** - **`attested`** types in Estonia; chimneyless. Named 1343 farm plans and museum exteriors are not 1343 fact.
  * **Footings under timber walls (R-1933)** - a shallow footing (~0.5 m) and a timber or mixed sill on urban timber houses (typology §2, H04/H26), and ground-level log buildings on stone pads rather than a high urban plinth (typology §3, H28) are **`plausible composite`**. The seamless city shows plank walls on a fieldstone sill under a squared sill beam, log walls on low fieldstone pads under a round sill log crossing at the corners, and dressed limestone courses under stone houses; stone sizes, sill sections and heights are **`unknown`** as 1343 measurements (presentation values).

## Daily Life & Social Relations (14th Century Reval)

* **Gothic Tenement Layouts (Dual-Purpose Homes)** - **`attested`**
  * *Context:* Stone houses in Reval's lower town combined residential and commercial/storage uses. Ground floors served as a workshop and living space, while upper floors under high gables were granaries accessed via a winch.
  * *Actionable hook:* Kalev’s smithy features an upper storage loft accessed by a winch, providing a plausible mechanic to hide contraband, rebel supplies, or illicit weapons from the city watch.
* **Dietary Stratification** - **`attested`**
  * *Context:* The basic 1340s diet relied heavily on seasonal grains (rye/barley bread, pottage), root vegetables, and Baltic herring. Fresh meat was a luxury for wealthy Hanseatic merchants; poorer citizens utilized offal. Ale was the ubiquitous safe drink.
  * *Actionable hook:* Food acts as a social indicator and bribery tool in-game. Offering rare meat or imported goods can buy silence or favor from a hungry watchman or indebted apprentice.
* **Ethnic and Class Divisions (All-linn vs. Toompea)** - **`attested`**
  * *Context:* Reval was sharply divided physically and socially. The upper town (Toompea) housed Danish and German aristocracy, while the lower town (All-linn) was run by German Hanseatic merchants. Native Estonians formed a large underclass (laborers, lesser artisans) facing increasing exclusion.
  * *Actionable hook:* Kalev encounters systemic prejudice from German guildsmen but can leverage his essential craft to bypass social barriers. The physical division between the towns dictates where and how openly rebel sympathizers can meet.
* **The Blacksmith's Mundane Indispensability** - **`attested`**
  * *Context:* A blacksmith’s primary income came from everyday necessities—horseshoes, nails, agricultural tools, locks, and cooking utensils—not just military weapons. 
  * *Actionable hook:* Side commissions involve repairing mundane items (e.g., a merchant's strongbox lock or an alewife's cauldron), giving Kalev unique conversational access to secrets and rumors from all social strata.
* **Rural Resentment and Weaponization of Tools** - **`attested`**
  * *Context:* The St. George's Night Uprising was fueled by oppressive taxes and forced labor imposed on the Estonian peasantry by German vassals. 
  * *Actionable hook:* Rural contacts like Kaja bring news of escalating abuses, allowing Kalev the choice to covertly modify everyday agricultural tools (like scythes or billhooks) into rebel polearms right under the authorities' noses.
* **Yard wells, not harbour drinking water** - **`attested`** (wells exist) / **`plausible composite`** (who shares them)
  * *Context:* Mid-14th-century Lower Town archaeology records courtyard wells and gutters; the Bishop's Garden and the Dominican close each have a medieval well. Household drinking and wash water are well and cistern water. Harbour, ditch, and moat water are not authored as drink. Well lining, depth, and wells-per-street remain unknown. Full tables: [`docs/reports/reval_domestic_infrastructure_1343.md`](./reports/reval_domestic_infrastructure_1343.md) (R-984).
  * *Actionable hook:* A dry well, a shared sweep, or a neighbour who controls the yard water is a pressure point without inventing a water-carrier guild.
* **No well on the market forum** - **`plausible composite`** (clear market reserve, carried water) / **`attested`** (Rataskaev 1375, later than our date)
  * *Context:* No reviewed document or excavation puts a well on the open *forum* (modern Raekoja plats) in 1343, so the absence is a **gap** in the record that we author as a negative; Neumann & Nottbeck 1904 record *Kramerbuden* by the Rathaus in 1339 but no market well, and the modern decorative well cover on the square edge is not evidence. The nearest documented public well is the **Sternsod / Rataskaev** wheel-well *sub monte*, first named **30 April 1375** in the Tallinn *pärusraamat* - 32 years after our April, so its well head is not 1343 fabric either. The *forum* is a market reserve kept clear for stalls and carts; water is **carried in** from yard and precinct wells. Full reasoning: [`history/dossiers/topography/raekoja-plats-extents-1343.md`](../history/dossiers/topography/raekoja-plats-extents-1343.md) (R-1207); water budget in [`docs/reports/reval_domestic_infrastructure_1343.md`](./reports/reval_domestic_infrastructure_1343.md) (R-984).
  * *Actionable hook:* Children and servants hauling buckets **across** the square, not queueing at a well head, is the gossip channel. Who lets you draw from their yard well is a favour, so a water errand opens a courtyard the player could not otherwise enter.
* **Swimming harbour folk** - **`plausible composite`** (ability) / **`invented`** (Kalev)
  * *Context:* Ordinary harbour folk, fishermen and some sailors in 14th-century Reval could swim, while many seafarers could not; it was a personal skill, not a norm. Medieval lists of knightly accomplishments (for example Petrus Alfonsi's *Disciplina Clericalis*) include swimming, and Vegetius, read in the period, recommends it for soldiers. No Reval-specific source is known.
  * *Actionable hook:* Kalev, a smith raised near the shore, can wade, swim and dive in [ADR 0021](adr/0021-swimming-and-diving.md). Being seen swimming at the harbour at night is a suspicious-activity hook, not a new meter.
* **Rear-yard privy and chip heaps** - **`plausible composite`**
  * *Context:* Burgher strip plots put the privy, well sweep, and firewood in the rear yard. NUKU courtyard layers include manure and chips. A 1343 municipal dung-cart contract and a Reval cesspit lining are unknown; Tartu 1335 stone latrines stay a foreign comparandum.
  * *Actionable hook:* Council pressure keeps night soil out of the forum throat. Kalev's ash pile is a neighbour complaint, not a sewer puzzle.
* **Hearth heat and charcoal at the forge** - **`attested`** (hearth and yard woodpile as types) / **`plausible composite`** (forge charcoal practice)
  * *Context:* Ordinary houses cook and heat at a corner hearth or hooded fire. A rear-yard woodpile is expected. Kalev's forge burns charcoal in a roofed crib; household winter cordwood volume and a 1343 Reval fire ordinance are unknown. Later brick chimney pots are rejected.
  * *Actionable hook:* Charcoal shortfalls after 23 April pinch the forge first. Bath *stupa* wood dues in AWB 553 are civic, not a household tax Kalev pays.
* **Market weekday is not canon** - **`invented`** (runtime fallback only)
  * *Context:* The civic market is the Raekoja *forum* (attested from 1313). No reviewed AWB or council line names the 1340-1343 weekly market day. `MarketDayModel` Wednesday/Saturday remains an implementation fallback.
  * *Actionable hook:* Dialogue may say *forum* or *markt*. It must not say "Wednesday market" as history.

## Door construction and entrance hardware (R-1692)

- **`attested` (later comparison, not 1343 evidence):** the King's Chapel door in Tallinn Dome has vertical pine planks, inclined bars and outer oak boarding (Läänelaid et al., *Dendrochronologia*, 2025, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dendro.2025.126462)). The [accessible secondary account](https://arkeonews.net/one-of-northern-europes-oldest-doors-found-estimated-to-be-650-years-old/) reports oak cut around the early 1380s. Do not silently backdate that surviving door to the playable period. Publisher full text was inaccessible in this pass.
- **`plausible composite`:** differentiated board leaves, ledges, straps, nail heads, iron rings and restrained branched ironwork at the council hall and churches in the seamless city. No exact named entrance fitting is claimed as attested in 1343. The modern [Doors of Tallinn](https://doorsoftallinn.com/) photo collection is inspiration only, not dating evidence or a source of runtime textures. Profile decisions, IDs and limitations: [City doors](./SYSTEMS/CITY_DOORS.md).

## Landmark entrance portals (R-1693)

- **`attested` (later than 1343):** Holy Spirit's north entrance is a stepped portal with a moulded archivolt and pointed arch, from the Gothic rebuild begun about the mid-14th century ([medievalheritage.eu](https://medievalheritage.eu/en/main-page/heritage/estonia/tallinn-holy-spirit-church-puha-vaimu-kirik/), after Naha and Peets 1933). The game shows a simpler two-order predecessor (**`plausible`**).
- **`attested`:** pointed, moulded portals at St Nicholas (tower and aisle; a 13th-century south portal with a trefoil archivolt) and a stepped pointed south portal at St Mary's (Dome), dated to the late 13th and early 14th centuries ([medievalheritage.eu](https://medievalheritage.eu/en/main-page/heritage/estonia/tallinn-st-nicholas-church/), [grande flanerie](https://grandeflanerie.com/gothictallinn/)). Exact order counts and mouldings in the game are **`plausible`**; St Olaf's portal profiles are **`speculative`**.
- **`attested` regional analogy:** medieval Gotland church leaves cut to their portal heads (Havdhem) and mid-14th-century stepped portals (Stånga). Not Tallinn evidence.
- **Caution:** some "medieval" Tallinn portals are largely 20th-century restorations (Paju 2022, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.12697/BJAH.2022.23.02)); surviving portals and doors are not copied. Decisions per door: [City doors](./SYSTEMS/CITY_DOORS.md#landmark-portals-r-1693).
