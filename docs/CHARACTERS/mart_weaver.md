## Naming rule
This character is **Mart Weaver**, not the vertical-slice apprentice **Mart** (`char.mart`). Player-facing copy must use the surname or the full name whenever confusion is possible. Do not merge this brief with `docs/CHARACTERS/mart.md`, create a second Kalev, or promote the Blackheads candidate seat into the eight-faction launch table.

## Visual Description
- **Clothes:** Undyed light-brown wool tunic, darker woven vest, grey linen trousers, and sturdy work boots; colours are practical and muted.
- **Age:** Early fifties.
- **Hair:** Light brown streaked with grey, tied back; full, neatly kept beard.
- **Physical Build:** Stocky and grounded, with strong hands and patient, deliberate movements.

## Motivations & Core
- **Want:** Protect Estonian craft knowledge and build enough mutual support that artisans can survive foreign rule without waiting for a noble protector.
- **Fear:** That a public act of defiance will bring fines, confiscation, or a watch raid onto every workshop in his network.
- **Contradiction:** He distrusts merchant power but uses Blackheads access and credit to shelter the very artisans he wants independent.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He has begun a quiet list of workshops willing to pass messages or supplies, but keeps it in pattern shorthand so no single arrest exposes the whole network.

## History (Biography)
Mart Weaver came to Reval from Harju County after violence displaced his family and learned the trade in a German-speaking workshop. Years of patient work made him a respected master among Lower Town artisans. He entered Blackheads circles for protection and access, then turned those relationships toward practical aid: finding work, moving materials, and preserving names that official ledgers would flatten into debts.

His candidate-seat role is local and bounded. He can support a forge contact, a banner commission, or an artisan message chain, but he does not become the apprentice Mart's replacement, a ninth launch faction leader, or a general rebellion commander.

## Ties & Relationships
- **Allies:** Lower Town weavers and dyers, trusted Blackheads organisers, and workers who need discreet material support.
- **Enemies:** Order requisitioners, guild officials who sell artisan names, and merchants who treat culture as disposable branding.
- **Initial View of the Main Player (Kalev):** A fellow craftsperson who understands that a useful object can protect a household as surely as a speech can inspire it.
- **Johann von Minden:** Appreciates Johann's reach but watches for the moment protection becomes ownership.
- **Mart the Apprentice:** Keeps careful distance and uses the surname "Weaver" in every written handoff; their goals and ages are not interchangeable.

## Daily Routines
He works at a loom from sunrise through the afternoon, checks dye and thread stocks with neighbouring artisans, and takes a quiet tavern table after dusk to hear what the market and watch are doing. He sleeps above the workshop among tools, samples, and concealed message patterns.

## Voice
Warm, deliberate, and image-rich without becoming theatrical. He compares politics to warp, weft, tension, and frayed thread. He gives advice in complete sentences, pauses before naming a risk, and prefers a practical offer to a heroic promise.

## Possible Quest Lines & Outcomes
- **The Weaver's Network:** Carry materials and messages between workshops while keeping the network's structure hidden.
- **A Banner Without a Name:** Help source legal-looking dyes and thread for a symbolic commission without turning the workshop into a public rebel headquarters.
- **Quest Objectives:** Protect one artisan, choose what information to record, and resolve whether the candidate-seat event should remain private or become public pressure.
- **Rewards:** An artisan contact, safer material access, or a candidate event tied to `ledger.blackheads.artisan_network`.
- **Arc:** Protected master -> quiet organiser -> accountable civic patron.
- **Endings:** Network preserved, network compromised but survivors protected, or Mart Weaver forced into public leadership he never wanted.
