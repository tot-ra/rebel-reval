## Naming and canon boundary
Brother Hermann is an adapted Order handler, not a claim that the legacy scene is a literal historical biography. His name and role remain revisable under the Act 3 design gate **P6-001**. He must not be used to add a new faction or a second Order commander to the launch cast.

## Visual Description
- **Clothes:** Undyed wool habit beneath a dark travelling mantle, plain leather belt, and weathered riding boots; restrained Order colours and no oversized heraldic display.
- **Age:** Mid-forties.
- **Hair:** Tonsured dark hair with iron-grey at the temples; close-trimmed beard.
- **Physical Build:** Spare, straight-backed, and economical in movement; a calm face trained to reveal little.

## Motivations & Core
- **Want:** Preserve Order authority in Reval by turning uncertainty into obedience before the city chooses a side.
- **Fear:** That local bargains and divided commanders will make the Order look weak enough for every district to test it.
- **Contradiction:** He calls coercion pastoral care while privately admiring the civic discipline he refuses to grant to Estonian townspeople.
- **Secret or withheld fact:** He keeps a private list of craftsmen whose work enters Order stores, using it as leverage rather than admitting how dependent the garrison is on local labour.

## History (Biography)
Hermann is a plausible composite assembled from the Order handler role in `story/STORY.md` and the campaign's confidence-labelled political landscape. He learned account-keeping, chapel protocol, and the practical management of requisitions before becoming a travelling intermediary between Order officers, Toompea authority, and Lower Town suppliers. He is useful because he can speak to a smith, a commander, or a prior without appearing to belong fully to any of them.

He enters Act 1 as a controlled source of pressure around Henning's watch and the Order's demands. The later forced-forge material is an Act 3 design hook, not current slice content; it must be reconciled with the accepted Act 3 plan before implementation.

## Ties & Relationships
- **Allies:** Livonian Order officers, Order quartermasters, and Captain Henning when the watch needs an official voice.
- **Enemies:** Black Cloaks organisers, uncooperative guild intermediaries, and any commander who turns a local dispute into open disorder.
- **Initial View of the Main Player (Kalev):** A capable local hinge whose craft can either stabilise the city or make resistance materially possible.
- **Captain Henning:** Uses Henning's authority but distrusts his appetite for visible punishment.
- **Mart:** Treats the apprentice as a useful pressure point, never confusing him with Martin of the Cloaks or Mart Weaver.

## Daily Routines
He rotates between Order storehouses, chapel offices, Toompea audiences, and short escorted visits to Lower Town workshops. He writes his own requisition notes at night and sleeps wherever the current duty route ends, usually in an Order lodging rather than a private household.

## Voice
Soft, formal, and precise. He asks questions as if they were mercies and uses religious language to make administrative demands sound inevitable. When angered, he becomes quieter and switches to short Low German terms familiar to merchants and guards.

## Possible Quest Lines & Outcomes
- **The Account That Does Not Balance:** Reconcile a requisition ledger without exposing whether a shortage came from theft, concealment, or an honest mistake.
- **Witness at the Gate:** Decide whether to repeat Hermann's version of a watch incident or preserve a craft worker's contradictory account.
- **Quest Objectives:** Inspect records, identify the pressure point, and choose whom the report protects; later Act 3 uses remain gated behind P6-001.
- **Rewards:** Access to an Order contact, a cleaner travel pass, or evidence of the Order's dependency on local workshops.
- **Arc:** Official intermediary -> coercive negotiator -> accountable architect of the player's forced choices.
- **Endings:** Reformed broker, exposed administrator, or Act 3 antagonist whose authority survives while his moral claim does not.
